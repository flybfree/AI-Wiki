## Summary  
The paper identifies a failure mode in on‑policy distillation where students achieve near‑perfect token agreement while the teacher’s reasoning is globally flawed, producing degenerate outputs. It shifts focus from token agreement to measuring mismatch between student and teacher responses, classifying mismatches into two types: student‑excess tokens (generated by the student but assigned low probability by the teacher) and student‑deficit tokens (teacher‑preferred tokens that are rarely sampled). The authors propose TIDE, a correction method that bounds Hellinger distance for excesses and injects the top‑K teacher probabilities to restore missing mass without requiring deficit tokens to be sampled. This approach improves performance on reasoning benchmarks by aligning student outputs with genuine teacher knowledge rather than superficial repetition.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Degenerate agreement arises from repetitive loops that cause near‑perfect token agreement despite globally flawed responses.  
- [Finding 2] Mismatch tokens are categorized into student‑excess and student‑deficit types, each with distinct impact on learning.  
- [Finding 3] TIDE corrects these mismatches using bounded Hellinger shaping to suppress severe excesses and an analytic teacher top‑K injection to restore deficient probability mass.

## Methodology  
The authors analyze token‑level probability distributions of teacher and student outputs to quantify excess and deficit tokens. They then apply a correction pipeline: first, they compute the Hellinger distance between sampled student tokens and the teacher distribution, applying a hard cap (bounded Hellinger shaping) to suppress high‑risk excesses; second, they inject the top‑K probability mass from the teacher’s distribution into the student token space, ensuring that missing high‑probability tokens are restored. The correction is applied per token during distillation without requiring deficit tokens to be observed.

## Results  
Experiments on multiple Qwen3 teacher‑student pairs across mathematical reasoning tasks show TIDE outperforms standard OPD and recent baselines in Avg@8 (6.9 % → 20.3%), reduces average response length by a factor of 3.6, and cuts formatting failures substantially. The gains are especially pronounced when teacher‑student mismatch is strong.

## Significance  
By moving beyond token agreement to measure genuine reasoning mismatch, TIDE provides a more robust distillation signal that aligns student outputs with the teacher’s actual knowledge rather than superficial repetition. This leads to higher accuracy, shorter answers, and fewer formatting errors, making it valuable for practical LLM fine‑tuning pipelines.
