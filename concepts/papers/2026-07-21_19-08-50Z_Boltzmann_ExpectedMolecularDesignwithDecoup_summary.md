## Summary  
The paper proposes Boltzmann‑Expected Molecular Design (BEMD) using Decoupled Annealing Flows (DECAF), treating molecular design as an expectation over the Boltzmann distribution of 3D configurations. It introduces a two‑stage flow model that separates a graph‑conditioned Boltzmann emulator from a coordinate‑conditioned generator, allowing ensemble statistics to drive optimisation rather than single‑conformer properties. The method also extends to higher‑moment objectives by jointly controlling variance and skewness of ensemble properties. Experiments on GEOM‑Drugs demonstrate measurable improvements in target shape descriptors compared with conventional approaches.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] DECAF factorises the joint graph‑coordinate distribution into two conditional flows: p(x|G) as a Boltzmann emulator and p(G|x) as a graph generator, enabling an alternating optimisation loop.  
- [Finding 2] Ensemble‑aware BEMD yields molecules whose mean radius of gyration and solvent‑accessible surface area shift toward design targets on GEOM‑Drugs, whereas single‑conformer baselines degrade for larger, more flexible drug‑like molecules.  
- [Finding 3] The framework is extended to multi‑objective trade‑offs and higher‑moment design, jointly optimising variance and skewness of ensemble statistics and verifying the resulting conformational bias via all‑atom MD.

## Methodology  
The authors recast molecular design as a Boltzmann‑expected problem, where the target is an expectation over configurations drawn from p(x|G). DECAF consists of two conditional flows: the graph‑conditioned flow p(x|G) approximates the Boltzmann distribution (acting as an emulator), and the coordinate‑conditioned flow p(G|x) proposes new graphs conditioned on 3D information. Optimisation proceeds via simulated‑annealing, accepting proposals based on scores evaluated from ensembles generated by p(x|G). The decoupled architecture means that changing objectives does not require retraining.

## Results  
On GEOM‑Drugs, BEMD produces molecules with mean radius of gyration and solvent‑accessible surface area consistently moving toward the design targets, while single‑conformer optimisation shows a clear degradation on larger molecules whose Boltzmann distributions are broad. The higher‑moment extension demonstrates that variance and skewness can be jointly tuned to generate flexible molecules biased toward a prescribed conformational regime; MD simulations confirm these statistical properties.

## Significance  
By aligning design objectives with the true ensemble of conformations, BEMD improves robustness for drug‑like molecules where single structures are insufficient. The decoupled flow architecture avoids retraining for new objectives and enables higher‑order statistical control, opening a path to more realistic molecular design that respects physical constraints and statistical realism.
