## Summary  
The paper seeks to map how authority in financial decision making is shared between humans and AI, moving beyond the topic of conversation to quantify the degree of delegated decision authority that users actually grant. By applying a behavioural measurement framework that links user intent with the extent of control they assign to an AI, the authors establish a baseline for assessing the transition toward increasingly agentic AI in finance. Their analysis of 1.5 million ChatGPT and Gemini interactions from 6,304 users across the United States and India reveals that while AI is widely used as an information source, human delegation of actual financial execution remains rare.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Consumers overwhelmingly use AI to retrieve information and shape their judgment, with most queries being informational rather than actionable.  
- [Finding 2] Delegation of decision authority is uncommon; the majority of financial decisions are still made by humans, indicating a limited shift toward autonomous AI agents.  
- [Finding 3] The intent‑delegated authority framework provides a novel behavioural metric that can track how much control users transfer to AI over time.

## Methodology  
The authors combined two dimensions: (1) **intent** – the type of user request expressed in the chat, and (2) **delegated decision authority** – the explicit or implicit level of control the user grants to the AI for executing a financial action. They collected 1.5 million real‑world interactions from 6,304 users in the US and India, focusing on queries related to banking, investing, and budgeting. The dataset was coded to classify each interaction as informational, advisory, or delegative, allowing statistical comparison across user groups.

## Results  
Quantitative analysis shows that roughly 80 % of financial‑related AI interactions are intent‑driven toward information retrieval, while only about 15 % involve any form of delegated execution. Delegation rates vary little between the two countries (US: 14 %, India: 16 %). The framework also identifies a clear hierarchy: users first seek advice, then occasionally grant limited authority for simple actions such as confirming a transaction, but rarely delegate complex financial decisions like portfolio rebalancing.

## Significance  
These findings matter because they provide the first empirical baseline for measuring how much decision‑making power is actually transferred to AI in everyday finance. By separating informational use from delegated authority, the work helps designers and regulators understand where human oversight is still essential and where AI can be safely empowered, guiding policies on trustworthy agentic systems.
