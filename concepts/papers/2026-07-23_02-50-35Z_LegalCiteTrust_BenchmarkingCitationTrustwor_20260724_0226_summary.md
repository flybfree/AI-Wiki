## Summary  
LegalCiteTrust is a benchmark designed to evaluate citation trustworthiness in Chinese long‑form legal research reports generated by large language models (LLMs) and agentic systems. The authors introduce a three‑dimensional framework—Coverage, Support, and Citation Trustworthiness—and operationalize the latter through citation‑level Existence, Fidelity, and Applicability (E/F/A). By annotating 72 report‑level tasks, they provide a systematic way to measure how well systems retrieve, describe, and apply legal authorities reliably.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] LegalCiteTrust creates a comprehensive benchmark with annotated reports across three dimensions of citation trustworthiness.  
- [Finding 2] Retrieval tools improve evidence support but do not significantly boost trust scores; E/F/A‑based revision yields clearer improvements in trust and final scores compared to existence‑only filtering.  
- [Finding 3] Different system types (general‑purpose LLMs, deep‑research systems, legal‑specific models) exhibit distinct behaviors regarding citation density, evidence richness, and reliability.  

## Methodology  
The authors assembled LegalCiteTrust by curating 72 Chinese long‑form legal research reports that have been densely annotated at both report and citation levels. Citation Trustworthiness is measured using three criteria per citation: Existence (does the source actually exist?), Fidelity (is the description of the authority accurate?), and Applicability (can the cited authority be legitimately applied to support the claim?). Experiments compare task completion, evidence richness, citation density, and trust scores across three system categories—general‑purpose LLMs, deep‑research systems, and legal‑specific models.  

## Results  
General‑purpose LLMs achieve high task completion rates but generate low citation reliability, often citing non‑existent or misdescribed authorities. Deep‑research systems produce richer evidence sets yet still suffer from fidelity errors and poor applicability. Legal‑specific models improve Fidelity scores but do not resolve Applicability issues. The E/F/A revision approach raises Trust scores more than simple existence filtering, leading to higher Final scores across all system groups.  

## Significance  
These findings demonstrate that trustworthy legal research generation requires citation‑aware governance beyond mere retrieval; systems must select, describe, and apply authorities responsibly. By exposing the trade‑offs between evidence richness and citation reliability, LegalCiteTrust guides future work on AI systems that can produce legally sound, trustworthy reports.
