## Summary  
EEG‑JEPA proposes a structured latent‑prediction framework for EEG foundation models that shifts pretraining from recovering missing voltage measurements to inferring hidden neural states. By organizing supervision along three complementary dimensions—target content, target support via Neurotopology‑Aware Multi‑scale Electrode‑Temporal Masking (N‑MET), and target depth at specific encoder layers—the method encourages the model to learn representations that are task‑transferable rather than artifact‑driven. The framework is evaluated against conventional masked waveform reconstruction on a large, unlabeled EEG corpus.

## Key Contributions  
- **Structured latent prediction**: EEG‑JEPA replaces voltage‑sample reconstruction with the inference of contextual latent states generated by an exponential‑moving‑average target encoder that observes the full input.  
- **Three‑dimensional mask design**: The framework introduces *target content* (what is predicted), *target support* (where prediction occurs across structured electrode–time regions using N‑MET), and *target depth* (which encoder layers receive supervision).  
- **Empirical gains**: Under identical backbone, corpus, and training time, EEG‑JEPA lifts the 14‑task frozen macro balanced accuracy from 40.49 % to 52.94 %, the highest average among evaluated foundation models on EEG‑FM‑Bench.

## Methodology  
EEG‑JEPA builds a masked context encoder that processes the complete EEG trace and outputs a latent state at each time step. The target encoder employs an exponential moving average (EMA) to generate a smooth, forward‑compatible prediction of this latent state. N‑MET defines structured electrode–time masks that isolate specific anatomical regions and temporal windows, ensuring that supervision is applied only where neural activity is expected to be meaningful. Supervision is then routed to selected encoder layers via the *target depth* parameter, allowing gradient flow at appropriate abstraction levels.

## Results  
- Frozen macro balanced accuracy: 40.49 % (baseline) → 52.94 % (EEG‑JEPA).  
- Multi‑source continuation further improves average performance to 52.94 %, the top score on EEG‑FM‑Bench.  
- Full fine‑tuning yields a nine‑task average balanced accuracy of 70.65 %, up from 68.98 % with conventional masked reconstruction.

## Significance  
By targeting latent neural states rather than raw voltage measurements, EEG‑JEPA reduces the risk that pretraining will simply memorize predictable background activity or acquisition artifacts. This yields representations that generalize across tasks and modalities, a critical advantage for large‑scale foundation models where data are scarce and labeled supervision is expensive.
