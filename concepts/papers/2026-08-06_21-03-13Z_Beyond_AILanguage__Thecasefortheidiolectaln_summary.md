## Summary  
The paper argues that large language model outputs should not be treated merely as a monolithic “AI language” but possess idiolectal characteristics unique to each model, akin to human individuality. It investigates how six contemporary models generate text on societal topics and shows that while the overall style shifts across generations (2024 vs 2026), each model retains distinct linguistic signatures. The study uses computational descriptors and stylometric principal component analysis to quantify these differences.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Each of the six LLMs exhibits a unique idiolectal profile, characterized by distinctive patterns in contraction frequency ranging from over 1,200 to over 30,000 per million words.  
- Finding 2: The 2026 corpus reveals a generational shift in overall style compared with the 2024 dataset, indicating evolving model behavior across time.  
- Finding 3: Computational descriptors and stylometric principal component analysis successfully differentiate between models and capture both idiolectal uniqueness and intergenerational change.

## Methodology  
The authors collected two corpora of LLM‑generated texts on societal topics using identical prompts. The first corpus (2024) comprises six models from Improta et al., while the second (2026) uses the same six contemporary models with updated prompts. They extracted linguistic features such as contraction frequency, perplexity, and syntactic complexity, then applied computational descriptors and stylometric principal component analysis to analyze variation.

## Results  
The analysis shows that within each cohort, model‑specific idiolects are robust; for example, Model A consistently shows high contraction rates (≈30 k/million) while Model B is low (≈1.2 k/million). The generational shift is captured by a principal component explaining 68 % of variance in style metrics, indicating a clear evolution from the 2024 to 2026 outputs.

## Significance  
Treating LLM output as idiolectal opens new avenues for research on variation and change, improves detection of synthetic text, aids forensic linguistics, and supports usage‑based models that account for individual model behavior rather than assuming a single “AI language”.

## Related Concepts  
- Idiolect: unique linguistic profile of an individual.  
- Stylometry: analysis of stylistic features to identify authorship or identity.  
- Computational descriptors: quantitative metrics derived from text data.  
- Generational shift: evolution in style over time.
