## Summary  
The paper proposes the “Grip on LLMs” framework to evaluate large language models for Dutch governmental applications by aligning evaluation with public‑administration values and non‑English linguistic needs. It introduces a benchmark suite that operationalises six dimensions—factuality, honesty, social bias, energy consumption, cost, and training data transparency—and tests over 30 multilingual and Dutch‑specific models to reveal unavoidable trade‑offs in performance versus environmental and financial impact.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: No single model excels across all evaluation dimensions; higher factuality and honesty are not mutually reinforcing.  
- Finding 2: Environmental impact and cost increase as model quality rises, indicating a clear sustainability‑financial trade‑off.  
- Finding 3: Social bias remains largely independent of both factuality and honesty, suggesting it is a separate issue to be addressed separately.

## Methodology  
The authors collaborated with domain experts from a major Dutch municipal organisation through an advisory board process, conducted user research on civil‑servant chatbot users, and surveyed stakeholders. This participatory approach identified the six evaluation dimensions and guided the creation of a benchmark suite covering diverse multilingual and Dutch‑specific models.

## Results  
Empirical testing showed that factuality (correctness of answers) and honesty (acknowledgement of uncertainty) are distinct properties: high factuality does not guarantee high honesty. Energy consumption and financial cost rise with model quality, while social bias shows little correlation with either. The benchmark suite demonstrates that improving one dimension often degrades another, highlighting the need for balanced selection.

## Significance  
These findings provide actionable guidance for non‑technical policymakers and engineers selecting LLMs in Dutch public services, ensuring that sustainability, cost, and bias are considered alongside performance. By making a user‑friendly model overview publicly available, the work supports inclusive decision‑making across the governmental ecosystem.
