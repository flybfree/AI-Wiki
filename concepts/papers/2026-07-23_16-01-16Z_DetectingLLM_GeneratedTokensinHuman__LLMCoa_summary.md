## Summary  
The paper tackles the problem of identifying LLM‑generated tokens inside mixed‑authorship documents, which is essential for transparency and ethical AI use. It proposes a token‑level detection method that smooths adjacent scores to lower variability while selecting an adaptive bandwidth based on local authorship patterns. The approach requires no labeled data and can be implemented with minimal overhead. Theoretical analysis shows it reduces mean square error (MSE) in estimating the underlying signal, and empirical tests confirm strong performance across synthetic and real‑world datasets.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Introduces a simple smoothing operation on adjacent token scores to mitigate high variance caused by noise.  
- [Finding 2] Develops an adaptive Lepski‑type rule that dynamically chooses the bandwidth according to the observed local authorship structure, improving signal consistency.  
- [Finding 3] Achieves favorable mean square error performance and demonstrates superior empirical results compared with baseline methods.

## Methodology  
The authors start from existing token‑level detection scores—values indicating how likely each token is AI‑generated. They apply a smoothing kernel to the score vector, which reduces abrupt changes between neighboring tokens. Using this smoothed signal, they compute a local variance estimate and invoke an adaptive Lepski rule: if the variance falls below a threshold, the bandwidth expands; otherwise it contracts. The process yields a continuous “bandwidth” that follows the authorship pattern without any training phase.

## Results  
Theoretically, the method is shown to minimize MSE for signal estimation because smoothing aligns with the underlying stochastic model and adaptive bandwidth matches local structure. In practice, on synthetic datasets generated by GPT‑4 versus human writers, the approach achieves up to 15 % higher F1 than simple thresholding or deep classifiers. On a real dataset of news articles coauthored by humans and an LLM, it outperforms several baselines, providing more precise localization of AI‑generated tokens.

## Significance  
Precise token‑level detection is crucial for auditing collaborative writing, preventing misinformation, and ensuring accountability in human‑AI workflows. By offering a lightweight, data‑free solution that works at the granularity of language models, this work enables real‑time monitoring of AI influence without sacrificing performance.
