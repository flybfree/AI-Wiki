## Summary  
The paper aims to provide a comprehensive tutorial and survey that unifies the theoretical foundations of agentic Artificial Intelligence (AI) with the control architectures of 5G and upcoming 6G networks. It addresses a critical gap by treating network AI and telecommunications standards as interdependent rather than isolated domains. Part I formalises the AI‑native planes—reasoning, planning, tool use, multi‑agent coordination, and evaluation—while Part II maps these capabilities onto existing 5G/6G control surfaces, standardization efforts, and future initiatives. The dual structure enables readers to understand both the technical architecture of autonomous agents and their integration into network standards.  

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: The authors formalise a unified model that delineates AI‑native layers (reasoning, planning, tool use) from traditional network control planes, establishing a clear boundary for where autonomous decision‑making begins.  
- Finding 2: They map each agentic capability onto specific 5G/6G control surfaces such as RAN orchestration APIs and network slicing mechanisms, providing concrete integration pathways.  
- Finding 3: The survey identifies open challenges—including real‑time latency constraints, cross‑domain evaluation metrics, and alignment with emerging 6G standards—that shape the development of autonomous telecom systems.  

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by constructing a two‑part tutorial‑and‑survey framework that first builds theoretical foundations in Part I, then applies those concepts to real‑world network architectures in Part II. This layered methodology ensures that abstract AI principles are grounded in concrete telecommunications protocols and standards.  

## Results  
The survey enumerates a taxonomy of LLM‑driven architectures—including network control agents, policy executors, and self‑optimising RAN controllers—and outlines the relevant 5G Release 18 specifications and 6G vision documents. It also proposes evaluation criteria that combine latency, reliability, and adaptability to assess autonomous system performance.  

## Significance  
This work bridges the gap between AI autonomy and telecommunications standards, enabling researchers to design self‑optimizing networks that can adapt in real time without manual intervention. By aligning agentic capabilities with existing 5G/6G frameworks, it paves the way for truly intelligent, resilient network infrastructures.
