## Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of discovering Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) from sparse and noisy observational data, a task that traditionally struggles with the instability inherent in coupled neural optimization processes. The authors identify three distinct behaviors in optimization paths—persistent support, transient appearance, or complete failure—and propose a novel "freeze-then-select" framework to decouple field reconstruction from equation selection. By introducing Structured Field Adapters and Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS), the method first reconstructs continuous fields without relying on PDE residuals, then identifies recurrent differential terms across independent weak-form systems. This approach significantly improves exact support recovery rates, particularly for complex dynamics like the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, demonstrating robustness where classical and other neural baselines fail.

## Key Contributions
- **Decoupling Optimization from Selection**: The authors demonstrate that separating field reconstruction from term selection via a "freeze-then-select" strategy mitigates the instability issues found in traditional coupled neural PDE discovery methods, allowing for more reliable identification of governing equations.
- **Structured Field Adapter with SVWS**: They introduce a novel architecture that factorizes fields into learned spatial features and temporal coefficients using cubic splines, combined with Stability-Validated Weak Selection to rigorously identify recurrent terms across independent weak-form systems.
- **Generalization Beyond Fixed Libraries**: The framework is successfully applied to expressions generated by genetic programming, enabling the recovery of unknown nonlinear diffusion functions (such as power-law forms) from sparse data, showcasing its flexibility beyond standard symbolic regression libraries.

## Methodology
The methodology begins with an analysis of optimization paths in coupled neural PDE discovery, revealing that exact support persistence is inconsistent. To address this, the authors develop a Structured Field Adapter trained directly on observations without any PDE residual constraints. This adapter factorizes the observed field into learned spatial features and temporal coefficients represented by cubic splines. Once the field is reconstructed and "frozen," the Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) algorithm is employed. SVWS identifies recurrent terms across independent weak-form systems, refits candidate supports, and selects the final equation on held-out weak-form systems. This two-stage process ensures that the selected terms are stable and not artifacts of the optimization trajectory. The method is tested on both fixed libraries and genetic programming-generated expressions to validate its robustness against noise and sparsity.

## Results
The proposed method achieves the highest exact support recovery rate across all six sparse regimes of the MDBench benchmark. Notably, it demonstrates the clearest gains over classical and neural baselines when dealing with challenging Kuramoto-Sivashinsky dynamics, which are known for their complexity and sensitivity to noise. The ability to recover power-law forms from unknown nonlinear diffusion functions further validates the method's effectiveness in handling non-standard and highly nonlinear systems where traditional libraries fall short.

## Significance
This work matters because it provides a robust, stable framework for PDE discovery from sparse data, a common scenario in physical sciences and engineering. By decoupling reconstruction from selection, it offers a more reliable path to discovering governing laws from limited observations, potentially accelerating scientific discovery in fields where data is scarce or noisy.
