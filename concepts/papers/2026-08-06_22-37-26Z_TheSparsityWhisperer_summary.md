## Summary  
The paper argues that existing pruning methods focus on preserving large activations or reconstructing layer outputs, overlooking a crucial computation performed by sparsity‑sensitive neurons: separating similar inputs into dissimilar outputs. To address this gap, the authors introduce three difference‑informed pruning techniques—Wisp, Wisp+, and Whisper—that explicitly score weights based on how well they maintain output differences. Their experiments show that these methods consistently improve accuracy while reducing inference cost across a wide range of LLMs, especially when structured sparsity is required.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Effective pruning should preserve not only activations but also the differences between outputs generated by similar inputs.  
- [Finding 2] The authors propose three algorithms: Wisp (first‑order, update‑free) that uses input‑difference norms; Wisp+ which refines scores neuronwise using the strongest separating pairs; and Whisper (second‑order) that employs a lightly regularized difference Hessian as its reconstruction objective.  
- [Finding 3] All three methods outperform strong reconstruction‑based baselines, with Wisp and Wisp+ beating activation‑aware approaches in constrained settings, and together they extend the accuracy‑runtime frontier beyond existing works like Wanda and SparseGPT.

## Methodology  
The authors first compute an input‑difference norm for each weight pair, which quantifies how much a neuron’s output changes when its inputs are perturbed. Wisp uses this norm directly to produce a sparse score without any gradient updates. Whisper goes one step further by constructing a difference Hessian—essentially the second derivative of the loss with respect to input differences—and regularizes it lightly to guide sparsification. Wisp+ combines the strengths of both: after obtaining a neuron‑wise score from Wisp, it refines the score based on which input pairs each neuron separates most strongly.

## Results  
Across Llama 2 models ranging from 7B to 405B parameters, Whisper consistently yields higher accuracy than reconstruction‑focused baselines while achieving lower latency. The update‑free variants Wisp and Wisp+ also surpass activation‑aware pruning methods, especially under tight sparsity budgets. When combined with stronger techniques such as RIA or ALPS, the difference‑informed scores further shift the frontier outward at negligible additional cost.

## Significance  
Preserving output differences is identified as a broadly useful and composable signal for post‑training LLM sparsification, enabling more balanced trade‑offs between accuracy and efficiency across diverse model families and sparsity constraints.
