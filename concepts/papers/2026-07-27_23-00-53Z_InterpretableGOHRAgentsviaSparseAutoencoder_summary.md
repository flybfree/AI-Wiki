## Summary  
The paper tackles the problem of extracting interpretable concepts from black‑box decision sequences generated by a tokenized autoregressive Transformer agent in the Game of Hidden Rules (GOHR). By training sparse autoencoders on the agent’s embeddings, it recovers the hidden rule structure that maps shapes to buckets without ever receiving explicit labels. The approach reveals that individual autoencoder dimensions correspond to concrete strategies such as probing one hypothesis and switching after negative feedback, thereby providing a transparent view of the learned policy.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Sparse autoencoders trained on agent decision‑token embeddings recover the hidden rule structure in GOHR.  
- [Finding 2] SAE dimensions are highly selective for simple concepts (e.g., chosen shape or bucket) and cover most decisions where those concepts appear.  
- [Finding 3] Individual SAE dimensions correspond to interpretable strategies such as probing one rule hypothesis and switching after negative feedback.

## Methodology  
The authors first train a tokenized Transformer agent on episodes generated by two hidden rules that permute shape‑to‑bucket mappings, producing decision sequences without any rule labels. To extract interpretability, they train sparse autoencoders to reconstruct these embeddings; held‑out decisions are labeled with simple concepts such as the selected shape or bucket. The reconstruction error per dimension is measured, and the alignment between high‑error dimensions and concept relevance is evaluated.

## Results  
The SAEs achieve strong dimensionality reduction while preserving rule information: most decision tokens can be reconstructed using a small set of dimensions that are selective for specific concepts. Crucially, each high‑selectivity dimension aligns with a known strategy—probing one hypothesis and switching after negative feedback—demonstrating that the latent space is directly interpretable.

## Significance  
This work introduces a framework for interpreting black‑box decision‑making agents by extracting concept‑specific latent dimensions from their token sequences, enabling explanations without explicit supervision. It bridges representation learning with human‑readable strategies in reinforcement‑learning settings like GOHR.
