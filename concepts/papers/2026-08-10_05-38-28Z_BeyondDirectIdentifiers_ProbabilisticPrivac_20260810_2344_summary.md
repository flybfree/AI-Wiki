## Summary  
The paper proposes a probabilistic privacy‑risk estimation framework that extends Privacy‑Conscious Delegation (PCD) beyond traditional PII detection, accounting for self‑disclosed quasi‑identifiers. It introduces k‑anonymity as an auxiliary metric to gauge the risk of re‑identification when users share non‑explicit personal traits. By training a model called PAPILLON on a newly curated dataset (PUPA‑SD) that captures natural user queries with self‑disclosure, the authors demonstrate how LLM‑driven probability estimates can improve both privacy and utility. The approach yields the best trade‑off for larger models such as Llama‑3.2‑3B while smaller models face greater difficulty balancing these objectives.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A probabilistic variant of PCD that incorporates LLM‑driven k‑anonymity estimation to capture privacy risk from PII‑free self‑disclosures.  
- [Finding 2] Creation of the PUPA‑SD dataset, a collection of naturalistic user queries with embedded self‑disclosure for training and evaluation.  
- [Finding 3] Optimization of PAPILLON that achieves the highest privacy‑utility balance on unseen conversations, especially for Llama‑3.2‑3B.

## Methodology  
The authors augment PCD’s loss function with a probabilistic k‑anonymity estimator generated by an LLM. They first build PUPA‑SD, which contains user queries and self‑disclosure statements without explicit identifiers. The PAPILLON model is trained to predict the probability that a query violates k‑anonymity given its content. During PCD execution, the local LLM uses both PII detection scores and the PAPILLON output to decide whether to forward or mask information, thereby balancing privacy protection with response quality.

## Results  
Experiments on multiple local LLMs show that PAPILLON‑augmented PCD improves performance on unseen conversations across all models. The best results are observed for Llama‑3.2‑3B, where the combined approach yields a superior privacy‑utility trade‑off compared to baseline PCD. Smaller models (e.g., 7 M parameters) still benefit but cannot match the larger model’s balance due to limited capacity for nuanced probability estimation.

## Significance  
This work moves privacy protection beyond static PII detectors, addressing hidden re‑identification risks that arise from seemingly innocuous self‑disclosures. By providing a principled probabilistic metric (k‑anonymity) and an LLM‑driven estimator (PAPILLON), the framework enables more robust, user‑centric privacy policies in real‑world deployment, fostering trust and encouraging broader adoption of local LLMs.
