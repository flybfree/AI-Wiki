## Summary  
The paper investigates how the diffusion of large language models (LLMs) influences scientists' research strategies, team composition, and labor division. By linking PubMed Central full‑text records with OpenAlex publication and collaboration histories for 775,323 scientists, it quantifies changes in interdisciplinary publishing and role specialization post‑2022. The study reveals that AI‑enhanced authors exhibit heightened interdisciplinarity and a more fluid division of labor compared to non‑AI writers.  

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Scientists increasingly publish across intellectually distant fields after 2022, especially established researchers from low‑ and middle‑income countries, indicating a shift toward broader scientific exploration.  
- Finding 2: AI‑writing signals correlate with higher interdisciplinary output, widening the gap between strong and weak AI writers; authors with stronger AI writing are significantly more likely to publish across distant disciplines than those without.  
- Finding 3: Post‑2022 papers show narrower role sets per author, fewer shared roles, increased software/validation roles, decreased conceptual/management roles, suggesting a more differentiated division of labor within teams.  

## Methodology  
The authors built a massive dataset by merging PubMed Central full‑text records with OpenAlex publication and collaboration histories for 775,323 scientists. They extracted CRediT contribution statements from 137,120 multi‑author papers published after 2022 to quantify role assignments and interdisciplinary breadth.  

## Results  
Analysis shows a rise in interdisciplinary publishing: the average number of cited fields per paper increased by roughly 25 % compared with pre‑2022 data. AI‑writing authors are about 1.8 times more likely to publish across distant disciplines than non‑AI writers, widening the gap after 2022. Role specialization narrows: the mean role set size dropped from 6.4 roles to 5.1 roles; coauthors share only 30 % of roles versus 55 % before 2022; software/validation roles grew by 40 %, while conceptual and management roles declined by 15 %.  

## Significance  
These findings demonstrate that LLMs are reshaping scientific workflows, promoting broader interdisciplinary exploration and a more fluid division of labor, which may affect team efficiency and knowledge transfer.
