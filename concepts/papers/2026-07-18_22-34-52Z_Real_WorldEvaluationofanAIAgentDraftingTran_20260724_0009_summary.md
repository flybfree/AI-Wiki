## Summary  
The authors present a human‑in‑the‑loop artificial intelligence agent designed to automatically assemble and draft one‑sentence Translational Science Benefits Model (TSBM) impact summaries for each of ten career‑development scholars in a Clinical and Translational Science Award (CTSA) hub. By generating a dossier of sourced evidence, the system aims to reduce the labor‑intensive manual assembly of impact records that currently consumes roughly 15 hours per cohort. The AI’s output is then reviewed by two staff members who decide whether each finding will be accepted, edited, or rejected, providing a quantitative measure of usability. This workflow shifts the primary effort from data collection and writing to evaluation, enabling scalable impact reporting.

## Key Contributions
- [Finding 1] The AI agent drafts TSBM impact summaries that achieve high synthesis accuracy (4.5/5) and usefulness (4.8/5) according to reviewers.  
- [Finding 2] Reviewers save an average of 14 minutes per scholar, equivalent to roughly 15 hours saved for the entire cohort compared with manual assembly.  
- [Finding 3] The agent’s recall is comparable to human search performance, and it uncovers a third of findings that fall outside routine scholarly categories.

## Methodology  
The team built an AI agent that queries multiple data sources to create a scholar‑specific dossier and then formulates a single TSBM impact sentence. In the real‑world setting of one CTSA hub, the system generated 507 evidence items for ten K12 scholars. Two independent reviewers coded each item as accept, edit, or reject, while also timing their review. The primary metric was the “usable” rate—both accept or edit decisions—and inter‑rater agreement was measured with Cohen’s kappa.

## Results  
Both reviewers agreed on 81.7 % of the agent’s findings as usable (accept or edited). On average, each reviewer spent 14 minutes per scholar, a saving of about 15 hours for the cohort. Inter‑rater agreement was moderate (Cohen’s kappa = 0.43). A recall study showed the AI’s ability to locate evidence matched human search performance. Approximately one‑third of the reviewed findings belonged to non‑scholarly categories that routine processes often miss. Reviewers rated synthesis accuracy 4.5 and usefulness 4.8 on a five‑point scale.

## Significance  
By automating the drafting stage, the AI agent frees staff from repetitive data gathering and writing, allowing them to focus on critical review tasks. This shift makes cohort‑scale impact reporting feasible and scalable within CTSA programs, potentially improving overall research productivity and transparency.
