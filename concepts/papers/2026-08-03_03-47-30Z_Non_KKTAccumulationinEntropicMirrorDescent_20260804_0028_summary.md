## Summary  
The paper investigates whether accumulation points of bounded mirror descent sequences generated by Legendre kernels must be Karush‑Kuhn‑Tucker (KKT) stationary; it shows they can include non‑KKT points due to boundary degeneracy. It constructs smooth objectives on the nonnegative orthant ℝⁿ (for n≥3) and the probability simplex Δₙ (for n≥4) where the accumulation sets contain arcs of non‑KKT points. The stepsizes satisfy αₖ≍k⁻ᵇ with β∈(1/2,1), objective values are nonincreasing, and entropy is relatively smooth. This provides the first counterexamples to KKT accumulation for bounded mirror descent with monotone objectives.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Construction of C¹‑smooth objectives on ℝⁿ (n≥3) and Δₙ (n≥4) yielding bounded mirror descent sequences whose accumulation sets contain non‑KKT points.  
- [Finding 2] Demonstration that the pathology arises from vanishing inverse entropy metric at the boundary, erasing dual feasibility in the KKT system, not from improper stepsizes or failure of descent.  
- [Finding 3] First counterexamples to KKT accumulation for bounded mirror descent with monotone objectives.

## Methodology  
The authors approach the problem by analyzing the Bregman geometry of Legendre kernels on constrained domains. They consider entropy‑relative smoothness and construct strictly convex, C¹‑smooth objectives whose feasible sets have non‑smooth boundaries (a circle). By choosing stepsizes αₖ≍k⁻ᵇ with β∈(1/2,1), they ensure boundedness while keeping the inverse entropy metric positive inside but vanishing on active constraints. This reveals how degeneracy at the boundary can produce accumulation points that are not KKT stationary.

## Results  
The constructed sequences satisfy monotone objective values and αₖ≍k⁻ᵇ, β∈(1/2,1). The set of accumulation points is a smooth circle in the feasible region containing an open arc of non‑KKT points. This confirms that bounded mirror descent can converge to non‑KKT stationary points when the Bregman geometry degenerates at the boundary.

## Significance  
This work resolves a longstanding open question about KKT accumulation for mirror descent, showing that boundary effects can produce counterexamples even with proper stepsizes and monotone objectives. It highlights the importance of analyzing Bregman geometry beyond standard assumptions and may guide algorithm design to avoid pathological convergence.
