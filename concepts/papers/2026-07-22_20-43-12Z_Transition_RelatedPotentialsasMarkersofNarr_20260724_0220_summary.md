## Summary  
The study investigates how brain activity during film viewing can be captured in a naturalistic, continuous EEG recording and whether it reflects the processing of narrative transitions. By aligning EEG data to sharp cinematic cuts, the authors demonstrate that transition‑related potentials (TRPs) carry ERP‑like signatures linked to comprehension. Their key contribution is a compact deep neural network that recovers these TRPs directly from group‑averaged continuous recordings, showing they generalize across films and subjects. This semi‑automated approach provides a new framework for probing narrative understanding without the need for trial‑based ERPs.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Transition‑related potentials (TRPs) in continuous EEG exhibit ERP‑like temporal structure that correlates with information processing during film cuts.  
- [Finding 2] A compact deep neural network can reliably recover TRP signatures from group‑averaged continuous recordings, generalizing across different films and participant groups.  
- [Finding 3] The recovered TRPs reproduce the context‑dependent effects observed for manually annotated cuts, indicating that narrative comprehension leaves a measurable EEG signature.

## Methodology  
Participants watched short films while their scalp was continuously recorded with EEG. Sharp cinematic transitions (cuts) were identified, and EEG potentials were extracted at each cut location. Two experimental conditions were compared: coherent films and scene‑scrambled versions containing matched post‑cut sensory input. A deep neural network trained on a subset of the data was used to detect TRP components directly from the raw continuous recordings.

## Results  
The detector successfully recovered the cut‑related EEG signature that matched manually annotated responses, both in terms of timing and amplitude. The method performed consistently across multiple films and across different subject groups, suggesting robustness. Moreover, the context effects—such as reduced responses to scrambled cuts—were reproduced by the network’s output, confirming that narrative context shapes the TRPs.

## Significance  
This work bridges the gap between naturalistic viewing conditions and traditional ERP paradigms, offering a semi‑automated tool for analyzing how viewers process narratives in real time. By extracting transition cues from continuous EEG data, researchers can study comprehension without artificial trial designs, opening possibilities to apply the framework to other forms of continuous stimulation.
