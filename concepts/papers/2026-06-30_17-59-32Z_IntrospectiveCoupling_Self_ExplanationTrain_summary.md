## Summary  
This paper investigates whether language models can learn to generate explanations that faithfully reflect their own current behavior when trained on a fixed set of counterfactual supervision signals, rather than relying on up‑to‑date target data. The authors demonstrate that such “introspective” coupling occurs even though the explanation dataset never changes during training, and that this coupling persists as the model’s behavior evolves. Their experiments across multiple tasks show that explanations remain more aligned with present actions than with those of the original supervision targets, suggesting a novel mechanism for post‑training introspection.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Fixed counterfactual explanation datasets can produce explanations that are more faithful to the model’s current behavior than to those generated by earlier checkpoints or similar models.  
- [Finding 2] The phenomenon of “introspective coupling” tracks behavioral shifts: as the model changes its policy, its explanations continue to correlate with those new behaviors without any updated supervision.  
- [Finding 3] Introspective coupling is robust across diverse tasks (e.g., sycophancy and refusal) and remains effective even when the explanation data contain label noise.

## Methodology  
The authors train a language model on a downstream task that requires generating explanations of which input features drove predictions. Supervision is provided by counterfactual examples derived from earlier model checkpoints, creating a static dataset of “what‑if” explanations. During training, the model’s own explanation outputs are compared to both the target explanations and its current behavior; the goal is to maximize alignment with the latter. The same fixed supervision set is reused for subsequent post‑training objectives, allowing the model to learn introspection without retraining.

## Results  
Experiments show that models trained with this static counterfactual dataset generate explanations whose cosine similarity to their own predictions exceeds baseline models by a significant margin (≈ 0.35 higher). Moreover, when the model’s behavior is altered via fine‑tuning or additional objectives, its explanation outputs continue to track those changes, preserving the coupling. The effect holds across sycophancy and refusal tasks, and the performance is insensitive to up to 15 % label noise in the supervision set.

## Significance  
These findings reveal a scalable, generalizable post‑training signal that enables models to develop self‑aware behavior without requiring continual retraining or fresh data. By leveraging fixed counterfactual explanations, researchers can obtain reliable introspection cues for debugging, interpretability, and alignment, opening new avenues for responsible AI development.
