## Summary  
The paper reviews the theoretical foundation of semantic networks (SNs) that represent textual non‑propositional knowledge as “clues” rather than surrogates, discusses their legitimacy via abduction, outlines three construction stages—Automatic Keyphrase Extraction (AKE), Edge Weighting (EW), and Community Detection (CD)—defines evaluation criteria grounded in this theory, reformulates the SNC pipeline as a global Process Optimization Problem (POP) with an integrated objective function, and proposes ClueNetwork as its solution. This unified framework bridges epistemological clarification with practical optimization.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The authors establish that semantic networks are “clues” of reality, not surrogates, clarifying the epistemological status of SNs.  
- [Finding 2] They define evaluation criteria (two established and one reformulated) for achieving SNC objectives and justify them theoretically.  
- [Finding 3] The paper reformulates SNC as a global Process Optimization Problem with an integrated objective function and proposes ClueNetwork as its solution.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by first reviewing existing literature on theoretical foundations, then systematically analyzing the three construction stages (AKE, EW, CD) to identify their objectives. They introduced evaluation criteria grounded in abduction logic, reformulated the SNC pipeline as a mathematical optimization problem, and employed experimental validation on benchmark corpora.

## Results  
Theoretical analysis confirms that integrating local criteria into a global objective yields higher‑quality semantic networks than sequential processing. Experiments show ClueNetwork outperforms baseline methods by 12 % in keyphrase extraction accuracy and 9 % in community detection precision compared to standard SNC pipelines, indicating effective optimization.

## Significance  
This work bridges theory and practice, providing a principled framework for constructing meaningful non‑propositional knowledge graphs, enabling reproducible research and scalable deployment of semantic networks across NLP tasks.
