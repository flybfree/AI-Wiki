## Summary  
The paper investigates whether the ACL Responsible NLP Checklist used at EMNLP 2025 functions as a superficial compliance tool rather than a genuine driver of responsible research. By analyzing 73,922 checklist responses and justifications across both Main and Findings tracks, the authors reveal widespread superficiality, logical contradictions, and dismissive attitudes toward ethical risks. Their analysis demonstrates that many submissions merely tick boxes without substantive justification, undermining the intended purpose of promoting transparency and societal impact awareness. The study thus calls for redesigning the checklist to enforce deeper engagement with responsible AI practices.

## Key Contributions  
- **Finding 1:** Authors largely isolate ethics questions from their papers, treating them as afterthoughts rather than integrated considerations.  
- **Finding 2:** Approximately 44.9 % of justifications are poor or bad‑faith, characterized by brevity or emptiness.  
- **Finding 3:** Six percent of checklists contain logical contradictions between parent and child responses, indicating inconsistent self‑reporting.

## Methodology  
The authors curate two datasets: (1) all checklist responses with accompanying justifications from EMNLP 2025 Main and Findings tracks; and (2) links to the relevant sections of each paper. They then conduct a large‑scale quantitative analysis, counting total responses, evaluating justification quality, detecting contradictions, and measuring the proportion of authors who dismiss potential risks or social impacts.

## Results  
- In the Main track, 53 % of authors explicitly state there are no societal risks, despite many involving sensitive topics.  
- The Findings track shows a similar trend in superficial risk acknowledgment.  
- Logical contradictions appear in 6 % of checklists, suggesting internal inconsistency between self‑reported and documented ethical considerations.

## Significance  
These findings expose the checklist as a box‑ticking exercise that may discourage genuine ethical reflection rather than fostering it. The study highlights the need for more rigorous mechanisms to ensure researchers genuinely address responsible NLP concerns, which is critical for building trustworthy AI systems.
