## Summary  
This paper investigates how human linguistic diversity fuels collective creativity and whether large language models can replicate or sustain that effect. Using a preregistered creative metaphor experiment with native (L1) and non‑native (L2) English writers, the authors compare three conditions: writing without AI, receiving AI‑generated ideas, and refining their own ideas with AI assistance. They also simulate entire writer pools using personas derived from participants’ backgrounds to test model‑driven diversity. The study shows that human groups naturally generate more diverse output than AI‑augmented ones, highlighting a gap between real‑world creative ecosystems and current generative systems.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] L2 writers produced greater collective diversity than L1 writers, with native‑language ideation yielding the most varied pools.  
- [Finding 2] AI‑ideation compressed collective diversity across all conditions and erased the detectable L2 advantage.  
- [Finding 3] Simulated writer pools built from personas fell below human pools; models only produced degenerate text, while individual AI‑ideation raised ratings by pitting private incentives against collective good—except when L2 writers used their native language, which benefited both.

## Methodology  
The authors conducted a preregistered creative metaphor experiment involving native and non‑native English speakers who wrote metaphors either alone (no AI), with AI‑generated ideas (AI ideation), or after receiving AI suggestions for refinement (AI refinement). To test scalability, they built personas from participants’ real backgrounds and simulated entire writer pools using three model families, native‑language prompting, and elevated sampling temperatures. This dual approach allowed both individual‑level and collective‑level comparisons.

## Results  
Collective diversity was highest among L2 writers and peaked with native‑language ideation; AI ideation uniformly reduced this diversity, making the L2 advantage undetectable. Simulated pools generated by personas consistently produced less diverse output than any human pool, and pushing model parameters further only yielded degenerate text. At the individual level, AI ideation increased self‑rated creativity but undermined group diversity, except when L2 writers used their native language, which boosted both personal and collective scores.

## Significance  
The findings demonstrate that human linguistic diversity remains a priceless source of creative innovation that current large language models cannot simulate or sustain. The design of human‑AI collaborative workflows—whether AI merely assists or replaces the human author—determines whether this valuable resource endures in practice.
