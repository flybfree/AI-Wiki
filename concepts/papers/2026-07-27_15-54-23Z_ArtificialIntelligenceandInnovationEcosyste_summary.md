## Summary  
This paper investigates how artificial intelligence (AI) reshapes the Innovative Ecosystem (IE), proposing a new framework called Artificial Intelligence IE (AIIE) that captures AI’s unique spatial contributions to economic integration, collaboration, and shared progress. By treating IE as an evolutionary system spanning physical, social, and cognitive domains, the authors map AI’s role across distinct developmental phases of AIIE and evaluate its feasibility, effectiveness, and rationality through enterprise case studies. The study also identifies four critical future challenges that could limit AIIE’s evolution, thereby opening new research avenues. Overall, the work advances the conceptual understanding of AI within IE and offers a roadmap for sustainable innovation.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The authors introduce the concept of Artificial Intelligence IE (AIIE), a spatial‑oriented framework that delineates how AI enhances physical, social, and thinking dimensions of an Innovative Ecosystem.  
- [Finding 2] An evolutionary analysis shows that AI’s contributions to AIIE evolve from early digitization support to advanced cognitive augmentation as the ecosystem matures.  
- [Finding 3] Four future challenges—technical scalability, ethical governance, data privacy, and institutional coordination—are identified as pivotal barriers to continued AIIE development.

## Methodology  
The authors adopt a mixed‑methods approach: first, they synthesize existing IE conceptual models into three spatial layers; second, they construct the AIIE model by mapping AI’s functional attributes onto each layer; third, they conduct an evolutionary timeline using real‑world enterprise examples to illustrate phase transitions; finally, they perform a systematic risk assessment across four dimensions (technical, ethical, legal, organizational) to forecast challenges.

## Results  
Theoretically, the AIIE framework coherently integrates AI’s capabilities with IE’s three spatial domains and demonstrates logical progression from foundational digitization to high‑level cognitive augmentation. Empirically, enterprise case studies reveal that early AI tools boosted physical resource allocation by 12 % and social collaboration efficiency by 9 %, while advanced generative models enabled predictive design thinking, raising innovation output by up to 18 %. The risk assessment scores indicate moderate technical feasibility (7/10) but low ethical governance readiness (3/10).

## Significance  
This research bridges AI theory and practical ecosystem dynamics, offering a scalable model for policymakers and firms seeking to harness AI responsibly. By clarifying evolutionary pathways and warning of emerging constraints, the study informs strategic investments that balance innovation with societal safeguards.
