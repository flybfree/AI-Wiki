## Summary  
The paper aims to achieve assurance closure in AI‑native large‑scale agile software development, where human intent and risk are managed while agents execute engineering tasks. It identifies six unresolved gaps that prevent existing assurance mechanisms from being fully machine‑operable, proposes a high‑level architecture with six capabilities grounded in a shared semantic assurance layer, and outlines four research questions to guide the next steps.

## Key Contributions  
- The authors pinpoint six residual gaps in making surrounding assurance reasoning sufficiently machine‑operable within AI‑native agile workflows.  
- They propose a high‑level architectural framework comprising six corresponding capabilities that are built on a shared semantic assurance layer, enabling systematic assurance reasoning.  
- They formulate four research questions to transform this architecture into a dependable, human‑on‑the‑loop R&D process.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a comprehensive literature review and a gap analysis of existing formal methods, testing, simulation, assurance cases, digital twins, and runtime assurance techniques. By mapping these mechanisms onto the AI‑native agile paradigm, they identified where machine operability is lacking. The high‑level architecture was then designed as a conceptual model rather than an implementation, followed by the articulation of research questions to guide empirical or theoretical exploration.

## Results  
The primary result is a theoretical framework that delineates six capability categories—such as intent validation, risk quantification, exception handling, evidence acquisition, credibility assessment, and authority bounding—that can be instantiated on a shared semantic assurance layer. The authors also present four research questions that target the integration of these capabilities into an AI‑native agile R&D loop, providing a roadmap for future work.

## Significance  
This work matters because it bridges the gap between human‑centric governance and autonomous agent execution in large‑scale agile environments, ensuring that trust is maintained through machine‑operable assurance reasoning. By formalizing assurance closure, organizations can reduce risk, improve confidence in AI‑driven development, and enable scalable, reliable software delivery.
