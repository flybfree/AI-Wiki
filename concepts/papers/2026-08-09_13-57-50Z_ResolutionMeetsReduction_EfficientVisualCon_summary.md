## Summary
Vision‑language models promise automated radiology report generation but struggle with the massive visual token sequences generated by 3D CT volumes. This paper investigates how to allocate the limited vision‑token budget among input field of view, spatial resolution, and projection to balance efficiency and clinical quality. By systematically comparing heterogeneous VEs, projectors, and LLMs across two large datasets, we identify strategies that maximize macro F1 scores while minimizing computational cost.

## Key Contributions
- [Finding 1] Anatomy‑guided ROI cropping is the most consistent strategy for improving clinical macro F1 scores across all configurations.  
- [Finding 2] The choice of visual projection critically determines how much input resolution can be increased without sacrificing performance, with PerceiverResampler paired with higher‑resolution Curia features delivering the best results.  
- [Finding 3] Our optimal configurations achieve state‑of‑the‑art clinical macro F1 values (49.5 on CT‑RATE and 49.0 on Merlin) at matched LLM token budgets.

## Methodology
The authors evaluate four heterogeneous vision encoders (two CNN‑based and two ViT‑based), five token‑reducing projectors plus a non‑reducing MLP baseline, and five instruction‑tuned LLMs ranging from 1.7B to 4B parameters. They fix the LLM token budget while varying input field of view, spatial resolution, and projection method across two large CT report datasets (CT‑RATE and Merlin) to systematically assess trade‑offs.

## Results
At matched LLM token budgets, ROI cropping consistently boosts macro F1 by +3.7 points for the 3D ViT Primus encoder and +1.1 points for the slice‑based 2D ViT Curia encoder. Higher input resolution yields gains only when paired with PerceiverResampler; the best configurations reach 49.5 on CT‑RATE and 49.0 on Merlin, matching or exceeding prior baselines.

## Significance
Efficient visual context enables scalable, high‑quality automated radiology report generation that reduces computational load while preserving clinical relevance, thereby accelerating workflow and improving diagnostic consistency across diverse imaging studies.
