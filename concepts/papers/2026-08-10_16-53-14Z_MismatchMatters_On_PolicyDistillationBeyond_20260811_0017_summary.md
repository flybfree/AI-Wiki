## Summary  
On‑policy distillation (OPD) is widely used to fine‑tune large language models, but the authors observe a failure mode in which students achieve near‑perfect token agreement while producing globally flawed responses—a phenomenon they call *degenerate agreement*. To move beyond token‑level agreement, they shift focus to *mismatch* tokens, distinguishing between *student‑excess* tokens (generated by the student but assigned negligible probability by the teacher) and *student‑deficit* tokens (preferred by the teacher yet rarely sampled). Their contribution is a correction framework called TIDE that addresses both directions of mismatch.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Student‑excess tokens cause unbounded log‑ratio corrections, destabilizing the update process.  
- [Finding 2] Student‑deficit tokens block the transfer of teacher reasoning patterns because they are rarely sampled.  
- [Finding 3] TIDE applies bounded Hellinger shaping to suppress severe excesses and an analytic top‑K injection to restore missing probability mass without requiring deficit tokens to be sampled.  

## Methodology  
The authors first conduct a token‑level analysis of OPD pipelines, identifying the two mismatch categories. They then propose TIDE: (1) bounded Hellinger shaping is applied to the student model’s distribution to suppress the most extreme excess probabilities, limiting their impact on gradient updates; (2) an analytic teacher top‑K injection injects the highest‑probability tokens from the teacher into the student’s probability mass function, thereby restoring missing mass that would otherwise be represented by deficit tokens. This approach does not depend on the presence of deficit tokens in the sampled data, making it robust to low‑frequency sampling.  

## Results  
Experiments on multiple Qwen3 teacher‑student pairs demonstrate that TIDE consistently outperforms standard OPD and recent token‑selection or reward‑shaping baselines. The most striking gains appear when teacher‑student mismatch is strong: average accuracy at 8 tokens rises from 6.9 % to 20.3 %, response length shrinks by a factor of 3.6, and formatting errors drop dramatically. These improvements indicate that TIDE effectively mitigates both excess and deficit mismatches.  

## Significance  
By focusing on mismatch rather than token agreement, the paper offers a more reliable OPD pipeline that can be deployed even when teacher‑student representations diverge substantially. The method reduces the risk of degenerate outputs, leading to higher accuracy, shorter responses, and fewer formatting failures—key metrics for real‑world LLM applications.
