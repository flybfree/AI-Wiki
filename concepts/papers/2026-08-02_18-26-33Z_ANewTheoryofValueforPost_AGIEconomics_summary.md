## Summary  
The paper proposes Flourishing Value Theory (FVT) as a new framework for post‑AGI economics, arguing that scarcity‑based value models collapse when cognitive work becomes widely automatable and that market price, profit, GDP, etc., are only partial signals. FVT redefines value as the counterfactual, distribution‑sensitive contribution of any system to the durable capabilities of persons and communities within social and planetary limits. It distinguishes value creation from value capture, retains price and productivity metrics as useful but incomplete indicators, and emphasizes a shift toward abundance, transformation, and positive‑sum dynamics. The central problem is not maximizing output but converting abundant intelligence into fair, regenerative flourishing.

## Key Contributions  
- FVT defines value as the counterfactual, distribution‑sensitive contribution of a system to durable human and community capabilities within planetary constraints.  
- It reframes post‑AGI economics around abundance, transformation, and positive‑sum dynamics rather than scarcity‑driven competition or zero‑sum rivalry.  
- The theory introduces Flourishing Metrics and Return on Flourishing (RoF) as a layered architecture for firms, governments, AI governance, and national accounting.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by synthesizing insights from AI economics, capability theory, automation studies, digital valuation, and ecological economics. They built FVT through theoretical analysis rather than empirical experiments, mapping how scarcity gives way to governed abundance and how value should be measured beyond traditional profit or GDP indicators.

## Results  
Theoretical results show that value is non‑compensatory at critical thresholds of planetary and social limits, turning price, profit, productivity, and GDP into partial signals. The model predicts a transition from zero‑sum rivalry to positive‑sum expansion of collective consciousness, supported by the layered Flourishing Metrics framework.

## Significance  
This work matters because it offers a normative foundation for policy in an era where AI can eliminate labor scarcity. By aligning economic incentives with durable human and planetary flourishing, FVT could reshape governance, corporate strategy, and national accounting to prioritize regenerative outcomes over mere output maximization.
