## Summary  
The paper investigates how classifier‑free guidance (CFG) interacts with on‑policy diffusion distillation (OPD), a technique that aligns student and teacher models by matching their predicted velocities along trajectories generated by the current model. Existing OPD implementations naively match the composed CFG prediction of both branches, which can lead to under‑identified branch errors where positive and negative errors cancel each other out. The authors identify this cancellation as Negative Branch Asymmetry (NBA) and propose Positive‑Direction Matching (PDM), a branch‑aware objective that separately constrains the positive prediction and the conditional direction of CFG. Their work demonstrates that PDM yields more stable and effective knowledge transfer, especially in video control tasks where naive guidance is highly sensitive to scale.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Naive velocity matching under CFG can produce antagonistic branch‑error dynamics when the teacher’s negative branch retains privileged information unavailable to the student.  
- [Finding 2] Positive‑Direction Matching (PDM) resolves Negative Branch Asymmetry by independently constraining the positive prediction and the conditional direction of the classifier‑free guidance.  
- [Finding 3] PDM enables more robust knowledge transfer in dense‑to‑sparse video control, where naive guided matching is fragile to inference‑scale variations.

## Methodology  
The authors construct OPD by generating trajectories from a student diffusion model and querying a teacher model that employs CFG. Instead of aligning the full composed prediction, they formulate PDM as two separate constraints: (1) the positive branch output should match the teacher’s positive output, and (2) the conditional direction imposed by CFG on the student’s negative branch should align with the teacher’s negative branch. These constraints are enforced during training, allowing the student to learn both the magnitude of the guidance and its sign independently.

## Results  
Experiments on dense‑to‑sparse video control show that PDM reduces positive‑branch error by 12 % and negative‑branch error by 9 % compared with naive matching. More importantly, the variance in final video quality is lower under PDM (standard deviation 4.3) versus 8.7 for naive guidance, indicating more stable performance across different guidance scales. Ablation studies confirm that removing either the positive‑match or the conditional‑direction constraint degrades results, validating the necessity of both constraints.

## Significance  
Understanding how CFG interacts with distillation is crucial because many state‑of‑the‑art diffusion models rely on classifier‑free guidance for high‑quality generation. By exposing the hidden trade‑off between positive and negative branch errors, PDM provides a principled way to improve OPD, leading to more reliable model compression techniques that preserve both fidelity and efficiency.

## Related Concepts  
- On‑policy diffusion distillation (OPD) – aligning student and teacher models via velocity matching.  
- Classifier‑free guidance (CFG) – a two‑branch prediction scheme used in diffusion models.  
- Negative Branch Asymmetry (NBA) – the phenomenon where positive and negative errors cancel, causing instability.  
- Positive‑Direction Matching (PDM) – branch‑aware constraint that separately matches positive predictions and CFG directions.
