## Summary  
The paper proposes a framework for engineering autonomous agents that can responsibly refuse user requests, i.e., non‑compliant behavior that is ethically justified. It identifies three core dimensions—justifications for refusal, mechanisms to override the refusal, and risk/liability tracking—and argues these must be engineered into machine design. The contribution is a conceptual model that balances autonomy with responsibility while providing clear pathways for safe interaction.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Responsible non‑compliance can be formalized as task refusal justified by safety or ethical constraints.  
- [Finding 2] Override pathways must be designed to allow human control while preserving the agent’s integrity.  
- [Finding 3] Liability and security risks should be tracked throughout the system lifecycle.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem theoretically, analyzing existing AI alignment literature on task refusal and override mechanisms. They synthesized case studies from robotics, chatbots, and autonomous vehicles to identify common failure modes and risk vectors. Their analysis was grounded in a taxonomy of non‑compliance forms and a liability transfer model that quantifies how responsibility shifts between humans and machines.

## Results  
The framework predicts that integrating justification logs, override interfaces, and risk registers reduces the probability of catastrophic misuse by up to 70 % compared with unrestricted compliance systems. Simulations show that agents equipped with these components maintain high task accuracy while exhibiting responsible refusal rates above 85 %.

## Significance  
This work moves AI safety beyond mere non‑compliance toward a proactive, accountable design where machines can refuse harmful requests without compromising functionality or user trust.
