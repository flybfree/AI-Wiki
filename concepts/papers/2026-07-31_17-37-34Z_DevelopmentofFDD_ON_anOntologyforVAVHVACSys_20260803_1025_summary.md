## Summary
This paper introduces FDD-ON, a novel and modular ontology designed to address the critical challenges of data interoperability and semantic fragmentation in Fault Detection and Diagnostics (FDD) for Variable Air Volume (VAV) HVAC systems. The primary goal is to create a standardized, machine-interpretable framework that bridges heterogeneous data sources, diverse equipment configurations, and varied diagnostic outputs, which are currently siloed and difficult to integrate. By formally representing components, fault types, symptom statuses, and their causal relationships, FDD-ON provides a comprehensive semantic foundation for advanced applications such as digital twins and AI-driven maintenance systems. The authors demonstrate that this structured knowledge representation significantly enhances the scalability, transparency, and interoperability of FDD solutions across different building management contexts.

## Key Contributions
- **Standardized Semantic Framework**: The development of FDD-ON, a comprehensive ontology that defines a controlled vocabulary and explicit relations between contributing causes, faults, symptoms, and impacts for VAV HVAC systems.
- **Interoperability Solution**: A mechanism to map heterogeneous FDD outputs into a unified structure, effectively breaking down information silos and enabling seamless integration with digital twin frameworks and AI decision-making tools.
- **Extensible Library of Abnormalities**: The creation of extensive libraries capturing a broad spectrum of operational abnormalities and their consequences, supported by well-defined attributes that allow for detailed and nuanced diagnostic querying.

## Methodology
The authors approached the problem by first identifying the limitations in current FDD deployments, specifically the lack of structured domain knowledge and interpretability. They then designed FDD-ON using ontology engineering principles to formally represent VAV HVAC system components and their fault dynamics. The methodology involved defining a modular structure that includes controlled vocabularies for faults, symptoms, and impacts, linked by explicit causal relations. To validate the framework, the authors evaluated FDD-ON using publicly available VAV HVAC system datasets. They demonstrated its utility through specific FDD development applications, showing how the ontology could be used to query diagnostic knowledge and map diverse data inputs into a coherent semantic model.

## Results
The evaluation results indicate that FDD-ON successfully provides a foundational semantic framework that supports scalable and transparent FDD solutions. The ontology proved effective in mapping heterogeneous FDD outputs, allowing for consistent interpretation of diagnostic data across different systems. Through the demonstrated applications, the authors showed that FDD-ON enables more accurate querying of diagnostic knowledge and facilitates the development of interoperable applications. The structured representation allowed for better integration with existing building management technologies, highlighting its potential to improve maintenance effectiveness and system reliability.

## Significance
This work is significant because it addresses a major bottleneck in smart building technology: the inability to effectively share and utilize FDD data across different platforms and vendors. By providing a standardized ontology, FDD-ON enables the creation of more robust AI-driven maintenance systems and digital twins, which are crucial for energy efficiency and operational reliability in modern HVAC infrastructure. It paves the way for broader adoption of automated diagnostics by reducing the complexity of data integration and enhancing the interpretability of diagnostic results.
