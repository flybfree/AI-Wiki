## Summary  
This paper investigates how pseudorandom streams embedded in diffusion models can be learned and influence both training dynamics and generation quality, revealing that the stochasticity is not merely a mathematical abstraction but a concrete structured input. The authors demonstrate that replacing real images with online random tensors—while preserving the diffusion architecture and objective—produces markedly different loss values and degraded outputs on standard benchmarks such as MNIST and CIFAR‑10. Their experiments show strong rank correlations between orbit predictability and degradation, even after controlling for marginal statistics and ruling out numerical artifacts. The findings suggest that pseudorandomness is model‑dependent and can be exploited to control generation quality.

## Key Contributions  
- **Finding 1:** Pseudorandom streams within diffusion models act as learnable inputs because the deterministic orbits they generate affect loss gradients and optimization trajectories.  
- **Finding 2:** A lightweight multilayer perceptron predicts the next value of an orbit from its recent history, providing a quantitative measure of sequence predictability.  
- **Finding 3:** The “diffusion probe” that swaps real images with online random tensors yields loss and generation quality differences that are statistically significant and independent of simple marginal‑statistics failures.

## Methodology  
The authors construct diffusion models that rely on stochastic inputs but replace the actual image data with temporally correlated random tensors generated by pseudorandom rules. They train a small MLP to forecast subsequent orbit values from recent history, thereby measuring general sequence predictability. The diffusion probe then substitutes real images with these online random streams while keeping the model architecture and training objective unchanged. By comparing the probe loss, true‑data loss, and sample quality against an IID baseline, they isolate the effect of structured stochasticity.

## Results  
On MNIST and CIFAR‑10, both the probe loss and the genuine diffusion loss follow empirical power laws with dataset‑specific exponents, indicating a non‑linear relationship between randomness structure and performance. Global rank correlations between orbit predictability (MLP output) and degradation metrics are strong, though local rankings differ across images. After controlling for marginal statistics and eliminating clear numerical failures, the remaining orbits still cause noticeable loss and quality drops, confirming that structured stochasticity is a genuine source of model‑dependent degradation.

## Significance  
This work shows that randomness in diffusion models is not just a placeholder but an active component whose structure can be learned and controlled. By treating pseudorandom streams as learnable inputs, the study opens new avenues for reproducibility, bias mitigation, and intentional manipulation of generation quality without altering the underlying data distribution.
