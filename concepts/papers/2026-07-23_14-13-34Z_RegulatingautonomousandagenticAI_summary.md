## Summary  
This paper tackles the difficulty of regulating activities in which the regulated parties employ autonomous and agentic AI, arguing that traditional regulatory assumptions about knowledge and control are obsolete because much of the relevant intelligence resides elsewhere in the AI supply chain. The authors propose a fresh, proactive governance framework that moves beyond reactive oversight to actively manage systemic risks generated by AI autonomy. By analysing four existing regimes—UK content‑platform rules, data‑protection law, UK financial services regulation, and the EU AI Act’s cross‑sectoral regime—the study identifies common shortcomings and outlines potential solutions. The contribution is a systematic critique of current approaches combined with concrete recommendations for regulators.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Existing governance models fail to address the knowledge and control that autonomous agents generate, creating blind spots in risk assessment.  
- [Finding 2] Autonomous AI introduces novel systemic risks that cannot be managed solely through retrospective supervisory oversight.  
- [Finding 3] A cross‑sectoral, proactive regulatory architecture is required to adapt quickly to the evolving capabilities of agentic systems.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by conducting a comparative analysis of four distinct regulatory regimes that currently govern AI‑related activities in the UK and EU. They examined how each regime’s assumptions about who holds knowledge and control apply—or do not apply—to autonomous and agentic AI, identified gaps in oversight, and mapped the systemic risks these gaps generate. The study combined doctrinal review with a risk‑mapping exercise to derive actionable policy insights.

## Results  
The analysis revealed that all four regimes rely on retrospective supervisory mechanisms that are ill‑suited for AI autonomy, leading to delayed detection of emergent threats. Moreover, the fragmentation of authority across sectors exacerbates systemic risk by allowing risks to propagate unchecked. The authors concluded that a unified, forward‑looking framework—incorporating continuous monitoring, shared data standards, and adaptive oversight—is essential.

## Significance  
This work matters because AI autonomy reshapes the nature of risk in ways that current law cannot anticipate or mitigate. By highlighting the inadequacy of existing governance structures, the paper urges regulators to adopt a proactive stance, ensuring that systemic threats are addressed before they materialise. The findings could inform future legislation and standard‑setting bodies seeking to keep pace with rapidly evolving AI capabilities.

## Related Concepts  
- Autonomous AI: systems capable of self‑directed operation without human intervention.  
- Agentic AI: AI agents that act on behalf of users, make decisions, and manage resources autonomously.  
- Systemic risk: the potential for AI failures to propagate across interconnected sectors, causing widespread harm.  
- Regulatory foresight: the ability of policymakers to anticipate future technological developments.  
- Cross‑sectoral governance: coordinated regulatory approaches that span multiple domains to address shared risks.
