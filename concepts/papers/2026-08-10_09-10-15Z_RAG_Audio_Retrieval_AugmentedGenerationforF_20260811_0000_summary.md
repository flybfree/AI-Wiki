## Summary  
Brain‑to‑audio reconstruction suffers from “prior domination,” where a pretrained generator produces realistic but stimulus‑inaccurate audio because it is conditioned on weak neural signals. RAG‑Audio addresses this by decoding fMRI data into a semantic audio embedding, retrieving the nearest real‑audio exemplar, and using that exemplar to initialize the frozen generator’s latent trajectory while keeping the embedding as conditioning information. This retrieval‑guided initialization breaks the prior bias and yields more faithful audio outputs. The method improves both stimulus identification accuracy and audio quality on the Brain2Music benchmark.

## Key Contributions  
- Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG‑Audio) mitigates prior domination by initializing the frozen generator’s latent trajectory with a retrieved real‑audio exemplar rather than relying solely on conditioning.  
- RAG‑Audio raises stimulus identification accuracy on Brain2Music from 0.14–0.18 to 0.40–0.43, approaching retrieval performance and moving away from the 0.10 chance level.  
- The Fréchet Audio Distance (FAD) for generated audio drops an order of magnitude from 13.49 to 1.25 using AudioLDM, indicating a substantial fidelity gain.

## Methodology  
RAG‑Audio first encodes the fMRI signal into a semantic audio embedding that captures its intended musical content. A similarity search then finds the real‑audio exemplar most closely matching this embedding. The frozen generator’s sampling trajectory is initialized from the retrieved exemplar’s latent code, while the decoded embedding remains as conditioning input throughout generation. This two‑step process—embedding decoding followed by retrieval‑driven initialization—provides a trajectory that aligns with actual audio rather than being driven only by prior statistics.

## Results  
Experimental evaluation on Brain2Music shows RAG‑Audio’s identification scores improve dramatically, from 0.14–0.18 to 0.40–0.43, indicating near‑random guessing before and high confidence after retrieval. FAD for AudioLDM generated by RAG‑Audio falls from 13.49 to 1.25, a tenfold reduction. An autoregressive negative control that lacks an initializable latent trajectory shows no comparable gain, suggesting the improvement stems specifically from trajectory initialization rather than from the retrieval step alone.

## Significance  
By breaking prior domination through retrieval‑guided initialization, RAG‑Audio produces audio that more faithfully reflects the underlying stimulus, which is crucial for downstream tasks such as brain‑computer interface applications. The method also demonstrates that simple retrieval can dramatically enhance both perceptual quality and task performance without retraining heavy generative models.
