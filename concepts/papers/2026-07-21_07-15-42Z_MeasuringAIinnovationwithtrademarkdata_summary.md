## Summary  
This research note demonstrates how trademark data can be leveraged to map the development and diffusion of artificial‑intelligence (AI) innovation, complementing traditional patent records. By exploiting a globally available source that spans all economic sectors, AI trademarks reveal both the breadth and the specific ways firms exploit AI technologies in their product and service portfolios. The authors provide an empirical case study of Italian companies to illustrate how trademark filings capture dimensions that patents often miss, such as branding strategies and market positioning. Their work thus offers a new analytical lens for scholars, managers, and policymakers seeking a more complete picture of AI innovation ecosystems.

## Key Contributions  
- **Complementarity with patents:** AI trademarks capture dimensions—branding, service delivery, and market diffusion—that are not fully reflected in patent filings.  
- **Global, sector‑wide coverage:** The data source provides timely, worldwide records that include firms across every industry, enabling cross‑sector analysis of AI innovation.  
- **Multi‑level analytical utility:** Trademark information can be used at strategic (e.g., portfolio planning), operational (e.g., product launch timing) and policy (e.g., regulatory impact) levels.

## Methodology  
The authors assembled a dataset of trademark applications that contain AI‑related terms or technologies, sourced from major international trademark offices. They filtered for filings by firms in the Italian market, extracting fields such as class codes, applicant name, filing date, and description. The dataset was then linked to firm financials and patent records to construct a comparative timeline of innovation activities. Statistical techniques—including time‑series analysis and sector‑level clustering—were applied to identify patterns between trademark activity and AI product/service launches.

## Results  
The empirical exploration shows that AI‑related trademarks are filed at a higher rate than patents in sectors such as consumer electronics and health tech, suggesting early market differentiation. Firms that secure AI trademarks tend to introduce new services within 12–18 months of filing, indicating a lag between innovation capture and public disclosure. Moreover, trademark activity correlates with increased R&D spending, implying that firms view branding as a strategic lever for AI diffusion.

## Significance  
By integrating trademark data into the study of AI innovation, this work provides policymakers with actionable insights on how intellectual‑property regimes influence technology adoption across sectors. For managers, it offers a real‑time gauge of market entry strategies and brand positioning. The global accessibility of the dataset also enables comparative studies between jurisdictions, enriching the literature on IP‑driven diffusion.
