## Summary  
Flow‑matching (FM) is a widely used action‑head paradigm for embodied agents, but its conditional generative nature does not expose an explicit source of uncertainty, leading to potentially unsafe actions when the model misinterprets scenes or receives out‑of‑distribution inputs. This paper addresses that gap by providing a geometric interpretation of FM’s inherent uncertainty and introducing a free proxy—denoising acceleration (accel)—that quantifies this uncertainty without additional training, resampling, or extra forward passes. By linking the model’s output to an ideal affine‑isotropic contraction field in velocity space, the authors show that deviations from this ideal geometry correspond directly to risk of failure. Their work therefore delivers a lightweight, real‑time mechanism for detecting when FM rollouts should be discarded.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A geometric interpretation of FM uncertainty as deviation from an ideal affine‑isotropic contraction field in the velocity field.  
- [Finding 2] The introduction of denoising acceleration (accel), a cost‑free proxy that measures trajectory bending from a single forward pass without extra model evaluations or training.  
- [Finding 3] Empirical validation that accel reliably identifies failing rollouts early, matching or exceeding the performance of costly resampling‑ and training‑based uncertainty baselines.

## Methodology  
The authors first analyze the velocity field generated by FM as a dynamical system: under perfect conditions it should behave like an isotropic affine contraction. They compute the instantaneous “bending” of the denoising trajectory—how much the predicted motion deviates from this ideal field—using only one forward pass through the model and its denoiser. This bending metric, called accel, serves as a proxy for uncertainty because larger deviations indicate that the model is operating far from its expected behavior, which correlates with higher risk of failure. The methodology avoids any additional computational budget: no resampling, no extra training loops, and no per‑step re‑evaluation beyond the standard denoising step.

## Results  
Theoretical analysis demonstrates a strong correlation between accel values and model uncertainty across diverse FM configurations. In practice, experiments on robotics tasks show that accel flags failing rollouts several steps before termination, often outperforming baselines that rely on resampling or additional training. Across three real‑world benchmarks, accel achieves comparable detection rates with a fraction of the latency, confirming its utility in online deployment where computational resources are limited.

## Significance  
Providing an inexpensive, geometry‑based uncertainty proxy enables safe, real‑time control for embodied AI systems that rely on flow matching. By detecting problematic actions early, accel reduces the risk of unsafe behavior without sacrificing performance or requiring costly retraining cycles—critical advantages for autonomous robots operating in uncertain environments.
