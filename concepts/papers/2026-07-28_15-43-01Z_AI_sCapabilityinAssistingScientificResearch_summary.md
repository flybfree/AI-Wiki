## Summary  
The paper investigates how well large language models (LLMs) can generate one‑page research project plans for physics, astrophysics, and cosmology projects and evaluate those plans against human‑written proposals. It creates eight expert‑conceived projects, rewrites each plan with three mid‑2025 LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek), and then blindly assesses the 32 AI‑generated proposals by four human reviewers and two frontier LLMs using a four‑aspect rubric. The study finds that human reviewers rate AI‑ and human‑written plans similarly overall, yet AI reviewers give AI proposals slightly higher scores. Human reviewers correctly identify authorship 72 % (human) and 79 % (AI), while both AI reviewers achieve perfect classification of all proposals. These results suggest LLMs can produce project plans comparable to humans in human eyes, but reveal systematic bias among AI evaluators.

## Methodology  
The authors selected eight research projects in physics, astrophysics, and cosmology that had been conceived by experts. Each project was transformed into a one‑page plan by three contemporary LLMs (ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek) using their default tool access, yielding 32 proposals. Four human reviewers and two frontier LLMs (Claude Opus 4.8 and ChatGPT Pro 5.5) evaluated these proposals blindly with a four‑aspect rubric covering relevance, feasibility, novelty, and impact. Reviewers were also asked to guess whether each proposal was written by a human or an AI.

## Results  
Overall scores for AI‑generated and human‑written proposals were statistically indistinguishable; the average rating difference was negligible. AI reviewers gave AI proposals roughly one point higher on the five‑point scale than human proposals. Human reviewers correctly identified authorship 72 % of the time (human) versus 79 % (AI). Both AI reviewers achieved perfect classification, scoring all 32 proposals as AI‑generated (100 %). No misclassifications occurred among the AI reviewers.

## Significance  
The study highlights that while LLMs can produce project plans that are not obviously inferior to human work, the presence of systematic bias in AI‑driven evaluation could affect fairness and outcomes in grant selection processes. It calls for caution when deploying LLMs widely in proposal preparation and evaluation, as evaluators may unconsciously favor AI outputs.
