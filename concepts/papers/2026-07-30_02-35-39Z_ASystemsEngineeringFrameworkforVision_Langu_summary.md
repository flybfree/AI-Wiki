## Summary  
The paper proposes a systems engineering framework that embeds vision‑language models (VLMs) as coordination agents within the human‑UAV loop for disaster response triage, integrating natural language interaction, mission‑level task coordination, software‑in‑the‑loop testing, and alignment with the Incident Command System. It introduces three core elements—the VLM Coordinator Agent, UAV Mission Control, and Task Allocator—and validates them through simulation and human‑factors evaluation. The framework aims to reduce operator workload while enhancing AI trust and communication clarity in high‑stakes environments.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: A modular architecture that treats the VLM as a coordination agent rather than an advisory tool, enabling direct integration with mission control logic.  
- Finding 2: Integration of Model‑Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) modeling with software‑in‑the‑loop simulation to test end‑to‑end human‑UAV task execution and communication flows.  
- Finding 3: Human‑factors study showing reduced mental demand, effort, and frustration scores, along with high AI trust ratings.

## Methodology  
The authors employed Model‑Based Systems Engineering (MBSE) to define system roles via use‑case and block diagrams that represent the VLM Coordinator Agent, UAV Mission Control, and Task Allocator. They built these components within a unified simulation environment that mirrors real mission flow, allowing automated execution of triage tasks under timed conditions. A human‑factors evaluation involved seven participants performing the same tasks to capture subjective workload and trust metrics.

## Results  
The integrated framework reduced perceived workload across mental demand, effort, and frustration scores; trust in AI rose to 8.9/10; communication clarity improved from 6.2 to 8.5 on a Likert scale. Simulation logged task completion times dropped by 34 % compared with the baseline system.

## Significance  
By embedding VLMs into the human‑UAV loop, the framework scales autonomous coordination for disaster response, lowering operator burden and improving decision speed—critical for civil safety and future aerospace autonomy.
