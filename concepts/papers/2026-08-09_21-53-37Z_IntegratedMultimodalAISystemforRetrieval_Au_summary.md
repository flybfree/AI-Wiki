## Summary  
The paper proposes an integrated multimodal AI system that combines retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG), thermal spectrum perception, vision foundation models, and exploratory wireless sensing to perform robust damage assessment in real‑world conditions. By grounding a locally hosted language model with project‑specific documentation through dynamic retrieval, the authors mitigate hallucinations and improve factual consistency compared with static few‑shot prompting. The system also fuses infrared (IR) and visible imagery for object detection and segmentation, while wireless signals are used to detect post‑event environmental changes where optical sensors fail.

## Key Contributions  
- Dynamic RAG grounding reduces hallucinations by pulling in project‑specific damage criteria, outperforming static few‑shot prompting.  
- Hybrid dense, sparse, and graph‑aware retrieval yields the strongest cross‑document reasoning for damage assessment queries.  
- Multimodal fusion of IR/visible spectra and wireless sensing provides robust object detection and post‑event environmental monitoring.

## Methodology  
The authors built a unified pipeline: first, they developed a RAG component that retrieves relevant documentation via dense vector search or a knowledge graph constructed from entity‑relation extraction. Second, infrared detectors generate candidate detections for objects under adverse lighting, which are then refined by vision foundation models and language models to produce synthetic damage imagery and classify severity. Third, wireless sensors monitor presence, motion, and environmental shifts after the event. All components operate locally, enabling offline inference.

## Results  
Controlled experiments show that dynamic retrieval improves grounding accuracy by up to 15 % relative to static prompting. Graph‑based retrieval outperforms vector‑only RAG on queries requiring multi‑document reasoning, achieving higher F1 scores. IR/visible fusion reduces detection failure modes in low light and heavy rain, increasing segmentation precision from 78 % to 92 %. Synthetic damage images generated by vision foundation models have an average classification accuracy of 94 % for severity levels. Wireless sensing detects post‑event changes with a false‑positive rate below 5 %.

## Significance  
The integrated system delivers reliable, hallucination‑resistant damage assessment across diverse lighting and weather conditions, enabling automated inspection workflows in logistics, construction, and disaster response. By extending retrieval capabilities to knowledge graphs and adding wireless sensing, the approach opens new avenues for contextual reasoning and situational awareness beyond traditional optical sensors.
