## Summary  
The paper proposes a label‑free Parkinson’s disease (PD) screening that combines face and voice data using frozen pretrained encoders, eliminating the need for any PD labels to touch the model inputs. It introduces a contrastive activation addition (CAA) direction derived from synthetic dysarthria degradation and evaluates it via an alignment principle based on cosine similarity with real disease directions. The authors demonstrate that when this similarity exceeds zero, the CAA detector functions effectively, achieving AUROC 0.765 for voice and 0.751 for face‑based anomaly detection. A late‑fusion approach further improves performance to AUROC 0.802 with a high NPV of 0.95, enabling rule‑out triage without external labels.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The study demonstrates that Parkinson’s disease can be screened label‑free by jointly analyzing face and voice embeddings from frozen Vision Transformer and HuBERT encoders.  
- [Finding 2] A contrastive activation addition (CAA) direction, created via time‑stretch and breathy degradation of healthy speech, works as a detector when its cosine similarity with the true disease direction is positive, yielding AUROC 0.765 for voice.  
- [Finding 3] Late‑fusion of the face‑anomaly score and voice CAA score improves overall performance to AUROC 0.802 (95% CI [0.70, 0.89]) with NPV 0.95, supporting clinical triage.

## Methodology  
The authors built two modality pipelines without any PD labels touching the model: a face‑expression Vision Transformer and HuBERT for visual input, and a synthetic‑dysarthria contrastive activation addition (CAA) for audio. The CAA direction is generated by applying time‑stretch and breathy degradation to healthy speech, producing a degradation vector that contrasts with normal speech embeddings. Face detection uses k‑nearest‑neighbor anomaly scoring against the control embedding cluster. Both pipelines are frozen pretrained models; only training controls serve as reference points.

## Results  
On the YouTubePD benchmark, voice CAA achieves AUROC 0.765 and face anomaly AUROC 0.751. The cosine similarity between synthetic and real disease directions is +0.37 for voice (CAA works) and –0.48 for face (CAA fails). Late‑fusion of the two scores reaches AUROC 0.802 with NPV 0.95, indicating strong rule‑out capability. An overfitting audit confirms clean transfer of the voice detector, while the face‑side score may be optimistic pending external validation.

## Significance  
This work enables privacy‑preserving, label‑free Parkinson’s disease screening that can be deployed in real‑time clinical settings without requiring extensive labeled data or expert annotation. By leveraging frozen encoders and a principled CAA alignment metric, the method reduces reliance on scarce PD datasets while maintaining high diagnostic utility, potentially accelerating early detection and resource allocation.
