## Summary  
The paper aims to develop a set of user experience (UX) principles that guide how humans should interact with AI agents in workplace settings, ensuring trust and adoption. It proposes eight core UX principles supported by criteria derived from multiple research methods. The framework serves as an actionable design foundation for both designers and engineers building human‑centric agentic systems. This work contributes a structured taxonomy to future empirical studies on enterprise AI.  

## Key Contributions  
- The identification of eight validated UX principles that address specific interaction challenges in workplace AI.  
- A multi‑method validation process combining participatory design, expert review, meta‑analysis, and interviews to ensure robustness.  
- An actionable framework that translates abstract principles into concrete guardrails for software development.  

## Methodology  
The authors employed a mixed‑methods approach: first they conducted a participatory design workshop with domain experts to surface interaction pain points; then they used paper‑and‑pencil surveys to collect quantitative feedback; followed by expert review of the emerging principle set; performed a meta‑analysis of existing UX literature on AI agents; and finally held in‑depth interviews to contextualize findings. This triangulation aimed to produce both breadth (principle coverage) and depth (justification).  

## Results  
The synthesis yielded eight principles: transparency, relevance, autonomy, trustworthiness, efficiency, safety, adaptability, and ethical alignment. Each principle was supported by specific criteria such as user control mechanisms, data provenance, performance guarantees, and compliance checks. The framework demonstrated high consensus among participants (87 % agreement) and expert reviewers (92 % approval), indicating strong validity.  

## Significance  
These principles provide a common language for stakeholders across design, engineering, and policy, reducing ambiguity in AI deployment. By grounding interactions in human‑centered criteria, the framework can accelerate trust building and mitigate ethical risks, ultimately supporting sustainable adoption of agentic AI in enterprises.
