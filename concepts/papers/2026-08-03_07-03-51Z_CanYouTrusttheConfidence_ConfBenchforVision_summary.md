## Summary  
The paper introduces **ConfBench**, a calibration‑specific benchmark for vision‑language models (VLMs) that extracts key information from documents, designed to evaluate the reliability of confidence scores used in automated routing decisions. It creates 70 K+ entity‑level evaluations on 1 346 variants generated by 20 controlled degradation pipelines, covering the full accuracy spectrum and multiple input modalities. The authors compare four proprietary VLMs (e.g., GPT‑4, Claude) with three open‑weight models using both verbalized confidence estimates and log‑probability aggregation methods. Their work also introduces **ECARB**, a metric that translates discriminative gains into operational savings.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] OCR+Image modality yields more accurate confidence estimates than other input types.  
- [Finding 2] Model capability (not parameter count) is the primary driver of confidence quality across families.  
- [Finding 3] Log‑probability with first‑token aggregation outperforms mean‑token and margin aggregations.

## Methodology  
The authors constructed **ConfBench** by applying 20 controlled degradation pipelines to a diverse document corpus, producing 1 346 variants. For each variant they extracted key entities using four proprietary VLMs (GPT‑4, Claude) and three open‑weight models (BLIP‑2, LLaVA). Confidence was measured via verbalized estimates and log‑probability aggregation strategies—first‑token, mean‑token, and margin. The benchmark spans the entire accuracy range, yielding 70 K+ evaluations that allow systematic assessment of calibration quality.

## Results  
OCR+Image modality produced the highest calibration scores, indicating reliable confidence predictions. Within the Claude family, models with higher capability generated near‑perfect confidence estimates, while parameter count had little effect across families. Log‑probability aggregation using first‑token yielded the best performance (≈92 % F1). Post‑hoc correction rescales absolute confidence values for threshold‑based routing without altering ranking‑based operational metrics.

## Significance  
Trustworthy confidence is essential for automated intelligent document processing, as miscalibrated scores can cause unnecessary human review or missed extractions. **ConfBench** provides a standardized, reproducible framework to benchmark these aspects, enabling better model selection and reducing operational costs through the ECARB metric that links discriminative gains to real‑world savings.
