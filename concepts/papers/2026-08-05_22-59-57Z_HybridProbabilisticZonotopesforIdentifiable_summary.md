## Summary  
The authors propose a Hybrid Probabilistic Zonotope (HProbZ) output head that simultaneously captures discrete mode selection, bounded systematic drift, and stochastic noise in neural network predictions. By modeling these three sources as binary, bounded, and stochastic generators of a zonotope with convolutional likelihood, they achieve identifiable and refinable uncertainty across prediction steps.

## Key Contributions  
- The HProbZ combines three distinct uncertainty components into a single algebraic structure that is analytically tractable.  
- It provides closed‑form likelihood via convolution, enabling refinement of predictive distributions in a single forward pass.  
- The generators are uniquely identifiable from the likelihood up to permutation, and the density cannot be represented by any finite Gaussian mixture.

## Methodology  
The authors define a zonotope as the Minkowski sum of linear subspaces generated by binary mode selectors, bounded drift vectors, and stochastic noise terms. The likelihood is obtained by convolving these generator contributions, which preserves algebraic coupling between successive prediction steps. Identifiability is proved through moment‑matching analysis and permutation invariance arguments, showing that the three generators can be recovered from the observed likelihood.

## Results  
Experiments on benchmark prediction tasks such as image classification and regression demonstrate that HProbZ reduces mean absolute error and improves calibration compared with Gaussian mixture baselines. Theoretical results confirm that the generated density is not a finite mixture of Gaussians and that per‑mode risk can be computed analytically without retraining.

## Significance  
This work bridges discrete mode uncertainty with continuous drift and noise, offering a unified framework for refining predictions in real time without model updates—a capability valuable for safety‑critical and streaming applications where interpretability and efficiency are paramount.
