## Summary  
This paper argues that trademark data can serve as a complementary and timely source for measuring AI innovation, filling gaps left by patents. By analysing the use of AI‑related trademarks across Italian firms, the authors demonstrate how these marks capture diffusion, market entry, and sectoral spread of AI technologies. The study highlights the potential of trademark records to reveal aspects of AI development that are invisible in patent filings alone. Overall, the work contributes a novel methodological framework for tracking AI innovation at multiple analytical levels.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: AI trademarks capture diffusion of AI innovations beyond patent filings.  
- Finding 2: Trademark data provide timely, global coverage across all economic sectors.  
- Finding 3: Empirical evidence from Italian firms shows stronger correlation between trademark activity and new product/service launches than with patents.

## Methodology  
The authors assembled a dataset of AI‑related trademarks filed in Italy over the past decade, matching each mark to its owner’s annual reports and product catalogs. They compared the frequency and timing of trademark registrations with patent applications and firm‑level innovation metrics (R&D expenditure, new hires). The analysis employed event‑study techniques to assess causal links between trademark activity and market diffusion.

## Results  
The empirical results reveal that AI trademarks are filed more frequently than patents for the same firms, especially in non‑technical sectors such as retail and hospitality. Firms with higher trademark activity exhibit a 12 % increase in new product/service launches within two years, suggesting early market testing before patent protection. Moreover, the dataset’s global reach allows cross‑country comparisons of AI innovation diffusion.

## Significance  
By integrating trademark data into AI innovation measurement, policymakers can obtain a broader view of commercial exploitation and sectoral impact that patents alone cannot reveal. This approach supports more accurate forecasting of AI market growth and helps identify early adopters in diverse industries.

AI innovation, trademark data, diffusion, patent complementarity, sectoral coverage, empirical event study, R&D expenditure, new product launches.
