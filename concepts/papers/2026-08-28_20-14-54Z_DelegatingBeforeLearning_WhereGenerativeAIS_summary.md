## Summary  
This paper investigates how twelve university students delegate professional writing tasks to generative AI, focusing on email communication with instructors and administrators. The authors model the AI‑mediated process as a “delegating before learning” workflow that removes the traditional skill‑building loop, shifts focus from recipient‑specific messaging, and redirects confidence to system performance rather than personal ability. By comparing this AI‑augmented process with unaided writing accounts and a classic writing‑process model, three distinct differences emerge that highlight emerging risks for individual development and communication authenticity.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The AI‑mediated email workflow eliminates the iterative learning loop that normally refines students’ professional writing skills.  
- [Finding 2] Messages are increasingly generated without tailoring to a specific recipient, reducing personalization and relevance.  
- [Finding 3] Success is measured by system output rather than writer confidence, shifting accountability away from individual competence.

## Methodology  
The authors conducted semi‑structured interviews with twelve students who regularly used generative AI for academic correspondence. Participants described the full range of AI involvement—from minor edits to complete message generation—and evaluated generated texts on two criteria: “AI‑like” appearance and “voice‑like” authenticity. The researchers then built a theoretical model of this process, juxtaposing it with an unaided writing account and a classic writing‑process framework (pre‑writing, drafting, revising) to identify structural divergences.

## Results  
The comparative analysis revealed three core differences: (1) the learning loop is absent because AI handles revision; (2) recipient specificity diminishes as AI produces generic templates; (3) confidence in successful communication transfers to the AI system. These findings were derived from participants’ narratives and the modeled workflow, confirming that AI delegation reshapes both cognitive and affective dimensions of professional writing.

## Significance  
Understanding this shift is crucial because it threatens the formation of individual writing expertise and erodes trust in human‑generated messages. If institutions merely adopt AI tools without addressing these dynamics, students may never develop authentic communication skills, leading to broader societal impacts on education quality and workplace credibility.
