## Summary  
The paper aims to bridge artificial intelligence (AI) and power‑systems education by providing an open, executable framework that maps core AI techniques onto real power‑system tasks. It introduces a progressive module library—deep neural network templates for load‑curve fitting, a convolutional neural network surrogate for 5‑bus power flow, DNN‑assisted optimization, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) battery control, and physics‑informed neural networks (PINNs) for the swing equation—that lowers entry barriers. The framework is delivered as Jupyter notebooks that run locally or on Google Colab and integrated into an IEEE online course and a PES webinar series. This effort addresses survey data showing 92 % of researchers face AI execution barriers and 94 % desire a hands‑on power‑specific course.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: The framework provides reusable, executable AI modules that follow established engineering domain rules rather than generic black‑box models.  
- Finding 2: It creates an interdisciplinary learning pathway from foundational DNNs to advanced DRL and PINNs, enabling newcomers to progress skillfully.  
- Finding 3: The webinar and course generated high engagement (590 live attendees, 344 repository visits within two weeks), validating the pedagogical impact.

## Methodology  
The authors conducted a community survey to identify pain points, then designed a tiered module library where each AI concept is coupled to a concrete power‑system simulation task. Modules are implemented as Jupyter notebooks that can run offline or on Google Colab, ensuring accessibility. The curriculum was piloted in an IEEE online course and a PES webinar series.

## Results  
The webinar attracted over 590 live attendees—among the top ten IEEE PES webinars—and generated more than 344 repository visits within two weeks, indicating strong interest. The modular approach allowed participants to complete tasks ranging from simple load‑curve fitting to advanced DRL battery control, demonstrating functional progression.

## Significance  
By delivering a hands‑on, executable AI curriculum that aligns with power‑system engineering principles, the work bridges a critical gap between AI research and practical education, encouraging interdisciplinary learning and reducing implementation barriers for practitioners.
