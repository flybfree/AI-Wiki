## Summary  
SciForge is an AI‑native, multimodal workbench designed to unify heterogeneous scientific artifacts—papers, code, datasets, figures, and team decisions—into a single, auditable research state. The system preserves the human graphical interface for judgment while routing complex tasks through modular agent services that perform translation, reasoning, workflow execution, and presentation generation. By integrating goal‑scoped decision governance, evidence provenance tracking, and collaborative workspace planning, SciForge enables reproducible, traceable scientific discovery. Its open‑source implementation runs as a desktop application with mobile supervision support, and future releases will deepen team collaboration.

## Key Contributions  
- **AI‑native multimodal workbench**: SciForge integrates diverse scientific objects (papers, code, datasets, figures) into a coherent research state while keeping the interface human‑centric.  
- **Modular agent services with governance**: The platform employs goal‑scoped decision gates, evidence‑DAG audit trails, and a Scientific Model Router to translate inputs, route them through domain translators, and execute workflows in an auditable manner.  
- **End‑to‑end user cases**: Demonstrated eight real‑world scenarios—including multi‑day agentic gene discovery sprints, AI‑guided de novo protein design, molecular optimization, and genome‑to‑BGC mapping—showcasing practical impact.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by constructing SciForge around five pillars: (i) goal‑scoped scientific decision governance with review gates; (ii) translate‑then‑reason for multimodal input via domain translators; (iii) evidence governance linking claims to provenance chains; (iv) collaborative team science enabling multi‑role governance; and (v) real‑world application scenarios. The system uses a thin interaction layer, contextual research capability patterns, an Agent Runtime & Workflow Engine, an Evidence‑DAG audit sidecar, and a Scientific Model Router to orchestrate tasks.

## Results  
SciForge has been evaluated through eight end‑to‑end user cases, with flagship demonstrations of multi‑day agentic gene discovery, AI‑guided de novo protein design, molecular optimization, and genome‑to‑BGC discovery. The system runs as a desktop application with mobile supervision support; future releases will incorporate deeper team collaboration features. All components are open‑source at https://github.com/AGI4Sci/SciForge.

## Significance  
By providing an auditable, traceable workflow that couples human judgment with AI execution, SciForge reduces manual curation and enhances reproducibility in scientific research. Its governance mechanisms ensure that claims are linked to provenance chains, supporting trustworthy decision‑making. The demonstrated real‑world applications illustrate how the platform can accelerate discovery across genomics, protein engineering, and molecular optimization.
