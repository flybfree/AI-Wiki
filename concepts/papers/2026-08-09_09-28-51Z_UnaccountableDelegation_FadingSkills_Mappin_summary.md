## Summary  
The paper seeks to map the socio‑technical risks that arise when AI agents are deployed in the workplace by creating a taxonomy that captures job‑specific hazards. It builds a three‑component framework linking agents, goals and environment, applies it to 2,078 O*NET tasks to generate 8,356 risk scenarios labeled by severity and deployment mode (automation or augmentation), validates these scenarios with workers and an LLM judge, and then extends an existing taxonomy into a 15‑category framework. The study finds that augmentation can erode skills, erroneous agent actions dominate severe risks, automation mainly threatens organizations while augmentation threatens workers, and the new taxonomy is preferred by workers in most comparisons.

## Key Contributions  
- Augmentation is not inherently safe because overreliance on agents can gradually erode workers' skills and oversight.  
- Erroneous Agent Actions account for the largest share of risk scenarios and have the highest concentration of severe risks, often occurring at the human‑agent boundary.  
- Automation is associated mainly with organizational risks, whereas augmentation is linked primarily to risks that affect individual workers.

## Methodology  
The authors first constructed a multi‑layer framework from a literature review that models three core components—agents, goals, and environment—and their interactions. This framework was embedded in a structured prompt and used to analyze 2,078 job tasks extracted from the O*NET database, producing 8,356 risk scenarios classified by severity and deployment mode (automation or augmentation). The generated scenarios were validated through surveys with 45 workers across ten job roles and an independent LLM judge to confirm plausibility and alignment. Finally, the authors refined an existing taxonomy into a comprehensive 15‑category system that encompasses all identified scenarios.

## Results  
The framework yielded 8,356 distinct risk scenarios covering a wide range of severities and deployment modes. Validation confirmed that these scenarios are realistic and directly tied to the original job tasks. The expanded taxonomy comprises fifteen categories that collectively capture every scenario. In user preference tests, workers rated the new taxonomy as easier than two alternative taxonomies in 64 % of non‑tied comparisons, indicating strong practical utility.

## Significance  
These findings demonstrate that workplace AI agent risks are not solely a function of the technology itself but also emerge from how people collaborate with agents and how deployment choices are made. By highlighting skill erosion linked to augmentation and the concentration of severe errors at human‑agent boundaries, the work underscores the need for policies that protect both organizational stability and individual competence. The resulting taxonomy offers a practical tool for risk assessment and mitigation in AI‑augmented environments.
