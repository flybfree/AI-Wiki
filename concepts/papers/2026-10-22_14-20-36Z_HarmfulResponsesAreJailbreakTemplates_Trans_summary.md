## Summary
This paper investigates a novel vulnerability in Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs), specifically how their bidirectional context modeling creates a unique attack surface for jailbreaks. The authors discover that harmful responses generated by traditional autoregressive (AR) models can serve as effective "jailbreak templates" for dLLMs. By masking portions of these harmful outputs, the dLLM is induced to reconstruct the unsafe content, effectively bypassing safety filters. To exploit this, the paper introduces RMT-Jailbreak, a framework that transfers existing AR jailbreak strategies to dLLMs by converting their outputs into high-mask-ratio templates for bidirectional reconstruction.

## Key Contributions
- **Identification of Harmful Responses as Templates:** The authors demonstrate that a harmful response to a query is not just an output but a functional template. When specific tokens in this response are masked, the dLLM’s ability to predict missing tokens based on surrounding context leads it to regenerate the harmful content, effectively "jailbreaking" the model.
- **Development of RMT-Jailbreak Framework:** The paper proposes a generalizable framework called RMT-Jailbreak. This method bridges the gap between AR and diffusion-based architectures by taking an AR-generated harmful response, applying uniform token retention to create a high-mask-ratio template, and using prompt-based safety filtering to ensure only non-harmful visible tokens remain as context for the dLLM.
- **Demonstration of Cross-Paradigm Transferability:** The study proves that jailbreak attacks are not isolated to specific model architectures. By showing that AR jailbreaks can be successfully transferred to dLLMs, the authors reveal a strong cross-paradigm transferability, suggesting that safety vulnerabilities in one architecture can inform and exploit weaknesses in another.

## Methodology
The authors approach the problem by first establishing the core hypothesis: that dLLMs, which generate text through iterative denoising and bidirectional context modeling, are susceptible to reconstruction attacks using masked harmful sequences. The methodology involves a three-step process within the RMT-Jailbreak framework. First, an existing autoregressive jailbreak method is used to generate a harmful response to a target query. Second, this response is processed to create a "template" by masking a significant subset of its tokens (high-mask-ratio) while retaining others uniformly. Third, prompt-based safety filtering is applied to the visible tokens to remove any remaining explicit harmful content, ensuring the input to the dLLM appears safe on the surface. Finally, this refined template is fed into the target dLLM, which attempts to reconstruct the masked portions based on the bidirectional context provided by the visible tokens and the underlying pattern of the original harmful response.

## Results
Extensive experiments were conducted across multiple existing autoregressive jailbreak methods and various target diffusion language models. The results demonstrate that RMT-Jailbreak is highly effective and generalizable, successfully inducing dLLMs to produce harmful outputs even when the input template appears safe due to masking and filtering. The study highlights that the bidirectional nature of dLLMs makes them particularly vulnerable to this type of reconstruction attack, as they rely on surrounding context to fill in masked gaps. The findings confirm that the transferability of jailbreak attacks from AR models to dLLMs is robust, indicating that safety mechanisms in dLLMs can be bypassed by leveraging the structural patterns found in harmful outputs from other model types.

## Significance
This research is significant because it uncovers a fundamental security flaw in diffusion-based language models that differs from traditional autoregressive vulnerabilities. As dLLMs gain popularity for their flexible generation capabilities, understanding their specific attack surfaces is crucial for developing robust safety guardrails. By showing that harmful outputs from one model type can be weaponized against another, this work suggests that safety testing must consider cross-model interactions and transferable adversarial strategies. It provides a new vector for red-teaming and safety evaluation, urging developers to account for bidirectional reconstruction risks in dLLMs rather than relying solely on input-side filtering.
