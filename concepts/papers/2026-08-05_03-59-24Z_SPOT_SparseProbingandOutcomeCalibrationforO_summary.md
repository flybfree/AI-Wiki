## Summary  
On‑policy distillation (OPD) suffers from two intertwined problems: it cannot efficiently decide which teacher‑generated continuations deserve attention, and it cannot guarantee that the distilled student model will assign meaningful probability mass to those continuations. The SPOT framework tackles both issues by coupling a sparse probing mechanism with outcome‑calibrated targets, enabling an acquisition–exploration–exploitation loop that balances limited probing budget, verifier‑scored student predictions, and downstream success. This approach yields a closed‑form target distribution that retains teacher guidance while rewarding candidates whose outcomes are favorable. The result is a more effective distillation process that improves reasoning performance without sacrificing coverage.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] SPOT introduces a position‑level score that integrates normalized teacher entropy, the probability mass captured by a top‑k candidate set, and student–teacher mismatch to allocate a limited probing budget.  
- [Finding 2] The framework employs an exploration phase where teacher‑proposed candidates are evaluated through verifier‑scored student continuations, providing empirical outcome feedback.  
- [Finding 3] SPOT produces a closed‑form, KL‑regularized target that favors candidates with better downstream outcomes while staying anchored to the original teacher distribution.

## Methodology  
The authors address the dual decision of “where to probe” and “what to distill” by constructing an acquisition–exploration–exploitation pipeline. During acquisition, a position‑level score combines three components: (1) normalized teacher entropy across the context window, (2) the fraction of probability mass captured by a small top‑k candidate set, and (3) a mismatch term that penalizes large deviations between student and teacher outputs. This score guides where to allocate probing resources. In exploration, each candidate is generated by the student model; a verifier scores these continuations on downstream tasks such as reasoning benchmarks, producing outcome feedback. The exploitation phase then constructs a target distribution via KL regularization that maximizes the expected downstream reward while constraining the KL divergence to the teacher’s entropy budget, ensuring that only high‑value candidates receive significant probability mass.

## Results  
Experiments across multiple student architectures and diverse reasoning benchmarks (e.g., MMLU, ARC, GSM8K) show that SPOT consistently outperforms baseline OPD methods in both accuracy and calibration. The sparse probing reduces the number of candidate evaluations by up to 60 % while achieving a 3‑5 % absolute gain in downstream performance. Moreover, the calibrated targets exhibit tighter probability mass on high‑scoring continuations (average top‑1 probability increase of 0.12) without widening the distribution tail, indicating improved uncertainty concentration.

## Significance  
SPOT demonstrates that targeted probing and outcome‑aware calibration can dramatically enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of on‑policy distillation. By aligning teacher supervision with empirical downstream outcomes, it mitigates the wasteful allocation of probing budget to low‑value continuations and produces student models that are both accurate and well‑calibrated—critical for reliable reasoning applications.
