## Summary  
The paper REGARD investigates how large language models (LLMs) from different regions express affective framing of post‑Soviet entities. It proposes a Valence‑Arousal‑Dominance (VAD) profiling framework to capture emotional intensity beyond simple sentiment scores, revealing systematic regional differences that conventional sentiment analysis misses.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] VAD profiling uncovers an arousal dimension that correlates with generic‑answer rates across models.  
- [Finding 2] Ward‑linkage clustering groups the 19 LLMs into three affective response clusters that transcend origin, family, and size.  
- [Finding 3] Models from certain regions consistently produce low‑arousal, templated responses to evaluative prompts.

## Methodology  
The authors query 19 regionally diverse LLMs on a set of 500 post‑Soviet target names, obtaining model outputs. Two independent LLM judges (GPT-4o-mini and Qwen3.6‑35B‑A3B) score each response using VAD metrics. A 300‑item human‑annotated subset validates the scores. Ward linkage clustering is applied to all responses to identify behavioral clusters.

## Results  
The results show a strong negative correlation between arousal scores and generic‑answer rate (r = -0.81). Clustering reveals three distinct affective profiles: high‑arousal, evaluative stance; moderate‑arousal, balanced framing; low‑arousal, templated deflection. These clusters are independent of model origin or parameter count.

## Significance  
By exposing an arousal dimension invisible to sentiment axes, REGARD provides a more nuanced measure of regional affective framing and highlights how LLM alignment may embed cultural biases that affect downstream applications such as content moderation and policy evaluation.
