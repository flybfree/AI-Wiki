## Summary  
The paper aims to develop a set of user experience principles for human‑AI agent interaction in the workplace, grounded in participatory design and expert validation. It proposes an eight‑principle framework that serves as actionable guardrails for designers and engineers. The study seeks to create a structured foundation for future empirical research on enterprise AI agents.

## Key Contributions  
- Identification of eight core UX principles that balance trust, clarity, and autonomy in human‑AI collaboration.  
- Validation of these principles through multiple methods including workshops, expert review, meta‑analysis, and interviews.  
- Provision of a concrete framework with criteria for each principle to guide implementation.

## Methodology  
The authors employed a mixed‑methods approach combining a participatory design workshop where stakeholders co‑created interaction scenarios, paper‑and‑pencil exercises to capture individual preferences, expert review panels to assess feasibility, meta‑analysis to synthesize findings across methods, and in‑depth interviews for qualitative insight. This triangulation ensured robustness.

## Results  
The synthesis yielded eight principles such as Transparency, Predictability, Autonomy, Supportiveness, Relevance, Efficiency, Safety, and Accountability. Each principle was associated with specific design criteria (e.g., transparency requires clear communication of AI decisions). The framework was applied to prototype interactions, showing improved user trust scores.

## Significance  
By offering a validated UX framework, the work reduces ambiguity in deploying AI agents at scale, fostering adoption and mitigating risk. It provides a reusable reference for designers and researchers, accelerating human‑centric AI development.

## Related Concepts  
Human‑AI collaboration, participatory design, trust, transparency, autonomy, workplace productivity, AI ethics, user experience (UX), meta‑analysis, expert review.
