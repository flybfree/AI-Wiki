## Summary  
This study investigates the Qiaojia‑Dongchuan seismic gap, a region that hosts China’s second‑largest hydropower reservoir and is known for its complex tectonic setting. By analyzing a dense catalog of high‑resolution seismicity, the authors demonstrate that shallow events are driven by fluid pressure in the reservoir (high b‑values), while deeper earthquakes reflect accumulated Coulomb stress at a “locked asperity” with low b‑values. The work reveals a vertical decoupling between reservoir‑triggered and tectonic processes, suggesting that induced seismicity can obscure the buildup of deep fault strain. This decoupling framework offers a new lens for evaluating seismic risk in reservoir‑fault systems worldwide.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Shallow activities exhibit high b‑values (> 1.0), indicating fluid‑driven, reservoir‑triggered seismicity.  
- [Finding 2] Deep earthquakes (≈ 20 km) show low b‑values (< 0.8) and rapid Coulomb stress accumulation, forming a locked asperity.  
- [Finding 3] A complex dipping structure is identified, implying compound fault kinematics that may enhance rupture potential.

## Methodology  
The authors employed a high‑resolution dense array catalog from the Qiaojia‑Dongchuan seismic gap, combined with numerical Coulomb stress calculations and b‑value analysis to differentiate shallow and deep events. They applied machine learning to classify seismicity based on amplitude, duration, and location, then integrated these classifications with tectonic loading models to assess strain accumulation.

## Results  
The data reveal a clear vertical separation: shallow quakes are clustered near the reservoir surface with b‑values exceeding 1.0, while deep quakes occur at depths of ~20 km with low b‑values and high stress flux. The calculated cumulative Coulomb stress exceeds critical thresholds, suggesting the gap is in a critical state. The dipping structure correlates with multiple fault segments, indicating compound faulting.

## Significance  
Understanding this decoupling is crucial because reservoir impoundment can mask tectonic strain buildup, leading to underestimation of long‑term rupture risk. By separating fluid‑driven shallow seismicity from deep tectonic locking, the study provides a predictive model for seismic hazard assessment in similar reservoir‑fault complexes.
