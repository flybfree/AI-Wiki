## Summary  
The paper investigates how generative AI is reshaping the commercial landscape of self‑published fiction by producing large volumes of low‑cost, detectable text that compete with human‑written books. By analysing 14,419 Amazon titles sold between 2023 and mid‑2026—without any disclosure of whether they contain AI‑generated content—the authors show that AI‑infused books dominate the catalog but capture a smaller share of sales than non‑AI books, while still climbing to top‑rank positions. Their findings reveal that the market is flooded with more titles than it is revenue, causing revenue per selling book to decline across most genres. This work directly informs the fair‑use defense in copyright litigation by quantifying how AI can dilute the value of human authorship at scale.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Books containing >25 % AI‑generated text constitute a large share of the catalog but only a modest portion of total sales, yet they still achieve commercial success and occupy top‑rank slots.  
- Finding 2: The number of books with observed quarterly sales grows 19.2‑fold while quarterly revenue rises only 8.9‑fold, indicating that AI diffusion inflates inventory without proportionally increasing earnings.  
- Finding 3: Among best‑selling titles, those with substantial AI text exhibit higher linguistic overlap with existing books than non‑AI titles, suggesting AI can amplify similarity and potentially affect fair‑use assessments.

## Methodology  
The authors compiled a dataset of self‑published genre‑fiction titles from Amazon, matched each title to daily sales records through June 2026, and applied an AI detection model to estimate the proportion of AI‑generated text per book. Sales data were aggregated quarterly to compute revenue, rank positions, and inventory growth. The study controlled for genre, author, and Kindle Unlimited availability to isolate the effect of AI diffusion.

## Results  
- AI‑heavy books make up a sizable portion of the catalog but capture less than half of total sales compared with non‑AI books.  
- Quarterly sales volume surged 19.2 times while revenue grew only 8.9 times, leading to a decline in average revenue per selling book across most genres.  
- The overlap between AI‑generated text and existing literature is higher for top‑selling AI‑infused titles than for non‑AI titles, creating a gradient that correlates with their revenue.

## Significance  
These results demonstrate that generative AI can flood markets with low‑cost content, diluting the commercial weight of human authorship and reshaping market dynamics through sheer volume rather than quality. The empirical evidence provides concrete data for legal arguments concerning fair use, copyright infringement, and the economic value of original works in an era of AI‑generated literature.
