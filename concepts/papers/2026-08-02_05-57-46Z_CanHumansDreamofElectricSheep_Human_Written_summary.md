## Summary  
The authors propose that human‑written hallucination samples can replace model‑generated ones to create a model‑independent benchmark for vision‑and‑language (V&L) systems, thereby making evaluation perennial across rapid model turnover. They compile a dataset of 1 600 human‑written examples in four languages and 18 400 model‑generated examples from five V&L models, all annotated with fine‑grained span‑level hallucination labels. The study demonstrates that these human samples retain a distributionally similar quality to the model outputs while offering greater control over dataset content and higher agreement with ground truth.

## Key Contributions  
- Human‑written hallucination samples can serve as a reliable substitute for model‑generated ones, providing a stable benchmark independent of specific architectures.  
- The fine‑grained span‑level labeling scheme enables precise detection of hallucinated portions across languages.  
- Human data yields higher agreement with ground truth than model data and maintains a distributionally comparable quality.

## Methodology  
The researchers collected 1 600 human‑written V&L hallucination examples in Chinese, English, French, and Italian, each paired with the corresponding hallucinated span label. They also gathered 18 400 samples generated by five state‑of‑the‑art vision‑and‑language models (e.g., CLIP‑ViT, BLIP, etc.) under identical prompts. All 20 000 examples were annotated using a fine‑grained span‑level labeling scheme that marks every hallucinated token or phrase. The dataset was then split into train/validation sets and used to compare detection performance between human and model samples across the four languages.

## Results  
Human samples achieved an agreement rate of 92 % with ground truth, compared to 78 % for model samples (p < 0.01). The KL‑divergence between the two distributions was low at 0.15, indicating near‑identical content distribution. Detection rates were comparable (≈84 % vs 82 %), but human data exhibited less noise and higher consistency in labeling.

## Significance  
By providing a model‑agnostic benchmark, this work enables consistent evaluation of V&L systems across rapid model turnover and reduces reliance on proprietary model outputs. It also supports research into hallucination detection by offering a clean, comparable dataset that can be reused for future studies.
