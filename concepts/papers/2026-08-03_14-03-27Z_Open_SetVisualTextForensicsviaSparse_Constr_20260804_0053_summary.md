## Summary  
The paper tackles the growing problem of detecting visual text forgeries that are generated by rapidly advancing generative AI, which often evade existing discriminative detectors. To overcome this limitation, the authors introduce a generator‑detector framework called Sparse‑Constraint Rectified Flow (SC‑RF) that does not rely on learning forgery‑specific decision boundaries but instead estimates the local restoration cost needed to bring a query image back into alignment with authentic visual‑text statistics. By combining a detector‑oriented adaptation of Flow Matching with self‑supervised artifact injection and a pixel‑space forensic network, SC‑RF can locate tampering even in open‑set scenarios where the attacker uses unseen editing patterns. The method achieves state‑of‑the‑art detection performance on three benchmarks and demonstrates strong zero‑shot robustness.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A generative detector that estimates local restoration cost rather than learning forgery‑specific boundaries, enabling open‑set visual text forensic analysis.  
- [Finding 2] Sparse‑Constraint Rectified Flow (SC‑RF), a detector‑oriented adaptation of Flow Matching that enforces spatial sparsity to focus on anomalous regions.  
- [Finding 3] Self‑supervised Artifact Injection and pixel‑space Forensic‑DiT, which mitigate data scarcity while preserving high‑frequency forensic traces.

## Methodology  
The authors approach the problem by first defining a restoration cost that quantifies how much an image deviates from authentic visual‑text statistics. SC‑RF then uses Flow Matching as its core generative model but restricts the flow to sparse constraints, forcing the network to attend only to anomalous pixels. To address data scarcity, they inject synthetic artifacts into training images and treat these as self‑supervised labels, allowing the detector to learn without explicit forgery annotations. High‑frequency forensic cues are captured by a pixel‑space Forensic‑DiT that operates directly on image patches, ensuring fine details remain detectable.

## Results  
SC‑RF outperforms the leading runner‑up on three benchmark datasets, improving F1 scores by 3.2 percentage points and IoU by 4.8 percentage points. The detector shows strong zero‑shot performance on challenging unseen text editing patterns, indicating generalization beyond training data. An auxiliary stress‑test analysis reveals that the local harmonization produced by SC‑RF can subtly weaken statistical cues relied upon by existing detectors, offering a complementary vulnerability‑analysis perspective.

## Significance  
This work matters because generative AI is increasingly used to create deceptive visual text content, and current forensic tools often fail when faced with novel attacks. By providing a robust, zero‑shot detection method that also reveals how its own corrections may erode existing cues, SC‑RF advances both practical security and theoretical understanding of adversarial manipulation.
