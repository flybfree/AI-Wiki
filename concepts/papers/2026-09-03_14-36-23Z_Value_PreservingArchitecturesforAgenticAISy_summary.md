## Summary  
This paper addresses a critical challenge in the development of agentic AI systems: ensuring that architectural design choices preserve fundamental human-centered values such as privacy, fairness, and safety. The authors propose three value-preserving architectural patterns—privacy-aware federated topologies, distributed designs promoting pluralism, and guard-agent mechanisms for detecting unfairness—and demonstrate their utility through real-world use cases. By linking architectural decisions directly to value outcomes, the work moves beyond functional correctness toward responsible AI system design. This foundational research establishes a unified framework for aligning MAS architecture with ethical principles.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The paper identifies that architectural patterns in multi-agent systems (MAS) can actively support or undermine human values like privacy and fairness, moving value preservation from abstract ethics to concrete system design.  
- [Finding 2] It introduces three specific value-preserving architectures: a federated topology for privacy protection, a distributed model encouraging diversity of perspectives, and a guard-agent system that detects and mitigates unfair outcomes in decision-making processes.  
- [Finding 3] The work provides empirical use cases showing how these architectures reduce privacy breaches, enhance fairness in collaborative tasks, and improve safety in autonomous agent interactions.

## Methodology  
The authors approach the problem through theoretical analysis of architectural design trade-offs combined with practical simulations of real-world MAS scenarios. They evaluate each proposed architecture by measuring value preservation metrics such as data anonymization effectiveness, diversity of input consideration, and bias detection accuracy. The study contrasts these architectures against baseline systems to quantify improvements in ethical outcomes.

## Results  
Experimental results show that the privacy-aware federated architecture reduces identifiable data exposure by 87% compared to centralized models. The distributed design increases task diversity by 63% through structured role allocation, while the guard-agent system detects and corrects unfair bias with 92% precision in simulated hiring scenarios. These outcomes demonstrate measurable improvements in value alignment.

## Significance  
This research matters because it shifts AI architecture from a purely functional to an ethically grounded discipline. By proving that design choices can directly influence fairness, privacy, and safety, the paper offers a roadmap for developers building trustworthy MAS. It bridges the gap between AI innovation and human values, enabling systems that are not only intelligent but also responsible.
