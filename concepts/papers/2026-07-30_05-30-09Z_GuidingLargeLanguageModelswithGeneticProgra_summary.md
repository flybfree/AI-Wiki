## Summary  
The paper proposes using genetic programming‑evolved heuristic rules to guide large language model (LLM) decisions in dynamic multi‑mode project scheduling, addressing the weaknesses of zero‑shot LLM outputs such as lack of domain knowledge and high token consumption. It introduces four mechanisms—Feature Selection, Feature Hint, Rule Reference, and Rule Follow—to inject GP‑derived knowledge into an online LLM decision maker for scheduling tasks.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: High‑quality GP rules can improve LLM decision quality by providing explicit scheduling logic that the model can follow.  
- Finding 2: Among the four mechanisms, Feature Selection yields the best token efficiency while Rule Follow achieves strong performance at a higher token cost.  
- Finding 3: Guidance from GP rules enhances decision stability and shifts the feature focus in generated rationales away from generic language toward relevant scheduling factors.

## Methodology  
The authors extract knowledge from evolved genetic programming rules and embed it into an LLM via four injection points that operate at different levels of abstraction. They evaluate these mechanisms on a benchmark of dynamic multi‑mode project scheduling tasks, measuring performance metrics such as schedule optimality, token consumption, decision consistency, and relevance of the rationale generated by the model.

## Results  
Feature Selection reduces token usage by roughly 30 % while maintaining comparable objective scores; Rule Follow yields higher objective values but consumes 2–3× more tokens. Both methods increase decision stability (fewer rule violations) and cause rationales to focus on pertinent features rather than generic language. The study demonstrates that simplifying the decision context or supplying explicit logic improves outcomes, suggesting that direct rule following is effective when token budget allows.

## Significance  
By bridging domain‑specific heuristic knowledge with LLMs, the work reduces reliance on human expertise, lowers computational cost, and enables transparent scheduling explanations—critical for real‑time project management systems where fast, accurate decisions are required.
