## Summary  
Scientific claim verification over a cited paper requires predicting both the claim–paper relation and the paragraphs that justify it. This setting faces two intertwined challenges: within‑paper distractors often resemble genuine evidence, while a classifier trained on gold evidence must operate on retrieved evidence at inference time. To address these issues, the authors propose HNR‑DAC, a two‑stage framework that trains each stage only on the cases it will encounter in practice. Hard‑Negative Reranking (HNR) quantifies how confusable non‑gold paragraphs are, while Distribution‑Aligned Classification (DAC) aligns classifier training with the inference input generated by the same frozen HNR.

## Key Contributions  
- Introduces HNR‑DAC, a two‑stage framework that jointly optimizes hard‑negative reranking and distribution‑aligned classification for scientific claim verification.  
- Develops Hard‑Negative Reranking (HNR) to quantify evidence confusability by comparing gold evidence against the most confusable non‑gold paragraphs using a base reranker’s scores.  
- Introduces Distribution‑Aligned Classification (DAC) that trains on the Top‑1 paragraph generated by the frozen HNR, ensuring classifier input matches inference data.

## Methodology  
The authors first train a base reranker on all non‑gold paragraphs to obtain relevance scores; these scores are used to identify the top three most confusable candidates for each gold evidence set. The resulting Hard‑Negative Reranking step produces both a ranked list and a frozen Top‑1 identifier that will serve as the input evidence for the second stage. In Distribution‑Aligned Classification, only instances where the Top‑1 paragraph is actually used at inference are included in training, guaranteeing that the classifier sees exactly what it will evaluate. This staged approach ensures that each component learns from its own data distribution without leakage.

## Results  
On the NLPCC 2026 Task 10 Track 2 benchmark, the final HNR‑DAC configuration achieves 97.21 % Hit@3, 95.79 % Macro‑F1, and 94.47 % Joint@3, yielding an average score of 95.13. The submission ranks third on the official leaderboard while attaining the highest overall Macro‑F1 of 93.05%, along with 70.16 % Joint@3 and an average score of 81.61.

## Significance  
HNR‑DAC demonstrates that separating hard‑negative mining from distribution‑aligned classification can substantially improve claim verification performance, especially when noisy or misleading evidence is present in scientific literature. The high scores indicate that the framework effectively handles within‑paper distractors and aligns training with real inference conditions, offering a practical solution for automated scientific knowledge extraction.
