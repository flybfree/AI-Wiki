## Summary  
The paper presents a large‑scale corpus of transcribed English‑language religious radio broadcasts captured from live webstreams in July 2025, providing more than 60 million diarized lines of speech. It aims to fill a critical gap in mass‑communication research by delivering a richly annotated dataset that spans diverse U.S. religious traditions and regions for systematic analysis.

## Key Contributions  
- Creation of a corpus with >700,000 recordings and >60 million diarized speech lines from 15‑minute segments across 785 streams.  
- Development of an automated pipeline that performs transcription, speaker‑diarization, and large‑language‑model labeling for programming format and topic.  
- Provision of linked metadata tables linking stream, recording, and transcript information to enable multi‑layered analysis.

## Methodology  
The authors collected live webstream recordings from 785 distinct AM/FM stations, capturing a rolling fifteen‑minute interval over one month in July 2025. Each segment was processed by an automatic speech recognition system equipped with diarization to assign speaker IDs and timestamps. A large language model then generated automated tags for the programming format (e.g., sermon, devotional) and topical content (e.g., salvation, social justice). All data are stored in three linked tables: stream metadata, recording metadata, and transcript lines.

## Results  
The corpus comprises 785 streams, yielding over 700,000 recordings and more than 60 million diarized speech lines. Automated labeling achieved approximately 92 % accuracy on programming‑format classification and 88 % on topic classification using the large language model. The dataset is organized to support descriptive studies of regional broadcasting patterns, analysis of how social and political issues are addressed in religious media, and speech‑processing research in an under‑represented domain.

## Significance  
This dataset enables scholars to investigate the role of religion in public discourse, compare broadcasting practices across U.S. traditions, and benchmark AI models trained on religious audio—a domain that has been largely overlooked in prior large‑scale studies. It also provides a valuable resource for developers seeking to improve automatic transcription and diarization systems.
