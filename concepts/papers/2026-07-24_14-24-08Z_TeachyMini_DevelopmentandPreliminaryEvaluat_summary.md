## Summary  
This paper aims to create a knowledge‑based generative social robot (Teachy Mini) that can provide responsible tutoring support in higher education by operationalizing the Knowledge‑Based Design (KBD) requirements identified in prior work. The authors integrated system prompting, retrieval‑augmented generation, and stateful prompt orchestration into the Reachy Mini platform to embed these principles. A preliminary evaluation compared Teachy Mini with a control robot that did not follow KBD. Results showed that Teachy Mini was perceived as more aligned with responsible tutoring behavior than the control version.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Participants rated Teachy Mini as significantly more aligned with responsible tutoring behavior than the control robot.  
- Finding 2: A manipulation check demonstrated that Teachy Mini consistently applied personalization, slide‑grounded explanations, Socratic questioning, affective support, and learner‑anchored feedback, whereas the control robot did not.  
- Finding 3: Exploratory analyses suggested a positive association between KBD implementation and objective learning gains when learner preferences were accounted for.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by selecting specific KBD requirements that define responsible tutoring behavior in higher education. They operationalized these requirements through system prompting, where the robot’s prompts are generated based on retrieved knowledge bases, retrieval‑augmented generation to fetch relevant information, and stateful prompt orchestration that maintains a coherent dialogue across turns. This pipeline was implemented within the Reachy Mini platform, enabling Teachy Mini to produce tutoring responses that are both generative and grounded in a structured knowledge base.

## Results  
Twenty‑four university students completed a robot‑guided learning session on research methodologies using either Teachy Mini or a control version. The KBD‑based robot received higher ratings for responsible behavior, and the manipulation check confirmed its consistent use of personalization, Socratic questioning, affective support, and learner‑anchored feedback. No significant differences emerged in system acceptance, intrinsic motivation, or overall learning effectiveness between conditions; however, exploratory analyses indicated that when learner preferences were considered, KBD implementation correlated with slightly higher objective learning gains.

## Significance  
This study provides an initial demonstration that Knowledge‑Based Design can shape the behavior of generative social robots toward responsible tutoring. By embedding KBD principles into a robot’s interaction pipeline, Teachy Mini shows promise for enhancing both ethical alignment and learning outcomes in educational settings, offering a template for future GSR development.
