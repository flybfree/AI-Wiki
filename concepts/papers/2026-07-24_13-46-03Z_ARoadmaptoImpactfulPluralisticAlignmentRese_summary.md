## Summary  
The paper argues that pluralistic value alignment—creating AI systems that reflect a wide range of human values and perspectives—has not yet translated into measurable impact on the models deployed by major labs. By auditing public behavior documents and evaluation frameworks, the authors find no explicit reference to pluralism in training or testing protocols, indicating a gap between normative aspirations and real‑world adoption. Their contribution is a roadmap that identifies three concrete research gaps: (1) empirical evidence of how pluralistic AI benefits users; (2) a clear operational definition of what “pluralistic” behavior should look like; and (3) trade‑off‑aware evaluation methods that fit production constraints. The authors call for moving beyond speculative justifications to data‑driven, implementable work.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] No public evidence that pluralistic alignment has been incorporated into training or evaluation of frontier AI systems.  
- [Finding 2] Lack of a concrete, operational goal for what constitutes desirable pluralistic behavior in deployed models.  
- [Finding 3] Existing metrics and trade‑off analyses are not “hill‑climbable” for production‑grade LLMs.

## Methodology  
The authors performed a systematic literature audit of public behavior documents, research papers, and evaluation frameworks from leading AI labs up to July 2026. They extracted references to pluralistic alignment, mapped the prevalence of each concept across sources, and identified where it was absent or only mentioned in normative contexts. This qualitative mapping was supplemented by interviews with a small set of researchers (n=5) to gauge perceived barriers to adoption.

## Results  
The audit revealed that out of 120 publicly available behavior documents, only two mentioned pluralism, both as abstract goals rather than operational practices. Evaluation frameworks typically prioritize safety, utility, or fairness without explicit pluralistic criteria. The three findings above were directly derived from these observations and the interview insights.

## Significance  
If AI systems serving billions of users do not incorporate pluralistic alignment, the promised societal benefits remain unrealized. Addressing these gaps could improve model relevance, reduce bias, and foster trust among diverse user groups, thereby accelerating responsible deployment.
