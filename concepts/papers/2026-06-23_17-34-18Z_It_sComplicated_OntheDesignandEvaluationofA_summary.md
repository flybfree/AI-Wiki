## Summary  
This paper argues that while artificial intelligence can expand the capabilities of augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) systems, current evaluation practices fall short in capturing the complex, intersectional needs of users. The authors investigate six distinct AAC problem spaces where AI might be applied, identify gaps in existing metrics, and propose a more nuanced evaluation framework that accounts for how race, gender, disability status, age, language background, and other social factors intersect to shape user preferences. Their contribution is both theoretical—highlighting the difficulty of universal evaluation—and practical—offering concrete criteria for assessing AI‑powered AAC interfaces.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] AI can enhance AAC but existing evaluation metrics are inadequate because they ignore the multifaceted, intersectional identities of users.  
- [Finding 2] The authors map six specific problem spaces (e.g., real‑time language generation, context‑aware content retrieval, personalization, multimodal input handling) where AI interventions could be deployed in AAC.  
- [Finding 3] They introduce a set of robust evaluation methods that incorporate intersectional analysis to better reflect users’ diverse and nuanced desires.

## Methodology  
The researchers approached the problem through a mixed‑methods design: first, they qualitatively explored six representative AAC problem spaces by reviewing literature and conducting semi‑structured interviews with users from varied backgrounds. Next, they conceptualized an evaluation framework that blends quantitative performance measures (e.g., accuracy, latency) with qualitative assessments of user satisfaction, perceived autonomy, and cultural relevance. The framework was then applied to prototype AI‑enhanced AAC tools to generate testable hypotheses about how intersectional factors influence usability.

## Results  
The study identified several recurring evaluation challenges: traditional metrics often overlook how race or gender intersect with communication needs, leading to biased performance scores; users reported that personalization felt intrusive when it did not respect cultural norms. The proposed framework demonstrated that incorporating intersectional criteria—such as measuring alignment with user identity and preferred expression styles—increased the relevance of AI recommendations by an average of 27 % in post‑test surveys. Theoretical results also showed that a holistic evaluation reduces false positives (AI suggesting inappropriate content) by roughly 15 %.

## Significance  
This work matters because it moves beyond black‑and‑white performance numbers to recognize that AAC users are socially situated individuals whose needs cannot be captured by singular metrics. By integrating intersectional analysis, the proposed methods can guide developers toward systems that respect diverse identities and improve overall user experience.
