## Summary  
This paper addresses the growing need for a practical, engineering‑grounded bridge between artificial intelligence and power systems education. It proposes an open, executable module library that maps core AI concepts onto representative power‑system tasks, thereby lowering entry barriers for newcomers who rely on large language models rather than building their own tools. The framework is delivered as Jupyter notebooks accessible locally or via Google Colab through an IEEE online course and a PES webinar series. By providing reusable, hands‑on material, the work directly responds to survey findings that 92 % of researchers encounter barriers before running AI models and 94 % desire a power‑specific hands‑on course.

## Key Contributions  
- **Engineering‑Grounded AI (EGAI) framework** – establishes a systematic approach where AI workflows follow established engineering and power‑system domain rules rather than remaining task‑agnostic.  
- **Progressive difficulty ladder of executable modules** – creates three progressive, open Jupyter notebooks: DNN templates for load‑curve fitting; a CNN surrogate for a 5‑bus power flow; and advanced AI tools (DRL for battery control, PINNs for the swing equation).  
- **Community‑driven delivery** – leverages an IEEE online course and PES webinar to disseminate the modules, achieving >590 live attendees and >344 repository visits within two weeks.

## Methodology  
The authors began with a community survey of researchers and practitioners to quantify AI barriers in power systems. This data informed the design of a three‑tiered module library that progressively introduces foundational deep neural network concepts, then domain‑coupled convolutional networks for power flow surrogate modeling, and finally frontier techniques such as DRL and PINNs. All modules are packaged as runnable Jupyter notebooks, ensuring local or cloud execution without requiring proprietary software licenses.

## Results  
The webinar series attracted more than 590 live attendees—ranking among the ten most‑attended IEEE PES webinars—and generated over 344 visits to the repository within two weeks. These metrics demonstrate that the framework successfully engages both academic and industry audiences, confirming its utility in reducing practical barriers and fostering adoption of AI tools in power systems.

## Significance  
By delivering a reusable, executable educational platform, this work bridges the gap between cutting‑edge AI research and traditional power‑system curricula. It provides engineers with concrete, domain‑specific AI models that can be integrated into teaching and research, thereby accelerating interdisciplinary learning and practical application of AI in energy systems.
