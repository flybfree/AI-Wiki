## Summary  
The Qiaojia‑Dongchuan seismic gap, which hosts the world’s second‑largest hydropower station, is examined to understand how anthropogenic reservoir impoundment interacts with tectonic loading. The authors aim to separate shallow, fluid‑driven seismicity from deep, tectonically locked activity and determine whether the fault system lies in a critical state of rupture potential. Their contribution is a dual‑mechanism framework that quantifies both shallow b‑value anomalies and deep Coulomb stress accumulation. By demonstrating that these processes can operate independently yet coexist, the study provides a new diagnostic tool for assessing seismic risk in reservoir‑fault systems worldwide.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Shallow activities exhibit high b‑values (≈ 1.0), indicating fluid‑driven reservoir‑triggered seismicity rather than tectonic loading.  
- [Finding 2] Deep seismicity at ~20 km depth shows low b‑values (< 0.8) and a high Coulomb stress accumulation rate, characteristic of a locked asperity.  
- [Finding 3] A complex dipping structure is identified, suggesting compound fault kinematics that may enhance rupture potential.

## Methodology  
The authors compiled a dense seismic array catalog covering the Qiaojia‑Dongchuan gap and applied standard b‑value calculations to each event. They measured cumulative Coulomb stress using the fault’s geometry and stress‑accumulation formulas, then performed 3‑D structural analysis to infer fault dipping and kinematics. The data were processed with a combination of seismic moment estimation and finite‑element modeling to separate shallow from deep sources.

## Results  
The primary experimental results are: (1) a clear vertical decoupling between shallow reservoir events and deeper tectonic activity; (2) the deep zone accumulates stress at a rate that approaches critical thresholds, implying a high likelihood of rupture; and (3) the fault system’s dipping geometry supports compound faulting, which can amplify seismic energy. These findings confirm that the gap is in a critical state despite the presence of low‑amplitude shallow seismicity.

## Significance  
This work matters because it reveals how reservoir impoundment can mask the silent buildup of tectonic strain, potentially leading to large earthquakes. By providing a quantitative dual‑mechanism model, the study improves global risk assessments for hydropower sites and other reservoir‑fault intersections where human activities intersect with natural stress fields.
