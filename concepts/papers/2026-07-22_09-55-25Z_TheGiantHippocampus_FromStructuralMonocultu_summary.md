## Summary  
The paper argues that AI models such as the Transformer represent a structural monoculture that misrepresents the brain’s hierarchical, modular architecture and proposes a “System of Systems” design to correct this error. It seeks to bridge insights from cognitive neuroscience with AI architecture by advocating heterogeneous topological networks that respect distinct computational needs.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The Transformer is a functional analog of the hippocampus rather than a general‑purpose cortex, reflecting a misapplication of structural principles.  
- [Finding 2] Historical cytoarchitectural evidence shows distinct cognitive functions are implemented by qualitatively different structures, contradicting the idea of scaling one template.  
- [Finding 3] Mixture‑of‑Experts merely partitions parameters among identical experts, offering no true diversity.

## Methodology  
The authors conduct a comparative analysis that integrates neuroanatomical data (Brodmann maps, Patch‑seq) with AI architectural studies. They trace the historical shift from convolutional networks to Transformers, emphasizing how hardware constraints and convenience led to adoption of a single architecture despite evidence for modularity.

## Results  
Empirical comparison demonstrates that CNNs achieve state‑of‑the‑art image recognition with far less data than Transformers, supporting the claim that local receptive fields and hierarchical depth encode prior knowledge. The paper also shows that MoE models lack true diversity because expert parameters are identical across partitions.

## Significance  
This work matters because it challenges the prevailing paradigm of monolithic scaling in AI, offering a principled design framework rooted in biological modularity. It encourages architects to specify heterogeneity early, reducing wasted compute and improving task suitability.
