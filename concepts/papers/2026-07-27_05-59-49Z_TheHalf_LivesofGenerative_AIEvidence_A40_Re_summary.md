## Summary  
The paper audits a purposive corpus of 40 empirical records published between July 2025 and July 2026 to examine how quickly generative‑AI evidence can become obsolete, using a “claim‑currency” framework that separates model age from the validity of its findings. It proposes six reporting practices for transparent AI‑assisted research and treats its own two‑day production process as a reflexive case of frontier‑model‑assisted creation. The audit reveals that the median age of the newest named model is 281 days, with ages ranging from 11 to 939 days across the dataset. By applying its own Model Facts and currency statement, the authors demonstrate how rapid AI assistance can be inspected without treating model output as independent validation.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The median age of the newest named generation in the 40‑record audit is 281 days (range 11–939 days), showing that AI evidence can become outdated within a few months.  
- [Finding 2] Model age and claim‑currency are distinct concepts; the authors introduce six reporting practices to capture both, enabling systematic tracking of model refreshes and superseded families.  
- [Finding 3] The reflexive case demonstrates that GPT‑5.6 Sol Pro can aid research creation (discovery, reconciliation, drafting) while the author retains full responsibility, illustrating a transparent AI‑assisted workflow.

## Methodology  
The authors collected a maximum‑variation purposive sample of 40 empirical records, each coded for publication route, execution timing, model identity, age of the newest named generation or immutable snapshot, same‑family supersession behavior, and refresh events. Thirty‑five records involved a superseded family; seven provided precise dated identifiers; three featured refreshed model evidence; one added a late sensitivity test. All 40 used an OpenAI system as a constant feature to control for platform effects.

## Results  
The audit shows that the median age of the newest named model is 281 days, with a middle 50 % spanning 75–478 days and a full range of 11–939 days. Twenty‑five journal articles were older (median 395 days), fourteen preprints younger (median 56 days) and one laboratory report the youngest (median 49 days). The claim‑currency framework identifies six reporting practices that capture model age, supersession, refreshes, and sensitivity testing.

## Significance  
This work matters because it exposes the rapid decay of AI‑generated evidence without conflating temporal distance with evidential reliability. By separating model age from claim currency and providing a concrete audit methodology, researchers can evaluate whether AI assistance is enhancing or misleading scientific communication. The reflexive case also offers a template for transparent, accountable AI‑assisted research production.
