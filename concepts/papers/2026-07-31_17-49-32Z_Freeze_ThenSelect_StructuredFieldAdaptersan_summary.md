## Summary
This research paper addresses the critical challenge of discovering partial differential equations (PDEs) from sparse and noisy observational data, a task that traditionally struggles with the coupled optimization of field reconstruction and term selection. The authors identify that standard neural approaches often fail to maintain the correct support of differential terms throughout training, leading to unstable or incorrect equation discovery. To resolve this, they propose a novel "freeze-then-select" framework that decouples these two processes by first reconstructing the continuous field using a structured adapter and then applying Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) to identify the governing equation. This methodology allows for robust PDE discovery even in regimes with extremely limited data, outperforming existing classical and neural baselines across multiple challenging dynamical systems.

## Key Contributions
- The authors provide a detailed analysis of optimization paths in coupled neural PDE discovery, categorizing support behavior into three distinct types: persistent exact support, transient appearance, or complete failure to emerge during training.
- They introduce a structured field adapter that factorizes the observed field into learned spatial features and temporal coefficients represented by cubic splines, enabling accurate reconstruction without relying on immediate PDE residuals.
- The development of Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS), a novel term selection mechanism that identifies recurrent terms across independent weak-form systems, refits candidate supports, and validates the final equation on held-out data, ensuring stability and generalizability.

## Methodology
The proposed approach operates in two distinct phases to mitigate the instability inherent in joint optimization. First, the structured field adapter is trained exclusively on sparse observations without any PDE residual constraints. This adapter factorizes the complex spatiotemporal field into a product of learned spatial features and temporal coefficients modeled by cubic splines, effectively reconstructing the underlying continuous dynamics. Once the field reconstruction is stabilized, the system is "frozen," and the second phase begins. The Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) algorithm then analyzes this fixed field to identify candidate differential terms. It does so by identifying terms that recur consistently across independent weak-form systems. After refitting the supports for these candidates, the method selects the final equation based on its performance on held-out weak-form systems, ensuring that the selected PDE is not merely a result of overfitting but represents a stable dynamical structure. The framework is flexible enough to work with fixed libraries as well as expressions generated by genetic programming.

## Results
The method was evaluated across six sparse regimes defined in the MDBench benchmark suite. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed approach achieves the highest exact support recovery rates among all tested methods. Notably, it shows significant improvements over both classical symbolic regression techniques and state-of-the-art neural baselines. The gains are particularly pronounced when dealing with challenging Kuramoto-Sivashinsky dynamics, where traditional methods often fail due to high nonlinearity and sparsity. Additionally, the authors successfully applied their method to genetic programming-generated expressions, recovering the power-law form of an unknown nonlinear diffusion function from sparse, noisy observations, thereby validating the versatility of the freeze-then-select principle.

## Significance
This work is significant because it fundamentally shifts the paradigm of PDE discovery by decpling field reconstruction from equation selection. By addressing the instability issues that plague coupled neural optimization, it enables reliable discovery in data-scarce environments where previous methods fail. This advancement is crucial for scientific applications involving complex physical systems where high-resolution data is often unavailable or prohibitively expensive to obtain.
