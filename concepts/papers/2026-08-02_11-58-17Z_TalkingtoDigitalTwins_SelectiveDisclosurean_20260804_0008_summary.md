## Summary  
The paper tackles the challenge of measuring what financial‑media influencers do not explicitly disclose on social media by creating “digital twins” that capture their hidden beliefs in real time. By conducting repeated, protocol‑driven interviews with these digital twins before any market move occurs, the authors generate a panel of belief proxies that can be linked to large‑cap stock returns without look‑ahead bias. The core contribution is showing that selective disclosure—information omitted from public posts—can still be quantified and used as a predictor of market movements.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A systematic method for extracting hidden belief information from social media personas via repeated real‑time interviews, producing a measurable panel of market views.  
- [Finding 2] The interview protocol eliminates look‑ahead bias because data are archived before the relevant return windows, allowing ex‑ante measurement of disclosures.  
- [Finding 3] Empirical evidence that the extracted belief proxies predict the direction of large‑cap stock returns with statistical significance.

## Methodology  
The authors monitor X accounts belonging to “finfluencers” and construct digital twins—synthetic agents that embody each follower’s public persona. A fixed interview protocol is applied at regular intervals, prompting the twin to express its current view on specific stocks. These interviews are generated in real time and stored before any subsequent market outcome is observed, thereby avoiding the look‑ahead bias typical of LLM queries performed ex post. The resulting dataset constitutes a panel where each row links a disclosed belief proxy to a later realized stock return.

## Results  
Statistical analysis reveals that the cross‑section of these belief proxies aligns with the expected direction of large‑cap stock returns, confirming that selective disclosure is not merely noise but carries predictive power. The predictive strength persists across multiple time points and asset classes within the sample, suggesting robustness to temporal variation in social media activity.

## Significance  
By turning voluntary, non‑public disclosures into quantifiable market signals, this work bridges the gap between social media analytics and financial forecasting. It offers a novel tool for investors seeking early insight from influencer behavior, potentially improving portfolio construction and risk management strategies.

## Related Concepts  
- Digital twin: a virtual replica of an online persona that can be interrogated in real time.  
- Selective disclosure: the practice of sharing only selected information on social media while withholding others.  
- Belief measurement: quantifying subjective opinions through structured interview protocols.  
- Financial social media: platforms where market participants discuss assets, influencing price movements.  
- Look‑ahead bias: a statistical artifact that occurs when future data are used to inform present decisions.
