## Summary
This paper addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of AI-text detectors by introducing the Authorship-Rewriting Benchmark (ARB), a novel dataset designed to test detector robustness against LLM-mediated rewriting of human content. The authors argue that standard benchmarks, which compare original human text against direct LLM generation, fail to predict detector performance when human-authored texts are paraphrased or rewritten by large language models. To investigate this discrepancy, the study constructs a matched dataset comprising 1,800 source texts and four distinct variants: human-written, directly generated by an LLM, human-to-LLM rewritten, and LLM-to-LLM rewritten. The primary goal is to determine whether high performance on conventional benchmarks translates to real-world scenarios where AI tools modify existing human writing.

## Key Contributions
- The introduction of ARB, the first benchmark specifically designed to evaluate AI-text detectors using matched authorship-rewriting pairs, thereby addressing the limitation of traditional human-vs-LLM evaluation metrics.
- Empirical evidence demonstrating that detector performance degrades significantly (by 60-78 percentage points) when applied to human text rewritten by an LLM, despite performing well on direct LLM generation.
- The finding that detectors remain largely robust when LLM-generated text is rewritten by the same model, highlighting a specific vulnerability in current detection systems regarding human-authored content modified by AI.

## Methodology
The authors constructed ARB using 1,800 human source texts drawn equally from three diverse datasets: XSum, WritingPrompts, and OpenWebText. For each source item, they generated four matched variants using four open-weight LLMs (Llama-3.2-3B, Qwen2.5-7B, Mistral-7B, and Gemma-2-9B). These variants included the original human text, direct LLM generation (Free-LLM), LLM-rewritten human text (H2L), and LLM-rewritten LLM text (LLM2L). Five state-of-the-art detectors—FastDetectGPT, Binoculars-falcon-7b, RADAR, BERT-Defense, and RoBERTa-Defense—were evaluated at a strict operating point of 1% false positive rate to measure true positive recall across all four regimes.

## Results
The experimental results reveal a stark contrast in detector performance depending on the source of the text. FastDetectGPT and Binoculars-falcon-7b achieved high recall rates of 91.2% and 93.5%, respectively, for direct LLM generation. However, their performance plummeted to 30.8% and 15.1% when detecting human text that had been rewritten by an LLM, representing a drop of 60-78 percentage points. In contrast, when LLM-generated text was rewritten by the same model, recall remained high at 78.3% and 83.0%. RADAR showed a similar pattern, dropping from 66.8% to 12.2%, while BERT-Defense and RoBERTa-Defense performed poorly across all categories, staying below 3% recall.

## Significance
This research is significant because it exposes a fundamental flaw in current AI-text detection standards. It proves that detector efficacy measured on conventional benchmarks does not generalize to scenarios where humans use AI for editing or paraphrasing. This has profound implications for academic integrity and content moderation, as detectors may falsely flag human-written work as AI-generated if it has been lightly edited by an LLM, while potentially missing more sophisticated AI-generated content.
