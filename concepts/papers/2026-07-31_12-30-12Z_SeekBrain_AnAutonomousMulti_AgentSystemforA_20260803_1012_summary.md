## Summary
SeekBrain represents a significant advancement in computational neuroscience by introducing an autonomous multi-agent system designed to overcome the analytical bottlenecks inherent in modern, multi-scale neural data integration. The primary goal of this research is to automate and accelerate the discovery process by dynamically constructing analysis recipes from existing code-paper pairs, thereby creating a scalable repository of domain-specific expertise. By coupling this codified knowledge with sophisticated agentic planning and execution engines, SeekBrain can generate hypotheses and analytical pipelines on demand without extensive manual intervention. The system’s efficacy is validated through rigorous benchmarking against state-of-the-art agent baselines and demonstrated through its ability to uncover novel neural representations in real-world biological datasets.

## Key Contributions
- **Autonomous Recipe Generation:** The authors developed a novel mechanism that dynamically extracts and constructs analysis recipes from heterogeneous code-paper pairs, creating a scalable repertoire of domain-grounded expertise that adapts to specific neuroscience challenges.
- **Superior Benchmark Performance:** SeekBrain substantially outperforms existing state-of-the-art agent baselines on the expert-annotated BrainArena benchmark, demonstrating superior capability in handling complex, multi-modal analysis tasks across various neural data types.
- **Real-World Biological Discovery:** The system successfully integrated behavioral, neural, and anatomical data to reveal structured, distributed neural representations of larval zebrafish behavior and identified a shared axis of regional decoding strength in mouse decision-making tasks, proving its utility in genuine scientific discovery.

## Methodology
The authors approached the problem of fragmented neuroscience workflows by designing SeekBrain as an autonomous multi-agent framework. This system operates through domain-grounded hierarchical planning, which allows it to break down complex research questions into manageable sub-tasks. The core innovation lies in the dynamic construction of a "repertoire" of analysis recipes; these are extracted from existing literature by pairing code with its corresponding paper descriptions. This codified expertise is then coupled with agentic planning and execution engines that can scalably generate hypotheses and analytical pipelines on demand. The framework is designed to handle highly heterogeneous data by leveraging cross-modal data analysis capabilities, allowing it to synthesize information from disparate sources such as behavioral recordings, neural activity traces, and anatomical maps.

## Results
Systematic evaluations conducted on the BrainArena benchmark demonstrate that SeekBrain significantly outperforms current state-of-the-art agent baselines across a variety of analysis tasks. The system’s performance is not limited to synthetic benchmarks; when deployed in real-world research scenarios, it successfully integrated behavioral, neural, and anatomical data from larval zebrafish to reveal structured, distributed neural representations associated with specific behaviors. Additionally, in studies involving mouse decision-making tasks, SeekBrain identified a shared axis of regional decoding strength across the brain, highlighting its ability to detect subtle, cross-regional neural patterns that might be missed by traditional analytical methods.

## Significance
This work establishes SeekBrain as a scalable and practical tool for accelerating data-driven discoveries in neuroscience. By automating the integration of multi-scale datasets and reducing the reliance on manual workflow construction, it addresses critical constraints in modern neuroscientific research. The ability to uncover novel neural principles autonomously suggests that such systems could significantly speed up the pace of discovery, allowing researchers to focus on interpretation rather than data processing logistics.
