## Summary  
Large language models (LLMs) can produce divergent political stances when the same underlying meaning is expressed through different linguistic constructions, yet prior work has largely ignored constructional choice and focused only on lexical realizations. This paper investigates how structural rewrites systematically alter stance and whether those effects are causally traceable within the model’s internal representations. By extending an English political statements dataset into six controlled rewrite types—preserving or inverting meaning—the authors demonstrate that both kinds of rewrites destabilize stance judgments. Using activation patching, they locate which decoder layers recover the original stance distribution after a rewrite, revealing strong restoration signals in mid‑to‑late layers and especially at final prompt positions.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Linguistic constructional choices (e.g., passive vs active) can systematically shift LLM stance judgments beyond simple lexical variation.  
- [Finding 2] Both meaning‑preserving and meaning‑inverting rewrites affect stance, indicating that the model’s output is sensitive to structural form rather than just content.  
- [Finding 3] Activation patching uncovers that decoder layers near the end of the forward pass—particularly block outputs at the final prompt position—provide the strongest signal for restoring original stance.

## Methodology  
The authors construct six rewrite variants from a curated political statements dataset, ensuring each variant either preserves or inverts the semantic meaning. They evaluate four open‑weight LLMs on these rewrites to measure stance consistency. To isolate causal impact, they employ activation patching: activations generated by the original statement are temporarily injected into the forward pass of the rewritten version, allowing them to observe which internal components recover the original stance distribution. This approach isolates the effect of linguistic construction from other model parameters.

## Results  
Empirical experiments show that stance instability occurs for both preservation and inversion rewrites across all models, with larger effects observed in more complex constructions. Activation patching reveals that decoder layers 12‑20 (mid‑to‑late) restore the original stance distribution most effectively, and the final prompt position’s block output is the strongest indicator of restoration. The magnitude of recovery correlates with how far back the rewrite occurs within the model.

## Significance  
Understanding these effects matters because it clarifies whether LLMs merely memorize surface forms or truly encode structural information that influences reasoning. This insight can guide more robust prompt engineering, improve interpretability, and prevent unintended stance shifts in applications like content moderation or policy recommendation.
