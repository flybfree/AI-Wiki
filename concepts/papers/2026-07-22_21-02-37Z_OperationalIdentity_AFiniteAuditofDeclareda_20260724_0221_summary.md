## Summary  
The paper introduces “Operational Identity,” a formal framework that audits the relationship between declared sameness rules and their actual implementation in record systems. It shows that these two partitions of a finite domain can diverge systematically without creating gaps or contradictions, and it defines a precise notion of faithfulness for this divergence. The audit is evaluated through a three‑valued lattice comparison that includes sibling‑basis splits, version increments, and global equality as regime substitution. A passing verdict is non‑monotone because later edits can merge declared classes, producing new witnesses among already examined records.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Formal definition of operational identity partitions and the faithfulness condition for a declared‑implemented split.  
- [Finding 2] Decidable divergence witnesses via pair enumeration that distinguish merged declarations from separated mechanisms.  
- [Finding 3] Classification of sibling‑basis splits into sub‑sibling, super‑sibling, or sibling‑incomparable cases.

## Methodology  
The authors treat the record domain as a finite set partitioned by declared sameness classes and by operational identity classes induced by system mechanisms. They construct a refinement lattice where each node represents a partition; faithfulness is measured by whether the declared class refines the operational one. Divergence witnesses are generated by enumerating all pairs of records that belong to different declared classes but opposite sides of an implementation split, enabling decidable verification.

## Results  
The audit yields three possible outcomes for any pair of records: sub‑sibling (local partition finer than sibling), super‑sibling (local partition coarser), or sibling‑incomparable. A version field increment creates a sub‑sibling divergence by splitting declared classes more finely than the imported basis. Global equality is defined separately as regime substitution, which does not follow from sibling alignment.

## Significance  
This work clarifies hidden inconsistencies between declarative and operational sameness rules, enabling reliable provenance tracking in record systems. By providing decidable witnesses and a non‑monotone audit verdict, it supports robust verification of data integrity across complex inheritance hierarchies.
