## Summary  
The paper investigates public perceptions of AI‑driven decision‑making in healthcare using structural equation modeling on a large survey panel. It aims to identify which factors shape perceived helpfulness, riskiness, and fairness of automated decision‑making (ADM). The study finds that familiarity with AI, confidence in clinicians’ ability to detect AI‑generated content, and use of conversational agents significantly influence these perceptions. Overall, perceived helpfulness and fairness are more tied to trust in professionals than to the technology itself.  

## Key Contributions  
- Familiarity with different forms of AI positively predicts perceived helpfulness.  
- Use of conversational agents reduces perceived risk while familiarity increases it.  
- Confidence in clinicians’ ability is the strongest predictor of perceived fairness, with modest additional effects from other variables.  

## Methodology  
The authors employed structural equation modeling (SEM) on a longitudinal survey panel of 3,915 respondents. They treated perceived helpfulness, riskiness, and fairness as dependent latent variables and included AI literacy, familiarity with various AI forms, confidence in clinician detection, use of conversational agents for health information, and reliance on traditional digital health sources as exogenous predictors.  

## Results  
SEM revealed significant positive paths from AI familiarity to helpfulness; conversational agent use linked to lower risk but higher risk perception among those familiar with AI; confidence in clinicians’ ability strongly predicts fairness, with additional small positive associations from AI familiarity, AI literacy, and conversational‑agent use. Traditional health information source use was also modestly associated with higher perceived risk.  

## Significance  
Understanding these perceptions is crucial for designing trustworthy AI systems and ensuring ethical deployment, as public acceptance hinges on human oversight rather than tech attributes alone. The findings guide policymakers in balancing technological benefits with societal concerns.
