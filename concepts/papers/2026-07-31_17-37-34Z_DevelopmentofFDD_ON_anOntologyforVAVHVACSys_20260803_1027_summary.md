## Summary
This paper introduces FDD-ON, a novel and modular ontology designed to address the critical challenges of interoperability and data interpretability in Fault Detection and Diagnostics (FDD) for Variable Air Volume (VAV) HVAC systems. The authors argue that current FDD solutions are hindered by fragmented information silos and heterogeneous data sources, which prevent effective integration with advanced frameworks like digital twins and AI-driven maintenance systems. To resolve this, FDD-ON provides a formalized semantic framework that explicitly maps the complex relationships between contributing causes, faults, symptoms, and their subsequent impacts within HVAC infrastructure. By establishing a controlled vocabulary and comprehensive libraries for fault types and symptom statuses, the proposed ontology serves as a machine-interpretable foundation that bridges diverse diagnostic outputs and equipment specifications. Ultimately, this work aims to facilitate scalable, transparent, and interoperable FDD applications across various building management contexts.

## Key Contributions
- The development of FDD-ON, a comprehensive and extensible ontology that formally represents VAV HVAC system components, fault types, symptom statuses, and associated attributes using a well-defined controlled vocabulary.
- The creation of explicit causal relations linking contributing causes to faults, symptoms, and impacts, which enables the mapping of heterogeneous FDD outputs and supports querying diagnostic knowledge in a standardized manner.
- The demonstration of FDD-ON’s utility through evaluation on publicly available VAV HVAC datasets, proving its effectiveness as a foundational semantic framework for developing interoperable applications such as digital twin-enabled FDD frameworks.

## Methodology
The authors approached the problem by first identifying the limitations of existing FDD systems, specifically focusing on the lack of structured domain knowledge and the resulting data silos. They then designed FDD-ON using ontology engineering principles to ensure modularity and extensibility. The methodology involved defining a controlled vocabulary for HVAC components and faults, and structuring the ontology to capture broad spectra of operational abnormalities. The team constructed comprehensive libraries for faults, symptoms, and impacts, ensuring that the semantic relationships between these elements were rigorously defined. Finally, they validated the ontology by applying it to publicly available VAV HVAC system datasets, demonstrating its capacity to integrate diverse data sources and support machine-interpretable queries.

## Results
The evaluation of FDD-ON using publicly available VAV HVAC datasets demonstrated that the ontology successfully captures a wide spectrum of operational abnormalities and their consequences. The results indicated that FDD-ON provides a robust semantic framework that enhances the interpretability of diagnostic outputs. By mapping heterogeneous FDD data, the ontology facilitated the development of interoperable applications, confirming its potential to support AI-driven maintenance decision-making and digital twin integration. The study showed that structured semantic representation significantly improves the ability to query and utilize diagnostic knowledge across different system configurations.

## Significance
This research is significant because it addresses a fundamental barrier in building automation: the inability of disparate FDD systems to communicate and share knowledge effectively. By providing a standardized semantic layer, FDD-ON enables the integration of diverse equipment types and data sources, which is essential for large-scale energy efficiency improvements and predictive maintenance. This work paves the way for more reliable, transparent, and scalable HVAC management solutions, ultimately contributing to smarter and more sustainable building operations.
