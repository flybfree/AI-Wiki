## Summary  
This paper addresses the behavioural question of how much decision‑making authority is transferred from humans to AI in everyday financial interactions. By introducing a measurement framework that distinguishes between information retrieval (intent) and delegated execution, it provides a baseline for tracking the shift toward increasingly agentic AI. The authors apply this framework to a large‑scale dataset of ChatGPT and Gemini conversations involving 6,304 users across the United States and India. Their findings reveal that while consumers heavily rely on AI for information and judgment‑shaping, actual delegation of financial actions is still uncommon.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Consumers overwhelmingly use AI to retrieve information and shape financial judgement, but rarely delegate execution of financial decisions.  
- [Finding 2] A behavioural measurement framework that combines intent (information‑seeking) with delegated decision authority quantifies the distribution of authority between human and AI agents.  
- [Finding 3] The study demonstrates that financial services are a substantial AI use case, establishing a baseline for measuring the transition to agentic AI.

## Methodology  
The authors built on existing user‑log data from ChatGPT and Gemini platforms, which record each interaction’s textual content and system response. They mapped each query to two dimensions: (1) intent – whether the user is seeking information or prompting a specific action, and (2) delegated authority – whether the AI is instructed to perform an actual financial transaction. By analysing millions of interactions across diverse users, they created a scalable behavioural baseline that can be compared over time.

## Results  
The dataset comprises 1.5 million real‑world interactions from 6,304 participants in the United States and India. Analysis shows that information‑seeking queries dominate (≈87 % of all requests), while delegated execution requests are rare (<13 %). The authors also report that users tend to shift authority gradually: early interactions involve only advice or explanation, whereas full delegation is still a minority occurrence. This establishes a clear behavioural pattern for the current state of human‑AI financial decision making.

## Significance  
Understanding where authority resides between humans and AI is crucial for designing trustworthy, transparent systems. By quantifying the gap between information provision and actual execution, this work informs designers on how to gradually increase delegated autonomy without compromising user safety or accountability. The baseline also enables longitudinal research to track whether delegation expands as AI capabilities improve.
