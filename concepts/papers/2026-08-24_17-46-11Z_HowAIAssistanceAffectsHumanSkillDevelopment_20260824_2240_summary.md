## Summary  
The paper investigates how AI assistance influences human skill development when learning logic puzzles, showing that while AI can boost short‑term performance, it may hinder long‑term skill acquisition. It introduces a controlled experiment with varying AI request costs and examines participants’ abilities before, during, and after assistance. Using Bayesian modeling to separate latent ability from post‑AI ability and individual skill change, the study finds that independent problem solving is crucial for lasting improvement. The findings suggest that substituting reasoning with AI weakens skill formation.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Lower‑cost AI assistance leads to more frequent use but poorer subsequent unassisted performance.  
- Finding 2: Participants who rely on AI during the assistance phase show reduced latent ability gains compared with those solving independently.  
- Finding 3: Independent reasoning effort is positively correlated with larger increases in latent ability, indicating skill development is weaker when AI substitutes for reasoning.

## Methodology  
The authors designed a within‑subjects experiment where participants completed logic puzzles under three conditions: pre‑AI (no assistance), during‑AI (assistance available at variable cost), and post‑AI (no assistance). They varied the cost of AI help to manipulate usage frequency. Throughout, they recorded task performance and estimated independent problem‑solving effort. Using a Bayesian latent ability model, they decomposed observed performance into initial ability, post‑AI ability, and skill change while controlling for individual differences.

## Results  
The results show that lower‑cost assistance increases AI use but leads to higher error rates after removal of help. The Bayesian model reveals that participants with higher independent reasoning during the AI phase exhibit larger latent ability gains, whereas those who depended heavily on AI show smaller or negative gains. Moreover, skill change is not fully explained by early performance; it requires sustained effort.

## Significance  
This work contributes to cognitive science and AI ethics by highlighting trade‑offs between immediate assistance and long‑term learning. It informs the design of human‑AI collaboration tools that encourage autonomous problem solving rather than dependency.
