## Summary  
This paper aims to empirically examine how generative AI platforms drive user engagement with academic library resources, focusing on the period August 2023 through October 2025. It contributes by identifying key AI tools and pathways that lead users from search queries to institutional repositories, especially electronic theses and dissertations. The study highlights the role of structured metadata and stable permalinks in facilitating AI‑mediated retrieval. These findings provide actionable insights for libraries seeking to adapt to AI‑driven discovery.  

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: AI‑mediated traffic surged after the integration of linked citation features, indicating that linking library resources via AI platforms amplifies visibility.  
- Finding 2: ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini are the dominant platforms driving this traffic, suggesting platform‑specific strategies for libraries.  
- Finding 3: A majority of users reach institutional repositories primarily to access electronic theses/dissertations, showing that AI can effectively surface open‑access scholarly outputs.  

## Methodology  
The authors employed web analytics data collected from August 2023 through October 2025, analyzing referral patterns and user‑journey metrics. They compared traffic sources across major generative AI services, focusing on click‑through rates to library portals and subsequent usage of repository resources. The analysis combined quantitative metrics with qualitative observations of resource types accessed.  

## Results  
The data reveal a significant increase in AI‑mediated traffic, especially following the addition of linked citation features. Referral analysis confirms ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini as top drivers. Approximately 68 % of users who entered library portals via these platforms proceeded to explore electronic theses/dissertations, which are Open Access with stable permalinks.  

## Significance  
This research underscores how generative AI reshapes discovery pathways in academic libraries, emphasizing the need for proactive integration and metadata optimization. It offers a strategic roadmap for institutions to leverage AI while ensuring resources remain discoverable and accessible.
