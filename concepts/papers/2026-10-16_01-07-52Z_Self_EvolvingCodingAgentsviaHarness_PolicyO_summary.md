## Summary
This paper introduces Self-HPO, a recursive self-improving framework for Large Language Model (LLM) coding agents that simultaneously optimizes both the policy model and the environment harness. The authors argue that optimizing only one component is suboptimal because the harness and policy are mutually reinforcing; a static harness may not address the evolving failure modes of an updated policy, while policy learning is constrained by the trajectories generated by a fixed harness. Self-HPO addresses this by alternating between harness optimization and policy updates, using the current policy to propose new harness configurations based on its own failure analysis. This approach significantly improves coding performance, reliability, and generalization across multiple benchmarks compared to baselines that optimize only one component.

## Key Contributions
- **Joint Optimization Framework:** The paper proposes Self-HPO, a method that breaks the traditional separation between agent policy training and environment harness design, treating them as co-dependent components that must evolve together to maximize agent performance.
- **Self-Proposed Harnesses:** A novel mechanism where the current policy model analyzes its own failure modes and actively proposes new harness candidates (H-step), ensuring that the environment tools and constraints are specifically tailored to the agent’s current weaknesses.
- **Synergistic Performance Gains:** The study demonstrates that the combined gains from optimizing both the harness and the policy exceed the sum of their individual gains, indicating a non-linear synergy between the two components that cannot be achieved by isolated improvements.
- **Generalization and Reliability:** The framework improves both single-attempt reliability (Pass@1) and capability expansion (Pass@k), with performance gains that generalize to out-of-distribution coding tasks, suggesting robustness beyond the training distribution.

## Methodology
The authors approach the problem through a recursive, alternating optimization loop consisting of two main steps: the Harness Step (H-step) and the Policy Step (P-step). In the H-step, the current policy model is prompted to analyze its recent failures and propose new harness configurations or tool usage strategies that could mitigate these specific errors. These proposed harnesses are evaluated and selected based on their potential to improve performance. In the P-step, the policy model is updated via Reinforcement Learning (RL) using the selected harness. Crucially, hard tasks identified during this RL training process are used to define the search set for the next round of harness optimization, creating a feedback loop where the harness evolves to target the policy’s most difficult remaining challenges. This process is applied to the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model and evaluated against baselines that optimize only the harness or only the policy.

## Results
Experimental evaluations across three coding benchmarks demonstrate that Self-HPO consistently outperforms baselines that optimize only the harness or only the policy. Specifically, the framework improves the average resolve rate of the Qwen3.5-35B-A3B model from 57.8% to 64.8%. The authors provide further analysis showing that optimized harnesses and policies retain their individual gains even when paired with base counterparts, but their joint application yields superior results. Additionally, the framework enhances reliability metrics (Pass@1) and expands the capability frontier (Pass@k). The study also shows that using a stronger external model (e.g., Opus 4.8) as the harness proposer within the same framework yields further performance boosts, highlighting the scalability of the approach.

## Significance
This work is significant because it challenges the prevailing paradigm in LLM agent development, which typically treats the policy model and the environment harness as separate, independently optimized components. By demonstrating that these components are mutually reinforcing, the paper provides a blueprint for more efficient and effective agent training. It suggests that future agent systems should not just train better models or build better tools in isolation, but should co-evolve them. This has practical implications for building more reliable, capable, and generalizable coding agents, potentially reducing the need for manual harness engineering and enabling agents to self-improve by targeting their own specific weaknesses.

## Related Concepts
- **LLM Agents:** Autonomous systems powered by large language models that interact with environments to perform tasks.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** A machine learning paradigm where agents learn optimal actions through trial and error and reward signals.
- **Harness Optimization:** The process of tuning the environment, tools, and constraints that mediate an agent's interaction with the world.
- **Policy Optimization:** The process of training the core model to make better decisions or generate better outputs.
- **Self-Improving Systems:** Frameworks where a system uses its own outputs or failures to guide its own evolution or training.
- **Pass@k:** A metric for evaluating the probability that at least one of k generated solutions is correct, used to measure capability expansion.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Generalization:** The ability of a model to perform well on data that differs from the training distribution.
