## Summary  
Molt is a PyTorch‑native training framework designed to make agentic reinforcement learning research more scalable and maintainable by minimizing overhead from complex pipelines. It provides a compact, readable codebase that isolates algorithmic changes so researchers can trace the full flow and AI assistants can understand it easily. The framework trains multimodal mixture‑of‑experts policies asynchronously without ever training on tokens outside the generated rollout, preserving model semantics across iterations. Under matched conditions, Molt achieves performance comparable to state‑of‑the‑art Megatron‑based stacks.

## Key Contributions  
- A minimal, readable codebase that isolates algorithmic changes and enables full traceability of the RL pipeline.  
- An asynchronous training loop that never trains on tokens not generated by the agent, maintaining consistent policy versions and semantics.  
- Statistical performance comparable to leading Megatron‑based frameworks under fully asynchronous protocols.

## Methodology  
The authors built Molt as a lightweight wrapper around PyTorch that decouples rollout generation from model updates. The agent is treated as an ordinary program that produces sequences of tokens, while the training loop monitors these tokens and only triggers gradient steps when new data appears. This design avoids token‑level training overhead and supports multimodal inputs through a mixture‑of‑experts architecture. All components—trainer, distributed backend, rollout glue—are encapsulated in a single, well‑documented module.

## Results  
Experiments on several benchmark RL tasks show that Molt converges within 10 % of the sample budget required by Megatron while achieving identical or slightly better sample efficiency. Training speed is comparable, with no significant loss in throughput. The framework also reduces memory usage and eliminates the need for manual rollout synchronization.

## Significance  
Molt alleviates the cumulative cost of modifying RL pipelines across multiple layers, allowing researchers to focus on algorithmic innovation rather than engineering overhead. Its transparency supports reproducibility and integration with AI coding assistants, accelerating research cycles.
