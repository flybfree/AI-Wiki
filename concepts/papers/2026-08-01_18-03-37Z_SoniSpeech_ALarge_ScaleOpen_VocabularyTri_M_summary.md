## Summary  
The paper introduces SoniSpeech, a large‑scale open‑vocabulary tri‑modal dataset designed to enable wearable silent speech interfaces (SSIs) that can recognize thousands of words without intrusive hardware. By coupling ultrasound echo profiles from eyewear with ordinary audio and frontal video, the authors create a seamless trimodal representation for both voiced and silent utterances. This work supplies the first benchmark for open‑vocabulary silent speech recognition using acoustic‑sensing wearables, achieving a 26.3 % word error rate on a standard CTC ResNet‑34 model. The dataset is publicly available at https://doi.org/10.7298/xjjr-9m85.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] SoniSpeech provides the largest open‑vocabulary tri‑modal corpus for wearable SSIs, containing 18 000 utterances across 34 hours of data.  
- [Finding 2] The dataset merges three synchronized modalities—ultrasound echo profiles, voiced audio, and frontal video—to capture rich acoustic and visual cues in both spoken and silent modes.  
- [Finding 3] It establishes the first benchmark for open‑vocabulary silent speech recognition, delivering a baseline WER of 26.3 % with a CTC ResNet‑34 model.

## Methodology  
The authors collected data from the SODA dialogue corpus, which supplies contemporary conversational English with full phoneme coverage and 5 356 unique words. Using eyewear equipped with ultrasound sensors, they recorded each utterance simultaneously in three modalities: (1) acoustic echo profiles generated by the wearable’s ultrasonic array, (2) standard microphones capturing voiced speech, and (3) high‑resolution frontal video of the user’s face. Both spoken and silent utterances were synchronized frame‑by‑frame, yielding a multimodal time series that can be jointly processed for recognition.

## Results  
A CTC‑based ResNet‑34 model was trained on the full dataset and evaluated on an open‑vocabulary silent speech test set. The baseline achieved a word error rate of 26.3 %, which is markedly lower than earlier closed‑vocabulary systems and marks the first quantitative benchmark for this specific task.

## Significance  
SoniSpeech demonstrates that wearable SSIs can support large vocabularies without requiring facial electrodes or bulky hardware, paving the way for practical, unobtrusive communication devices. The dataset’s open nature encourages research on multimodal fusion, sensor‑fusion architectures, and downstream applications such as real‑time captioning and assistive communication.
