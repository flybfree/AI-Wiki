## Summary  
The present study aims to validate the Spanish version of the Large Language Model Dependency Scale (LLM‑D12‑SP) and to demonstrate its psychometric soundness across a new linguistic context. By extending prior English and Arabic validations, the authors contribute a culturally adapted tool that can reliably measure both instrumental and relationship dependencies on LLMs for Spanish‑speaking populations in organizational settings.

## Key Contributions  
- The first comprehensive validation of LLM‑D12‑SP among Spanish speakers.  
- Confirmatory factor analysis confirms the original two‑factor structure (instrumental vs. relationship).  
- Both dimensions show positive, statistically significant associations with internet addiction and perceived trustworthiness of LLMs while showing weak or no link to need for cognition.

## Methodology  
A total of 386 Spanish‑speaking participants (mean age 28 years) completed the LLM‑D12‑SP. The instrument was administered online, followed by a series of psychometric analyses: confirmatory factor analysis to test the two‑factor model, reliability assessment via Cronbach’s alpha for each subscale and the total scale, discriminant validity tests to ensure the subscales measure distinct constructs, and external validation linking scores to measures of internet addiction (e.g., Bergen Scale) and trustworthiness perceptions. The design followed standard cross‑sectional validation protocols.

## Results  
Cronbach’s alphas were 0.89 for the total scale, 0.86 for instrumental dependency, and 0.85 for relationship dependency, indicating strong internal consistency. Confirmatory factor analysis replicated the two‑factor structure with significant loadings on both factors. Discriminant validity analyses revealed that while the subscales are related, they differ meaningfully (e.g., via Fornell‑Larcker criterion). External validation showed a moderate positive correlation between instrumental dependency and internet addiction (r ≈ 0.42) and between relationship dependency and perceived trustworthiness of LLMs (r ≈ 0.38), whereas associations with need for cognition were negligible.

## Significance  
These findings provide a psychometrically validated instrument that can be used to study how Spanish‑speaking professionals experience psychological reliance on large language models in work contexts, supporting cross‑cultural research and informing design of LLM‑assisted tools. The evidence of comparable structure across English, Arabic, and Spanish samples strengthens the global applicability of dependency assessments.
