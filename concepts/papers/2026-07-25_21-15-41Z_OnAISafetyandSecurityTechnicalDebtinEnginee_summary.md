## Summary  
The paper seeks to characterize artificial‑intelligence safety and security technical debts (AITDs) that arise in engineering AI‑enabled systems, propose a taxonomy of 31 distinct AITD types grouped into seven root‑cause classes, and map each type to trust‑related concerns. It also synthesizes 34 actionable guidelines for mitigating these debts across the AI lifecycle. The authors introduce **AITD‑MAP**, an integrated framework that links the taxonomy, risk impacts, and mitigation strategies into a single structure. This work advances the understanding of how latent technical debt can degrade trustworthiness in high‑stakes domains.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Identification of 31 distinct types of AI technical debt across seven root‑cause classes.  
- Finding 2: Mapping of each AITD type to 18 trust‑related concerns, including six safety hazards and twelve security vulnerabilities.  
- Finding 3: Development of the **AITD‑MAP** framework that integrates taxonomy, risk impacts, and mitigation strategies.

## Methodology  
The authors performed a systematic literature review of 60 primary research papers on AI system engineering. They extracted definitions, root causes, and consequences of AITDs, then organized these findings into a taxonomy based on seven classes (data governance, model implementation, algorithm design, architectural decisions, operational processes, documentation practices, testing adequacy). Each class was linked to trust concerns through a mapping exercise that quantified safety hazards and security vulnerabilities.

## Results  
The review identified 31 AITD types grouped into the seven root‑cause classes. These were associated with 18 trust concerns: six safety hazards (e.g., model drift, unsafe inference) and twelve security vulnerabilities (e.g., data poisoning, adversarial attacks). Thirty‑four actionable guidelines—eight for safety, twenty‑six for security—were synthesized to address prevention, detection, and reduction of AITDs throughout the AI lifecycle.

## Significance  
Understanding AITDs is critical because latent technical debt can erode system reliability, increase failure rates, and expose organizations to regulatory or reputational risk. By making these debts visible through a unified framework, engineers can prioritize remediation early in development, aligning safety and security with the broader goal of AI trustworthiness.
