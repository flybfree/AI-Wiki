## Summary  
The paper addresses the ambiguity in goal reasoning within Non‑Axiomatic Logic (NAL) where avoidance is often misrepresented as pursuit of a negated event, leading to paradoxes. It proposes anti‑goal reasoning with a dedicated definition and operation prevent to differentiate avoidance from negation. Four minimal case studies demonstrate that the framework can correctly distinguish pursuit, passive avoidance, active prevention, and inaction.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: The conflation of “avoid G” as ¬G is clarified by introducing anti‑goal reasoning.  
- Finding 2: A formal definition of anti‑goals and a mental operation prevent are introduced to link anti‑goal reasoning with ordinary goal reasoning.  
- Finding 3: Four minimal case studies show that the rules correctly differentiate pursuit, passive avoidance, active prevention, and withholding action.

## Methodology  
The authors start from NAL’s basic goal definition—an adaptive system selects means to realize a desired event given limited knowledge. They then analyze how “avoid G” is commonly encoded as ¬G, which conflates two distinct intentions. To resolve this, they extend the theory with an anti‑goal concept: an intention to prevent G from occurring without treating it as pursuing ¬G. The operation prevent maps active prevention (preventing a harmful event) to a goal that blocks its realization. They formulate minimal scenarios where agents have limited actions and observe outcomes, using these cases to test the new rules.

## Results  
The framework yields four distinct rule sets: (1) pursuit of G, (2) passive avoidance of G (no action), (3) active prevention of G (prevent operation applied), (4) withholding action to preserve G. In each case study the predicted behavior matches intuitive expectations, confirming that anti‑goal reasoning correctly distinguishes between pursuing a negated event and avoiding a positive one.

## Significance  
By disentangling avoidance from negation, the paper resolves a longstanding paradox in NAL goal reasoning and provides a principled way to model active prevention. This advances theoretical understanding of adaptive systems’ decision processes and offers a practical tool for designing agents that can both avoid undesirable outcomes and prevent them proactively.

## Related Concepts  
- Goal Reasoning: selection of means to achieve desired events.  
- Non‑Axiomatic Logic (NAL): logic without fixed axioms, used in adaptive reasoning.  
- Anti‑Goal: intention to prevent a positive event rather than pursue its negation.  
- Prevent Operation: mental operation linking anti‑goal with ordinary goal.  
- Pursuit vs. Avoidance: distinction between seeking an outcome and preventing its occurrence.
