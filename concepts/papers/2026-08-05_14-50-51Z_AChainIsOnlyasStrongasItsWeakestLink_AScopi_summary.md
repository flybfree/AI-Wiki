## Summary  
The paper’s goal is to map the current state of AI audits that explicitly treat system integration as a core evaluation dimension, arguing that model‑centric approaches alone cannot capture risks arising from component interactions and deployment environments. By conducting a scoping review of 4,259 documents and extracting 58 relevant studies, the authors demonstrate that system integration remains an under‑explored but emerging facet of AI risk assessment. Their contribution is a systematic classification of these audits into three integration sites—inter‑component, system‑environment, and multi‑system—and identification of persistent gaps in measurement and audit practice.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The corpus represents an emerging yet fragmented form of AI auditing, with only a handful of measures specifically targeting integration‑specific risks.  
- [Finding 2] Large gaps remain between the current audit practices and traditional safety‑critical audit expectations, such as comprehensive risk determination across all interaction points.  
- [Finding 3] Access to necessary information and resources significantly influences both the design and feasibility of system‑integration audits.

## Methodology  
The authors employed reflexive thematic analysis on a curated set of 4,259 documents to identify studies that treat system integration as an integral part of AI evaluation. This approach allowed them to systematically extract elements, actors, enablers, and constraints from each audit, resulting in the identification of 58 relevant papers. The review also classified the audits into three distinct sites—inter‑component, system‑environment, and multi‑system—to capture how integration risks are explored, determined, coordinated, and regulated.

## Results  
The findings reveal that while integration is acknowledged as a critical component, few existing measures focus exclusively on it; most audits still rely heavily on model‑centric criteria. The three integration sites serve distinct functions: inter‑component audits explore compatibility and completeness, system‑environment audits assess environmental impact and oversight, and multi‑system audits coordinate across heterogeneous platforms. Moreover, the audit design is heavily constrained by data availability and technical resources, limiting the depth of risk exploration.

## Significance  
Prioritizing system integration as a core strategy will enable AI developers to anticipate failures that propagate across components, environments, or systems—failures invisible to component‑level evaluations alone. By highlighting the need for audits that capture cross‑boundary risks, the paper urges the AI community to develop more holistic assessment practices capable of bridging gaps between model performance and real‑world deployment.
