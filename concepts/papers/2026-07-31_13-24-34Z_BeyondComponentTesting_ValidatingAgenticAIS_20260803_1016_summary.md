## Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of validating Agentic AI systems, arguing that traditional component-based testing is insufficient for evaluating complex, multi-step decision-making processes. The authors synthesize findings from 257 diverse papers to propose a comprehensive five-dimension taxonomy that captures behavioral, safety, temporal, regulatory, and multi-agent concerns inherent in agentic validation. By mapping current approaches against this taxonomy, the study exposes significant gaps in temporal validity and runtime evidence maintenance while highlighting the maturity of behavioral evaluation methods. Ultimately, the work establishes that trustworthy deployment requires shifting focus from isolated component assessment to holistic trajectory validation within dynamic contexts.

## Key Contributions
- The development of a novel five-dimension taxonomy specifically designed to characterize the complex validation problems associated with agentic AI systems, moving beyond static input-output evaluations.
- A comprehensive mapping of 257 interdisciplinary papers that reveals a disparity in research maturity, showing that while behavioral evaluation is relatively advanced, temporal validity and regulatory legibility remain critically under-developed.
- The introduction of three cross-domain case studies (medical care, industrial operations, smart-mobility) that operationalize the taxonomy dimensions using real-world failure patterns to illustrate practical validation challenges.

## Methodology
The authors conducted a systematic literature review spanning multiple disciplines, including agent evaluation, software assurance, cyber-physical systems, runtime monitoring, and regulatory guidance. They analyzed 257 papers to identify recurring themes and gaps in current validation practices. The methodology involved organizing existing approaches into a five-dimension taxonomy and applying this framework to three distinct safety-critical case studies to demonstrate how theoretical concepts manifest in operational settings.

## Results
The analysis indicates that behavioral evaluation is the most mature area of agentic validation, whereas temporal validity, runtime evidence maintenance, regulatory legibility, and open-ended multi-agent systems assurance are significantly under-developed. The case studies demonstrated that failure patterns in safety-critical environments consistently recur across the five taxonomy dimensions, underscoring the need for integrated validation strategies rather than siloed testing.

## Significance
This research is vital because it redefines the standards for trustworthy AI deployment. By proving that acceptable system behavior depends on temporal unfolding and environmental adaptation, it urges the industry to adopt lifecycle-oriented agendas focused on bounded-autonomy specifications and audit-ready evidence structures, ensuring safety in complex real-world applications.
