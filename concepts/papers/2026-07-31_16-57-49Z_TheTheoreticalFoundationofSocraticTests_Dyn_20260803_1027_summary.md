## Summary
This paper introduces the "Socratic Test," a novel theoretical framework for automated, computer-mediated conversational assessments that aims to overcome the limitations of traditional static and oral examinations. By integrating Dynamic Assessment principles with multimodal workspaces and advanced taxonomic structures, the proposed system seeks to map a student's cognitive boundaries in real-time rather than merely measuring final outputs. The authors argue that this approach mitigates the performative anxiety and power imbalances inherent in face-to-face oral exams while avoiding the deficit-based penalties of static grading. Ultimately, the work formalizes a non-compensatory, additive grading architecture designed to prioritize mastery and ensure high measurement reliability through human-AI alignment.

## Key Contributions
- Theoretical formalization of graduated scaffolding as a mechanism to quantify the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) within an automated assessment context.
- Development of a hybrid evaluation model that combines Bloom’s Taxonomy for real-time proctoring with the SOLO Taxonomy for structural evaluation of cognitive complexity.
- Proposal of a non-compensatory, additive grading architecture that shifts the focus from penalty-based scoring to mastery-based measurement, thereby reducing construct-irrelevant variance.

## Methodology
The authors approach this problem through theoretical synthesis and architectural design rather than empirical experimentation. They analyze the psychological and sociological flaws in current assessment models, specifically targeting the subtractive nature of static tests and the anxiety-inducing dynamics of traditional oral exams. To address these issues, they construct a theoretical model for an automated conversational agent that utilizes multimodal inputs to engage students dynamically. The methodology involves defining specific algorithms for real-time proctoring using Bloom’s Taxonomy to monitor cognitive engagement levels, while simultaneously employing the SOLO Taxonomy to evaluate the structural complexity of student responses. Furthermore, the design incorporates principles from Dynamic Assessment to introduce graduated scaffolding, allowing the system to adapt its difficulty and support based on the learner's immediate performance, effectively mapping their Zone of Proximal Development.

## Results
As a theoretical paper, the primary result is the successful formalization of the Socratic Test framework. The authors demonstrate how integrating these disparate pedagogical theories creates a coherent system for dynamic examination. The model establishes clear protocols for how an AI proctor should interact with students to minimize anxiety and maximize diagnostic accuracy. It details how the non-compensatory grading structure operates, ensuring that partial mastery is recognized without being obscured by errors in other areas, thus providing a more nuanced and accurate picture of student capability than traditional binary or weighted scoring systems.

## Significance
This work matters because it proposes a paradigm shift in educational assessment, moving away from static, high-stakes testing toward continuous, diagnostic, and supportive evaluation. By leveraging AI to simulate the benefits of face-to-face oral exams without their associated psychological burdens, it offers a scalable solution for measuring true cognitive mastery. This approach has significant implications for ensuring fairness in education, reducing bias related to anxiety or socioeconomic status, and providing educators with deeper insights into student learning processes.
