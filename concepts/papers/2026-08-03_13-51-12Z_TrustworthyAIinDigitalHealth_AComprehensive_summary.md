## Summary  
This paper aims to provide a comprehensive synthesis of trustworthy AI in digital health by focusing on two core dimensions—robustness and explainability—that are essential for safe, ethical deployment. It organizes recent research into an accessible framework that links technical challenges with practical healthcare applications, thereby supporting researchers and practitioners in building reliable models. The review highlights how robustness mitigates failures under data scarcity or distributional shifts, while explainable methods improve stakeholder confidence through interpretable insights. By integrating these perspectives, the authors fill a gap left by fragmented contributions on trustworthy AI.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A structured synthesis of robustness and explainability techniques tailored to digital health contexts.  
- [Finding 2] Application‑specific trust considerations across intensive care, neonatal health, and metabolic health domains.  
- [Finding 3] Recent advances in robust AI under data scarcity/distributional shifts and a taxonomy of explainable methods (feature attribution, gradient‑based, counterfactual explanations).

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by first outlining the pillars of trustworthy AI—robustness, explainability, fairness, accountability, privacy—and then mapping these pillars onto the full AI lifecycle in healthcare. They organized the review into three parts: (1) theoretical foundations and ethical challenges; (2) domain‑specific trust analysis; (3) methodological advances. This synthesis combines literature review with a systematic categorization of methods and evaluation criteria.

## Results  
The review presents a comprehensive overview of techniques that enhance robustness, such as data augmentation, adversarial training, and uncertainty quantification, and those that improve explainability, including SHAP values, LIME, and counterfactual explanations. It also introduces evaluation metrics—validity (model alignment with true labels), fidelity (representation accuracy), and diversity (coverage of input space)—to quantify trustworthiness.

## Significance  
Understanding and improving robustness and explainability are critical for deploying AI in high‑stakes health settings where errors can have life‑changing consequences. By providing a unified framework, the paper equips clinicians, engineers, and policymakers with actionable insights to mitigate risk, ensure accountability, and foster public trust.
