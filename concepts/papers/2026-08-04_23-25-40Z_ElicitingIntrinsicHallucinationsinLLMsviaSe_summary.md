## Summary  
The paper aims to study intrinsic hallucinations in large language models and propose a framework that stress‑tests these phenomena using natural, semantically equivalent variations of user queries generated by adversarial optimization. It evaluates how model faithfulness degrades under such perturbations while preserving query meaning. The authors find that even state‑of‑the‑art LLMs suffer up to 50 % loss in contextual faithfulness.

## Key Contributions  
- The framework of semantically equivalent adversarial attacks can reliably induce hallucinations without altering the underlying semantic content.  
- State‑of‑the‑art LLMs exhibit a significant degradation (up to 50 %) in contextual faithfulness when subjected to these attacks.  
- These results demonstrate that grounding in LLM outputs is fragile, regardless of model size or training data.

## Methodology  
The authors generate adversarial queries that are semantically equivalent to the originals using optimization techniques while enforcing a strict hallucination objective. They apply this framework across white‑box, gray‑box, and black‑box attack settings on five open‑source and five closed‑source generator models evaluated on three datasets.

## Results  
Experiments show that adversarial perturbations degrade contextual faithfulness by up to 50 % for GPT‑5‑mini and similarly for other models. The attacks succeed even in black‑box conditions, indicating widespread vulnerability. Intrinsic hallucinations are prevalent under semantically equivalent prompts.

## Significance  
These findings reveal that reliance on retrieval‑augmented generation or in‑context evidence remains fragile, undermining trust in LLM outputs. They motivate the development of robust grounding architectures and training objectives that enforce factual consistency independent of surface query form.
