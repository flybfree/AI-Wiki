## Summary  
Pangram 4 is a deep‑learning model designed to classify whether a given text was generated by an AI system or written by a human, aiming to surpass the performance of its predecessor Pangram 3 while improving robustness and out‑of‑distribution (OOD) detection. The authors report an AUROC of 0.9916 with a false positive rate of 0.0041% and a false negative rate of 0.3396%, demonstrating state‑of‑the‑art accuracy across diverse benchmarks. In addition, Pangram 4 excels at distinguishing fine‑grained edits and mixed AI‑human co‑authored text, enhancing boundary detection tasks and revealing interleaved AI assistance more reliably than before.

## Key Contributions  
- **High Accuracy Metrics:** Achieve an AUROC of 0.9916 with a false positive rate of 0.0041% and a false negative rate of 0.3396%, surpassing Pangram 3’s performance.  
- **Robustness & OOD Generalization:** Exhibit superior out‑of‑distribution generalization and greater resilience to adversarial attacks compared with earlier models.  
- **Fine‑Grained Edit Detection:** Improve the ability to differentiate fine‑grained edits and mixed AI‑human co‑authored text, leading to better boundary detection and interleaved AI assistance identification.

## Methodology  
The authors built Pangram 4 as a deep neural network classifier, leveraging transformer architectures trained on large annotated corpora of human versus AI-generated text. Training incorporates adversarial examples to boost robustness, and the model is fine‑tuned with a loss function that penalizes both false positives and false negatives equally. Evaluation follows standard AI detection benchmarks, measuring AUROC, FPR, FNR, and OOD performance across multiple domains.

## Results  
Pangram 4 attains an AUROC of 0.9916, translating to a 0.0041% false positive rate and a 0.3396% false negative rate—state‑of‑the‑art figures on the AI text detection leaderboard. The model’s OOD generalization surpasses Pangram 3, maintaining high accuracy even when encountering unseen styles or adversarial perturbations. Benchmark results confirm that Pangram 4 is the top performer across varied settings and domains.

## Significance  
These advances matter because reliable AI detection is crucial for content moderation, academic integrity, and trust in automated systems. By reducing false positives to a negligible level and improving OOD robustness, Pangram 4 enables safer deployment of generative models while preserving human‑authored content integrity.
