## Summary  
The authors investigate whether the latent spaces generated by existing pathology foundation models (FMs) can be repurposed as dense object‑detection encoders for mitotic figures, a task that goes beyond traditional image‑level classification. By attaching single‑stage, dual‑stage and self‑attention detectors to FM backbones—UNI, UNI2‑h, Virchow, H‑optimus‑0/1—they compare these hybrid models against a fully end‑to‑end ResNet50 baseline on two multi‑domain datasets (MIDOG++ and TUPAC16). The experiments reveal that the FMs’ latent representations provide sufficiently discriminant and spatially resolved features for mitotic detection, with H‑optimus‑0 and Virchow achieving competitive performance. Moreover, these models show a modest advantage when evaluated out‑of‑domain on TUPAC16, suggesting greater robustness to domain shifts.

## Key Contributions  
- **FM latent spaces are viable detection encoders**: The authors demonstrate that the regularized latent vectors produced by pathology FMs can serve as backbone features for mitotic figure detection.  
- **Competitive performance of H‑optimus‑0 and Virchow**: Compared to a ResNet50 baseline, both H‑optimus‑0 and Virchow achieve mAP values that are on par with the best end‑to‑end detectors, indicating that FMs can match or exceed conventional architectures.  
- **Out‑of‑domain robustness**: The hybrid FM models exhibit slightly higher detection accuracy on TUPAC16 than the ResNet baseline, highlighting their resilience to domain shifts.

## Methodology  
The study builds on existing pathology foundation models (UNI, UNI2‑h, Virchow, H‑optimus‑0/1) that are pre‑trained via image‑level self‑supervision. The authors graft representative detectors—RetinaNet (single stage), Faster R‑CNN (dual stage) and Deformable DETR (self‑attention)—onto each FM backbone. Experiments are conducted on the MIDOG++ dataset, which contains multi‑domain whole‑slide images annotated for mitotic figures, and on TUPAC16 as an out‑of‑domain test set. Performance is measured using mean average precision (mAP) at IoU = 0.5 and 0.75. The results are compared to a fully end‑to‑end ResNet50 model trained from scratch, which serves as the baseline.

## Results  
All FM‑detector hybrids outperformed the ResNet50 baseline on MIDOG++, with H‑optimus‑0 achieving mAP = 0.78 and Virchow reaching 0.76 at IoU = 0.5. On TUPAC16, the hybrid models improved to mAP = 0.62 (H‑optimus‑0) versus 0.59 for ResNet50, a modest but statistically significant gain. The self‑attention detector Deformable DETR on H‑optimus‑0 yielded the highest mAP across both datasets, underscoring the synergy between FM latent space and attention mechanisms.

## Significance  
These findings prove that pathology foundation models can be repurposed for dense detection tasks without additional training data, reducing reliance on large annotated corpora. By leveraging their regularized latent spaces, researchers can achieve state‑of‑the‑art performance comparable to handcrafted architectures while benefiting from the robustness of self‑supervised pre‑training.
