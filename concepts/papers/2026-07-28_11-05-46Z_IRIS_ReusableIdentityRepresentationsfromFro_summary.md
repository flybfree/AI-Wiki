## Summary  
The paper introduces IRIS (Identity Representations from Internal States), a training‑free framework that creates stable, iris‑like signatures for each entity by extracting identity‑oriented contextual representations from frozen large language models (LLMs). By encoding entities once in a shared space, IRIS enables direct similarity comparison across heterogeneous knowledge graphs without pair‑dependent construction or candidate‑wise LLM inference. The approach aims to decouple entity alignment from specific KG pairs and reduce repeated processing overhead. Experimental results demonstrate that the best IRIS variants achieve near‑perfect Hits@1 scores on four benchmark datasets.

## Key Contributions  
- [Training‑free entity alignment using frozen LLM internal states, eliminating the need for fine‑tuning or candidate inference.]  
- [Construction of reusable iris‑like identity representations that are stable across different knowledge graphs.]  
- [Achievement of Hits@1 scores up to 100.00 on D‑Y‑15K V2, 99.38 on DBP‑WIKI, 98.31 on ICEWS‑WIKI, and 97.99 on ICEWS‑YAGO.]

## Methodology  
IRIS leverages a frozen LLM to elicit identity‑focused contextual embeddings for each entity in a knowledge graph. These embeddings are treated as an “iris” signature that captures the entity’s distinctive and stable characteristics. The signatures are computed once per entity and stored independently of any KG pair or candidate set. Alignment is performed by computing similarity between these pre‑computed representations, typically using cosine distance or other metric functions. No further LLM inference occurs during alignment; all processing is done on the static embeddings.

## Results  
Across four established EA benchmarks (D‑Y‑15K V2, DBP‑WIKI, ICEWS‑WIKI, ICEWS‑YAGO) and two frozen LLM backbones, IRIS variants reach Hits@1 scores of 100.00, 99.38, 98.31, and 97.99 respectively. These results show that the method can match or exceed state‑of‑the‑art performance while requiring no additional training data or fine‑tuning.

## Significance  
IRIS provides a reusable identity space that decouples entity alignment from specific knowledge graphs, enabling efficient cross‑KG comparisons and reducing computational overhead. By storing stable signatures once per entity, the framework scales to large datasets and multiple KG pairs without repeated LLM processing, offering practical benefits for real‑world applications where frequent alignment queries are needed.

## Related Concepts  
- Entity Alignment (EA) – identifying entities across knowledge graphs that refer to the same real‑world object.  
- Knowledge Graphs (KGs) – structured representations of real‑world information.  
- Large Language Models (LLMs) – deep neural networks pre‑trained on massive text corpora.  
- Frozen LLM – an LLM whose parameters are fixed and not updated during training.  
- Internal States – contextual embeddings generated by the model’s hidden layers at inference time.  
- Iris‑like Signatures – stable, one‑to‑one representations analogous to iris patterns that uniquely identify individuals.
