## Summary  
Roberto Bruno and Ugo Vaccaro revisit the operation of negating a discrete probability vector \(\mathbf{p}\) as introduced by Yager (2015), where each component is transformed to \((\,1-p_i\,)/(n-1)\).  Their aim is to give this seemingly ad‑hoc rule a rigorous foundation by analysing it through information‑theoretic lenses and majorization theory.  The paper unifies several previously scattered results, extends the construction to broader class of distributions, and strengthens the theoretical justification for Yager’s negation as the most natural choice under entropy and majorization criteria.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A unified information‑theoretic framework that simultaneously satisfies entropy monotonicity and majorization ordering for Yager’s negation.  
- [Finding 2] Generalisation of Yager’s rule to any probability vector, producing a family \(\overline{\mathbf{p}}_k\) with parameters \(k\in[0,1]\) that retain the same information‑theoretic properties.  
- [Finding 3] A set of stronger bounds on the entropy difference between \(\mathbf{p}\) and its negation, showing that the negated vector is always more “mixed” than the original under all admissible parameter ranges.

## Methodology  
The authors employ tools from information theory—particularly Shannon entropy—and majorization theory to compare two probability vectors.  They compute the Kullback‑Leibler divergence between \(\mathbf{p}\) and its Yager‑negated counterpart, then relate this divergence to the mutual information generated by a binary encoding of each component’s value relative to its complement.  By exploiting the convexity of entropy functions, they derive monotonic relationships that hold for any dimension \(n\ge2\).  The generalisation step introduces an auxiliary parameter \(k\) that rescales the transformation while preserving the same functional form, allowing the analysis to be extended beyond the original binary case.

## Results  
The theoretical results show that for every \(\mathbf{p}\) with entries in \([0,1]\) and sum = 1, the negated vector \(\overline{\mathbf{p}}\) satisfies: (i) its entropy is strictly larger than that of \(\mathbf{p}\); (ii) \(\overline{\mathbf{p}}\) majorises \(\mathbf{p}\) in the sense of von Neumann ordering; and (iii) the Kullback‑Leibler divergence between them is bounded by a function of \(k\) that decreases monotonically as \(k\to0\).  These properties hold for all dimensions, confirming that Yager’s negation is not an isolated curiosity but a principled operation consistent with information‑theoretic principles.

## Significance  
By grounding Yager’s rule in entropy and majorization theory, the authors provide a solid theoretical justification that the negated distribution is the most natural choice when one wishes to “flip” probabilities while preserving well‑defined information‑theoretic orderings.  This not only clarifies why the definition works but also opens avenues for applying such negations in statistical learning, where flipping class priors may be required.
