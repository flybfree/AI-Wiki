## Summary  
The paper investigates the scaling paradox in human‑AI collaboration, where AI capabilities improve predictably as systems scale yet joint performance may deteriorate if humans misperceive those capabilities. It proposes an analytical model linking AI scaling to system outcomes contingent on the accuracy of human perception. The authors show that over‑perception can reverse the scaling benefit, causing larger AI to reduce profit and performance, while under‑perception yields slower but still positive gains. They also suggest operational policies such as cost internalization can mitigate these distortions.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Human misperception of AI capabilities can reverse the scaling benefit, leading to a paradox where larger AI reduces joint performance and profits.  
- [Finding 2] When humans under‑perceive AI, performance still improves with scale but at a markedly slower rate than optimal.  
- [Finding 3] Firms can manage these distortions through operational policies such as cost internalization and perception alignment, whose effectiveness depends on deployment economics.

## Methodology  
The authors construct an analytical model that quantifies the joint system performance of human‑AI collaboration by varying AI scale while holding human perception fixed. They use a simulated firm environment where AI capabilities are represented by a parameterized function, and human decisions (e.g., resource allocation) are modeled as functions of perceived capability. The model predicts outcomes under different misperception scenarios and incorporates cost internalization mechanisms to assess policy interventions.

## Results  
Theoretical analysis demonstrates that when humans over‑perceive AI, increasing AI scale leads to diminishing returns and profit loss, illustrating the scaling paradox. When humans under‑perceive AI, performance improves with scale but the marginal gain diminishes sharply, reflecting a slower scaling trajectory. Simulations confirm these predictions across multiple parameter settings.

## Significance  
These findings highlight that the value of AI investment is contingent on managing human beliefs rather than merely upgrading model size, prompting organizations to prioritize perception alignment and cost internalization. They challenge the assumption that larger AI systems automatically improve outcomes, emphasizing a behavioral dimension in AI scaling.
