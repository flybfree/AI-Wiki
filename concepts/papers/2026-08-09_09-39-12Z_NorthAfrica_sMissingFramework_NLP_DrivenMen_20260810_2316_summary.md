## Summary  
This paper introduces a conceptual framework that examines how Natural Language Processing (NLP) could be leveraged to support mental healthcare in Algeria, a region largely absent from existing NLP‑mental health literature. By synthesising global NLP research, Algerian healthcare documentation, and low‑resource NLP techniques, the authors identify four structural barriers—language‑of‑care gaps, geographic inequities, stigma‑related help‑seeking obstacles, and missing digital infrastructure—and map each to potential NLP applications, implementation constraints, and required technical, institutional, and governance measures. The work culminates in a research‑and‑policy roadmap that prioritises data resources, multilingual language technologies, evaluation frameworks, and regulatory capacity, offering a practical guide for deploying AI‑enabled mental health services in under‑represented settings.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A comprehensive conceptual framework that situates NLP within Algeria’s unique linguistic, historical, and healthcare context.  
- [Finding 2] Identification of four structural barriers to mental healthcare and a systematic mapping of existing NLP capabilities to each barrier.  
- [Finding 3] Proposal of a research‑and‑policy roadmap that prioritises data resources, multilingual language technologies, evaluation frameworks, and regulatory capacity for AI‑enabled mental health deployment.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through narrative synthesis rather than experimental testing. They collected and synthesised three knowledge streams: (1) global NLP research on mental health applications; (2) Algerian literature on mental healthcare systems and challenges; and (3) low‑resource NLP methodologies that operate with limited data, multilingual corpora, or offline processing. This interdisciplinary synthesis allowed them to construct a theoretical mapping of NLP tools to specific barriers and to outline the technical, institutional, and governance prerequisites for their deployment.

## Results  
The primary results are theoretical: (1) a clear articulation of how language‑specific NLP models could bridge the “language‑of‑care” gap; (2) concrete suggestions for mitigating geographic inequities through mobile‑first NLP services; (3) strategies to reduce stigma by integrating culturally sensitive chatbot interfaces; and (4) recommendations for building local digital infrastructure and regulatory oversight. No empirical experiments were conducted; instead, the outcomes are a structured analysis and a policy‑oriented roadmap.

## Significance  
This work matters because it provides the first systematic framework that translates NLP research into actionable mental health interventions for Algeria and other multilingual, low‑resource, post‑colonial contexts. By highlighting the need for culturally appropriate language technologies and robust governance, the paper helps bridge a long‑standing gap between AI innovation and underserved healthcare populations.
