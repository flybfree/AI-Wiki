## Summary  
The paper investigates how generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) reshapes academic writing and publishing, focusing on the impact on linguistic diversity in World Englishes. It aims to identify whether GenAI can democratise scholarly production or reinforce existing hierarchies, thereby contributing a nuanced, policy‑oriented perspective for scholars concerned with equity in AI‑mediated communication.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Generative AI tools often default to dominant, Standard English norms, marginalising minoritised varieties and flattening the nuance of World Englishes.  
- Finding 2: The dialogue highlights three core ethical challenges—linguistic (in)justice, researcher agency, and institutional responsibility—that must be addressed in AI governance.  
- Finding 3: Researchers call for equity‑informed policies, critical AI literacy, and inclusive co‑design processes to ensure GenAI serves linguistic diversity rather than erasing it.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a structured scholarly dialogue involving five sociolinguists from World Englishes and adjacent fields. Each participant answered five guiding questions about writing practices, language norms, and ethical implications, producing a collective reflection that synthesises diverse viewpoints into actionable insights.

## Results  
The dialogue produced three overarching themes: (1) linguistic (in)justice arising when AI reproduces dominant language standards; (2) researcher agency being limited by tools that do not recognise multilingual identities; and (3) institutional responsibility to embed equity‑focused policies in AI development. The results also reveal a call for critical AI literacy among scholars and a push for inclusive co‑design of GenAI systems.

## Significance  
This matters because it directly addresses the tension between technological efficiency and sociolinguistic fairness, offering a roadmap for institutions to mitigate bias while harnessing AI’s potential in global scholarship. By foregrounding diversity, the study contributes to broader debates on responsible AI use in academia.
