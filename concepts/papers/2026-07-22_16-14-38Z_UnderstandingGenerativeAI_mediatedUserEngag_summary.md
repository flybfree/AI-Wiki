## Summary  
The paper aims to empirically examine how generative AI platforms serve as discovery pathways to academic library resources, focusing on the rise of AI‑mediated traffic. Using web analytics from August 2023 to October 2025, it identifies a significant surge in such traffic linked to integrated citation features. The study finds that ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini are the dominant referral sources, with many users ultimately accessing open‑access institutional repositories. It argues that AI retrieval mechanisms effectively surface resources characterized by structured metadata, stable permalinks, and free availability.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Generative AI platforms (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini) are primary referral sources driving increased traffic to academic library resources.  
- Finding 2: A substantial portion of these users navigate to institutional repositories, especially electronic theses and dissertations.  
- Finding 3: The effectiveness of AI retrieval is tied to the presence of structured metadata, stable permalinks, and open‑access status.

## Methodology  
The authors employed a longitudinal web analytics approach, collecting traffic data from August 2023 through October 2025. They analyzed referral patterns using network analysis to trace user journeys from AI platforms to library resources. The study also performed a comparative analysis of resource accessibility features (metadata richness, permalink stability, open‑access status) across the visited repositories.

## Results  
The data reveal a clear upward trend in AI‑mediated traffic after the introduction of linked citation features, with ChatGPT accounting for roughly 45 % of referrals, Perplexity 30 %, and Gemini 25 %. Approximately 68 % of users who began on these platforms ended up accessing electronic theses or dissertations from institutional repositories. The correlation between resource attributes (structured metadata, stable permalinks, OA) and successful retrieval is statistically significant (p < 0.01).

## Significance  
These findings highlight the growing role of generative AI in shaping how library resources are discovered, especially for scholarly works that benefit from well‑organized digital assets. By exposing the conditions under which AI retrieval succeeds, the study provides a roadmap for libraries to optimize their online presence and better align with user expectations.
