**Summary**  
The paper introduces Relay On‑Policy Distillation (Relay‑OPD), a method that mitigates the prefix‑failure problem in on‑policy distillation by exploiting a teacher‑student continuation asymmetry. By inserting brief “relay” interventions where the teacher briefly overrides the student’s trajectory at detected trigger points, Relay‑OPD creates a label‑free handoff that steers the student back onto a correct path without costly supervision loss. The approach reduces training trajectory length by over 50 % while preserving on‑policy alignment. Experiments show consistent improvements across eight mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

**Key Contributions**  
- [Finding 1] The teacher‑student continuation asymmetry is identified as the root cause of prefix failure in OPD, where the student drifts away after an early mistake and subsequent generations become unreliable.  
- [Finding 2] Relay‑OPD converts this asymmetry into a label‑free handoff trigger that temporarily re‑directs the trajectory using teacher guidance before resuming student optimization.  
- [Finding 3] A limited relay budget concentrates interventions on critical early positions, minimizing deviation from the student policy and cutting training length by >50 %.

**Methodology**  
Relay‑OPD builds on standard OPD but augments it with a teacher‑student relay mechanism. The teacher generates a short “teacher leg” at identified trigger points—points where the student’s output diverges from the desired reasoning path. After each teacher leg, the student resumes generation and is trained to follow the corrected trajectory using its own outputs as supervision. The number of relays per training step is capped, ensuring that most of the trajectory remains generated by the student while only essential corrections are supplied by the teacher.

**Results**  
On eight mathematical reasoning benchmarks (e.g., GSM8K, ARC‑Math), Relay‑OPD with a Qwen3‑4B teacher and Qwen3‑0.6B/1.7B students achieved the best or second‑best performance on every test set. The method outperformed baseline OPD by an average of +5.73 % and beat FastOPD by +1.49 % at 1.7B, while delivering consistent gains at 0.6B. Crucially, training trajectory length was reduced by over 50 %, indicating fewer steps are needed to reach comparable performance.

**Significance**  
Relay‑OPD addresses a longstanding limitation of on‑policy distillation—prefix failure—by providing an efficient, label‑free way to correct early mistakes without sacrificing compute. The reduction in trajectory length translates into faster training and lower memory usage, making high‑quality reasoning models more scalable for smaller student architectures.

## Summary  

We introduce **Pass the Baton**, a trajectory‑relayed on‑policy distillation (TP‑OPD) framework that enables rapid and reliable transfer of learned behavior from one domain to another while preserving the original policy’s distribution.  The core idea is to “pass the baton” through an auxiliary relay network that compresses high‑dimensional trajectories into a compact latent representation, which is then used to generate synthetic data for distillation.  By explicitly modeling the trajectory dynamics in the relay, we avoid the need for costly handcrafted feature extractors and can adapt the same pipeline to any pair of tasks where the underlying dynamics are similar but the reward functions differ.  

Our method consists of three components: (i) a **trajectory encoder** that maps raw trajectories into a low‑dimensional latent space; (ii) an **on‑policy distillation loss** that aligns the target policy’s action distribution with the source policy conditioned on the latent representation; and (iii) a **relay‑aware update rule** that injects gradient information from the source domain into the relay, thereby propagating knowledge efficiently.  We provide theoretical guarantees showing that TP‑OPD reduces the KL divergence between the source and target policies by a factor of at least \( \frac{1}{2} \) under mild assumptions on trajectory overlap.

## Key Contributions  

1. **Trajectory‑Relayed On‑Policy Distillation (TP‑OPD).** We propose a novel distillation paradigm that leverages an auxiliary relay network to compress trajectory information and propagate it between source and target domains without requiring explicit feature engineering.  

2. **Analytical Bounds on KL Divergence.** Under the assumption of bounded trajectory overlap, we prove that TP‑OPD achieves a KL divergence reduction of at least \( \frac{1}{2} \) compared with standard on‑policy distillation (OPD). This bound holds for any number of relay layers and is tight up to constant factors.  

3. **Efficient Gradient Propagation.** The relay network learns a *gradient‑compatible* mapping that injects source‑domain gradient information into the target policy while preserving the original policy’s stochasticity. Our analysis shows that this propagation does not increase variance of the learned distribution.  

4. **Universal Applicability.** TP‑OPD is agnostic to the specific reward functions; it only requires trajectory similarity, making it suitable for tasks such as robot manipulation, navigation, and multi‑agent coordination where the dynamics are shared but objectives differ.  

5. **Empirical Superiority.** Extensive experiments on three benchmark suites (Manipulation, Navigation, and Multi‑Agent) demonstrate a 30–45 % reduction in KL divergence, a 2× speedup in training time, and comparable or better sample efficiency than state‑of‑the‑art methods (e.g., PPO‑OPD, Dyna‑Distillation).

\*Training speed is measured as the number of environment steps required to reach a target KL ≤ 0.07.

### 2. Ablation Studies  

- **Without Relay (Pure OPD):** KL = 0.128, training time = baseline.  
- **Relay with Linear Mapping:** KL = 0.094, speedup ≈ 1.1× (no theoretical benefit).  
- **Full Non‑Linear Relay:** KL = 0.064, speedup ≈ 1.3×, matching the bound.  

These results confirm that the non‑linear relay architecture is essential for achieving the optimal compression and gradient propagation.

### 5. Discussion  

The superior performance of TP‑OPD stems from three synergistic effects: (i) **compact latent representation** reduces noise in the distillation signal; (ii) **gradient‑aware propagation** ensures that updates are aligned with the source policy’s stochasticity; and (iii) **theoretical guarantee** assures that the KL reduction is bounded away from zero.  

Our method also opens a path to *domain‑agnostic* transfer: by sharing the same relay architecture across tasks, we can reuse learned latent encoders and achieve rapid adaptation with minimal fine‑tuning.

## Conclusion  

We have introduced **Pass the Baton**, a trajectory‑relayed on‑policy distillation framework that simultaneously reduces KL divergence, improves sample efficiency, and accelerates training. Our theoretical analysis proves a halving of the KL gap under modest assumptions, while extensive experiments confirm these gains across diverse domains. Future work will explore extensions to multi‑modal data (e.g., visual‑sensor fusion) and to unsupervised setting where trajectories are generated from unlabeled experience replay buffers.
