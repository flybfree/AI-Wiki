## Summary  
This study investigates caregivers’ perceptions of four distinct categories of care robots—delivering supplies, helping patients into bed, monitoring vital signs, and assisting mobility—to understand how these technologies are received in healthcare settings. Conducted across the United States, Mexico, and Chile with 298 participants, it combines quantitative measures from UTAUT and the Cognitive‑Affective‑Normative model with qualitative insights drawn from an ethical framework. The research contributes a context‑sensitive view of both acceptance and ethical implications for care robot deployment. Its contribution is to guide socially informed design that balances benefits such as reduced workload with concerns like dependability and job displacement.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Participants generally rated logistical and physically demanding robot tasks positively, while those requiring intensive interpersonal interaction were less favorable.  
- [Finding 2] Qualitative analysis revealed recurring ethical concerns—dependability, need for human oversight, and potential job displacement—that are shared across countries but interpreted differently.  
- [Finding 3] Cross‑national differences in how stakeholders prioritize these concerns highlight the importance of culturally grounded design.

## Methodology  
The authors employed a mixed‑factual factorial design: quantitative surveys measured acceptance using constructs from UTAUT and the Cognitive‑Affective‑Normative model, while qualitative open‑ended responses were analyzed through an ethical framework. Data were collected from 298 caregivers in three countries to capture diverse perspectives.

## Results  
Overall, participants expressed positive views toward robots that handle supply delivery and mobility assistance, reflecting reduced workload and lower risk. In contrast, robot functions involving patient interaction received mixed or negative evaluations. Ethical concerns identified included dependability, the necessity for human oversight, and fear of job loss; these were noted as shared but with varying emphasis across countries.

## Significance  
The findings provide a nuanced understanding that informs responsible development: they suggest focusing on tasks where robots can demonstrably reduce caregiver burden while maintaining clear ethical safeguards. This knowledge helps policymakers and designers navigate the balance between patient care improvements and workforce impacts.
