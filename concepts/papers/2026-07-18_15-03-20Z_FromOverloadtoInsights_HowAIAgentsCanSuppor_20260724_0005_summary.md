## Summary  
The paper proposes an AI‑agent system that helps scientists at the European XFEL efficiently analyze massive, complex datasets by bridging domain expertise with fragmented technical documentation and tools. By employing a design‑science research methodology, the authors evaluate two prototypes that combine knowledge retrieval and source‑code generation to reduce analysis bottlenecks. The study demonstrates how an agent can act as a persistent, adaptable assistant within a high‑performance computing environment. This work offers concrete guidance for building maintainable AI support in highly specialized scientific settings.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Scientific data analysis at European XFEL is hampered by knowledge that is scattered across documentation, tools, and support channels, creating a “knowledge overload” problem.  
- [Finding 2] The study derives specific requirements for an AI agent—namely, reliable knowledge retrieval and automated generation of source code—to address these challenges.  
- [Finding 3] Design recommendations are proposed to make the AI system adaptable as new AI tools become available, ensuring long‑term maintainability.

## Methodology  
The authors followed a design science research approach: they performed a rapid literature review, systematically evaluated 16 existing AI tools, conducted multiple interviews with domain experts, held focus groups, and carried out a user study involving European XFEL scientists. Using these insights, they developed and iteratively tested two prototypes that integrate knowledge retrieval capabilities with code generation within the HPC infrastructure of the facility.

## Results  
The evaluation identified key knowledge challenges in scientific data analysis and translated them into concrete functional requirements for an AI agent. The prototypes were assessed against criteria such as relevance, latency, and ease of integration, showing measurable improvements in time‑to‑insight compared with manual workflows. The results confirmed that the proposed design meets the derived requirements and can be operationalized within the existing XFEL environment.

## Significance  
This research provides a blueprint for deploying AI agents as sustainable assistants in specialized scientific domains, reducing cognitive load and accelerating discovery cycles. By formalizing knowledge‑centric design principles, it helps organizations future‑proof their support systems against rapid technological change.
