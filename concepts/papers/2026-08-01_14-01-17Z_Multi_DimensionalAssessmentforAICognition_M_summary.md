## Summary  
The paper proposes the Multi‑Dimensional Assessment for AI Cognition (MAAC) as a theoretical framework that shifts evaluation of text‑based AI systems from outcome‑focused benchmarks to process‑oriented cognitive analysis. It defines nine cognitively motivated dimensions—Cognitive Load, Tool Execution, Content Quality, Memory Integration, Complexity Handling, Hallucination Control, Knowledge Transfer, Processing Efficiency, and Process‑Outcome Alignment—that are grounded in established cognitive science theories. By mapping each dimension to a specific theory and constructing a coverage matrix, the authors aim to provide a coherent, testable model for evaluating how AI “thinks.” This framework complements existing accuracy or fairness metrics with a richer, process‑level perspective.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] MAAC introduces nine cognitively motivated dimensions that capture essential aspects of AI cognition beyond task performance.  
- [Finding 2] The framework is theoretically grounded through a dimension‑to‑theory mapping, a coverage matrix assessing breadth and non‑redundancy, a formal gap analysis relative to current evaluation practice, a worked diagnostic illustration, and a set of a priori interdependency predictions for future empirical testing.  
- [Finding 3] MAAC offers an operational tool that can be integrated with existing outcome‑based benchmarks to yield a more complete assessment of AI cognition.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by first reviewing relevant cognitive science literature—Marr’s tri‑level hypothesis, Baddeley’s working memory model, Sweller’s cognitive load theory, and unified theories of cognition—to identify candidate dimensions. They then created a coverage matrix to ensure each dimension is distinct yet collectively exhaustive. A formal gap analysis compared these nine dimensions against existing AI evaluation practices, highlighting missing or redundant aspects. A diagnostic illustration demonstrated how hallucination control could be evaluated using the Hallucination Control dimension. Finally, they derived interdependency predictions that describe how changes in one dimension may affect others.

## Results  
The theoretical results include a clear mapping of each MAAC dimension to an established cognitive theory, confirming their relevance. The coverage matrix shows high breadth with minimal redundancy, satisfying the non‑redundancy criterion. The gap analysis identifies three dimensions (Complexity Handling, Knowledge Transfer, and Process‑Outcome Alignment) that are absent from most current benchmarks, indicating a systematic deficiency in process evaluation. The diagnostic illustration provides a concrete example of how Hallucination Control can be operationalized as a measurable metric. A set of interdependency predictions is offered for future empirical work, suggesting that improving Cognitive Load may reduce hallucinations and enhance Knowledge Transfer.

## Significance  
MAAC matters because it shifts AI research toward understanding the internal cognitive processes that drive performance, rather than merely measuring output accuracy. By providing a multi‑dimensional lens, the framework can guide more robust model design, improve diagnostic reasoning about failures such as hallucination or memory integration errors, and ultimately lead to AI systems that are not only accurate but also cognitively coherent.
