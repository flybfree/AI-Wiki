## Summary  
The paper aims to map the cognitive capability gaps that persist in generative and agentic AI systems, thereby providing a roadmap for more reliable long‑term reasoning and adaptive behavior. It organizes recent literature around five core dimensions: persistent state modeling, goal‑directed autonomy, self‑monitoring and control, environment interaction, and learning and adaptation. By synthesizing these findings, the authors introduce an Adaptive Cognitive Intelligence Architecture (ACIA) that integrates the missing capabilities into a unified framework. The work also proposes a taxonomy‑based evaluation method to assess progress toward Cognitive AI and AGI.

## Key Contributions  
- Identify five core cognitive capability gaps: persistent state modeling, goal‑directed autonomy, self‑monitoring and control, environment interaction, and learning and adaptation.  
- Propose a unified taxonomy that organizes existing research around these dimensions.  
- Introduce the Adaptive Cognitive Intelligence Architecture (ACIA) as a conceptual framework for integrating these capabilities.

## Methodology  
The authors performed a comprehensive literature review of recent AI papers on generative models and autonomous agents, extracting case studies that illustrate how each system addresses or neglects the five cognitive dimensions. They applied a coding scheme to map every study onto the taxonomy, then synthesized the results into a coherent set of findings. The Adaptive Cognitive Intelligence Architecture (ACIA) was derived from this synthesis, outlining how components could be combined to fill the identified gaps.

## Results  
The taxonomy reveals that most systems excel in language generation but lag in persistent state modeling; autonomy is limited to single‑task execution; self‑monitoring lacks feedback loops; environment interaction remains simulated rather than embodied; and learning is shallow and non‑adaptive. The ACIA demonstrates a coherent integration of these gaps, offering a blueprint for multi‑modal cognitive systems that can sustain reasoning over extended horizons.

## Significance  
Understanding these gaps clarifies why current AI falters in long‑term reasoning and adaptive behavior, guiding researchers toward more reliable Cognitive AI. By providing a common framework, the work accelerates progress toward AGI by highlighting priority research areas and evaluation metrics.
