## Summary  
The paper proposes a six‑phase “Curriculum as Code” architecture that merges Generative AI with LaTeX/Beamer for slide generation and Python for figure creation, aiming to automate the production of STEM instructional materials. By encoding pedagogical rules in code rather than relying on ad‑hoc prompts, the framework seeks to reduce faculty workload while guaranteeing mathematical accuracy, visual consistency, and preservation of tacit teaching knowledge. The system replaces manual prompt engineering with a systematic, reproducible workflow that can be shared across multiple instructors. Validation shows that generated assets meet high quality standards and are widely accepted by students.

## Key Contributions  
- A systematic six‑phase pipeline replaces ad‑hoc prompt engineering with a code‑driven workflow that enforces pedagogical constraints throughout the design process.  
- The architecture generates LaTeX Beamer slides and Python figures with high reproducibility, achieving minimal hallucinations compared to earlier AI‑assisted tools.  
- Independent peer review across six faculty members and over 600 student evaluations confirms scalability and high quality (ratings 8.5–9.9/10), significantly lowering instructor preparation time.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by defining a six‑phase pipeline where each phase is guided by explicit pedagogical constraints, contextual calibrations, and automated review cycles. Text‑based interfaces let instructors encode rules in LaTeX templates and Python scripts, allowing the system to produce visual assets that are both technically precise and aligned with institutional branding.

## Results  
Validated over one year across eight modules and twenty‑eight project contexts within a Project‑Based Learning environment, the framework reduced instructor workload while maintaining rigorous quality. Generated materials were independently peer‑reviewed, deployed by six different faculty members, and received high student satisfaction scores (8.5–9.9/10). Independent evaluation confirmed that hallucinations were rare and reproducibility was strong.

## Significance  
This work matters because it offers a scalable, reproducible method for creating STEM instructional content that respects both technical accuracy and pedagogical intent, thereby alleviating faculty burden and enhancing learning outcomes across diverse educational contexts.
