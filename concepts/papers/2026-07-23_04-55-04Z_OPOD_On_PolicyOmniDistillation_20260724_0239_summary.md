## Summary  
Omni‑modal models strive to handle text, image, and audio inputs in a single system, but pooling all modalities often degrades each component’s performance. Existing on‑policy distillation (OPD) learns from teacher evaluations of the same response generated by a student model, yet multiple teachers can conflict and bias learning toward one modality. OPOD solves this by routing each student output to the matching modality teacher, applying guidance only when that teacher’s probability exceeds the student’s, and adjusting each teacher’s influence independently while also assessing both answer correctness and reasoning support. This coordinated approach yields the best average scores across multimodal benchmarks.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Routing responses to the appropriate modality‑specific teacher prevents cross‑modal interference and keeps guidance focused on the relevant expertise.  
- [Finding 2] Dynamic adjustment of each teacher’s influence balances performance across modalities, ensuring no single modality dominates training.  
- [Finding 3] The teacher evaluates both the final answer’s correctness and whether its reasoning supports that answer, providing dual feedback for holistic learning.

## Methodology  
The authors introduce On‑Policy Omni Distillation (OPOD), a framework that integrates multiple modality teachers into one student model. For every generated token sequence the system computes which teacher is most likely to have produced it; only that teacher contributes gradient updates when its predicted probability exceeds the student’s. The influence of each teacher can be tuned per training step, and the teacher also assesses whether the reasoning chain aligns with the correct answer, delivering both correctness and reasoning support as feedback.

## Results  
Across twelve multimodal benchmarks and three model sizes (3B, 7B, 30B), OPOD achieves top scores of 70.8, 51.7, and 46.2 respectively, surpassing all comparators by 2.1, 1.8, and 1.7 points. At the 30‑billion scale it outperforms both a baseline pooled model and a joint pre‑trained omni model on every benchmark and ranks first or second when individual specialists are included.

## Significance  
This work shows that coordinated modality‑specific teacher guidance can substantially boost shared omni‑modal performance without sacrificing balance, offering a scalable method for training large multimodal models with modular supervision. The findings highlight the power of routing and dynamic influence adjustment to improve joint capabilities while preserving each modality’s strengths.

## Related Concepts  
On‑policy distillation, omni‑modal models, modality routing, probability‑based teacher selection, dual feedback (answer correctness and reasoning support), dynamic influence adjustment.
