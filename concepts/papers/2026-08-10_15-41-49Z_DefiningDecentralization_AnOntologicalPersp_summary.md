## Summary  
The paper addresses the lack of a universally accepted definition of decentralization in computer science, which has become especially urgent with emerging paradigms such as decentralized AI and machine learning. It proposes an ontological framework that defines decentralization as a relational property of communication systems, distinguishing it from related notions like distribution. The framework introduces two novel evaluation metrics—Void Tolerance and Imperviousness—and provides a browser‑based tool for automated classification and metric computation across arbitrary systems. This work establishes a domain‑independent foundation for comparing heterogeneous architectures.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: A formal, graph‑based ontology that defines decentralization as a relational property while explicitly separating it from distribution.  
- Finding 2: Two new quantitative metrics—Void Tolerance (absence of trusted gateways) and Imperviousness (resistance to centralized control)—that enable systematic assessment.  
- Finding 3: A browser‑based implementation that automates classification and metric computation for any system, achieving high accuracy.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by analyzing its formal‑semantic, epistemological, and pragmatic foundations. They constructed a graph ontology that captures relational aspects of communication systems, derived Void Tolerance and Imperviousness as measurable properties, and built a web interface to classify arbitrary architectures and compute these metrics automatically.

## Results  
The framework yields consistent, comparable assessments across federated learning and blockchain architectures, whereas existing definitions often produce incomplete or contradictory conclusions. The two metrics reliably differentiate decentralized from distributed designs, and the browser tool correctly classifies test cases with over 90 % accuracy.

## Significance  
This work resolves a longstanding ambiguity in the field, enabling rigorous analysis of decentralization independent of implementation details—critical for advancing research in AI, IoT, secure communication protocols, and other domains where decentralization is a core objective.
