## Summary  
The paper introduces **LegalCiteTrust**, a benchmark for evaluating citation trustworthiness in Chinese long‑form legal research reports generated by LLMs and agentic systems. It aims to address the risk that cited authorities may be misrepresented or overapplied even when they exist. By operationalizing trustworthiness into three dimensions—Existence, Fidelity, Applicability (E/F/A)—the authors provide a systematic evaluation framework. Experiments across multiple system types reveal how citation governance impacts overall report quality.  

## Key Contributions  
- LegalCiteTrust creates 72 annotated Chinese legal reports with citation‑level annotations for Coverage, Support, and Citation Trustworthiness.  
- Empirical findings show that retrieval tools improve evidence support but do not reliably boost trust scores, highlighting a gap between retrieval performance and trustworthy output.  
- E/F/A‑based revision significantly improves both the Trust score and Final score more than existence‑only filtering.  

## Methodology  
The authors constructed LegalCiteTrust by annotating 72 legal research reports with citation metadata and trustworthiness judgments across three dimensions. They evaluated a suite of models—general‑purpose LLMs, deep‑research systems, and legal‑specific systems—on task completion, evidence richness, citation density, and Trust scores derived from E/F/A metrics.  

## Results  
Retrieval improvements increased citation density but had minimal effect on the Trust score; revision using E/F/A criteria raised Trust by an average of 12 % compared with existence‑only filtering. The study demonstrates that trustworthy generation requires citation‑aware governance beyond simple retrieval.  

## Significance  
This work clarifies the distinction between retrieving legal authorities and responsibly applying them, guiding developers to integrate trustworthiness checks into evidence selection pipelines. It also provides a benchmark for future research on AI‑generated legal content.
