## Summary  
This paper proposes a set of practical guidelines—named GUEST (GenAI Use and Evaluation in SLR Tasks)—for researchers who wish to incorporate generative AI or large language models into systematic literature reviews (SLRs) and for those evaluating such tools. The authors argue that while GenAI can assist with repetitive tasks and provide preliminary insights, it cannot replace the rigour, transparency, and human oversight required for a valid SLR. Their contribution is both conceptual: identifying the process challenges of using GenAI in SLRs; and practical: offering a structured framework (GUEST) to plan, conduct, and report SLRs that involve AI assistance. By highlighting the gap between automated summarisation and systematic review standards, the work aims to improve reproducibility and trustworthiness in this emerging field.

## Key Contributions  
- The authors identify three core process issues that arise when using GenAI for SLRs: (1) lack of clear task definition leading to ambiguous AI prompts; (2) insufficient human oversight resulting in unvalidated conclusions; (3) inadequate reporting mechanisms that obscure the role and limitations of AI‑generated content.  
- They introduce the GUEST framework, a six‑step process that integrates planning, execution, validation, documentation, and evaluation of GenAI assistance within SLRs.  
- The paper provides concrete recommendations for both conducting SLRs with GenAI (e.g., using AI only for data extraction) and evaluating its performance (e.g., blind testing, reproducibility checks).

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a rapid literature review of existing guidelines on evaluating GenAI and LLMs for academic tasks. They supplemented this with thought experiments that simulate typical SLR workflows and drew on their own experience conducting both SLRs and AI‑assisted evaluations. By synthesising these sources, they distilled a set of actionable recommendations rather than running formal experiments.

## Results  
The main findings are the identification of three recurring problems (as listed above) and the formulation of GUEST as a practical guide. The authors note that while no new experimental data were generated, the framework is grounded in observed inefficiencies and missteps reported by researchers using GenAI for SLRs.

## Significance  
This work matters because it addresses a growing need: systematic literature reviews are cornerstones of evidence‑based practice, yet their integrity can be compromised when AI tools generate unchecked content. By offering GUEST, the authors help ensure that any reliance on GenAI is transparent and accountable, thereby supporting both scientific quality and methodological rigor.
