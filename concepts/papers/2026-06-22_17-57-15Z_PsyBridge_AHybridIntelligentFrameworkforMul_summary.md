## Summary  
The paper introduces PsyBridge, a hybrid intelligent framework that integrates clinically validated screening tools (PHQ‑9 and GAD‑7), cognitive evaluations, and personality profiles to deliver multi‑dimensional mental health assessments with interpretable risk classifications. By employing a weighted aggregation mechanism within a modular architecture, PsyBridge aims to replace isolated, single‑indicator models with a comprehensive decision‑support system that is both accurate and explainable for telehealth applications. The framework was evaluated on a semi‑synthetic dataset of 500 patient profiles spanning various severity levels, demonstrating superior performance over stand‑alone instruments.  

## Key Contributions  
- **Integrated multi‑modal assessment**: PsyBridge combines PHQ‑9, GAD‑7, cognitive tests, and personality inventories into a single decision pipeline.  
- **Interpretable risk classification**: The weighted aggregation yields transparent mental health risk scores and actionable recommendations.  
- **Performance gains**: Ablation studies show that adding cognitive and personality components improves precision, recall, and F1‑score while stabilizing moderate‑risk predictions.  

## Methodology  
The authors constructed a hybrid model that first normalizes each modality’s output to a 0–1 scale, then applies a learned weighting scheme (trained via gradient boosting) to balance their contributions according to clinical relevance. The unified architecture outputs a composite mental health risk score and classifies patients into low, moderate, or high‑risk categories using a logistic regression classifier. A semi‑synthetic dataset was generated by sampling from known distributions of PHQ‑9, GAD‑7, cognitive scores, and Big Five personality traits to ensure realistic variability across severity levels.  

## Results  
On the 500‑patient test set, PsyBridge achieved an overall accuracy of 84 %, with precision of 0.81, recall of 0.79, and F1‑score of 0.80—significantly higher than the baseline PHQ‑9 (accuracy 62 %) or GAD‑7 (accuracy 58 %). Sensitivity analysis revealed that removing cognitive or personality inputs reduced accuracy by up to 4 % and introduced classification drift in moderate‑risk cases, confirming their stabilizing effect.  

## Significance  
PsyBridge offers a scalable, explainable AI solution for digital mental health care, enabling clinicians to make balanced risk assessments without over‑reliance on any single symptom scale. By integrating cognitive and personality data, the framework addresses the limitation of isolated screening tools, potentially reducing misclassification in telehealth settings where patient context is limited.
