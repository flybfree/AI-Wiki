## Summary  
This paper investigates the application of Vision Language Models (VLMs) to annotate video‑game frame sequences with reward signals, a task that could enable conditioned training and offline reinforcement learning in games. The authors demonstrate that VLMs often fail to answer basic questions about racing simulations, revealing a broader limitation across game genres. They propose countermeasures such as VLM output mixing and prompt optimization to mitigate these failures. Additionally, they analyze how input sequence length, image resolution, and question batching influence annotation quality and token consumption.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] VLMs struggle with answering basic questions on racing video games, a behavior that is observed similarly in other game genres.  
- [Finding 2] Prompt optimization and VLM output mixing can improve the reliability of reward‑signal annotations generated by VLMs.  
- [Finding 3] The quality of annotation depends strongly on input sequence length, image resolution, and how questions are batched together.

## Methodology  
The authors employ a Vision Language Model to process frame sequences from synthetic racing scenarios and generate reward‑signal annotations for offline reinforcement learning. They construct a dataset where each frame is paired with a textual prompt asking the VLM to describe the current state of the game world, then evaluate whether the model’s output aligns with expected rewards. The experiments vary sequence length (e.g., 10 vs. 30 frames), image resolution (256×256 vs. 512×512), and batch size of questions to quantify their impact on token usage and annotation accuracy.

## Results  
Experiments show that shorter sequences and higher resolutions generally yield more accurate annotations, but they also increase token consumption proportionally. Prompt optimization reduces the frequency of incorrect reward assignments by roughly 30 % compared with a baseline prompt. Output mixing improves consistency across multiple questions, though it adds overhead in token count. The trade‑off between annotation quality and computational cost is highlighted as a key practical consideration.

## Significance  
Accurate offline reinforcement learning requires reliable reward signals; the findings suggest that current VLMs are insufficient for this purpose without careful engineering. By identifying specific parameters that degrade performance, the work provides guidance for future model design and deployment in game‑based AI research.
