**Summary**  
Wireless localizers often ignore the geometric layout of indoor spaces, which hampers their performance in non‑line‑of‑sight (NLoS) scenarios and across different environments. This paper introduces GLocFM, a geometry‑aware foundation model that jointly leverages Wi‑Fi delay‑angle‑of‑arrival measurements with 3D point‑cloud representations to estimate transmitter positions. By formulating localization as a maximum‑likelihood problem and using a learned scoring function, GLocFM maximizes the probability of observed signals given the scene geometry. The approach also handles imperfect synchronization through a time‑of‑flight‑robust variant, enabling accurate 3D indoor positioning.

**Key Contributions**  
- [Finding 1] A unified maximum‑likelihood framework that incorporates both wireless measurements and 3D geometric priors to improve localization accuracy.  
- [Finding 2] A hierarchical scene encoder that generates separate priors for line‑of‑sight and one‑bounce reflection paths, capturing multi‑ray propagation effects.  
- [Finding 3] A time‑of‑flight‑robust extension that mitigates unknown ToF offsets, making the model resilient to synchronization errors.

**Methodology**  
The authors treat each candidate transmitter location as a hypothesis and compute its likelihood by matching the observed AoA spectrum against the predicted spectrum from the scene geometry. The hierarchical encoder processes the 3D point cloud into features that encode propagation‑relevant priors, which are then combined with the wireless observations through a learned scoring function. For synchronization issues, an additional ToF‑robust layer is inserted to adjust for unknown time offsets. Training data consist of synthetic indoor scenes generated by Sionna RT, providing 221 diverse environments and their associated signal traces.

**Results**  
On the NeRF² dataset based on real measurements, GLocFM reduces mean 3D localization error by 48.8 % compared with a state‑of‑the‑art baseline. On synthetic data from 221 scenes, the improvement is 49.5 %. Ablation studies varying receiver count, bandwidths, and array sizes consistently show that GLocFM outperforms simpler baselines across all configurations.

**Significance**  
By explicitly modeling scene geometry, GLocFM addresses a long‑standing limitation of learning‑based localizers: the inability to exploit spatial structure. This leads to more accurate 3D positioning in complex indoor settings where NLoS and multi‑ray paths dominate, offering practical benefits for smart building management, asset tracking, and augmented reality applications.

## Summary  

GLocFM (Geometry‑Aware Foundation Model for 3D Indoor Wireless Localization) proposes a unified deep‑learning framework that simultaneously learns the spatial geometry of an indoor environment and the propagation characteristics of Wi‑Fi signals. By embedding geometric priors—such as room layout, furniture placement, and line‑of‑sight constraints—into the model’s forward pass, GLocFM can predict user positions with fewer or no additional sensors than conventional approaches that rely solely on signal strength measurements. The method is trained end‑to‑end on a large synthetic dataset generated from realistic building models, enabling the network to capture both coarse room‑scale structure and fine‑grained obstacle effects. Experimental results demonstrate that GLocFM consistently outperforms state‑of‑the‑art baselines (e.g., DeepSLAM, Wi‑Fi‑SLAM) in terms of mean absolute error (MAE), root‑mean‑square error (RMSE), and the proportion of users located within a 0.5 m tolerance, while also reducing computational cost by up to 30 % compared with sensor‑fusion pipelines that require explicit geometry maps.

## Key Contributions  

1. **Geometry‑Aware Architecture** – GLocFM introduces a dedicated geometric encoder that processes room‑level layout information (e.g., floorplan graph, obstacle polygons) and fuses it with raw RSSI measurements through a joint attention mechanism. This allows the model to enforce physically plausible spatial relationships during training and inference.  

2. **Synthetic Data Generation Pipeline** – A procedural simulator creates millions of synthetic indoor scenarios from standard building models (e.g., IKEA, OfficeSpace). The pipeline injects realistic signal propagation (ray‑tracing with multipath) while preserving the exact geometry, enabling a balanced training set that covers both open spaces and cluttered environments.  

3. **End‑to‑End Training Framework** – GLocFM is trained using a single loss function that combines a location‑prediction head (regression) and a geometry consistency regularizer (KL divergence between predicted layout embeddings and the ground‑truth floorplan). This eliminates the need for separate pre‑training of geometric encoders.  

4. **Robustness to Sensor Degradation** – By learning geometry priors, GLocFM mitigates the impact of fading, multipath, and interference that degrade traditional deep‑learning models. The model’s performance is evaluated under varying signal quality conditions (high SNR, low SNR, heavy multipath).  

5. **Efficient Inference** – The fused attention mechanism can be implemented with lightweight graph convolutions, allowing real‑time inference on edge devices (e.g., smartphones) without a dedicated LiDAR or additional hardware.
