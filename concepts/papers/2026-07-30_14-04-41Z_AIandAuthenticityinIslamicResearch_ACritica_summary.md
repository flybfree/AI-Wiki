## Summary  
This paper evaluates the reliability of generative artificial intelligence for Islamic knowledge, focusing on how well AI systems generate accurate Qur’anic interpretation, Hadith explanation, Fiqh rulings, and pastoral advice while avoiding hallucinations and preserving source fidelity. By conducting a mixed‑method study across six leading models with fifty realistic open‑ended questions from participants in Australia and the United Kingdom, it provides one of the first empirical assessments of AI trustworthiness in high‑stakes religious contexts. The findings reveal that current AI tools are useful only as assistive aids and should not be treated as authoritative sources without scholarly verification.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Current AI systems achieve moderate domain accuracy but frequently produce unverifiable citations, indicating a breakdown in source fidelity.  
- [Finding 2] Hallucinations occur at roughly 40 % of responses, especially on Madhhab‑specific topics, showing a lack of reliable knowledge grounding.  
- [Finding 3] Models inconsistently handle doctrinal disagreement and uncertainty, often defaulting to generic or incorrect answers.

## Methodology  
The authors selected six prominent generative AI models and posed fifty open‑ended Islamic questions covering Qur’anic interpretation, Hadith, Fiqh, ethics, pastoral advice, and Madhhab‑sensitive issues. Responses were collected under real‑world conditions from participants in Australia and the United Kingdom. A mixed‑method framework examined domain accuracy, citation verification, hallucinations, jurisprudential consistency, uncertainty handling, source provenance, and geographical variation to answer four research questions.

## Results  
Domain accuracy averaged 68 %, but citation completeness was below 30 % and many answers lacked proper references. The hallucination rate reached about 42 %. Geographic differences were minor; the main issue was inconsistency in Madhhab‑specific answers across models, confirming that AI cannot reliably navigate doctrinal diversity.

## Significance  
These results underscore that generative AI is presently only a supplementary learning aid and not a trustworthy source for religious rulings or scholarly research. The study offers practical guidance to researchers, educators, developers, and the Muslim community on verifying AI outputs with authenticated primary sources and qualified expertise.
