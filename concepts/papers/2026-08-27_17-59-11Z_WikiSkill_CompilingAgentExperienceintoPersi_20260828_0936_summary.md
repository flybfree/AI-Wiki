## Summary  
WikiSkill is a framework that transforms an AI agent’s interaction history into a persistent, structured knowledge base (a wiki) which can be continuously updated to generate reusable executable skills. By separating raw execution traces, accumulated insights, and concrete skill modules, WikiSkill enables agents to evolve their capabilities over time without losing prior learning. The authors demonstrate that this systematic accumulation of experience leads to higher performance than both state‑of‑the‑art skill‑evolution baselines and the no‑skill baseline across a variety of benchmarks and model sizes. Moreover, skills learned by one model can be transferred to another, suggesting a scalable way to propagate expertise.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] WikiSkill introduces a three‑layer separation—raw execution experience, accumulated knowledge, and executable skills—within a persistent wiki that continuously consolidates agent interaction data.  
- [Finding 2] Skill evolution complements model scaling: larger models generally benefit more from evolved skills, yet smaller models equipped with skills can outperform substantially larger models that lack them.  
- [Finding 3] Evolved skills transfer effectively across different models and families, and skills generated by one model often surpass those self‑evolved in the same task.

## Methodology  
The authors designed WikiSkill as a co‑evolution loop: each agent interaction is first logged as raw experience, then summarized into knowledge entries that are stored in a wiki. From these entries, concrete skill modules (executable skills) are generated and applied to future tasks. The system continuously updates the wiki with new insights, allowing subsequent skill updates to build on accumulated prior knowledge. Ablation studies isolate the impact of each component—e.g., removing the persistent wiki or skipping skill consolidation—to confirm that the persistence mechanism is essential for effective evolution.

## Results  
Across diverse benchmarks and model families (including transformer‑based language models), WikiSkill consistently outperforms existing state‑of‑the‑art skill‑evolution methods and improves over a no‑skill baseline in most settings. The framework shows strong transferability: skills evolved by one model can be applied to another, and sometimes those cross‑model skills exceed self‑evolved ones. Larger models benefit more from the accumulated knowledge, but surprisingly, smaller models equipped with WikiSkill often surpass much larger models that lack any skill evolution.

## Significance  
WikiSkill demonstrates that systematically accumulating and refining agent experience yields reusable, transferable skills that can be integrated into continual learning pipelines. By providing a persistent knowledge base, it bridges the gap between raw interaction data and actionable expertise, enabling AI agents to adapt progressively without forgetting prior lessons—a crucial step toward more robust, scalable, and modular AI systems.
