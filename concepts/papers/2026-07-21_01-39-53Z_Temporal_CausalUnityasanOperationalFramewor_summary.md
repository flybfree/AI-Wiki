## Summary  
The paper proposes **temporal‑causal unity (TCU)**, a conceptual bridge that links the philosophical view that time is the unfolding of causal change to an operational model for collective dynamics. It introduces a measurable causal‑progress coordinate τ(t) defined by an integral of event intensity, and shows how this coordinate can be used to distinguish consensus from bipolar polarization in noisy Kuramoto networks. The authors also derive a conditional synchronization threshold that depends on drift width and noise, providing a falsifiable hypothesis for comparing causal‑progress with chronological time. TCU is presented as a disciplined framework rather than an empirical claim about spacetime physics.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A formal definition of **causal progress** τ(t) = ∫₀ᵗ λ(s|𝔍_s) ds, where the intensity λ is independent of outcomes, offering a quantitative measure of becoming.  
- [Finding 2] An analytical expression for the **conditional synchronization threshold** K_c = 2(Δ + D) in all‑to‑all noisy Kuramoto models with Lorentzian drift width Δ and noise amplitude D, explaining why synchronization depends on system parameters rather than a universal constant.  
- [Finding 3] A clear distinction between **first‑harmonic consensus** (uniform phase) and **second‑harmonic polarization** (bipolar distribution), demonstrated through order‑parameter analysis.

## Methodology  
The authors adopt a mixed approach: first, they construct the theoretical model by specifying event intensity functions λ(s|𝔍_s) that encode causal change independent of results. Next, they simulate stochastic networks where agents possess orientation phases and activation amplitudes, subject to weighted interaction, heterogeneous drift, external input, anchoring, and diffusion. The evolution of τ(t) is tracked, and first‑/second‑harmonic order parameters are computed to separate consensus from polarization. Numerical illustrations are generated to visualize the collapse of causal‑clock curves at K_c.

## Results  
Theoretical analysis predicts that synchronization initiates precisely when the coupling strength exceeds K_c = 2(Δ + D). Below this threshold, τ(t) evolves without phase locking, producing a dispersed “causal clock.” Above it, τ(t) collapses into a single harmonic peak, yielding consensus. Simultaneously, second‑harmonic order parameters rise only after K_c is crossed, indicating the emergence of polarization. The framework also provides an out‑of‑sample protocol for testing whether observed collective dynamics align with causal‑progress versus chronological time.

## Significance  
TCU offers a rigorous operationalization of the philosophical claim that time is causal unfolding, enabling quantitative predictions about social and cognitive synchrony. By separating interpretive thesis from measurable coordinate and stochastic model, it supplies falsifiable hypotheses and a comparative protocol, advancing both process ontology and complex‑system modeling without claiming to replace spacetime physics.
