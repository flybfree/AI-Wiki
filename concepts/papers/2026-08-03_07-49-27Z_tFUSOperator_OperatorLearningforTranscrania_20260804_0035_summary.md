## Summary  
The paper introduces **tFUSOperator**, a coordinate‑aware neural operator that learns to map free‑field pressure, skull anatomy and treatment parameters into an intracranial acoustic field for transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS). By treating the simulation as an operator learning problem, the authors avoid voxel‑wise regression on a fixed grid and instead capture how energy propagates through the skull. The model is evaluated on both CT and MR‑derived skulls, achieving high Dice scores while running orders of magnitude faster than numerical solvers. This work provides a fast, radiation‑free pathway for patient‑specific digital twins in tFUS therapy.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] tFUSOperator is the first operator‑based formulation for predicting tFUS acoustic fields, moving beyond voxel‑wise regression.  
- [Finding 2] The model localizes the acoustic focus with ~90% Dice on CT skulls and ~72% Dice on MR skulls, demonstrating robust performance across modalities.  
- [Finding 3] It outperforms numerical simulation by a factor of ~5.6 × 10⁴ while preserving accuracy, offering a practical computational advantage.

## Methodology  
The authors cast the tFUS problem as an operator learning task: they define a neural operator that takes three inputs—free‑field pressure vector, skull geometry (CT or MR), and treatment parameters—and outputs the reconstructed intracranial pressure field. The operator is trained to map these inputs into a shared physical coordinate frame, enabling it to propagate acoustic energy through the heterogeneous skull without explicit voxel discretization. Training leverages gradient descent on simulated data generated by high‑fidelity numerical solvers.

## Results  
On unseen CT and MR skulls, tFUSOperator achieves Dice scores of 0.90 and 0.72 for focus localization, respectively. Benchmarking against the baseline numerical solver shows that tFUSOperator completes a full simulation in ~5 × 10⁴ times less time while maintaining comparable accuracy. The speed‑accuracy trade‑off is validated across multiple treatment scenarios.

## Significance  
By replacing computationally expensive simulations with a lightweight neural operator, tFUSOperator enables real‑time patient‑specific planning without ionizing radiation, accelerating clinical workflows and improving safety. This breakthrough paves the way for scalable digital twins that can be updated as anatomy or treatment parameters evolve.
