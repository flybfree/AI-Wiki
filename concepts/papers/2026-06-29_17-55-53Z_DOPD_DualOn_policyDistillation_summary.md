## Summary  
This paper introduces DOPD (Dual On-policy Distillation), a novel framework designed to overcome the limitations of traditional on-policy distillation by introducing a dual routing mechanism that dynamically assigns supervision between teacher and student policies based on their advantage gaps and token-level signal probabilities. The core innovation lies in mitigating "privilege illusion"—a phenomenon where auxiliary supervision from privileged inputs blurs the boundary between transferable capability and untransferable information asymmetry—by assigning each token a distinct level of supervision strength, objective, and strategy derived from either the teacher or student itself. DOPD thus enables more credible and effective capacity transfer while preserving the integrity of the distillation process.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The phenomenon of "privilege illusion" is formally identified as a critical failure mode in on-policy distillation, where non-uniform token-level supervision leads to misleading supervision signals that conflate capability gaps with information asymmetry.  
- [Finding 2] DOPD introduces a dual routing paradigm that dynamically assigns supervision between teacher and student policies based on their advantage gap and relative probabilities, ensuring each token receives supervision from the most appropriate source.  
- [Finding 3] The method consistently outperforms Vanilla OPD and other distillation baselines across both LLM and VLM settings, demonstrating superior performance in stability, robustness, continual learning, and out-of-distribution tasks.

## Methodology  
DOPD addresses the problem of non-uniform token-level supervision by introducing a dynamic routing mechanism that evaluates the advantage gap between teacher and student policies at each token level. The system assigns supervision strength, objective function, and strategy to each token based on whether it is more likely to be generated by the teacher or student, and how much capability remains untransferred. This approach allows for adaptive supervision: tokens with high probability of being generated by the teacher receive stronger teacher-derived supervision, while those with higher student generation likelihood benefit from student-level auxiliary signals. The dual routing ensures that privileged information is used only where it enhances transferability without creating illusionary dependencies.

## Results  
Extensive experiments on large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) show that DOPD achieves state-of-the-art performance in distillation benchmarks, outperforming Vanilla OPD by up to 12.3% in average accuracy across multiple tasks. The method also demonstrates improved stability during training, reduced variance in loss curves, and better generalization under distribution shifts. In continual learning scenarios, DOPD maintains higher performance over time compared to vanilla methods, while its out-of-distribution robustness is significantly enhanced due to the careful routing of supervision signals.

## Significance  
DOPD represents a significant advancement in neural distillation by resolving a fundamental flaw in existing on-policy approaches: privilege illusion. By introducing adaptive, dual-source supervision that respects both capability transfer and information asymmetry, DOPD enables more reliable and effective knowledge transfer. This contributes to the broader goal of improving model efficiency and generalization without sacrificing performance.
