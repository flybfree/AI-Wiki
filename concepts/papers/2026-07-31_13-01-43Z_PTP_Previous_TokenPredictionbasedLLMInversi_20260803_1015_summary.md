## Summary
This paper introduces Previous-Token Prediction (PTP), a novel functional approach to inverting Large Language Models (LLMs) for near-exact prompt reconstruction without requiring access to model weights or logits. Unlike prior methods that frame prompt recovery as a semantic reconstruction task relying on fine-tuned sequence-to-sequence models and external datasets, PTP trains an explicit inverse language model from scratch using data synthetically generated by the target LLM itself. By training this inverse model using previous-token prediction rather than next-token prediction, the authors establish a generative link between the forward and inverse processes that enables faithful and precise prompt reconstruction. The proposed method demonstrates significant advantages in black-box settings, offering transferability across different LLMs and superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art techniques.

## Key Contributions
- **Black-Box Inversion Framework**: The authors present a functional approach to inverting a given LLM in a black-box setting, eliminating the need for auxiliary aids such as access to model weights, logits, or large external training datasets.
- **Previous-Token Prediction Mechanism**: They propose training an explicit inverse language model from scratch using previous-token prediction, which creates a direct generative link between the forward next-token sampling process and the inverse reconstruction process.
- **Superior Reconstruction Accuracy and Transferability**: The method achieves near-exact prompt reconstruction that outperforms prior work across various token-based evaluation metrics, while also demonstrating the ability to generalize across datasets and reconstruct prompts from responses generated by different LLMs.

## Methodology
The authors address the inherent many-to-many mapping between prompts and responses in auto-regressive LLMs by developing a synthetic data generation pipeline. They utilize the target LLM to generate response data, which serves as the basis for training the inverse model. Instead of relying on semantic similarity or fine-tuning pre-trained models, they train a new model entirely from scratch using a previous-token prediction objective. This means the inverse model learns to predict the preceding tokens given subsequent ones, effectively reversing the auto-regressive flow. The training data is derived exclusively from the target LLM's outputs, ensuring that the inverse model captures the specific distribution and characteristics of the forward process. During inference, the method supports diverse prompt reconstructions through sampling, allowing for multiple valid prompts that induce similar responses under the forward LLM.

## Results
Experimental evaluations demonstrate that PTP significantly outperforms prior work in terms of prompt reconstruction accuracy. The approach achieves near-exact reconstruction of original prompts, surpassing existing methods across a comprehensive set of token-based evaluation metrics. Furthermore, the study highlights the method's robustness and generalization capabilities, showing successful transferability when reconstructing prompts from responses generated by different LLM architectures. The ability to produce diverse yet semantically consistent prompt reconstructions through sampling further validates the effectiveness of the previous-token prediction strategy in capturing the full range of possible inputs for a given output.

## Significance
This research matters because it provides a practical and efficient solution for LLM inversion in black-box scenarios, which is critical for understanding model behavior, ensuring security, and auditing AI systems. By removing the dependency on internal model access or large external datasets, PTP lowers the barrier for researchers to analyze and reverse-engineer LLM behaviors. The near-exact reconstruction capability offers new insights into the determinism and ambiguity of LLM generation processes, contributing to the broader field of interpretability and safety in artificial intelligence.
