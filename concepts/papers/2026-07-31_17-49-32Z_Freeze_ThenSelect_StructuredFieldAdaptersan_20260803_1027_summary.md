## Summary
This research addresses the critical challenge of discovering partial differential equations (PDEs) from sparse, noisy observations by decoupling the complex tasks of field reconstruction and term selection. The authors identify that traditional coupled neural optimization often fails to reliably recover the exact support of differential terms due to unstable optimization paths. To overcome this, they propose a novel "freeze-then-select" framework that utilizes a structured field adapter for continuous representation followed by Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) for robust equation identification. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in recovering exact supports across challenging dynamical regimes, including the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, demonstrating superior stability and accuracy compared to existing classical and neural baselines.

## Key Contributions
- The authors rigorously analyze optimization paths in coupled neural PDE discovery, categorizing them into three distinct behaviors: persistent exact support, transient appearance, or complete failure to emerge, thereby highlighting the instability of end-to-end training.
- They introduce a novel methodological framework that separates field reconstruction from equation selection, utilizing a structured field adapter with cubic splines and a Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) mechanism that identifies recurrent terms across independent weak-form systems.
- The study demonstrates the versatility of their approach by extending it beyond fixed libraries to expressions generated by genetic programming, successfully recovering the power-law form of unknown nonlinear diffusion functions from sparse data.

## Methodology
The proposed methodology operates in two distinct phases to mitigate the instability inherent in joint optimization. First, a structured field adapter is trained directly on sparse observations without relying on PDE residuals. This adapter factorizes the observed field into learned spatial features and temporal coefficients, which are represented using cubic splines to ensure smooth and accurate continuous reconstruction. Once the field is accurately reconstructed, it is "frozen" to prevent further modification during the selection phase. In the second phase, Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) is applied. This process involves identifying differential terms that recur consistently across multiple independent weak-form systems derived from the frozen field. The method refits candidate supports and ultimately selects the final equation based on its performance on held-out weak-form systems, ensuring robustness against noise and sparsity. Additionally, the authors adapt this principle to work with genetic programming-generated expressions, allowing for the discovery of complex nonlinear forms like power laws without pre-defined libraries.

## Results
The method was evaluated across six sparse regimes in the MDBench benchmark. It attained the highest exact support recovery rate among all tested methods. The most significant improvements were observed in challenging dynamics, specifically the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation, where classical and neural baselines struggled. The approach successfully recovered the power-law form of unknown nonlinear diffusion functions when applied to genetic programming outputs, confirming its ability to handle complex, non-library-based structures.

## Significance
This work matters because it provides a stable, reliable pathway for PDE discovery from limited data, which is crucial for scientific modeling where observations are often sparse and noisy. By decoupling reconstruction from selection, it offers a more robust alternative to end-to-end neural methods that are prone to optimization instability.
