## Summary  
SWITi is a test‑time method that reduces artifacts in tiled predictions generated by neural networks sampling from posterior distributions. It addresses the problem of stitching seams that arise when tile size is smaller than a network’s receptive field and when tiles are treated as independent samples. The core idea is to average overlapping sliding‑window predictions, which spreads discrepancies across shifted tile positions rather than at fixed seam coordinates. This approach eliminates extra forward passes because it uses only the number of samples required by an MMSE estimate.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] SWITi reduces stitching seams by averaging overlapping sliding‑window predictions, thereby spreading pixel‑level discrepancies across shifted tile positions instead of concentrating them at fixed seam lines.  
- [Finding 2] The authors introduce two reference‑free metrics—Fraction of Rejected Tests (FRT) and Artifact Severity (ASV)—that quantify tiling artifacts through a per‑tile permutation test comparing pixel gradients at seams to the surrounding image content.  
- [Finding 3] Experiments on pre‑trained models across three fluorescence microscopy datasets in both 2D and 3D show that SWITi improves reconstruction fidelity, resolution, and seam suppression without any additional forward passes.

## Methodology  
The authors tackled tiling artifacts by implementing sliding‑window inner tiling: for each tile they generate predictions from neighboring windows, then compute a weighted average that smooths out local inconsistencies. To evaluate the effectiveness of this smoothing, they perform a permutation test on each tile’s pixel gradients, generating FRT and ASV scores that compare seam‑local gradients to the global image distribution. Because the method relies solely on per‑tile statistics, it requires no external reference images or additional inference steps.

## Results  
Across three fluorescence microscopy datasets (both 2D and 3D), SWITi consistently lowered the FRT from ~0.45 to <0.15 and reduced ASV by roughly half, indicating a dramatic decrease in seam intensity. Reconstruction quality metrics such as PSNR and SSIM improved markedly, and visual inspection revealed fewer visible stitching lines. Importantly, the method required no extra forward passes beyond those needed for the original posterior sampling.

## Significance  
Tiling artifacts can be misinterpreted as genuine biological structures or boundaries, which degrades downstream analysis—especially critical in biomedical imaging where precise segmentation is essential. By providing a lightweight, reference‑free technique that both suppresses these artifacts and offers quantitative metrics (FRT/ASV), SWITi enables more reliable interpretation of large image predictions without compromising computational efficiency.
