## Summary
The paper evaluates how large language models can assist literature reviews for physics, astrophysics, and cosmology research. It conducts a controlled comparison between human experts and AI prompters across eight expert‑conceived projects, measuring the overlap of selected references. The study also distinguishes two types of AI hallucinations: fabricated references to nonexistent papers and metadata mismatches in real papers. A 2026 model (ChatGPT Pro 5.5) is shown to achieve zero fabrication or metadata errors in a single‑project test.

## Key Contributions
- [Finding 1] The human‑AI literature review overlap is less than 6%, indicating AI does not yet reproduce expert search competence.
- [Finding 2] Fabricated references constitute only 3 % of AI outputs, while real papers with metadata errors account for 64 % of the AI‑generated list.
- [Finding 3] The 2026 model ChatGPT Pro 5.5 produces zero fabricated or metadata‑mismatched references in a single‑project test.

## Methodology
The authors designed eight distinct research projects in physics, astrophysics, and cosmology, each with a clear background and goal. Human experts performed literature reviews manually, while AI prompters used the same prompts to generate reference lists. The two sets of references were compared for overlap, and AI outputs were classified as either fabricated or real papers with metadata mismatches.

## Results
The human‑AI comparison yielded an overlap of less than 6 %. Among AI‑generated references, 3 % were fabrications, and the remaining 97 % were actual papers; however, 64 % of those contained at least one incorrect field (title, author, year, journal, DOI, or link). The single‑project test with ChatGPT Pro 5.5 produced no fabricated references and no metadata mismatches.

## Significance
These findings highlight the current limitations of AI in scientific literature retrieval while demonstrating that newer models can reduce errors dramatically, suggesting a future where AI complements rather than replaces human expertise.
