## Summary  
The paper introduces Temporal‑Causal Unity (TCU), a framework that links the philosophical claim “time is the ordered unfolding of causal change” to an operational model of collective dynamics, separating an interpretive thesis about becoming from two measurable components: a nonnegative causal‑progress coordinate τ(t) and a stochastic network description. It argues that synchronization in a noisy all‑to‑all Kuramoto system depends on drift width rather than a universal threshold, and it distinguishes consensus (first‑harmonic order parameter) from bipolar polarization (second‑harmonic order parameter). The authors provide theoretical derivations, illustrative numerical scenarios, and an out‑of‑sample protocol for comparing causal‑progress with chronological time.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A causal‑progress coordinate τ(t)=∫₀ᵗλ(s|𝕋ₛ) ds defines a nonnegative measure of change that is independent of the outcomes it produces.  
- [Finding 2] The synchronization threshold for an all‑to‑all noisy Kuramoto model with Lorentzian drift Δ is K_c = 2(Δ+D), not a constant, and this threshold collapses the causal‑clock curve as Δ→0.  
- [Finding 3] First‑ and second‑harmonic order parameters separate consensus dynamics from bipolar polarization, offering an operational diagnostic for collective states.

## Methodology  
The authors construct a theoretical model that combines process philosophy with stochastic network theory. Agents possess an orientation phase and activation amplitude; weighted interactions generate heterogeneous drift, external input, anchoring effects, and diffusion. The evolution of τ(t) is derived analytically, and the order parameters are computed from Fourier analysis of the phase field. Numerical simulations illustrate the collapse of the causal‑clock curve under Lorentzian drift and demonstrate the distinct behavior of consensus versus polarization.

## Results  
The model yields explicit formulas for τ(t), K_c, and the harmonic order parameters. As the Lorentzian drift width Δ decreases, K_c approaches 2D, causing the cumulative causal‑progress curve to flatten and converge. The first‑harmonic order parameter reaches a maximum at consensus, while the second‑harmonic peaks only in bipolar polarization. Six historical episodes are treated as scenario probes rather than empirical data points.

## Significance  
TCU bridges process ontology with complex‑system modeling by offering a falsifiable framework that distinguishes time from causation and provides an out‑of‑sample protocol for comparing theoretical models. It clarifies the role of causal progress in collective dynamics, improves diagnostic tools (consensus vs polarization), and supplies a principled bridge between philosophical insight and quantitative simulation.
