## Summary  
This study investigates how the public perceives automated decision‑making (ADM) in healthcare, focusing on three dimensions: helpfulness, riskiness, and fairness. Using a large longitudinal survey panel of 3,915 respondents, the authors employ structural equation modeling to test which personal factors shape these perceptions. The contribution is that the research clarifies that trust in clinicians outweighs trust in the technology itself when gauging public attitudes toward AI‑driven care decisions.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Perceived helpfulness of ADM is most strongly linked to respondents’ familiarity with different forms of AI, higher confidence that clinicians can distinguish AI from human content, and active use of conversational health agents.  
- [Finding 2] Perceived risk of ADM decreases when people use conversational agents but rises with greater AI familiarity and reliance on traditional digital health sources such as websites or apps.  
- [Finding 3] Fairness judgments are dominated by confidence in clinician ability to handle AI, with only modest positive associations from AI literacy, familiarity, and conversational‑agent use.

## Methodology  
The authors analyzed data collected over time from a longitudinal survey panel that now includes 3,915 participants. They treated perceived helpfulness, risk, and fairness as dependent constructs and incorporated five exogenous variables: (i) AI literacy, (ii) familiarity with various AI types, (iii) confidence in clinicians’ ability to detect AI‑generated content, (iv) use of conversational agents for health information, and (v) reliance on conventional digital health sources. Structural equation modeling was used to estimate the causal pathways among these variables.

## Results  
Greater familiarity with AI and higher confidence that clinicians can identify AI output predicted a more positive view of ADM as helpful. Conversely, frequent use of conversational agents correlated with lower perceived risk, while greater reliance on traditional digital health sources and higher AI familiarity were linked to higher perceived risk. Fairness perception was most robustly driven by confidence in clinician competence; AI literacy, familiarity, and conversational‑agent use added only small positive effects.

## Significance  
Understanding these perceptual drivers is crucial for policymakers and healthcare providers aiming to foster public acceptance of AI tools. If trust in clinicians can be maintained while improving transparency about AI use, the barriers to adoption may diminish, ultimately enhancing patient safety and equitable care delivery.
