## Summary  
The paper introduces a human‑in‑the‑loop artificial intelligence agent designed to automatically assemble evidence and draft one‑sentence Translational Science Benefits Model (TSBM) impact summaries for Clinical and Translational Science Award scholars, thereby reducing the time required for manual documentation. The tool was tested in the real‑world workflow of a CTSA hub that serves ten career‑development scholars, producing 507 evidence findings that were then reviewed by two independent staff members. Both reviewers agreed on accept or edit actions for 81.7 % of the drafts, indicating high usability while preserving scholarly rigor. By delegating drafting to AI and focusing staff on review, the system makes cohort‑scale impact reporting feasible.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The AI agent achieves an 81.7 % unanimous usable rate (accept or edit) across all 507 findings generated for ten scholars.  
- [Finding 2] Review staff spent a median of 14 minutes per scholar, which corresponds to roughly half the time saved compared with the original 15‑hour manual assembly estimate.  
- [Finding 3] The agent’s recall matches human search performance and uncovers about one third of findings that fall outside routine scholarly categories.

## Methodology  
The authors constructed an AI agent that ingests data from multiple research platforms, extracts relevant evidence, and synthesizes it into concise TSBM impact statements. These drafts are presented to two independent reviewers who independently code each finding as accept, edit, or reject. The workflow follows a human‑in‑the‑loop paradigm: the AI provides first‑pass output while humans retain final authority over usability.

## Results  
The evaluation yielded 81.7 % of findings deemed usable (accept or edited), with reviewers spending an average of 14 minutes per scholar—significantly less than the estimated 15 hours required for manual assembly. Inter‑rater agreement on the usable versus reject decision was moderate, measured by Cohen’s kappa of 0.43. A profile discovery study confirmed that the agent’s recall is comparable to human search capabilities and that roughly a third of the reviewed findings belong to non‑scholarly domains typically missed by standard processes. Reviewers rated synthesis accuracy at 4.5/5 and usefulness at 4.8/5 on a five‑point scale.

## Significance  
By automating evidence aggregation and drafting, the AI agent frees CTSA staff from repetitive data collection and writing tasks, enabling rapid, scalable impact reporting across cohorts of scholars. This shift not only reduces administrative burden but also improves the consistency and completeness of translational science documentation, supporting broader institutional goals for research impact tracking.
