## Summary  
The paper systematically evaluates off‑the‑shelf large language models (LLMs) against traditional privacy policy analysis tools to determine whether LLMs can replace specialized software for a range of functionalities and tasks without further engineering or domain‑specific training. It compares two state‑of‑the‑art LLMs—GPT‑5.2 and Gemini‑2.5—in six representative functions (contradiction detection, regulatory compliance analysis, privacy policy summarization/aggregation, structured data extraction via tuples, Semantic Role Labeling, manual labeling) on a custom dataset of ten privacy policies. The evaluation shows that LLMs consistently match or exceed the capabilities of existing tools across all evaluated tasks.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Off‑the‑shelf LLMs can perform all six functionalities (contradiction detection, regulatory compliance analysis, summarization/aggregation, structured data extraction using tuples, SRL, manual privacy policy labeling) without additional training.  
- Finding 2: LLM performance on manual labeling of first‑party collection entities reaches an average precision of 81.8 % and recall of 70.9 %, while for third‑party sharing entities it achieves higher precision (91.4 %) and similar recall (70.8 %) compared to the OPP‑115 dataset.  
- Finding 3: Across all tasks, LLM outputs match or surpass those of traditional tools, indicating that LLMs can replace specialized privacy analysis software.

## Methodology  
The authors constructed a custom dataset containing ten diverse privacy policies spanning multiple domains and functionalities. They defined six representative tools covering three major functionalities (contradiction detection, regulatory compliance analysis, summarization/aggregation) and three intermediate tasks (structured data extraction via tuples, Semantic Role Labeling, manual labeling). The evaluation involved prompting GPT‑5.2 and Gemini‑2.5 in various configurations to execute each task directly on the dataset, measuring their outputs against ground truth generated by the tools.

## Results  
LLMs outperformed traditional tools on all tasks. For first‑party collection labeling, LLMs achieved 81.8 % precision and 70.9 % recall; for third‑party sharing labeling, they reached 91.4 % precision and 70.8 % recall—both higher than the benchmark OPP‑115 dataset. Structured extraction using tuples also produced comparable or better results from LLMs. SRL and summarization tasks likewise showed LLM superiority.

## Significance  
This work demonstrates that modern LLMs can replace specialized privacy analysis tools for a broad range of functionalities, reducing reliance on domain‑specific software, accelerating compliance checks, and enabling flexible policy analysis pipelines without retraining models.
