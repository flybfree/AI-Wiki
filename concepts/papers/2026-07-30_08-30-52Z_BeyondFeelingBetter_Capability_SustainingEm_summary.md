## Summary  
The paper proposes a new longitudinal research paradigm called capability‑sustaining emotional dialogue (CSED) that moves beyond relief‑oriented support toward sustaining users’ capacities for emotion regulation, coping, self‑endorsed decisions, and social connection across interaction cycles. It critiques existing emotional‑dialogue literature for focusing on short‑term empathy or support, lacking evaluation of long‑term capability outcomes.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A PRISMA‑ScR audit of 60 system‑building papers shows that only 43 % include capability‑relevant functions and none evaluate longitudinal outcomes.  
- [Finding 2] In a corpus of 300 ESConv supporter turns, generic suggestions dominate (22 %), while capability‑focused functions appear in 43 %, indicating a gap between design intent and realized support.  
- [Finding 3] The authors release a protocol for extending the audit to model behavior, linking latent user capability to six design commitments, four evaluation timescales, and lifecycle constraints.

## Methodology  
The authors conducted a systematic literature‑and‑corpus audit using PRISMA‑ScR guidelines, categorizing papers into relief‑oriented versus capability‑sustaining goals, and performed a functional analysis of supporter turns to quantify the prevalence of capability‑relevant functions versus generic suggestions. This dual approach—audit of scholarly work and empirical inspection of dialogue data—provides a comprehensive view of current practice.

## Results  
Out of 60 system‑building papers, 95 % pursue relief‑oriented goals; none assess capability or longitudinal outcomes, and only one considers dependency, autonomy, or termination risk. Functional analysis shows 43.0 % of supporter turns involve capability‑relevant functions (e.g., self‑efficacy support), 22.0 % generic suggestions, 4.0 % reappraisal, 6.7 % self‑efficacy support, and 0.3 % boundary behavior.

## Significance  
This work highlights a critical gap in emotional dialogue research: the field prioritizes immediate relief over sustained capability building, leading to interventions that may fail long‑term. By introducing CSED as a longitudinal paradigm and releasing an audit protocol, it enables rigorous evaluation across data, policy design, training, evaluation, and governance.
