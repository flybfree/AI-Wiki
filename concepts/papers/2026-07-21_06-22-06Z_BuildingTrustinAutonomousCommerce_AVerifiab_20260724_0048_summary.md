## Summary  
The paper proposes a verifiable global event timeline to provide tamper‑evident, interoperable auditability for agentic commerce protocols like AP2 and ACP. It introduces cryptographic mechanisms that enforce deterministic serialization, batch formation, Merkle commitments, and blockchain anchoring. These components enable AI‑ready fraud intelligence by binding risk labels to provably anchored evidence. The solution also includes a dataset lineage model for reproducible AI training pipelines.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A verifiable global event timeline using canonical schemas, deterministic batching, Merkle append‑only commitments, and blockchain anchoring.  
- [Finding 2] A cryptographically signed fraud marker that links risk labels to evidence via an unforgeable provenance chain.  
- [Finding 3] A dataset lineage model providing tamper‑evident reproducibility for AI training pipelines.  

## Methodology  
The authors tackled the lack of interoperability and temporal ordering in agentic commerce by designing four core components. First, they defined canonical event schemas that guarantee deterministic serialization across heterogeneous domains. Second, they implemented deterministic batch formation to order events without synchronized clocks. Third, they built Merkle‑based append‑only trees with logarithmic inclusion proofs for efficient verification. Fourth, they anchored the timeline on a blockchain to create a tamper‑evident temporal backbone. The fraud marker is generated by signing evidence hashes and linking them to risk labels, while the lineage model records dataset versions and provenance.  

## Results  
In prototype testing, Merkle tree construction of 50,000 events took 47 milliseconds; end‑to‑end verification completed in under 0.013 ms regardless of batch size. Inclusion proof sizes grew logarithmically from 320 bytes at 1,000 events to 512 bytes at 50,000 events. Verification using Merkle trees outperformed linear scans by a factor of 14.4 at the largest scale.  

## Significance  
This work bridges trust gaps in autonomous commerce with AI‑ready fraud detection, enabling secure, auditable transaction histories and reproducible AI training data pipelines. By providing logarithmic‑cost proofs and fast verification, it scales to millions of events while maintaining low latency, supporting real‑time risk assessment without compromising integrity.
