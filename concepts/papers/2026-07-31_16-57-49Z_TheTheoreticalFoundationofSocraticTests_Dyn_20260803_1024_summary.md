## Summary
This paper introduces the theoretical framework for "Socratic Tests," a novel approach to automated, computer-mediated conversational assessment that seeks to overcome the limitations of traditional static and oral examinations. By integrating Dynamic Assessment principles with multimodal workspaces and advanced taxonomic structures like Bloom’s and SOLO, the proposed system aims to map a student's cognitive boundaries in real-time rather than merely measuring final outcomes. The authors argue that this method reduces construct-irrelevant variance caused by anxiety and power imbalances while providing diagnostic feedback that encourages ambition rather than penalizing it. Ultimately, the work establishes a formalized architecture for graduated scaffolding and non-compensatory grading to ensure reliable measurement of mastery through human-AI alignment.

## Key Contributions
- The formalization of Socratic Tests as a dynamic assessment tool that quantifies the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) through graduated scaffolding within an automated conversational interface.
- The development of a non-compensatory, additive grading architecture that prioritizes demonstrated mastery over penalty-based deduction, thereby aligning assessment outcomes with genuine learning capabilities.
- The integration of Bloom’s Taxonomy for real-time proctoring and the SOLO Taxonomy for structural evaluation, creating a robust framework for evaluating complex cognitive processes in multimodal environments.

## Methodology
The authors approach this problem through theoretical synthesis rather than empirical experimentation, drawing upon established educational psychology frameworks to construct a new assessment model. They analyze the deficits of traditional static assessments, which rely on subtractive grading, and the sociological issues inherent in face-to-face oral exams, such as performative anxiety and hierarchical power dynamics. To address these, they propose a computer-mediated system that utilizes multimodal workspaces to allow students to demonstrate knowledge through various media. The methodology involves mapping student responses against Bloom’s Taxonomy to assess cognitive depth during the interaction and using SOLO Taxonomy to evaluate the structural complexity of their answers. Furthermore, the authors detail how graduated scaffolding techniques can be algorithmically applied to probe the limits of a student's understanding, effectively measuring their ZPD without the pressure of traditional high-stakes testing environments.

## Results
As a theoretical paper, the primary result is the successful construction of a coherent mathematical and pedagogical model for Socratic Tests. The framework demonstrates how dynamic interaction can replace static snapshots of knowledge, allowing for a more accurate depiction of a learner's potential. By defining specific algorithms for scaffolding and evaluation, the authors show that it is possible to automate the diagnostic process previously reserved for skilled human examiners. The proposed architecture ensures that assessment is additive rather than subtractive, meaning points are awarded for demonstrated competencies rather than deducted for errors, leading to a more positive and accurate representation of student ability.

## Significance
This research matters because it addresses critical flaws in current educational assessment practices, particularly regarding equity, anxiety, and the accuracy of measuring true cognitive capacity. By removing the sociological power imbalances and performative pressures of traditional oral exams, Socratic Tests offer a more inclusive and psychologically safe environment for evaluation. Furthermore, by automating dynamic assessment, it scales personalized diagnostic feedback to larger populations, potentially transforming how higher education and professional certification measure mastery. This shift from deficit-based to mastery-based evaluation aligns educational measurement with modern understandings of learning as a developmental process rather than a fixed state.
