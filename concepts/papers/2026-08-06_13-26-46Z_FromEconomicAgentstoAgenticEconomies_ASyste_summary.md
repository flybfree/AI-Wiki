## Summary  
The paper proposes a systems‑level blueprint for building Economic World Models (EWMs) that simulate economies from within by modeling heterogeneous agents, their beliefs, actions, and the market and institutional mechanisms through which those interactions generate aggregate outcomes. It organizes EWM systems into a six‑tier capability ladder that spans fixed rule‑based worlds up to adaptive LLM‑driven agents, self‑evolving agents, evolving institutions, and sim‑to‑real economic twins aligned with real observations. The authors highlight that current research remains concentrated in the lower tiers while higher‑level capabilities such as endogenous institution evolution and persistent empirical alignment are rare. By translating this agenda into an implementation roadmap, they aim to accelerate the creation of high‑fidelity sandboxes for decision‑makers and as training, planning, evaluation, and safety substrates for AI agents.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The authors introduce a six‑level capability ladder that categorizes EWM systems from fixed rule‑based agent worlds to adaptive LLM‑driven agents, self‑evolving agents, evolving institutional worlds, and sim‑to‑real economic twins.  
- [Finding 2] A systematic literature survey shows that existing work is overwhelmingly focused on the first two levels, with only a small fraction addressing higher tiers such as self‑evolution or endogenous institutions.  
- [Finding 3] The paper releases an implementation blueprint and curated resource list to guide future research toward higher‑level EWM capabilities.

## Methodology  
The authors performed a systematic literature survey across all six capability levels, classifying each study by its agent heterogeneity, belief representation, interaction mechanisms, institutional evolution, and degree of empirical alignment. They mapped these findings onto the implementation ladder to identify gaps and propose concrete steps for advancing each tier. The analysis combined qualitative categorization with quantitative counts of papers per level.

## Results  
The survey revealed that roughly 15 % of surveyed papers address self‑evolving agents or endogenous institutions, and none provide validated economic mechanisms beyond simple equilibrium models. Moreover, only a handful of works implement sim‑to‑real twins that are calibrated to real‑world data. The blueprint therefore outlines incremental milestones: (1) fixed rule‑based agent worlds, (2) adaptive LLM agents with belief updating, (3) self‑evolving agents with institutional feedback loops, (4) evolving institutions, and (5) fully aligned sim‑to‑real twins.

## Significance  
This matters because it enables realistic sandbox environments for policymakers to test macroeconomic scenarios, provides AI safety substrates that can expose emergent risks from self‑aligned agents, and supports training programs where AI systems learn economic dynamics in a controlled generative setting. By bridging theory and practice, the blueprint could transform abstract economic models into actionable tools.
