## Summary  
Memory‑augmented LLM agents are susceptible to memory injection attacks, where attackers can poison the agent’s stored memories and cause it to deviate from its intended task. Existing defenses either demand heavy computational resources or generate redundant information in multi‑turn interactions. To overcome these trade‑offs, the authors introduce MIND—a lightweight, intent‑aware Information Bottleneck (IB) framework that filters malicious memories while preserving useful cross‑turn attack signals. The proposed method extracts compact intent–behavior representations and employs a simple detector to flag poisoned entries without invoking repeated LLM audits. Extensive experiments demonstrate that MIND markedly lowers attack success rates while maintaining the agent’s accuracy and inference speed.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Benign and poisoned trajectories exhibit distinguishable relationships between the initial user intent and subsequent behavior, enabling a principled separation of useful from harmful information.  
- [Finding 2] MIND employs an intent‑aware Information Bottleneck to compress these representations, preserving intent‑relevant cross‑turn attack signals while discarding task‑irrelevant or repetitive data.  
- [Finding 3] A lightweight detector is trained on the distilled representations to identify malicious memories, thereby mitigating information redundancy without costly repeated audits.

## Methodology  
The authors first collect benign and poisoned trajectories from a benchmark dataset (ReAct‑StrategyQA). They then apply an intent‑aware Information Bottleneck that jointly models the user’s original goal and the agent’s turn‑level outputs, producing a compact latent vector. This bottleneck is designed to retain any cross‑turn signals that indicate a memory injection attempt while suppressing noise generated by benign interactions. A second stage introduces a lightweight classifier that learns to distinguish between benign and poisoned latent vectors based on their deviation from the intended behavior trajectory. The final defense injects only the safe memories into the agent’s memory bank, effectively neutralizing the attack.

## Results  
Experiments on ReAct‑StrategyQA show that MIND reduces mean ASR‑r (average success rate) by 55.4% and mean ASR‑a (average accuracy drop) by 55.3%, compared with the undefended agent. Crucially, the defended system matches the baseline in overall task accuracy and inference latency, confirming that MIND’s filtering is effective yet non‑intrusive. Ablation studies confirm that removing either the intent‑aware IB or the detector degrades performance, underscoring their necessity.

## Significance  
MIND addresses a critical security gap in memory‑augmented LLM agents by providing an efficient, intent‑sensitive defense mechanism. By eliminating redundant and harmful information without sacrificing computational cost, it enables real‑time deployment of secure agents across diverse multi‑turn scenarios. This work advances the field toward practical, scalable defenses that preserve both safety and performance.
