## Summary
The paper addresses the critical challenge of long-term forecasting for large-scale multivariate time series generated by heterogeneous IoT sensors, where accurate predictions are essential for resource scheduling and predictive maintenance. While recent foundation models demonstrate strong generalization capabilities, they suffer from static parametric knowledge and lack dynamic access to external historical patterns during inference. To overcome these limitations, the authors propose CrossRAG, a novel retrieval-augmented forecasting framework designed to integrate shape-aware memory with future-consistent contrastive learning. This approach aims to retrieve informative historical references that are not only similar in shape but also consistent with future outcomes, thereby enhancing forecast accuracy across diverse data sources.

## Key Contributions
- The introduction of Shape-Aware Memory (SAM) combined with RevIN normalization, which enables magnitude-robust retrieval by focusing on the structural shape of time series rather than absolute values, effectively handling heterogeneity across different sensor types.
- The development of Future-Consistent Contrastive (FCC) learning, a mechanism that distinguishes informative historical references from hard negatives—specifically those cases where historical patterns are similar but future trajectories diverge significantly.
- The design of Cross-Attention Temporal Fusion (CATF), a module that seamlessly fuses retrieved historical-future reference pairs into the backbone model’s representations at the feature level, allowing for dynamic knowledge integration during inference.

## Methodology
The authors tackle the problem by first identifying the limitations of existing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) applications in time series forecasting, particularly regarding magnitude variations and the mismatch between historical similarity and future consistency. To address these issues, they construct a framework that begins with Shape-Aware Memory (SAM). This component utilizes RevIN normalization to standardize data magnitudes, allowing the system to retrieve references based on their temporal shape rather than absolute scale, which is crucial for heterogeneous IoT data. Next, the methodology incorporates Future-Consistent Contrastive (FCC) learning. This step ensures that the retrieved historical patterns are not just visually similar in the past but also predictive of the future by contrasting informative references against hard negatives. Finally, the Cross-Attention Temporal Fusion (CATF) module is employed to integrate these retrieved pairs into the forecasting backbone. By operating at the representation level, CATF allows the model to dynamically attend to relevant historical contexts without altering the core parametric structure, effectively bridging the gap between static models and dynamic external knowledge.

## Results
Extensive experiments conducted on seven public benchmarks demonstrate that CrossRAG consistently outperforms both parametric-only baselines and existing retrieval-augmented forecasting methods. The results indicate significant improvements in long-term forecasting accuracy, validating the effectiveness of combining shape-aware retrieval with future-consistent learning. The framework shows robustness across different datasets, confirming its ability to handle the complexities of heterogeneous IoT sensor data.

## Significance
This research is significant because it provides a practical solution for improving the reliability of predictive maintenance and resource scheduling in industrial IoT environments. By enabling dynamic access to relevant historical patterns during inference, CrossRAG enhances the adaptability of time series foundation models, making them more suitable for real-world applications where data distributions vary widely.
