## Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of reliably verifying candidate answers generated by Large Language Models (LLMs) in mathematical problem-solving contexts. The authors identify that existing methods, which rely on natural language reflection or direct program generation, suffer from either computational unreliability or premature coupling of modeling and implementation. To resolve this, they propose Agentic Mathematical Tool-Flow Verification (AMTFV), a novel framework that decouples verification logic from concrete execution through an interrupt-execute-resume interface known as Mathematical Tool Flow (MTF). By leveraging a dedicated mathematical toolbox agent for exact computation, AMTFV enables precise adjudication and iterative self-correction of model outputs.

## Key Contributions
- **Decoupled Verification Architecture**: The introduction of the Mathematical Tool Flow (MTF) interface effectively separates high-level mathematical modeling from low-level code execution, allowing for modular and reliable verification processes.
- **Agentic Self-Correction Mechanism**: AMTFV facilitates a dynamic loop where verification agents construct workflows, dispatch exact computational requests to a toolbox agent, and utilize the resulting precise outputs to revise both answers and verification strategies.
- **Superior Empirical Performance**: Extensive evaluations demonstrate that AMTFV significantly outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across multiple LLM configurations, with notable accuracy gains particularly on problems requiring medium to high verification complexity.

## Methodology
The authors developed AMTFV as an agentic system comprising two primary components: a verification agent and a mathematical toolbox agent. The verification agent is responsible for constructing a verification workflow and encoding specific mathematical objects and computational intents into MTF requests. These requests are sent to the mathematical toolbox agent, which parses the intent, generates executable code calls, and dispatches them to a backend for exact computation. This architecture ensures that complex calculations are handled with precision rather than relying on the LLM's inherent probabilistic reasoning. The outputs from the toolbox are then fed back to the verification agent to adjudicate candidate answers, revise incorrect responses, or update the verification workflow itself. This iterative process allows the system to correct errors and refine its approach dynamically.

## Results
The study evaluated AMTFV on five challenging mathematical reasoning datasets using seven model configurations from prominent providers including DeepSeek, GPT, and Gemini. The experimental results indicate that AMTFV consistently outperforms representative baselines overall. Specifically, under individual model configurations, AMTFV improved average accuracy by up to 8.3 percentage points compared to the strongest baseline. The performance gains were particularly pronounced for samples exhibiting medium and high verification complexity, suggesting that the method is especially effective for difficult problems where exact computation is crucial.

## Significance
This research matters because it tackles a fundamental bottleneck in deploying LLMs for rigorous mathematical tasks: the lack of reliable self-verification. By decoupling logic from execution, AMTFV provides a robust pathway to enhance trustworthiness and accuracy in AI-driven problem solving. This approach sets a new standard for agentic systems that require high precision, offering a scalable solution for educational tools, automated tutoring, and scientific computing assistance where error tolerance is minimal.
