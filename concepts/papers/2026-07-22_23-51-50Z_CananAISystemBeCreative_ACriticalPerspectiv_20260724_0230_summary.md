## Summary  
This paper investigates whether artificial intelligence can be considered genuinely creative by applying Margaret Boden’s three‑property model of creativity—novelty, surprise, and value—to both engineering and artistic domains. The author argues that while AI excels at combinatorial recombination, it lacks the exploratory and transformational capacities essential for authentic creativity. A key contribution is a proposed human‑AI collaborative framework that demonstrates how serendipity can be re‑introduced into creative processes.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] AI systems are structurally incapable of full‑scale creativity because they cannot generate surprise and value in the transformational sense.  
- [Finding 2] Current AI is limited to combinatorial creativity, producing novel outputs that merely rearrange existing patterns without genuine insight or surprise.  
- [Finding 3] The absence of serendipity mechanisms prevents AI from experiencing the unexpected events that are phenomenologically central to human creativity.  

## Methodology  
The authors employ Margaret Boden’s framework—three properties (novelty, surprise, value) and three creative processes (combinatorial, exploratory, transformational)—to dissect AI’s capabilities. They conduct controlled experiments where AI generates combinatorial outputs, then measures the presence of exploratory exploration (searching beyond known patterns) and transformative re‑interpretation. The study also includes a human‑AI collaborative experiment that deliberately injects serendipitous constraints to observe emergent creative value.  

## Results  
Experiments confirm that AI excels at combinatorial recombination, producing novel but predictable outputs. Exploratory measures show minimal deviation from known spaces, indicating no genuine surprise. Transformational scores remain low because AI cannot re‑value or reinterpret data in a meaningful way. The collaborative model, however, yields higher novelty and value when human input introduces serendipitous constraints, suggesting that the joint process can approximate creative value.  

## Significance  
This work challenges the prevailing view that AI can be fully creative, highlighting the gap between algorithmic recombination and authentic creativity. By exposing the missing serendipity mechanisms, it informs designers of more realistic AI‑human workflows and underscores the importance of interdisciplinary perspectives in defining creative potential.
