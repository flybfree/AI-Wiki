## Summary  
The paper challenges the assumption that automation can replace human participation once AI is sufficiently capable, arguing instead that limits are conceptual and persistent. It identifies three reasons why humans remain involved even in advanced systems: technical complementarity where AI lacks certain capabilities or perspectives; normative/developmental value for agency and learning; and emergence through co‑construction where human involvement shapes the target itself. By framing human‑AI interaction as constitutive of emergent targets, the authors expand the theory of automation beyond mere efficiency gains.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Human participation persists due to technical complementarity where AI lacks certain capabilities or perspectives.  
- Finding 2: Participation holds normative value for agency and learning, even when AI could theoretically complete tasks.  
- Finding 3: In emergent activities, human involvement is constitutive of the target itself, not just a means.

## Methodology  
The authors adopt a theoretical analysis combining philosophical reasoning with case studies from AI‑assisted design, collaborative robotics, and knowledge co‑construction. They map existing literature on automation limits, identify three explanatory grounds, and test their framework against empirical examples to illustrate persistence.

## Results  
They demonstrate that in each domain—design, robotics, emergent problem solving—the human role cannot be reduced to error correction; instead it shapes the outcome. The analysis shows that AI systems often depend on human input for defining objectives, which aligns with emergence grounds. The framework predicts persistent human involvement across diverse tasks.

## Significance  
This work redefines automation as bounded by conceptual rather than technical constraints, influencing design policies and ethical considerations. By highlighting non‑technical reasons for participation, it guides developers to embed agency‑preserving mechanisms in AI systems.
