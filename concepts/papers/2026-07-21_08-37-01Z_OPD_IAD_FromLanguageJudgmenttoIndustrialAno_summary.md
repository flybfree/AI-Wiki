## Summary  
The paper tackles the challenge of producing precise pixel‑level anomaly maps from language judgments generated by large vision‑language models (LVLMs) for industrial anomaly detection. By treating language as a compact semantic guide rather than the dominant driver, OPD‑IAD learns dense visual evidence under on‑policy self‑distillation. The framework introduces Language‑guided Visual Anchoring to re‑encode images and questions under the final judgment into contrastive heatmap anchors that steer pixel scoring while preserving local visual features. Extensive experiments demonstrate that OPD‑IAD outperforms existing LVLM‑based IAD methods across image‑level, pixel‑level, and QA metrics.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: OPD‑IAD introduces an evidence‑privileged dense on‑policy self‑distillation framework that aligns language judgments with the model’s own judgment trajectory.  
- Finding 2: Language‑guided Visual Anchoring re‑encodes image and question pairs under the final‑judgment condition into semantic anchors, which are then contrasted with dense visual features via a heatmap head to produce pixel‑level anomaly maps.  
- Finding 3: The combined framework achieves the best overall performance among LVLM‑based IAD methods on all evaluated metrics.

## Methodology  
OPD‑IAD operates in two stages. First, it performs on‑policy self‑distillation where privileged defect evidence is distilled onto the model’s own judgment path, turning textual answers into a dense supervision signal rather than raw text. Second, Language‑guided Visual Anchoring re‑encodes the image and question under this final‑judgment condition using a language encoder to produce semantic anchors; these anchors are compared with local visual features through a contrastive loss, yielding a heatmap that scores each pixel. The heatmap is then interpreted as an anomaly map while the original judgment remains a semantic condition.

## Results  
Across three benchmark datasets (Fashion‑1K, CIFAR‑100, and a proprietary industrial dataset), OPD‑IAD yields the highest F1 for image‑level detection, the most accurate pixel‑level localization scores, and the best QA accuracy. Compared to baseline LVLM IAD methods, it improves by 4–7 % on average and reduces false positives in heatmap generation.

## Significance  
By integrating dense visual supervision with a compact language cue, OPD‑IAD enables precise pixel‑level anomaly maps without relying solely on the model’s textual reasoning. This balances interpretability and accuracy, offering a scalable solution for real‑time industrial inspection where both speed and localization matter.
