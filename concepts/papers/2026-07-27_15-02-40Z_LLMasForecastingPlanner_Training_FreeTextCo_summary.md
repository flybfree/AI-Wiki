## Summary  
The paper introduces a training‑free framework called \rc{} that combines the strengths of time‑series foundation models (TSFMs) and large language models (LLMs) to perform text‑conditioned forecasting. Instead of asking an LLM to directly generate numeric values, it treats forecasting as a planning problem where a frozen TSFM simulates possible numerical trajectories while an LLM acts as a policy and value function. The authors implement this plan using Monte Carlo tree search with a Ranker‑based policy and a Judge‑based value function. Experiments demonstrate that the approach consistently outperforms baseline methods across multiple model choices.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: The authors propose a training‑free integration of TSFMs and LLMs via a planning framework that separates simulation (TSFM) from decision making (LLM).  
- Finding 2: They employ Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) where the policy is generated by an LLM (Ranker) and the value function is evaluated by another LLM (Judge).  
- Finding 3: The method yields consistent improvements on benchmark tasks across different TSFM backbones, LLMs, and forecasting horizons.

## Methodology  
The authors model forecasting as a sequential planning problem over the forecast horizon. A frozen TSFM generates candidate numerical continuations, simulating possible future states. An LLM‑based Ranker policy selects promising trajectories by ranking them according to their relevance to the textual context, while a second LLM serves as a Judge that scores completed trajectories against the given constraints and objectives. The two LLMs are used without any fine‑tuning or joint training; they operate independently on the generated trajectories.

## Results  
Experiments were conducted on the Context‑is‑Key and Time‑MMD datasets using both Chronos and TimesFM TSFM backbones together with four different LLMs. The \rc{} framework achieved statistically significant gains in mean absolute error and prediction accuracy compared to baseline models that either rely solely on the TSFM or only on the LLM. Improvements were observed across all model configurations, indicating robustness of the approach.

## Significance  
This work bridges the modality gap between numerical time‑series data and natural language context without retraining either component, enabling practical deployment in real‑world forecasting where external events must be incorporated. By treating forecasting as a planning problem, \rc{} showcases how LLM reasoning can complement strong statistical models, opening avenues for more interpretable and adaptable predictive systems.

## Related Concepts  
- Time‑series foundation models (TSFMs) such as Chronos and TimesFM.  
- Large language models (LLMs) used as policy (Ranker) and value function (Judge).  
- Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) for sequential decision making.  
- Text‑conditioned forecasting, where textual context influences numerical predictions.  
- Training‑free integration, meaning no joint optimization of the two models is required.
