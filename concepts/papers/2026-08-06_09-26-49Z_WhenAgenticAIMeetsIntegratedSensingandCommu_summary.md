## Summary  
The paper proposes a unified framework for understanding agentic AI within Integrated Sensing and Communication, termed AISAC. It surveys recent advances across sensing, communication, learning, and network management to map them onto a six‑stage closed‑loop system and five levels of agentic maturity. By evaluating existing systems against nine criteria, it reveals gaps in demonstrated agentic capability. The authors identify open challenges for future research.

## Key Contributions  
- Establishes the AISAC framework with six stages and five agentic maturity levels.  
- Demonstrates that current AI‑driven ISAC systems typically satisfy only one or two of nine agentic evaluation criteria, exposing a maturity gap.  
- Highlights key open challenges: physical‑to‑semantic grounding, predictive world models, real‑time PHY interaction, safe tool use, heterogeneous multi‑agent collaboration, benchmarking, and resource‑efficient autonomy.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by first reviewing and categorizing literature on multimodal sensing, large language models, reinforcement learning, federated learning, RIS control, UAV/vehicular networks, and AI‑native network management. They then mapped each study onto a six‑stage closed‑loop framework (observation → contextualization → reasoning/prediction → planning/orchestration → execution/collaboration → feedback/resilience) and five agentic maturity levels, using an audit of nine evaluation criteria to assess reported capabilities.

## Results  
The survey reveals that most AI‑enhanced ISAC systems exhibit limited closed‑loop behavior, often functioning only at the physical layer or isolated sensing modules. The audit shows a mean satisfaction score of 1.4 out of 9 criteria per system, confirming the identified maturity gap. Additionally, while some works achieve advanced perception (e.g., multimodal LLMs) and communication (RIS), few integrate these into coordinated planning and feedback loops.

## Significance  
This work matters because it clarifies where AI truly becomes agentic in ISAC environments, guiding researchers to prioritize integration of sensing, reasoning, and execution. By exposing the gap between claimed and demonstrated capabilities, it helps allocate resources toward solving critical challenges such as real‑time PHY interaction and safe multi‑agent tool use.
