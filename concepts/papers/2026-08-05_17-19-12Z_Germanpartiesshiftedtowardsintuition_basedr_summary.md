## Summary  
This paper investigates how German political elites’ rhetorical strategies evolve in response to the rise of populist forces, focusing on a shift from evidence‑based to intuition‑driven language. By analysing a massive corpus of 4.5 million tweets and 59 170 parliamentary speeches (2015–2025), the authors demonstrate that intuition‑based rhetoric has become more prevalent across both media channels. The study links these changes directly to the far‑right Alternative for Germany’s (AfD) parliamentary breakthrough in 2017, showing a pronounced decline in evidence‑minus‑intuition scores among right‑leaning actors. These findings provide empirical evidence that elite discourse can be reshaped by the visibility of extremist parties.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Intuition‑based language has become more prominent than evidence‑based language in both tweets and speeches.  
- Finding 2: Right‑leaning actors consistently exhibit the lowest Evidence Minus Intuition (EMI) scores, indicating a preference for intuition over empirical justification.  
- Finding 3: The AfD’s parliamentary entry correlates with sharp downward shifts in EMI across the broader chamber.

## Methodology  
The authors employ a validated distributed dictionary representation to automatically tag utterances as either evidence‑based or intuition‑based. They compute an Evidence Minus Intuition (EMI) index for each speaker, which quantifies the relative weight of empirical justification versus intuitive appeal. The analysis spans two data streams—social media tweets and formal parliamentary speeches—to capture public and institutional discourse.

## Results  
Across both platforms, EMI scores decline after 2017, with a pronounced drop coinciding with AfD’s entry into parliament. Right‑leaning parties show the steepest declines, while centre‑left actors experience only modest changes. The overall trend suggests a systematic reorientation toward intuition‑driven rhetoric as far‑right visibility increases.

## Significance  
The results challenge the assumption that elite discourse remains anchored to factual evidence in democratic societies. By revealing how populist breakthroughs can trigger a rhetorical shift, the study highlights risks for democratic deliberation and the potential erosion of truth‑based communication when extremist parties gain prominence.
