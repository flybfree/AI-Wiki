## Summary  
This paper presents a domain‑centric survey that maps the full pipeline of face de‑identification, from physical concealment and sensor capture to digital post‑processing. By separating the work into three distinct domains—physical, sensor, and digital—the authors provide a unified overview that highlights technical advances, persistent challenges, and evaluation gaps across each stage. The contribution is both conceptual (a comprehensive taxonomy) and practical (a curated project page with datasets and code).  

## Key Contributions  
- A systematic taxonomy that delineates methodologies in the physical domain (wearable accessories), sensor‑level privacy mechanisms, and digital post‑processing algorithms.  
- Identification of evaluation shortcomings, advocating for standardized benchmarks that evaluate both privacy guarantees and downstream utility.  
- Introduction of a public project page aggregating literature, datasets, and open‑source implementations to accelerate research.  

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by conducting a literature review across three domains, extracting methodological details into a comparative matrix, and then synthesizing findings into a narrative survey. They also curated existing datasets and code repositories into a single GitHub project, thereby operationalizing the taxonomy for reproducibility.  

## Results  
The survey reveals that physical‑domain concealment techniques are limited to niche applications, sensor‑level privacy mechanisms have shown modest gains in robustness but require extensive calibration, and digital post‑processing remains dominant yet suffers from lack of standardized evaluation protocols. The authors conclude that a unified benchmark is needed to compare cross‑domain approaches fairly.  

## Significance  
The work matters because it bridges computer vision and privacy research, offering a roadmap for developers seeking to embed de‑identification at the earliest possible stage. By exposing gaps in evaluation, it promotes responsible AI practices that protect user identity while preserving data utility.
