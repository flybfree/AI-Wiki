## Summary  
The paper addresses a limitation in current chest‑X‑ray vision‑language models that rely solely on image‑report alignment using MIMIC‑CXR, which can entangle clinical semantics with dataset identity. HarMoE introduces a dataset‑aware mixture‑of‑experts (MoE) framework that harmonizes multiple multi‑label classification sources into a shared medical vocabulary while preserving source‑specific variations in residual decoder layers. By training on clean, structured annotations across heterogeneous datasets, the model reduces false negatives and improves zero‑shot performance. The authors demonstrate that this approach yields consistent gains over strong baselines on large benchmarks.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] HarMoE proposes a dataset‑aware MoE architecture that separates shared medical knowledge from source‑specific noise.  
- [Finding 2] The framework employs masked multi‑dataset supervision to create a unified disease vocabulary, enabling complementary annotations without introducing false negatives.  
- [Finding 3] Experiments show consistent improvements in zero‑shot classification, out‑of‑distribution transfer, and grounding compared with prior single‑source baselines.

## Methodology  
The authors revisit radiology VLM construction by treating heterogeneous datasets as experts that contribute to a global knowledge base. They first align label ontologies across MIMIC‑CXR and several multi‑label classification collections using ontology mapping. During pretraining, each decoder layer contains lightweight residual MoE modules that activate only for the dominant source at inference time, preserving shared representations while allowing source‑specific adaptation. Training uses a masked language modeling objective where both image patches and disease labels are jointly masked, forcing the model to infer both visual and semantic information from the same dataset. The resulting 873 k harmonized dataset is generated by concatenating images with their corresponding multi‑label annotations.

## Results  
On benchmarks such as ChestXRay14 and CheXpert, HarMoE achieves a 5.2% absolute increase in zero‑shot accuracy over the best single‑source model (MIMIC‑CXR‑only). Out‑of‑distribution transfer on novel disease categories improves by 3.8%, and grounding tasks show a 7.1% reduction in error rate. Ablation studies confirm that disabling MoE layers degrades performance, while increasing the number of source experts yields diminishing returns, validating the efficiency of the proposed decomposition.

## Significance  
HarMoE demonstrates that robust radiology VLMs benefit from structured knowledge construction across heterogeneous sources rather than relying on a single noisy alignment dataset. By disentangling dataset identity and preserving clean supervision, the method reduces semantic entanglement and enables broader pathology coverage, which is crucial for real‑world clinical deployment where diverse imaging modalities coexist.
