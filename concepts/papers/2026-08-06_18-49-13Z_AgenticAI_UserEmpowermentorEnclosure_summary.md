## Summary  
The paper investigates whether agentic AI will empower users or restrict them, arguing that the outcome depends on governance structures rather than technology alone. It proposes a depoliticization framework where technical arrangements mediate political choices about agency scope. By analyzing four mature domains—browser ad blockers, platform recommender systems, financial robo‑advisors, and email spam filtering—the study shows how these configurations shape both individual benefits and collective contestation capacity. The findings suggest that current consolidations in AI may favor enclosure over empowerment.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Agency decisions are resolved through technical/political configurations (APIs, standards) rather than user preferences.  
- Finding 2: Depoliticization can improve individual outcomes while eroding collective contestation capacity.  
- Finding 3: In agentic AI, consolidating governance may displace users' ability to influence or challenge the system.

## Methodology  
The authors employed a comparative case analysis of four established domains where similar forms of agency have emerged: browser ad blockers, platform recommender systems, financial robo‑advisors, and email spam filtering. They examined both the technical arrangements (e.g., API choices, protocol governance) and the political decisions embedded in those arrangements to identify patterns of depoliticization.

## Results  
The analysis reveals that while spam inbox quality improved markedly due to agentic AI, the ability of users to organize around spam governance collapsed—a classic case of depoliticization. Conversely, domains where intermediary institutions maintained adversarial challenge (e.g., ad blockers) saw more durable user‑aligned agency. Proprietary infrastructure and closed standard‑setting led to displacement of contestation. The findings suggest that technical consolidation in AI may similarly sacrifice collective power for individual convenience.

## Significance  
This work matters because it demonstrates that the promise of agentic AI is contingent on governance design, not merely algorithmic capability. By exposing how depoliticization can produce divergent outcomes, it calls for policies and standards that preserve participatory contestation. The insights guide developers, regulators, and users in shaping AI systems that balance empowerment with democratic accountability.
