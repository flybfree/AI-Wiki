## Summary  
The paper addresses a growing problem in endoscopic visual question answering: many state‑of‑the‑art models generate complex answers that are judged correct even though the underlying atomic components are inconsistent or incorrect. To highlight this discrepancy, the authors introduce EndoCA, a paired benchmark that measures both atomic and complex‑atomic answer consistency across 11 vision‑language models (VLMs). They then propose Atomic‑Support Reconciliation (ASR), a training‑free technique that reconciles model‑generated atomic answers with complex responses through contextual revision or selective abstention. The work demonstrates that ASR can improve paired correctness and answer accuracy without sacrificing overall performance, offering a practical consistency‑aware solution for real‑world endoscopic VQA.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] EndoCA introduces a novel benchmark that pairs complex answers with their constituent atomic answers to quantify consistency gaps in current models.  
- [Finding 2] Atomic‑Support Reconciliation (ASR) provides two training‑free mechanisms—ASR‑Revise, which revises complex answers using atomic premises, and ASR‑Selective, which allows abstention from low‑confidence cases—to reconcile inconsistencies.  
- [Finding 3] Experiments on four publicly available models show that ASR‑Revise modestly boosts paired correctness while preserving complex‑answer accuracy, and ASR‑Selective raises answered‑case accuracy by enabling selective abstention.

## Methodology  
The authors first curated EndoCA into two suites: EndoCA‑Core for compact question patterns and EndoCA‑Diagnostic to explore increasing complexity. They evaluated 11 VLMs across open, medical, endoscopy‑adapted, and closed‑source categories using standard VQA metrics. ASR is implemented without fine‑tuning; it leverages the atomic answers generated by the model as premises for revising complex responses (ASR‑Revise) or for deciding whether to answer at all (ASR‑Selective). The consistency of each pair is measured via a binary consistency score and overall accuracy.

## Results  
On EndoCA, the 11 models exhibit high complex‑answer accuracy but low atomic‑answer accuracy; paired consistency scores are markedly lower than expected. Introducing ASR‑Revise improves the paired correctness metric by an average of 4 % while keeping complex‑answer accuracy unchanged. ASR‑Selective raises answered‑case accuracy by roughly 6 % through abstention, and combined ASR (Revise + Selective) yields a 7 % overall gain in consistency without degrading primary VQA scores.

## Significance  
By exposing the hidden inconsistency between complex and atomic answers, EndoCA guides future research toward more reliable endoscopy‑specific models. ASR offers a lightweight, training‑free reconciliation strategy that can be integrated directly into existing pipelines, reducing reliance on costly fine‑tuning while improving diagnostic reliability—a crucial advancement for clinical applications.
