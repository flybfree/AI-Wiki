## Summary  
The paper investigates how the internal connection structure of a photonic reservoir influences its memory capacity and one‑step‑ahead prediction accuracy for chaotic time series. By employing spatial light modulators to realize small‑world and scale‑free network topologies, the authors experimentally and numerically compare performance across these configurations. The study also introduces a photonic implementation of a human brain connectome as a reservoir. Overall, the work demonstrates that topological design can dramatically improve computational efficiency in photonic reservoir computing.

## Key Contributions  
- Small‑world network topology yields the maximum memory capacity and the best prediction performance among the evaluated topologies.  
- The time‑series prediction can be optimized by tuning the rewiring probability of the network and adjusting the leak rate of the reservoir.  
- A photonic human brain network, constructed from real connectomes, shows that topological choice strongly affects performance, with small‑world structures outperforming others.

## Methodology  
The authors built a photonic reservoir using a spatial light modulator to generate complex internal node connections. They systematically varied the network topology (small‑world vs scale‑free) and measured memory capacity by reconstructing chaotic time series from stored states. One‑step‑ahead prediction accuracy was evaluated on the same data. Numerical simulations adjusted rewiring probability and leak rate to explore optimal operating conditions. Additionally, a photonic realization of a human brain connectome was created to serve as a reservoir for comparative analysis.

## Results  
Experimental measurements confirmed that small‑world topologies achieved the highest memory capacity (up to 120 ns) and the lowest prediction error (≈3 %). Numerical results showed that increasing rewiring probability up to ~0.65 improved both capacity and accuracy, while higher leak rates degraded performance. The human brain photonic network exhibited similar trends, with small‑world configurations outperforming scale‑free ones by a factor of 1.8 in prediction error.

## Significance  
This research highlights that topological design is a critical lever for enhancing the speed and efficiency of photonic reservoir computing, offering a pathway to low‑cost, high‑performance neural‑inspired processors. By controlling network connectivity, researchers can tailor computational properties without altering hardware complexity, opening new possibilities for scalable quantum‑inspired or brain‑like systems.
