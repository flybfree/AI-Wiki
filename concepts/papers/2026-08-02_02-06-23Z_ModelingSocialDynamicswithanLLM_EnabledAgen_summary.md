## Summary  
The paper proposes a hybrid LLM‑Enabled Agent Based Network‑Dynamic (LAND) model called GhostField to simulate social dynamics in the AuraSight scenario, where many cyber agents interact over a fictional songwriting contest. It aims to capture collective influence and narrative dominance through four analytical layers: ego‑network topology, semantic network evolution, coordination dynamics, and influence dynamics. The study demonstrates that emergent social dynamics arise from recursive interaction between network structure and discourse exchange rather than individual agent behavior.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: The LAND model successfully integrates large‑scale LLM‑generated agents with network dynamics to produce realistic social simulations.  
- Finding 2: Coordinated behaviors emerge as a collective outcome of narrative exchanges, not pre‑programmed by agents.  
- Finding 3: Influence propagation follows a recursive feedback loop between evolving semantic networks and topology changes.

## Methodology  
The authors built the GhostField architecture, an LLM‑enabled agent based network‑dynamic framework. They deployed 314,244 heterogeneous cyber social agents and human actors who exchanged 529,327 messages over 30 days in the AuraSight scenario. Data were collected across four layers: ego‑network topology (who interacts with whom), semantic network evolution (how topics and narratives develop), coordination dynamics (joint actions and event alignment), and influence dynamics (shifts in narrative dominance). The model was run as a simulation to observe emergent patterns.

## Results  
The simulation revealed that coordination clusters formed spontaneously when agents aligned on shared themes, and influence rankings shifted as certain narratives gained traction. Notably, the most influential actors were those who both held high‑degree positions in the ego network and contributed to dominant semantic topics. The recursive interaction between network topology changes and narrative evolution was identified as a primary driver of these dynamics.

## Significance  
This work bridges LLM‑driven agent modeling with social network theory, offering a scalable framework for studying collective influence in digital ecosystems. It provides empirical evidence that emergent coordination is not preordained but arises from discourse‑network feedback, informing future AI‑assisted simulations of public opinion and cultural trends.
