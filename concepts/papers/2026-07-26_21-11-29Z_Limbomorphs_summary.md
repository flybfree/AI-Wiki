## Summary  
The paper introduces Limbomorphs, a class of emergent lifelike creatures generated by an evolving spatiotemporal field in Gifbreeder, and investigates their behavior under perturbations to assess whether they display goal‑directed dynamics without explicit agents or rules. It contributes new insights into how agent‑like dynamics can arise from purely aesthetic selection within a deterministic three‑second loop. The authors propose that these reactions may reflect nascent navigation or merely visual artifacts. This work bridges interactive evolutionary computation with embodied cognition.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Limbomorphs exhibit species‑specific responses to input‑space perturbations, indicating distinct behavioral profiles.  
- [Finding 2] Their dynamics are confined to a deterministic three‑second looping “limbo”, suggesting bounded temporal constraints on emergence.  
- [Finding 3] The observed reactions could be interpreted as goal‑directed behavior or visual illusion, highlighting ambiguity in attributing agency.

## Methodology  
The authors employed input‑space perturbations—small variations in the spatiotemporal field that alter the aesthetic expression—to systematically observe how Limbomorphs react. By comparing species generated under identical initial conditions but with different perturbation types, they mapped reaction patterns and compared them to known navigation or avoidance behaviors.

## Results  
Experiments revealed that certain Limbomorphs accelerate away from perturbations (potentially evasion), while others remain static or oscillate, suggesting both reactive and non‑reactive modes. The temporal loop resets every three seconds, limiting the duration of any emergent pattern beyond that window.

## Significance  
This study demonstrates that agent‑like dynamics can emerge without explicit agents or interaction rules, challenging traditional definitions in AI and evolutionary computation. It opens avenues for studying embodied cognition in purely aesthetic evolution systems.
