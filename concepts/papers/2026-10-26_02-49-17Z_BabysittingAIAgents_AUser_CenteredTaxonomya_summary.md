## Summary
This paper addresses the growing challenge of "agentic delegation failures," where AI agents perform long-running tasks in ways that deviate from user expectations, leading to suboptimal outcomes or unnoticed errors. The authors introduce MANDATE, a comprehensive taxonomy comprising 23 failure categories organized into seven distinct groups, derived from an analysis of over 160,000 Reddit posts discussing AI agent usage. Through a follow-up study with 73 participants, the research investigates the relationship between the prevalence of these failures and user perceptions of their severity and noticeability. The study reveals critical insights into how users perceive and detect agent errors, highlighting that severe failures are often harder to notice and identifying specific high-prevalence failures that have received insufficient attention in existing literature.

## Key Contributions
- **Development of MANDATE Taxonomy:** The authors created a structured classification system for agentic delegation failures, organizing 23 specific failure types into seven broader groups based on real-world user experiences shared on Reddit.
- **Insight into Noticeability and Severity:** The study demonstrates a counterintuitive relationship where failures rated as more severe by users are paradoxically harder to notice, suggesting that critical errors may go undetected during delegation.
- **Identification of Under-Researched Failures:** The paper highlights specific failure modes, such as agents running without making progress, that are commonly encountered by users but have received little attention in academic research, pointing to gaps in current safety and reliability studies.

## Methodology
The authors employed a mixed-methods approach combining large-scale data mining with user-centered qualitative research. First, they analyzed a corpus of 163,487 Reddit posts describing AI agent use to identify common patterns of failure, which informed the development of the MANDATE taxonomy. Following this descriptive phase, they conducted a follow-up study involving 73 AI agent users. This study examined participants' perceptions regarding the prevalence, severity, and noticeability of various failure types identified in the taxonomy. The methodology aimed to bridge the gap between theoretical classifications of agent errors and the practical realities of how users experience and detect these failures during delegation tasks.

## Results
The primary result is the validation of the MANDATE taxonomy as a useful framework for categorizing user-reported issues with AI agents. The follow-up study found that prior experience with a specific failure type was positively associated with greater noticeability, implying that familiarity helps users detect errors. However, the data also showed that failures perceived as more severe were considered harder to notice, indicating a potential blind spot in user oversight for critical tasks. Furthermore, the assessment of research maturity revealed that several commonly encountered failures, particularly those involving lack of progress or subtle deviations from instructions, are under-studied in the current literature.

## Significance
This work is significant because it shifts the focus from purely technical metrics of AI performance to user-centered experiences of delegation failure. By providing a concrete taxonomy (MANDATE), it offers researchers and developers a structured way to categorize and address real-world agent errors. The findings underscore the need for improved oversight mechanisms that help users notice severe failures more easily. Additionally, by identifying under-researched failure modes, the paper guides future research priorities toward improving agent reliability and safety in practical, long-running workflows, ultimately aiming to make delegation safer and more transparent for end-users.
