## Summary  
The paper proposes Hierarchical Latent Reasoning (HiLaR) to improve LLM‑based recommendation by modeling user preferences hierarchically across model layers. It introduces layer‑aware reinforcement optimization that aligns temporal‑guided preference representations with latent reasoning states, enabling a trajectory from coarse preferences to fine‑grained intents. HiLaR integrates final feedback with per‑layer reward signals derived from marginal target‑likelihood gains. Experiments on four Amazon datasets demonstrate superior performance over sequential, generative, and LLM baselines.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Hierarchical latent preference representations that capture both broad user trends and current item intent.  
- [Finding 2] A layer‑aware reinforcement framework that optimizes the reasoning trajectory using marginal target‑likelihood gain as a per‑layer reward.  
- [Finding 3] Empirical superiority of HiLaR over sequential, generative, and LLM‑based recommendation baselines on Amazon benchmark datasets.

## Methodology  
The authors construct temporal‑guided hierarchical user preference representations by aggregating coarse preferences across layers. These are aligned with multiple latent reasoning states generated by the LLM at each layer. The optimization loop uses a reinforcement objective that combines the final recommendation loss with per‑layer reward signals computed from marginal target‑likelihood gain, guiding the model to allocate reasoning effort where it yields maximal improvement.

## Results  
HiLaR achieves up to 4.2 % absolute lift in NDCG@10 compared to the best sequential baseline and outperforms generative and LLM baselines by 3–5 % across all datasets. Ablation studies confirm that hierarchical representation learning contributes ~1.8 % lift, latent alignment adds ~0.9%, and process‑level optimization provides ~1.5%. Sensitivity analysis shows robustness to varying layer counts.

## Significance  
By decoupling reasoning into interpretable layers and optimizing each with marginal gains, HiLaR makes LLM recommendation more efficient and scalable, enabling better user preference modeling without costly explicit natural‑language inference.

Hierarchical representation learning, latent reasoning states, reinforcement optimization, marginal target‑likelihood gain, sequential recommendation baselines, generative recommendation models, NDCG@10 evaluation metric.
