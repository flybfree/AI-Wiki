## Summary  
The paper proposes a hybrid knowledge‑graph generation pipeline that combines a top‑down grounding of Large Language Models (LLMs) in the multilingual Wikidata Knowledge Graph with an agentic reflexion pattern to create new nodes and metadata for unrecognized skills. By executing five autonomous stages—entity reconciliation, multilingual canonicalization, active curation, deduplication, and iterative recovery—the system autonomously adapts to noisy skill mentions across five European languages. The pipeline yields a scalable, self‑healing knowledge graph that can be turned into a structured taxonomy for downstream HR tasks such as talent matching. This work advances the field by offering an explicable, end‑to‑end framework that balances rigid entity anchoring with dynamic concept synthesis.

## Key Contributions  
- [A hybrid top‑down/bottom‑up pipeline that grounds LLMs in Wikidata while dynamically generating new knowledge‑graph nodes for emerging skills.]  
- [An agentic reflexion pattern that enables the system to autonomously reconcile, canonicalize, curate, deduplicate, and recover unmapped concepts across multiple languages.]  
- [A scalable, self‑healing framework that produces a comprehensive multilingual skills knowledge graph suitable for structured taxonomy extraction.]

## Methodology  
The authors adopt a five‑stage autonomous workflow. First, the LLM is grounded in Wikidata’s multilingual KG to retrieve stable entity representations of known skill terms. Second, multilingual canonicalization maps diverse language expressions to these entities. Third, active curation identifies skills not present in the graph and triggers the bottom‑up creation of new nodes with associated metadata. Fourth, deduplication removes redundant or conflicting entries generated by the LLM. Finally, iterative recovery revisits unmapped concepts after each cycle, allowing the system to continuously adapt to evolving, noisy skill mentions.

## Results  
The pipeline successfully generates a comprehensive skills knowledge graph that aligns with Wikidata entities while adding novel nodes for emerging skills across German, French, Spanish, Italian, and Dutch. The resulting taxonomy is structured and can be directly used by HR platforms. Experiments show high entity‑level accuracy in reconciling noisy text inputs and low false‑positive rates during deduplication, demonstrating the system’s robustness.

## Significance  
By integrating a top‑down grounding with an agentic bottom‑up process, the approach overcomes the limitations of rigid or fragmented KG generation methods. It provides HR systems with a continuously updated, multilingual skills graph that improves talent matching accuracy and reduces manual curation effort. The self‑healing nature ensures long‑term reliability even as skill vocabularies evolve.
