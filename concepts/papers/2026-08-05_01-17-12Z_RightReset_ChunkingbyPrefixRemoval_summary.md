## Summary  
The paper introduces Right Reset (RR), a method that identifies natural chunk boundaries by measuring how much the hidden‑state trajectory of a causal language model changes when left context is removed, converting this into variable‑length chunks for text segmentation. By applying RR to flattened corpora—where topically similar records are concatenated after removing separators and layout—they recover 47.7 % of original records as clean units, outperforming conventional BGE embedding boundaries (25.9 %). The method also shows robustness across rendering, OCR, and multiple language models, suggesting that context dependence itself can serve as a reliable segmentation signal when surface structure is weak.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] RR identifies boundary edges where removing left context leaves the right‑hand hidden‑state trajectory largely unchanged, enabling precise chunk detection.  
- [Finding 2] The dynamic program translates these edge scores into variable‑length chunks that recover a higher proportion of original records (47.7 %) than baseline BGE embeddings (25.9 %).  
- [Finding 3] RR‑selected cuts consistently produce less local output disruption across six language models and even when surface cues are absent, indicating strong context‑driven segmentation.  

## Methodology  
The authors compute a Right Reset score for each token by comparing the hidden‑state trajectory before and after left‑context removal; scores are fed to a dynamic programming algorithm that selects cut points maximizing preservation of the right‑hand trajectory. The resulting cuts define variable‑length chunks, which are then evaluated on flattened text generated by concatenating topically similar records while stripping separators and layout artifacts.  

## Results  
Experimental evaluation shows RR recovers 47.7 % of original records versus 25.9 % for BGE boundaries; the improvement holds after rendering and OCR. Passive scores from Qwen3‑4B layer and direct prompting of a same‑scale instruction model perform worse on flattened records, confirming that surface cues are not the primary driver. Across six language models, RR‑selected cuts exhibit consistently lower local output disruption than unselected edges. An observed‑token likelihood‑ratio readout competes with RR in some architectures, supporting the central claim that context dependence provides a boundary signal.  

## Significance  
This work demonstrates that natural segmentation can be derived from internal model behavior rather than relying on explicit surface cues, offering a flexible, task‑agnostic approach to chunking. It could improve downstream tasks such as document summarization, retrieval, and layout reconstruction where precise boundaries are needed but human labeling is costly.
