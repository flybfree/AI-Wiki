## Summary  
This paper introduces ORACLE, an open‑source reinforcement‑learning framework that tackles analog circuit design as a multi‑objective problem without resorting to scalar rewards or retraining the model for each new trade‑off setting. By representing the relative importance of several objectives in a preference vector and conditioning the learning process on this vector, ORACLE can generate designs across diverse Pareto fronts with a single trained policy. The authors also propose two guidance strategies—normalized‑weight guidance and cosine‑aligned guidance—to accelerate convergence, and integrate a large language model (LLM) to filter actions that are likely to produce suboptimal or high‑runtime outputs. Overall, ORACLE bridges the gap between MO optimization and practical circuit design automation.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A vector‑valued reward system with a user‑specified preference vector enables a single RL model to handle any combination of competing objectives without retraining.  
- [Finding 2] Two preference‑guided strategies (normalized‑weight guidance and cosine‑aligned guidance) significantly improve the convergence speed and stability of the MO optimizer.  
- [Finding 3] An LLM‑driven action selection mechanism pre‑filters candidate actions, reducing runtime and preventing designs that violate critical specifications.

## Methodology  
ORACLE treats analog circuit design as a sequential decision problem where each action corresponds to adjusting a set of circuit parameters. Instead of aggregating multiple objectives into one scalar reward, the model receives a preference vector **p** = (w₁,…,wₙ) that encodes the relative importance of each objective. During training, the policy is conditioned on **p**, so the learned behavior adapts to different trade‑off priorities while remaining a single trained network. To enforce adherence to the specified weights, the authors employ normalized‑weight guidance, which rescales the preference vector to unit length and aligns it with cosine‑aligned guidance, ensuring that the gradient of the loss is orthogonal to directions that would violate the intended weighting scheme. An LLM is integrated as a pre‑filter: for each candidate action generated by the policy, the model evaluates its textual description (e.g., “increase resistor value X”) and discards actions that are likely to produce suboptimal specifications or excessive simulation time. This combination of preference conditioning, guidance strategies, and LLM filtering creates a robust MO optimizer.

## Results  
Experimental evaluation on 2,000 test cases across multiple circuit topologies demonstrates that ORACLE reduces overall runtime by a factor ranging from **20.4× to 104.4×** compared with state‑of‑the‑art methods. The optimizer meets the specifications of **99.9 %** of the target designs, and the resulting figure of merit improves by **5.1× to 318.6×**. These gains are attributed to the efficient exploration guided by the preference vector and the LLM’s ability to prune low‑quality actions.

## Significance  
ORACLE addresses a longstanding bottleneck in analog circuit automation: the need for separate, retrained models for each multi‑objective scenario. By providing a single, flexible framework that respects user‑defined trade‑offs, it lowers development time and cost while delivering designs that are both optimal and manufacturable. The open‑source release encourages broader adoption across academia and industry, fostering research on scalable MO optimization in hardware.
