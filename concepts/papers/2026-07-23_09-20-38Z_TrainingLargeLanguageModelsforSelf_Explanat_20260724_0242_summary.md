## Summary  
The paper proposes a reinforcement learning framework that directly optimizes the faithfulness of self‑explanations generated by large language models. It introduces a per‑sample reward derived from the Phi‑CCT correlation metric and fine‑tunes LLMs to produce explanations that accurately reflect their internal decision factors. This work bridges a gap between evaluation methods and parameter optimization for unfaithful reasoning.

## Key Contributions  
- The authors demonstrate that RL can be used to train models to identify influential factors in their decisions, improving faithfulness scores from near zero to around 0.664 on in‑distribution tasks.  
- They show substantial cross‑intervention generalization: a model trained only with random‑word insertions also improves performance on user‑bias prompts, suggesting latent factor learning.  
- The study rules out reward gaming by analyzing model behavior and confirming that improvements are genuine.

## Methodology  
The researchers modify existing faithfulness metrics into an RL objective. They generate self‑explanations for a set of tasks, compute Phi‑CCT scores per sample as rewards, and fine‑tune Llama3.1‑8B and Qwen3‑8B using reinforcement learning. Two intervention types are used: random‑word insertions to probe model attention and user‑bias phrases to test generalization.

## Results  
In‑distribution Phi‑CCT scores rise from near zero to 0.664 for Llama3.1‑8B, while out‑of‑distribution tasks like StrategyQA reach up to 0.691. Cross‑intervention transfer is observed only in one model (Llama3.1‑8B), indicating model‑dependent effects.

## Significance  
This work provides a scalable method for reducing unfaithful reasoning by aligning model outputs with internal decision factors, offering a new direction beyond evaluation‑only approaches.
