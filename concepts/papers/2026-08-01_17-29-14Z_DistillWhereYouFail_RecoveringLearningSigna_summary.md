## Summary  
[The paper tackles the problem of sparse reward signals in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, where Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) loses gradients when all responses in a group receive identical rewards. It introduces RSTG—Recovering Learning Signals via Adaptive Teacher Guidance—to selectively apply on‑policy distillation only where it is most beneficial, thereby recovering the missing learning signals. By restricting OPD to negative zero‑variance prompts weighted by teacher confidence and targeting tokens with high entropy or large divergence, the method mitigates the three main degradation causes of naive GRPO+OPD. Experiments show that RSTG improves performance over the baseline by +4.02 % on math tasks and +3.05 % on code tasks.]

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1: Naive GRPO+OPD degrades because not all samples benefit from distillation, fitting too quickly to the teacher undermines exploration, and OPD’s advantages are asymmetric, suppressing most tokens.]  
- [Finding 2: RSTG restricts OPD to negative zero‑variance prompts and weights them by the teacher’s confidence score, ensuring only informative samples receive gradient updates.]  
- [Finding 3: At the token level, distillation is applied exclusively to tokens exhibiting high student entropy or large teacher‑student divergence.]

## Methodology  
[The authors adopt a two‑level selective approach. First, they identify “negative zero‑variance prompts” where all responses in a group share the same reward; these are the only candidates for OPD because they lack positive RL signals. Each such sample is weighted by the teacher model’s confidence, giving higher influence to more reliable guidance. Second, at the token level, they compute student entropy and divergence from the teacher; tokens with high entropy or large divergence are targeted for distillation updates. Finally, they augment training with supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) on correct trajectories generated by the teacher, injecting positive gradient signals where RL yields none.]

## Results  
[Main experimental results show that RSTG outperforms naive GRPO+OPD by +4.02 % on math benchmarks and +3.05 % on code benchmarks, indicating a clear gain in both reasoning and programming tasks.]

## Significance  
[This work bridges the gap between RL with verifiable rewards and distillation, providing a principled way to recover learning signals where traditional RL methods fail. By focusing gradient updates only on informative samples and tokens, RSTG preserves exploration capacity while improving policy performance, which is crucial for high‑stakes applications such as math solving and code generation.]
