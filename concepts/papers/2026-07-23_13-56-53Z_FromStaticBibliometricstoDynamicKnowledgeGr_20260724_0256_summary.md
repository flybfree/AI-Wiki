## Summary  
The paper argues that conventional bibliometric indicators are too static and semantically limited to capture the evolving, non‑linear nature of science, technology, and innovation (STI) ecosystems. It introduces a hybrid, symbolic‑first framework that fuses open scholarly data, a dynamic versioned knowledge graph, LLM‑driven semantic augmentation, rigorous validation, and an analytics layer. The core contribution is treating validation as the mediating principle that balances semantic flexibility with epistemic discipline, thereby producing analytically admissible STI insights while preserving scientific rigor.  

## Key Contributions  
- A hybrid framework that integrates static bibliometric metrics, a dynamic knowledge graph, and LLM‑assisted augmentation under explicit methodological constraints.  
- Validation as the mediating principle linking semantic flexibility with epistemic discipline, ensuring analytically admissible outputs.  
- An analytics layer capable of trend detection, science‑to‑technology pathway mapping, and policy‑oriented gap analysis while recording full provenance at every stage.  

## Methodology  
The authors organized their solution into five layers: an open scholarly data backbone supplies raw bibliometric records; a dynamic versioned knowledge graph stores evolving entities and relationships; the constrained LLM‑assisted semantic augmentation layer proposes candidate enrichments without overriding existing structure; a multi‑layer validation pipeline checks structural, evidentiary, comparative, and selective expert criteria before accepting any enrichment; finally, an analytics layer consumes validated data to generate both traditional indicators and advanced graph‑based analyses. Provenance is recorded at each step to guarantee reproducibility.  

## Results  
The framework provides a theoretically grounded architecture that can be instantiated with existing STI datasets, enabling the generation of provisional candidate enrichments that are only accepted after passing the validation pipeline. While no large‑scale empirical experiment was reported, the design demonstrates feasibility: candidates are filtered by structural consistency, evidentiary support, comparative relevance, and expert judgment, producing a curated set of analytics outputs. The framework thus offers a reproducible pathway from raw bibliometric signals to validated STI insights.  

## Significance  
By embedding validation into the LLM‑augmentation process, the approach mitigates hallucination, opacity, and corpus bias while preserving semantic richness. This aligns STI analytics with the evidentiary standards of science‑of‑science research, delivering timely trend detection and policy‑relevant gap analysis that static bibliometrics cannot provide. The governance model also promotes auditability, reproducibility, and mitigation of algorithmic bias.
