## Summary  
The paper introduces MMAC, a massive multi‑dimensional benchmark designed to evaluate the quality of open‑ended audio captions generated by large language models for audio (AudioLLMs). By moving beyond simple generation metrics, MMAC assesses whether captions capture relevant information across 15 evaluation dimensions and remain consistent with reference labels. The benchmark aggregates 5,638 audio clips from over 20 diverse sources covering six capability categories, enabling a comprehensive diagnostic of model performance.

## Methodology  
To construct MMAC, the authors assembled a diverse collection of audio clips from multiple public and proprietary sources, each tagged with one of six capability categories (e.g., scene description, action identification). For each clip, fifteen evaluation dimensions were defined that capture aspects such as temporal coherence, spatial detail, semantic accuracy, and stylistic appropriateness. A caption is considered “correct” for a dimension if it mentions relevant content and aligns with the reference label. The authors then generated captions using both open‑source and proprietary AudioLLMs, scoring each model on every dimension to produce a multi‑dimensional performance profile.  

## Results  
Experimental results demonstrate clear variations among models across dimensions: some models excel at high‑level scene description yet omit fine‑grained actions; others are accurate in temporal ordering but lack spatial specificity. The benchmark quantifies information coverage—how many of the fifteen dimensions each model satisfies—and reliability—the proportion of correct, consistent captions. Quantitative analysis shows that while overall generation quality correlates with a few high‑impact dimensions, many models still fail to cover lower‑priority aspects, exposing blind spots in AudioLLM training data and objectives.  

## Significance  
MMAC shifts the focus from isolated generation metrics to a holistic diagnostic of caption adequacy, enabling researchers to pinpoint where models deviate from user expectations. By quantifying both coverage and reliability, it guides model improvement strategies and informs the design of future audio‑LLM architectures. The open release of the benchmark and code promotes community scrutiny and accelerates progress in fine‑grained audio description tasks.
