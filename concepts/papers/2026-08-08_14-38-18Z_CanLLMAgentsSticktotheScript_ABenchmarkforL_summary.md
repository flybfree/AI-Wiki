## Summary  
The paper tackles the problem of maintaining long‑horizon logical consistency in interactive narratives generated by Large Language Models, framing it as Narrative Commitment Preservation (NCP). It introduces NCP‑Bench, a benchmark comprising 100 narrative environments derived from movie synopses, each equipped with structured specifications that can be automatically verified throughout the dialogue. The authors show that high linguistic quality does not guarantee commitment preservation, revealing a substantial consistency gap across state‑of‑the‑art models.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: LLM agents frequently generate content that contradicts earlier narrative commitments despite producing fluent text.  
- Finding 2: Even the best model, GPT‑5.2, survives only about 42 % of 20‑turn interactions while preserving all commitments.  
- Finding 3: Fact‑conflict rates across models range from 40 % to 68 %, indicating frequent logical inconsistencies.

## Methodology  
The authors constructed NCP‑Bench by automatically generating narrative specifications for each environment, including trajectory, commitment statements, and initial facts. They then evaluated a suite of state‑of‑the‑art LLMs by measuring two metrics: the survival rate—the percentage of runs that satisfy all commitments within a fixed turn limit—and the fact‑conflict rate—a quantitative measure derived from systematic adversarial interventions that probe for contradictions.

## Results  
Across the 100 environments, GPT‑5.2 achieved an average survival rate of roughly 42 % after 20 turns, while other models performed worse on average. Fact‑conflict rates varied widely, with several models exceeding 68 % conflict, and only isolated runs succeeded in satisfying every commitment throughout the entire interaction.

## Significance  
These findings demonstrate a critical gap between the linguistic quality of LLM outputs and their ability to preserve long‑term narrative integrity, which is essential for reliable interactive storytelling. The results highlight that current models cannot be trusted for open‑ended games or adaptive narratives without additional constraints or post‑processing safeguards.
