## Summary  
The paper investigates how ideological polarization evolves over time in political discussions on Reddit, moving beyond single‑point cross‑sectional analyses that focus only on affective reactions. By constructing temporally aligned word embeddings for each opposing community, the authors quantify semantic divergence as a measure of polarization. Their longitudinal approach reveals that both concept‑level and topic‑level differences have grown substantially during the study period. The work therefore provides a scalable computational framework for tracking the diachronic dynamics of ideological split in large‑scale social media discourse.

- [Finding 2] The authors introduce a longitudinal method that uses community‑specific word embeddings aligned across time to capture semantic divergence rather than affective sentiment.  
- [Finding 3] Their framework is scalable, enabling systematic analysis of large social media datasets for temporal changes in ideological framing.

## Methodology  
The researchers gathered Reddit posts from two opposing political communities and computed separate word‑embedding vectors for each community at regular time intervals. By aligning the embeddings temporally, they could compare how the same political concepts are represented across periods within a given community. Polarization was quantified as the average cosine similarity distance between matching concept embeddings over time, allowing them to track increases in semantic divergence.

## Results  
Experimental results show that the average semantic distance between opposing communities rose significantly from the start to the end of the analysis period. This increase is observed both when comparing individual political concepts (e.g., “immigration,” “climate change”) and when aggregating across topics, indicating a broadening of ideological gaps. The magnitude of divergence suggests a measurable escalation in polarization over time.

## Significance  
This study matters because it offers the first longitudinal computational evidence that ideological polarization is not static but intensifies as discourse evolves online. By providing a scalable method for diachronic analysis, the framework can inform scholars, policymakers, and platform designers about the temporal trajectory of political split in digital environments.
