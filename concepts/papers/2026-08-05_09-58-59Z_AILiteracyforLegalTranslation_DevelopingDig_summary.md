## Summary  
This paper investigates how generative AI reshapes legal translation, highlighting both its transformative potential and the attendant linguistic, technical, legal, ethical, and cognitive challenges. By analysing these dimensions, the authors argue that AI does not replace the core aims of legal translation but demands an expanded professional competence: AI literacy. The work introduces a four‑dimensional framework—foundational, procedural, critical, and strategic—that positions AI literacy as a transversal component of legal translator competence, thereby fostering digital resilience within the profession.

## Key Contributions  
- **Finding 1:** Generative AI creates new opportunities for legal translation while simultaneously introducing significant risks across multiple professional domains.  
- **Finding 2:** The authors propose an AI‑literacy framework composed of four mutually reinforcing dimensions that integrates AI competence into existing legal‑translation competencies.  
- **Finding 3:** They outline classroom activities designed to cultivate AI literacy in legal translator education, enabling future professionals to use AI critically, responsibly, and in line with professional standards.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a theoretical synthesis rather than empirical experimentation. Drawing on interdisciplinary literature from linguistics, technology studies, law, ethics, and cognitive science, they mapped the multifaceted impacts of generative AI on legal translation. The framework was constructed by identifying core competencies that must be retained (foundational), procedural steps for using AI tools (procedural), critical thinking skills to evaluate AI outputs (critical), and strategic planning for integrating AI into client‑focused projects (strategic). Pedagogical activities were derived from these dimensions, aiming to embed them within curricula.

## Results  
The primary result is the conceptualisation of a comprehensive AI‑literacy framework that can be operationalised in legal translator training. The paper does not present quantitative experiments; instead, it offers a theoretical model and illustrative classroom exercises that demonstrate how each dimension can be taught and applied. The outcomes are therefore qualitative: a clear set of competency levels and pedagogical strategies.

## Significance  
This research matters because it equips legal translators with the digital resilience needed to navigate an AI‑augmented workflow without compromising quality or ethical standards. By embedding AI literacy into professional competence, the framework supports sustainable practice, reduces reliance on opaque black‑box tools, and aligns with evolving regulatory expectations for responsible use of generative AI.
