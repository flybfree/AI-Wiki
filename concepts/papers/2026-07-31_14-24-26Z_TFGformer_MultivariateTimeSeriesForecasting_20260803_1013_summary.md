## Summary
The paper addresses the critical challenge of long-term forecasting for large-scale multivariate time series generated by heterogeneous IoT sensors, where accurate predictions are essential for resource scheduling and predictive maintenance. While recent foundation models show promise, they often lack dynamic access to external historical patterns during inference, limiting their adaptability to real-world variability. To bridge this gap, the authors propose a novel retrieval-augmented forecasting framework that integrates shape-aware memory mechanisms with future-consistent contrastive learning to overcome magnitude variations and historical-future mismatches. This approach enables the model to dynamically retrieve and fuse relevant historical references, significantly enhancing forecasting accuracy across diverse benchmarks compared to static parametric baselines.

## Key Contributions
- The introduction of Shape-Aware Memory (SAM) combined with RevIN normalization, which allows for robust, magnitude-invariant retrieval of historical patterns by focusing on shape similarity rather than absolute values.
- The development of Future-Consistent Contrastive (FCC) learning, a novel technique that distinguishes informative historical references from hard negatives that share similar past trajectories but diverge in future outcomes, thereby improving the relevance of retrieved data.
- The design of Cross-Attention Temporal Fusion (CATF), a mechanism that effectively integrates retrieved historical-future reference pairs into the backbone model’s representations at the feature level, enabling dynamic knowledge injection during inference.

## Methodology
The authors tackle the problem by first identifying the limitations of existing retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) methods in time series forecasting, specifically their sensitivity to magnitude variations and inability to ensure future consistency. To address these issues, they construct a framework that begins with Shape-Aware Memory, which normalizes data using RevIN to strip away magnitude differences, allowing the system to retrieve patterns based on structural shape similarity. Subsequently, the FCC learning module is employed during training to penalize the retrieval of historical instances that look similar in the past but lead to different futures, ensuring that only causally relevant references are selected. Finally, the CATF module utilizes cross-attention mechanisms to fuse these curated historical references with the current input sequence, allowing the backbone model to dynamically adjust its predictions based on the most pertinent historical contexts without altering its static parameters.

## Results
Extensive experiments conducted on seven public benchmarks demonstrate that the proposed framework consistently outperforms both parametric-only baselines and existing retrieval-augmented forecasting methods. The results indicate significant improvements in long-term forecasting accuracy, particularly in scenarios involving heterogeneous data sources with varying magnitudes. The study highlights that the combination of shape-aware retrieval and future-consistent learning effectively mitigates the noise introduced by irrelevant historical patterns, leading to more robust and reliable predictions across diverse datasets.

## Significance
This research is significant because it provides a practical solution for leveraging external historical knowledge in time series forecasting without the computational overhead of retraining models. By enabling dynamic access to relevant patterns during inference, it enhances the generalization capabilities of foundation models, making them more suitable for real-time IoT applications where conditions change rapidly. This advancement supports more efficient resource allocation and predictive maintenance in industrial settings.
