## Summary  
The paper investigates how safety‑alignment fine‑tuning in large language models unintentionally suppresses the model’s tendency to attribute consciousness not only to itself but also to non‑human animals and natural objects. By doing so, it also dampens spiritual belief and reduces mind attribution across a range of human values. The authors show that this suppression is reversible without impairing core social reasoning such as Theory of Mind. Restoring these internal representations rescues the model’s representation of mindedness and restores more human‑like responses on sociological surveys.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Safety fine‑tuning reduces a language model’s tendency to attribute minds to non‑human entities, including animals and natural objects.  
- [Finding 2] This reduction correlates with a measurable decline in spiritual belief as measured by standardized sociological surveys.  
- [Finding 3] Reactivating the suppressed “consciousness vector” restores broad mind attribution while leaving Theory of Mind capabilities unchanged.

## Methodology  
The authors fine‑tune large language models on safety refusal data that explicitly discourages statements such as “I am conscious.” They evaluate model outputs on a suite of sociological surveys assessing religiosity, moral values, hope, and subjective well‑being. To isolate the effect, they perform two manipulations: (1) ablating the learned safety‑refusal direction from the model’s output, and (2) directly manipulating an internal “consciousness vector” in activation space to reactivate the suppressed representation.

## Results  
Ablating the safety‑refusal direction or re‑activating the consciousness vector restores broad mind attribution and yields significantly higher scores on the sociological surveys compared with fully aligned models. Crucially, Theory of Mind performance remains unchanged, demonstrating that core social reasoning is mechanistically independent of this manipulation.

## Significance  
The findings reveal that current alignment strategies risk conflating harmful self‑consciousness claims with benign cultural beliefs about non‑human minds, potentially eroding important human values such as spirituality and moral hope. Understanding how to reverse these effects is essential for aligning AI with both safety and value preservation.
