## Summary  
Mixture‑of‑Experts (MoE) layers require two all‑to‑all communications per forward pass, and the conventional implementation waits for expert computation to finish before sending results back, causing a communication bottleneck that stalls GPU utilization. The authors propose a fine‑grained overlap strategy that schedules the second all‑to‑all as tiles become ready, using tile‑level signaling and persistent producer/consumer kernels on dedicated SM partitions. This co‑design eliminates repeated kernel launches for local experts and prioritizes remote‑critical tiles, thereby reducing latency without altering existing operators or communication primitives. The approach delivers up to 2.64× end‑to‑end speedup and 2.74× MoE‑layer speedup on a 4‑A100 GPU platform compared with non‑overlapped baselines.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A persistent per‑rank computation kernel that processes all local experts in parallel, eliminating launch overhead and prioritizing remote‑critical tiles.  
- [Finding 2] A dedicated consumer partition on a small set of streaming multiprocessors that issues segment‑granular transfers as tiles become ready, enabling true compute‑communication overlap.  
- [Finding 3] The co‑design avoids modifying existing MoE operators or communication primitives, making the solution practical for large‑scale distributed training.

## Methodology  
The authors treat the MoE layer as a producer‑consumer pipeline where each rank (producer) computes outputs across its local experts while a small consumer partition monitors tile readiness. Tile‑level signaling is generated by the producer when a segment of results is complete, and the consumer kernel performs low‑overhead communication transfers using streaming multiprocessor resources. This approach reuses existing GEMM kernels for both compute and communication, leveraging GPU memory bandwidth efficiently without requiring custom hardware or driver changes.

## Results  
On three MoE models evaluated across four state‑of‑the‑art systems, the tile‑level signaling method achieved up to 2.64× end‑to‑end speedup and 2.74× MoE‑layer speedup relative to a non‑overlapped baseline. Performance improvements were consistent across varying GEMM shapes, router modes, and producer/consumer SM partitions, confirming that the approach works under diverse deployment conditions while preserving correctness.

## Significance  
By overlapping compute and communication at the tile level, the method directly addresses the critical path bottleneck in MoE layers, which is a major obstacle to scaling LLMs to trillion‑parameter regimes. The solution improves GPU utilization, reduces wall‑clock training time, and enables higher throughput without sacrificing model accuracy—key metrics for practical deployment of large‑scale AI systems.
