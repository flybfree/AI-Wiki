## Summary
This paper introduces FDD-ON, a novel and modular ontology designed to address the critical challenges of interoperability and data interpretability in Fault Detection and Diagnostics (FDD) for Variable Air Volume (VAV) HVAC systems. The authors argue that current FDD solutions are hindered by fragmented information silos and heterogeneous data sources, which prevent seamless integration into advanced frameworks like digital twins and AI-driven maintenance systems. To resolve this, FDD-ON provides a formal, machine-interpretable semantic framework that explicitly maps the complex relationships between contributing causes, faults, symptoms, and their subsequent impacts. By establishing a standardized controlled vocabulary and comprehensive libraries for fault types and symptom statuses, the ontology aims to bridge the gap between diverse diagnostic outputs and structured domain knowledge, thereby facilitating scalable and transparent FDD applications across the built environment sector.

## Key Contributions
- The development of FDD-ON, a comprehensive ontology that formally represents VAV HVAC system components, fault types, symptom statuses, and associated attributes using a well-defined controlled vocabulary.
- The creation of explicit semantic relations linking contributing causes to faults, symptoms, and impacts, which enables the mapping of heterogeneous FDD outputs and supports machine-interpretable querying of diagnostic knowledge.
- The provision of extensive fault, symptom, and impact libraries that capture a broad spectrum of operational abnormalities, serving as a foundational resource for developing interoperable FDD-related applications and digital twin frameworks.

## Methodology
The authors approached the problem by designing a modular ontology structure that integrates HVAC system FDD semantics with existing standards to ensure extensibility and compatibility. They constructed comprehensive libraries detailing various fault types and symptom statuses specific to VAV systems, ensuring that the controlled vocabulary was robust enough to handle diverse equipment types and operational contexts. The methodology involved defining explicit logical relations between causal factors and their observable symptoms to create a coherent knowledge graph. To validate the ontology's utility and accuracy, the authors evaluated FDD-ON using publicly available VAV HVAC system datasets. This evaluation process tested the ontology's ability to support querying diagnostic knowledge and mapping disparate FDD outputs, demonstrating its practical application in real-world scenarios through developed FDD applications.

## Results
The evaluation results indicate that FDD-ON successfully provides a foundational semantic framework that enhances the scalability and transparency of FDD solutions. By utilizing publicly available datasets, the authors demonstrated that the ontology effectively supports the mapping of heterogeneous diagnostic outputs and facilitates interoperable application development. The structured representation allowed for clearer interpretation of fault impacts and symptom statuses, reducing the ambiguity often associated with traditional FDD data silos. The practical demonstrations confirmed that FDD-ON can serve as a reliable basis for integrating diverse data sources into cohesive diagnostic systems, thereby improving the overall effectiveness of maintenance decision-making processes.

## Significance
This work is significant because it addresses the critical lack of standardization in HVAC fault detection, which has historically limited the adoption of AI and digital twin technologies in building management. By providing a machine-interpretable ontology, FDD-ON enables seamless data exchange between different systems and vendors, promoting interoperability across the industry. This standardization is essential for advancing energy efficiency, improving system reliability, and enabling predictive maintenance strategies that rely on high-quality, structured data. Ultimately, FDD-ON paves the way for more intelligent, automated, and efficient building operations by removing semantic barriers to information sharing.
