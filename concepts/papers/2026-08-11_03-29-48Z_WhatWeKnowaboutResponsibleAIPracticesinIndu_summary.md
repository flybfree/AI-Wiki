## Summary  
The paper aims to synthesize a large body of empirical research on responsible AI practices in industry over the past six years, identifying trends and persistent challenges. It reviews 161 studies that employed interviews, surveys, workshops, and ethnographies to map how practitioners perceive, implement, and sustain RAI. The synthesis reveals progress in awareness and professionalization but also ongoing barriers such as limited training and unsupported interventions. This work provides a comprehensive account of industry RAI beyond any single study.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Practitioner awareness has increased over the half‑decade, with more formal engagement with responsible AI.  
- Finding 2: RAI activities have become more professionalized through the adoption of toolkits and guidelines.  
- Finding 3: Persistent barriers include limited training, uneven organizational support, and a lack of interventions tailored to day‑to‑day work practices.

## Methodology  
The authors conducted a systematic literature review of empirical studies published between 2019 and 2025 that employed qualitative methods such as interviews, surveys, workshops, and ethnographies to gather data from industry practitioners. They screened 161 relevant papers, extracted themes, and organized findings into categories of awareness, professionalization, interventions, and barriers.

## Results  
The synthesis shows a clear upward trend in practitioner awareness and the adoption of RAI toolkits and guidelines, indicating professionalization. However, many respondents report insufficient training, fragmented organizational support, and that existing resources do not align with daily work tasks, highlighting persistent gaps.

## Significance  
This matters because it informs researchers about real‑world implementation dynamics, helps practitioners design more effective RAI programs, and guides policymakers to create interventions that address actual industry constraints rather than theoretical ideals.
