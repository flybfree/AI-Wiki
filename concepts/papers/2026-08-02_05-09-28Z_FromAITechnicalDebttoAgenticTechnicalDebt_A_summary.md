## Summary  
This paper tackles the gap between conventional AI Technical Debt (AITD) and the emergent liabilities of Agentic AI systems, which operate autonomously, collaborate with other agents, and persist across time. By re‑interpreting existing AITDs through a theory‑informed transformation lens, the authors introduce **Agentic Technical Debt (AgTD)** as a new construct that captures how technical debt evolves into system‑level liabilities such as memory inconsistencies, orchestration fragility, cascading failures, and unsafe autonomous decisions. The work provides the first systematic mapping of 31 established AITDs to their agentic manifestations, thereby extending the traditional notion of technical debt beyond static software artifacts to include dynamic agent behaviors and coordination mechanisms.

## Key Contributions  
- **Introduces Agentic Technical Debt (AgTD)** as a framework that explains how technical debt accumulates, propagates, and amplifies within autonomous, multi‑agent AI ecosystems.  
- **Provides a systematic mapping** of 31 existing AITDs to their agentic equivalents—memory inconsistencies, orchestration fragility, cascading failures, and unsafe autonomous decision‑making—demonstrating concrete transformations from component‑level debt to system‑level liability.  
- **Extends the technical‑debt taxonomy** beyond artifacts to encompass agent behaviors, coordination protocols, and interactions among agents, tools, and execution environments.

## Methodology  
The authors employed a theory‑informed transformation methodology that categorizes each identified AITD into three modes: direct transformation (retaining the original debt’s essence), contextual transformation (adapting it to dynamic agentic contexts), and manifestation expansion (broadening its impact across agents, tools, and environments). This approach was applied to a systematic scoping review of 31 established AITDs organized under seven root‑cause categories. The mapping process combined qualitative analysis with the proposed transformation framework to generate a comprehensive taxonomy.

## Results  
The results show that conventional technical debts—such as outdated code patterns or undocumented assumptions—re‑emerge in agentic systems as persistent liabilities that affect memory consistency, orchestration reliability, and safety of autonomous actions. The mapping reveals that debt can cascade across agents, causing one faulty decision to trigger downstream failures, thereby amplifying risk. Moreover, the findings highlight how technical debt influences AI Trust, Risk, and Security Management (AI TRiSM), affecting governance, operational resilience, and sustainability.

## Significance  
This work establishes AgTD as a foundational software‑engineering construct for autonomous multi‑agent AI systems, offering a taxonomy, transformation framework, and research agenda that guide engineers in anticipating and mitigating debt before it becomes catastrophic. By linking technical debt to trustworthiness, security, and sustainability, the study provides actionable insights for responsible development of agentic AI.
