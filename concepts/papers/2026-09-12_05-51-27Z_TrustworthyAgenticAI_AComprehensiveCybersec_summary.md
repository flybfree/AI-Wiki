## Summary
This paper addresses the critical security vulnerabilities inherent in the evolution from passive foundation models to autonomous, goal-directed agentic AI systems. The authors argue that granting neural cores execution authority over complex infrastructure dissolves traditional security perimeters by allowing natural language to function simultaneously as data, code, and protocol. To address these risks, the study synthesizes 206 foundational studies and regulatory standards into a comprehensive systems-security reference framework. It establishes a formal architecture for stateful agents and defines a six-dimensional taxonomy for evaluating trustworthiness across security, safety, privacy, explainability, fairness, and accountability.

## Key Contributions
- The authors formalize the general agent architecture as a stateful 5-tuple, providing a standardized mathematical model to describe recursive cognitive loops, persistent memory, live tool execution, and multi-agent collaboration.
- A comprehensive six-dimensional trustworthiness taxonomy is introduced, covering security, safety, privacy, explainability, fairness, and accountability, which serves as a baseline for evaluating agentic AI systems.
- The paper proposes a multi-layered zero-trust defense-in-depth architecture that integrates Dual-LLM isolation, Capability-Based Access Control (CBAC), kernel eBPF probes, and sandboxed runtimes to mitigate the Turing-complete blast radius of untrusted data.

## Methodology
The authors employed a systematic literature review approach, synthesizing 206 foundational studies and international regulatory standards to map the current threat landscape. They utilized theoretical modeling to define the stateful 5-tuple architecture and developed a conceptual framework for defense architectures. The methodology involved analyzing intra-execution loops and interaction planes to identify specific threat surfaces, followed by mapping technical controls against existing AI governance frameworks to ensure compliance and robustness.

## Results
The research identifies that natural language interfaces create a Turing-complete blast radius where untrusted input can execute as instructions, effectively bypassing classical security boundaries. The proposed defense architecture demonstrates how integrating Dual-LLM isolation with kernel-level eBPF probes significantly reduces the attack surface for code injection and privilege escalation. Furthermore, the study maps technical controls to international AI governance frameworks, providing a structured approach for aligning agentic AI development with global regulatory standards.

## Significance
This work is significant as it provides the first comprehensive systems-security reference framework specifically tailored for trustworthy agentic AI. By addressing the unique risks of autonomous execution and multi-agent collaboration, it offers critical guidance for developers and policymakers navigating the complex intersection of AI capabilities and cybersecurity. The established taxonomy and defense architectures serve as essential tools for mitigating emerging threats in next-generation AI systems.
