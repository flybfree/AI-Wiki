## Summary  
Social‑media recommendation systems often serve users’ immediate impulses rather than the preferences they would retain after thoughtful consideration, creating a persistent misalignment between what is shown and what users truly want. Compass addresses this gap by delivering lightweight in‑situ reflections that prompt users to articulate their deeper preferences while simultaneously simulating behavioral signals and directly reshaping feed content without requiring user effort. The system embeds these prompts within the YouTube Shorts experience, enabling continuous alignment throughout everyday browsing sessions.

## Key Contributions  
- Compass introduces a continuously adaptive mechanism that surfaces in‑situ reflection notifications to guide users toward more purposeful feed consumption.  
- It achieves feed realignment by simulating behavioral signals and directly manipulating content, bypassing the need for explicit user configuration or effortful self‑reflection.  
- A 10‑day field study with 15 participants demonstrates that Compass increases reflective behavior, encourages iterative preference adjustment, and strengthens alignment between feed and user preferences while preserving a casual browsing experience.

## Methodology  
The authors embedded Compass within the YouTube Shorts platform, where users regularly consume short video clips. During the experiment, Compass delivered brief in‑feed notifications that asked users to reflect on why they liked or disliked a particular video, prompting them to articulate underlying preferences. The system then used these reflections to generate simulated behavioral signals and to adjust the subsequent feed items, effectively aligning content with the user’s reflective stance. All interactions were recorded over ten consecutive days for each of the 15 participants, who were compared against a control group that received no Compass prompts.

## Results  
The experimental results show that users exposed to Compass spent more time reflecting on video choices and made iterative adjustments to their preferences as new content appeared. Feed alignment metrics—measured by relevance scores and user‑reported satisfaction—were significantly higher in the Compass condition than in the baseline. Importantly, there was no measurable reduction in the casual nature of browsing; users continued to engage with Shorts at comparable rates. The study confirms that continuous, effortless reflection can improve both user experience and system performance.

## Significance  
By tackling the chronic misalignment between immediate feed content and deeper preferences, Compass offers a scalable solution for social‑media platforms seeking to foster more intentional use without burdening users with extra steps. Its in‑situ design reduces cognitive load, making reflective feedback an integral part of the browsing flow rather than a separate task.
