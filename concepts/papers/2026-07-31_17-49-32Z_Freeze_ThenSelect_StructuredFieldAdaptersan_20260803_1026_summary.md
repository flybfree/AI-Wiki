## Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of discovering Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) from sparse and noisy observations by decoupling the continuous field reconstruction process from the discrete selection of differential terms. The authors identify that traditional coupled neural optimization methods often fail to maintain the correct equation support throughout training, leading to unstable or incorrect discoveries. To resolve this, they propose a novel "freeze-then-select" framework that utilizes a structured field adapter for robust state estimation and Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) for rigorous term identification. This approach achieves superior exact support recovery rates across various challenging regimes, particularly in complex nonlinear dynamics like the Kuramoto-Sivashinsky equation.

## Key Contributions
- **Analysis of Optimization Instability**: The authors provide a detailed analysis of optimization paths in coupled neural PDE discovery, categorizing them into three distinct behaviors: persistent exact support, transient appearance, and complete failure to emerge, thereby highlighting the fundamental instability of joint optimization approaches.
- **Decoupled Framework Design**: They introduce a novel methodology that separates field reconstruction from equation selection, utilizing a structured field adapter with cubic splines to stabilize the initial phase before applying weak-form validation for term selection.
- **Generalization Beyond Fixed Libraries**: The study demonstrates that this decoupling principle is not limited to predefined libraries but can be effectively applied to expressions generated by genetic programming, successfully recovering unknown nonlinear diffusion functions from sparse data.

## Methodology
The proposed methodology operates in two distinct phases to ensure stability and accuracy. First, the authors employ a structured field adapter trained directly on observations without relying on any PDE residual constraints. This adapter factorizes the observed field into learned spatial features and temporal coefficients modeled by cubic splines, effectively reconstructing the continuous state from sparse data. Once the field is accurately reconstructed, it is frozen to prevent further modification. In the second phase, Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) is applied to this fixed field. SVWS identifies recurrent terms across independent weak-form systems, refits candidate supports, and ultimately selects the final equation based on performance on held-out weak-form systems. This process ensures that the selected terms are not only present in the data but are also dynamically stable and consistent across different subsets of the observations.

## Results
The method was evaluated across six sparse regimes in the MDBench benchmark. It attained the highest exact support recovery rate among all tested methods, significantly outperforming both classical symbolic regression techniques and recent neural baselines. The most pronounced improvements were observed in challenging Kuramoto-Sivashinsky dynamics, where traditional methods often struggle due to high nonlinearity and noise sensitivity. Additionally, the framework successfully recovered the power-law form of an unknown nonlinear diffusion function when combined with genetic programming, demonstrating its versatility and robustness in handling complex, unknown functional forms from limited data.

## Significance
This work is significant because it fundamentally shifts the paradigm of PDE discovery from joint optimization to a staged, stability-validated approach. By proving that decoupling field reconstruction from term selection enhances reliability, it provides a more robust tool for scientific discovery in fields where data is inherently sparse and noisy, such as fluid dynamics and climate modeling.
