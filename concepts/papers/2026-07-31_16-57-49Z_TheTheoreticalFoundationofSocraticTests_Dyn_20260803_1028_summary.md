## Summary
This paper introduces the theoretical framework for the "Socratic Test," a novel, automated assessment model designed to overcome the limitations of traditional static and oral examinations. The authors argue that current grading systems often penalize ambition through deficit-based models or introduce severe bias via performative anxiety in face-to-face settings. To address these issues, the proposed system utilizes computer-mediated conversational interactions to dynamically map a student's cognitive boundaries in real-time. By integrating Dynamic Assessment principles with advanced taxonomic frameworks, the Socratic Test aims to provide a more accurate, reliable, and equitable measurement of student mastery.

## Key Contributions
- The formalization of a non-compensatory, additive grading architecture that prioritizes demonstrated mastery over the subtraction of points for errors, thereby reducing the negative impact of anxiety on performance.
- The development of a method for quantifying the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) through graduated scaffolding within an automated conversational interface, allowing for precise measurement of cognitive potential rather than just static knowledge.
- The integration of Bloom’s Taxonomy for real-time proctoring and the SOLO Taxonomy for structural evaluation, creating a robust theoretical basis for assessing complex cognitive structures in multimodal learning environments.

## Methodology
The authors approach this problem by synthesizing established educational psychology theories with modern artificial intelligence capabilities. They construct a theoretical model that replaces static question banks with dynamic, multimodal workspaces where students engage in conversational exchanges with an AI proctor. The methodology relies on the principle of Dynamic Assessment, where the system provides graduated scaffolding to help students solve problems they could not initially handle alone. This interaction allows the system to observe how much support is required for success, thereby mapping the student's ZPD. The evaluation framework combines Bloom’s Taxonomy to monitor the depth of cognitive processing during the conversation and the SOLO Taxonomy to evaluate the structural complexity of the student's final responses.

## Results
As a theoretical paper, the primary result is the successful formalization of the Socratic Test architecture. The authors demonstrate how the integration of these taxonomies creates a system capable of distinguishing between superficial recall and deep conceptual understanding. They outline how the non-compensatory grading model prevents high-performing students from masking gaps in knowledge with correct answers in other areas, ensuring that every component of mastery is verified independently. The theoretical analysis suggests this approach significantly reduces construct-irrelevant variance caused by sociological power imbalances and anxiety inherent in traditional oral exams.

## Significance
This research matters because it proposes a paradigm shift in educational assessment from punitive measurement to diagnostic support. By leveraging AI to create a supportive, adaptive testing environment, the Socratic Test has the potential to democratize academic evaluation. It reduces the bias associated with human graders and the stress of traditional exams, offering a more humane and accurate way to measure student capability. This framework could lead to more personalized learning paths and fairer academic outcomes for diverse student populations.
