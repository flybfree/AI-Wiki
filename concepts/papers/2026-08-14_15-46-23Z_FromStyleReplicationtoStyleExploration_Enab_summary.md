## Summary  
This paper introduces the Analyze-Experiment-Resituate (AER) framework, designed to shift generative AI from mere style replication toward meaningful artistic exploration by supporting three core practices: interpreting references, experimenting with stylistic possibilities, and reflecting on social reception. The framework enables artists to move beyond passive output generation to actively engage in creative decision-making processes that mirror professional digital art practice. By integrating human-centered design insights from interviews with ten professionals, AER fosters a more nuanced and agency-rich exploration of style than traditional AI tools offer.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The AER framework provides an interpretable decomposition of artistic styles into actionable stylistic elements, allowing artists to understand and manipulate references systematically.  
- [Finding 2] The experimental phase enables controlled, user-guided generation where artists can test stylistic variations within a structured yet flexible environment.  
- [Finding 3] The resituate component simulates social perspectives on emerging styles, encouraging reflection on how new aesthetics might be received by peers or audiences.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a mixed-methods research design combining qualitative interviews with ten professional digital artists to identify pain points in current AI-assisted style tools and quantitative evaluation with 16 artists over two weeks. The AER framework was implemented as a prototype system that integrates three modules: Analyze (stylistic analysis), Experiment (controlled generation), and Resituate (reflection on social context). This iterative workflow replaced the linear, output-focused approach of standard style-transfer tools.

## Results  
Compared to direct style-transfer workflows, AER significantly increased artists’ agency and reflective practice. In the field study, four participants demonstrated more frequent experimentation and deeper reflection during daily creative sessions. The framework facilitated not only stylistic innovation but also meta-cognitive awareness—artists reported greater satisfaction with their creative process and a stronger sense of control over AI-generated outputs.

## Significance  
This work matters because it addresses a critical gap in AI art tools: they currently prioritize aesthetic replication over creative exploration, potentially stifling artistic growth. AER demonstrates that human-centered design can transform AI from a passive tool into an active collaborator in the artist’s journey. By emphasizing reflection and social context, AER aligns with professional practices of iterative learning and adaptation.
