## Summary
This research paper addresses the critical challenge of objectively evaluating Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) in high-risk domains by introducing a novel, model-agnostic metric known as the EPC score. The authors propose this metric as an extension of the existing Explainability-Performance Coefficient to explicitly quantify explanation quality by balancing the trade-off between feature selection sparsity and preserved model performance. Through extensive empirical validation across tabular, text, and image data modalities, the study demonstrates that the EPC score effectively uncovers operational dependencies among network activations, data dimensionality, and explainer performance. Crucially, the work validates the EPC score against independent human-based explanations, proving a strong alignment between higher EPC scores and human lexical sentiment judgments as well as spatial visual annotations.

## Key Contributions
- The development of a robust, model-agnostic metric called the EPC score that quantifies explanation quality by explicitly balancing feature selection sparsity with preserved model performance, addressing the lack of objective evaluation standards in XAI.
- Empirical evidence demonstrating that the EPC score effectively uncovers operational dependencies among network activations, data dimensionality, and explainer performance across diverse data modalities including tabular, text, and image data.
- Validation of the EPC score against independent human-based explanations, establishing a strong correlation between higher EPC scores and human lexical sentiment judgments and spatial visual annotations, thereby linking computational metrics to human-centered understanding.

## Methodology
The authors approached the problem by extending the existing Explainability-Performance Coefficient (EPC) into a new metric, the EPC score, designed to be model-agnostic and applicable across various deep learning architectures. They conducted an empirical validation process that involved testing this metric across three distinct data modalities: tabular datasets, text-based corpora, and image datasets. To validate the human-centered aspect of their metric, they compared the computational outputs of the EPC score against independent human-based explanations. This involved gathering human lexical sentiment judgments for text data and spatial visual annotations for image data, allowing the researchers to measure the alignment between the automated metric and human perceptual or interpretive standards.

## Results
The experimental results indicate that the proposed EPC score is highly effective in quantifying explanation quality across different types of data. The study found that higher EPC scores strongly align with human lexical sentiment judgments in text-based tasks and spatial visual annotations in image-based tasks. This alignment suggests that the metric successfully captures aspects of explainability that resonate with human intuition and understanding. Additionally, the analysis revealed significant operational dependencies among network activations, data dimensionality, and explainer performance, providing deeper insights into how different factors influence the quality of explanations generated by various models.

## Significance
This work is significant because it bridges the gap between objective computational metrics and subjective human-centered understanding in Explainable AI. By providing a validated metric that correlates with human judgment, it offers a reliable tool for evaluating XAI systems in high-risk domains where trustworthiness and interpretability are paramount. This advancement facilitates the development of more trustworthy AI systems by ensuring that explanation quality is not just mathematically sound but also meaningful to human users.
