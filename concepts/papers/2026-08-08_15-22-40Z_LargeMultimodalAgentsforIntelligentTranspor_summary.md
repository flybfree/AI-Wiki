## Summary  
The paper aims to provide an auditable evidence map of large multimodal agents (LMAs) applied to intelligent transportation systems (ITS), clarifying how they are defined, measured, and validated. Its contribution is a systematic review that separates model‑level multimodality from system‑level agency, evaluates empirical performance across functional capabilities, validation settings, and methodological concerns, and ultimately recommends a bounded orchestration approach rather than full replacement of human operators. By mapping 42 primary study families released between January 2023 and August 2026, the authors expose gaps in provenance, challenge handling, and outcome comparison that hinder trustworthy deployment.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The review constructs a comprehensive evidence map of 42 LMA families across a corpus of 91 mapped sources, distinguishing multimodality, agency, empirical performance, and deployment readiness.  
- [Finding 2] LMAs are most effectively deployed for semantic interpretation, intent translation, evidence organisation, scenario authoring, explanation, and specialist‑tool coordination; they should remain bounded rather than replace human authority.  
- [Finding 3] Only one family reaches the highest validation level (E3), none reach E4, and fourteen families achieve C3 functional capability while thirteen are limited to E2.

## Methodology  
The authors surveyed a curated corpus of 91 sources covering all primary study families released between January 2023 and August 2026. They applied an evidence‑mapping framework that evaluates each family on eight methodological‑concern dimensions (Q1–Q8), functional capability levels C0–C3, validation settings E0–E4, and three evidence propositions (P1–P3). The mapping isolates transportation semantics (P1) in 23 families, multidimensional integration (P3) in 24 families, and attempts to reconcile provenance‑challenge‑handling‑comparison‑outcome chains (P2), which remain unresolved.

## Results  
Fourteen families achieve C3 functional capability; thirteen are capped at E2 validation. Semantic evaluation is performed in 23 families, while multidimensional integration is assessed in 24. Evidence reconciliation (P2) is missing across all families. The highest validated outcome (E3) is observed only once, and no family reaches E4, indicating limited real‑world deployment readiness.

## Significance  
This systematic mapping informs stakeholders that LMAs should augment, not supplant, human expertise in ITS. By highlighting where evidence is robust and where it is lacking, the review supports a staged roadmap for accountable deployment, reduces risk of unvetted autonomous decision‑making, and aligns with safety standards that require independent verification.
