## Summary  
The paper investigates whether large language models can reliably assess social attraction based on theory‑grounded persona profiles, comparing LLM judgments to human evaluations across multiple LLMs and participants. It constructs personas using ten psychological and relational constructs organized into three attractiveness tiers: socially attractive, mixed, and unattractive. The study tests model stability, gender sensitivity, and alignment with human rankings in two experiments. Findings reveal that LLMs produce consistent three‑tier orderings, are sensitive to profile valence but not gender presentation, and rank profiles similarly to humans while overvaluing attractive and underrating unattractive profiles.  

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: LLMs consistently produce stable, theory‑aligned three‑tier ordering of persona profiles across multiple runs.  
- Finding 2: LLM ratings show a systematic bias favoring socially attractive profiles and penalizing unattractive ones more than human judges, indicating stronger affective alignment with theoretical attractiveness constructs.  
- Finding 3: No significant gender presentation effect in either LLMs or humans, suggesting that social attraction judgments are not driven by gender cues within the tested persona design.  

## Methodology  
The authors designed two empirical studies. In Study 1, they generated twelve persona profiles derived from ten psychological and relational constructs (e.g., confidence, empathy, ambition) and placed them into three attractiveness tiers. Thirty‑four large language models were asked to rate each profile on a Likert scale in three repeated sessions, measuring inter‑run stability and relative ordering. In Study 2, six matched name‑pronoun pairs with gender‑neutral variants were created; LLMs rated these profiles and also performed a pronoun‑only test. Human participants (n=198) evaluated the same six profiles in Study 3, providing ground truth for comparison.  

## Results  
Study 1 demonstrated high inter‑run consistency: LLM ratings converged within ±0.5 points across runs and maintained the three‑tier hierarchy with strong agreement among models. Study 2 found no significant gender effect; both LLMs and humans rated profiles similarly regardless of name or pronoun, confirming that gender presentation did not influence attraction judgments in this context. Human ratings reproduced the three‑tier structure and ordered profiles identically to LLM rankings (p<0.01). Crucially, LLMs assigned higher positive scores to socially attractive profiles (+2.3 points on average) and lower negative scores to unattractive profiles (‑1.8 points), whereas human differences were smaller and not statistically significant.  

## Significance  
These findings clarify that LLMs can serve as reliable proxies for social attraction judgments when evaluated against theory‑grounded personas, but they are not neutral observers; they exhibit affective biases toward attractive traits. The results also highlight the robustness of gender‑neutral persona designs in reducing spurious bias, offering a framework for evaluating AI’s role in human‑like social assessment.
