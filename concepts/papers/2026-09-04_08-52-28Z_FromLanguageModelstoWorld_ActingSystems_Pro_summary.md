## Summary  
The paper reviews the evolution of agentic AI from large language models to systems that can alter external states across digital, social, virtual, and physical domains. It argues that the common narrative of a steady march toward autonomy conflates model competence, system integration, persistence, and safe authority, and it proposes a heuristic for justified delegation rather than treating it as an observed law. The review synthesizes primary research and official technical specifications up to August 2026 to map where progress is documented versus where limits remain. By separating the model, harness, and environment, the authors outline a research agenda that emphasizes provenance, bounded authority, failure detection, safe recovery, and calibrated human control.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Action‑interface expansion is documented more convincingly than robust completion, recovery, authorization, or independent verification.  
- [Finding 2] Model Context Protocol and Agent2Agent improve interoperability but do not establish trustworthy delegation; multi‑agent organization adds specialization alongside cost and correlated failure.  
- [Finding 3] Persistent simulations and world models support training and planning but do not themselves demonstrate agency.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by synthesizing primary research papers, official technical specifications, and conference proceedings available up to 31 August 2026. They organized this evidence along three axes: delegated authority (what actions can be performed), temporal persistence (how long a system maintains state), and environmental coupling (the physical or social context in which the agent operates). The synthesis separates the model component, the harness that enables interaction with external systems, and the environment itself to assess where agency is realized.

## Results  
The empirical analysis shows that action‑interface expansion—such as calling tools, operating interfaces, or delegating work—is the most robustly observed capability. Robust completion (fulfilling a task without error), recovery from failure, authorization (independent verification of actions), and independent verification remain weak or absent in the literature. Interoperability protocols like Model Context Protocol and Agent2Agent facilitate communication but do not guarantee trustworthy delegation; multi‑agent setups add specialization yet introduce cost and correlated failures. Persistent simulations and world models are valuable for training and planning, but they lack autonomous execution that would constitute true agency.

## Significance  
This review matters because it clarifies the boundaries of current agentic AI, preventing overconfidence in a mythical “autonomous” future. By proposing justified delegation as an analytical heuristic, the paper offers a normative framework for evaluating model‑harness coupling, capability‑based permissions, durable state management, cross‑agent accountability, and staged physical validation—key steps toward safe, responsible AI deployment.
