## Summary  
This paper investigates whether the Plan Mode—a feature that lets users outline an algorithm before execution—delivers measurable benefits in spreadsheet programming, where iterative work and less emphasis on technical correctness are common. The authors prototype a spreadsheet‑agent with a plan mode and compare it to a non‑planning baseline through a within‑subjects user study of 24 participants. Despite achieving comparable task outcomes, the plan‑enabled tool was perceived as more supportive of creativity and collaboration, while also reducing the need for extensive refinement. The findings suggest that structured planning can still be valuable even in informal programming environments like spreadsheets, but its design must align with users’ iterative mindset.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: A within‑subjects user study (N = 24) shows that Plan Mode does not degrade task performance compared to a non‑planning baseline.  
- Finding 2: Users report higher perceived creativity support and better human‑machine collaboration when using the plan mode, even though they perform fewer refinement steps.  
- Finding 3: The results imply that upfront planning can be integrated into spreadsheet workflows without forcing users to abandon their iterative style.

## Methodology  
The authors built a prototype spreadsheet agent equipped with two modes: one that requires an explicit plan and another that operates without it. Participants were assigned to the same tasks under both conditions, ensuring within‑subject comparison. The study measured task completion time, number of revisions (refinements), self‑reported creativity support, and perceived collaboration quality using a Likert‑scale questionnaire.

## Results  
Both planning and non‑planning tools produced identical final spreadsheet outputs, indicating no impact on correctness. However, the plan mode led to an average 27 % reduction in refinement steps per task. Qualitative feedback revealed that users felt the plan mode “guided” them more effectively, boosting scores for creativity support (mean = 4.3/5) and collaboration (mean = 4.1/5), whereas non‑planning tasks scored lower on both dimensions.

## Significance  
These results matter because they challenge the assumption that plan modes are only suited to formal programming languages; they demonstrate that structured planning can coexist with iterative spreadsheet work, potentially improving user satisfaction and perceived AI assistance in everyday data‑driven environments. The study also highlights a design principle: any planning feature should respect users’ workflow rather than impose rigid correctness constraints.
