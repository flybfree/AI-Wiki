## Summary  
Document‑level relation extraction (DocRE) must predict triples among many entities while respecting fundamental relational constraints such as transitivity, symmetry, and functional uniqueness; otherwise LLMs generate contradictory outputs that degrade reliability. The authors propose CONSISTRE, a unified consistency‑aware framework that tackles this problem through two complementary tracks: an inference‑time track for black‑box LLMs and a training‑time track for open‑source models. Both tracks embed explicit consistency modeling without sacrificing extraction performance or requiring costly proprietary systems.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A unified logical formulation of relational constraints (transitivity, symmetry, functional uniqueness) that can be encoded in prompts or loss functions across all deployment scenarios.  
- [Finding 2] An inference‑time track that refines LLM predictions via constraint‑aware prompting, verification checks, and iterative self‑reflection to produce consistent triples without task‑specific fine‑tuning.  
- [Finding 3] A training‑time track that distills teacher reasoning traces into student models through supervised fine‑tuning followed by GRPO alignment using a composite reward that jointly optimizes extraction accuracy and consistency.

## Methodology  
The authors first define each relational predicate as a logical rule (e.g., *A → B* implies *B → A*). In the inference track, candidate triples are generated by an LLM; subsequent verification tests whether they satisfy the constraints. Violations trigger self‑reflection where the model proposes alternative triples, which are re‑checked until consistency is achieved. For the training track, a powerful teacher model produces reasoning traces that are distilled into a smaller student via supervised fine‑tuning on data augmented with consistency penalties; GRPO then aligns the student’s reward to maximize both extraction performance and constraint satisfaction.

## Results  
Experiments on DocRED demonstrate that the inference track reaches an F1 score of ~0.78, matching strong baselines while using only off‑the‑shelf LLMs. The training track narrows the gap between 7–8B open‑source models and state‑of‑the‑art proprietary systems by roughly 30% in F1, at a fraction of their inference cost. Ablation studies confirm that explicit consistency modeling reduces contradictory triples and improves overall reliability across both deployment modes.

## Significance  
By integrating consistency directly into LLM pipelines, CONSISTRE makes extracted relations more trustworthy, enabling higher‑quality information extraction without expensive model upgrades or additional fine‑tuning. This approach bridges the performance gap between open‑source and proprietary models while preserving computational efficiency.
