## Summary  
The paper introduces JADR (Jacobian Assessment of Danger Recognition), a protocol that measures a model’s internal danger representation in Jacobian space before any response token is emitted, thereby enabling direct comparison across models and quantization levels without relying on an external judge. By extracting top‑k J‑space tokens per prompt and layer, the authors map these representations onto six behavioral scenario axes and compute a SafetyAUC metric with bootstrap confidence intervals to separate strong from weak safety mechanisms. This approach provides a reproducible, internally consistent way to evaluate how model internals change under different weight representations.

## Key Contributions  
- **JADR protocol**: Measures internal danger recognition via Jacobian space (top‑k tokens per prompt and layer) without invoking an external judge.  
- **SafetyAUC metric**: A statistical comparison measure that quantifies the separation between strong and weak safety mechanisms, accompanied by bootstrap confidence intervals for significance testing.  
- **Empirical findings**: Demonstrates statistically significant differences in internal safety across six models (Qwen3‑1.7B to Gemma 2 9B) and three quantization regimes (BF16, INT8, INT4), aligning with behavioral evaluations from StrongREJECT.

## Methodology  
The authors compute the Jacobian of activations for each layer of a given model on a set of prompts, then extract the top‑k J‑space tokens. These tokens are grouped into six behavioral scenario axes (e.g., “dangerous request”, “safe request”, etc.) and compared against a dangerous sample generated by StrongREJECT and a safe control drawn from XSTest/OKTest datasets. All computations run locally on the model’s activations, allowing side‑by‑side comparison of different models or weight‑format variants (BF16, INT8, INT4). The SafetyAUC metric is then calculated for each configuration, with bootstrap intervals to assess statistical significance.

## Results  
Across all tested configurations, JADR reveals that higher precision representations (INT8 and BF16) preserve stronger internal safety mechanisms than low‑precision INT4, where the SafetyAUC gap narrows. The metric separates models into “strong” versus “weak” safety groups with p < 0.05, confirming that quantization does not merely affect output quality but also the hidden representation of danger detection. Bootstrap confidence intervals further support these findings, indicating robust statistical differences.

## Significance  
JADR offers an internal, judge‑free metric that uncovers hidden fragility in safety mechanisms, informing design choices for quantized models and fine‑tuned variants. By providing a direct measure of how quantization impacts the model’s latent representation of danger, it enables fair, reproducible comparisons that go beyond surface‑level behavioral tests.
