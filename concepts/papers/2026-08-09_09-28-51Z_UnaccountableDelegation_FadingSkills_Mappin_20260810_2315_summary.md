## Summary  
This paper addresses the need for a job‑specific taxonomy of risks posed by workplace AI agents, arguing that existing risk taxonomies are too broad and miss the nuanced hazards introduced at the human‑agent interface. The authors develop a multi‑layer framework linking agents, goals, and environment, apply it to 2 078 O*NET tasks, generate 8 356 labeled risk scenarios, and extend an existing taxonomy into a 15‑category model that captures both automation and augmentation risks. Their work demonstrates that AI‑agent deployment is not merely a technical issue but also hinges on how workers adapt their skills and interact with the system.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Augmentation can erode worker skills through overreliance, making it unsafe even when agents are technically sound.  
- [Finding 2] Erroneous agent actions constitute the largest share of risk scenarios and often occur at the human‑agent boundary, producing high‑severity outcomes.  
- [Finding 3] Automation mainly creates organizational risks, whereas augmentation primarily threatens workers’ capabilities; workers also prefer this taxonomy for classification.

## Methodology  
The authors first constructed a three‑component framework—agents, goals, environment—to model interactions. They then used the O*NET database to describe 2 078 job tasks, feeding these into the framework to produce 8 356 risk scenarios that were classified by severity and deployment mode (automation or augmentation). Validation involved 45 workers across ten roles and an independent LLM judge, confirming the plausibility of each scenario. Finally, they expanded an existing AI‑risk taxonomy into a 15‑category system covering all generated scenarios.

## Results  
The study produced 8 356 risk scenarios, grouped into 15 distinct categories that capture both automation and augmentation risks. Workers found this taxonomy easier to use than two alternative taxonomies in non‑tied comparisons (64% preference). The LLM judge also validated the scenario alignment with task descriptions, supporting the robustness of the classification.

## Significance  
Understanding these job‑specific hazards is crucial for designing safer AI deployments. By highlighting that risks stem from both agent capabilities and human‑agent dynamics, the work underscores the need for policies that protect skill development and promote responsible collaboration, ultimately fostering more resilient workplaces.
