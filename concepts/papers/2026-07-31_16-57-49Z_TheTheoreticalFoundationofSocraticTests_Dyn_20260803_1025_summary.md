## Summary
This paper introduces the Socratic Test, a novel theoretical framework for automated, computer-mediated conversational assessments that aims to overcome the limitations of traditional static and oral examinations. By integrating Dynamic Assessment principles with multimodal workspaces and advanced taxonomic structures, the proposed model seeks to map a student's cognitive boundaries more accurately than conventional methods. The authors argue that current grading systems often penalize ambition through deficit-based models or introduce performative anxiety via sociological power imbalances inherent in face-to-face interactions. Consequently, this research formalizes a non-compensatory, additive grading architecture that prioritizes mastery and human-AI alignment to ensure unprecedented measurement reliability in educational evaluation.

## Key Contributions
- The development of a theoretical foundation for the Socratic Test, which utilizes graduated scaffolding to quantitatively measure the Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) rather than relying on static knowledge retrieval.
- The integration of Bloom’s Taxonomy for real-time proctoring and the SOLO Taxonomy for structural evaluation, creating a dynamic system that adapts to the student's cognitive level during the examination process.
- The proposal of a non-compensatory, additive grading architecture that shifts the focus from penalizing errors to rewarding mastery, thereby reducing construct-irrelevant variance caused by anxiety and hierarchical power dynamics.

## Methodology
The authors approach this problem through a theoretical synthesis rather than empirical experimentation, drawing upon established pedagogical frameworks to construct a new assessment model. They analyze the flaws in traditional static assessments, which use subtractive grading, and face-to-face oral exams, which exacerbate anxiety and power imbalances. To address these issues, they combine Dynamic Assessment principles with multimodal workspaces, allowing for real-time interaction and feedback. The methodology involves formalizing how graduated scaffolding can be used to probe a student's ZPD, while simultaneously applying Bloom’s Taxonomy to guide the conversation and SOLO Taxonomy to evaluate the structural complexity of the student's responses. This theoretical construction aims to create a system that is both diagnostically rich and objectively reliable through human-AI alignment.

## Results
As a theoretical paper, the primary result is the formalization of the Socratic Test framework itself. The authors demonstrate how the integration of these diverse taxonomic tools can create a coherent assessment environment that actively maps cognitive boundaries. Theoretical analysis suggests that this approach eliminates the need for fixed question banks by allowing the examination to evolve dynamically based on the student's performance. The proposed non-compensatory grading structure is shown to theoretically prioritize mastery, ensuring that partial understanding is valued appropriately without being negated by unrelated errors, thus offering a more nuanced view of student capability.

## Significance
This research matters because it challenges the fundamental assumptions of how student competence is measured in academic settings. By moving away from deficit-based grading and anxiety-inducing oral formats, the Socratic Test offers a more equitable and accurate way to assess learning. It provides a roadmap for leveraging AI not just as a tool for automation, but as a sophisticated pedagogical partner that can engage in meaningful, diagnostic dialogue with students. This could lead to significant improvements in educational fairness and the quality of feedback provided to learners.
