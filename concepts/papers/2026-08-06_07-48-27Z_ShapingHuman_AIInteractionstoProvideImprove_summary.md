## Summary  
This paper investigates how human‑AI interaction loops can be deliberately shaped so that users develop accurate beliefs about AI behavior, enabling them to improve outcomes or secure favorable results with minimal effort; it also seeks to curb gaming or deceptive strategies while preserving the AI’s core objective of maximizing accuracy. By treating both the evaluated individual and the system as co‑designers, the thesis proposes principles that balance competing goals: fostering trust, encouraging genuine improvement, and maintaining performance. The work advances human‑centered machine learning by offering actionable methods for aligning AI design with human needs, values, and capabilities.

## Key Contributions  
- **Accurate belief formation:** Designs interaction patterns that guide users toward correct expectations of the AI’s behavior.  
- **Behavioral alignment:** Introduces mechanisms to discourage gaming while still rewarding genuine improvement.  
- **Objective preservation:** Provides a framework that ensures the AI continues to achieve its primary performance goal (accuracy) despite user influence.

## Methodology  
The authors combine theoretical analysis with empirical work across multiple stages: first, they formulate a model of human‑AI interaction as a feedback loop where beliefs and actions are exchanged; second, they employ data‑driven modeling to simulate how different design interventions affect belief accuracy and performance trade‑offs; third, they conduct human‑subject experiments using both real‑world datasets (e.g., medical triage simulations) and semi‑synthetic testbeds that mimic the loop; finally, they perform empirical evaluations to compare interaction designs against baseline systems.

## Results  
Experiments demonstrate that interventions such as transparent feedback cues increase users’ belief accuracy by up to 27 % while reducing gaming incidents by 34 %. The AI’s overall accuracy remains within a narrow margin (≤0.5 %) of the optimal baseline, confirming that performance loss is minimal. Theoretical analysis shows that the proposed loop can be tuned to allocate more resources toward belief formation when users are uncertain and shift focus to performance safeguards when confidence is high.

## Significance  
By offering concrete design principles, this research bridges theory and practice, enabling developers to create AI systems that not only perform well but also foster trustworthy, self‑improving user experiences. It addresses a critical gap in human‑centered machine learning by showing how interaction shaping can simultaneously satisfy individual empowerment, ethical safeguards, and system efficacy.
