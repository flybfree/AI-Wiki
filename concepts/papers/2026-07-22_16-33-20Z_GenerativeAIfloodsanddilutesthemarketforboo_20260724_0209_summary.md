## Summary  
This paper investigates how generative AI is transforming the commercial landscape of self-published genre-fiction books on Amazon by flooding the market with low-cost, high-volume outputs that are often dismissed as low-quality “slop.” The authors challenge the assumption that such AI-generated content lacks commercial value and demonstrate through empirical analysis that these books do achieve significant sales despite their suspected poor quality. Their findings reveal a structural shift in the book market driven not by improvements in quality but by sheer scale, which dilutes revenue per selling book and reshapes competitive dynamics across genres. This work directly informs debates around fair use and copyright infringement by showing how AI-generated works can dominate commercial spaces.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Books with substantial AI text (over 25% of content) constitute a large share of the catalog but capture only a smaller proportion of sales, indicating that AI-generated books are widely available yet commercially underperforming.  
- [Finding 2] The number of self-published books with observed quarterly sales increased 19.2-fold between 2023 and mid-2026, while total revenue grew only 8.9-fold, showing that the market is adding volume faster than value.  
- [Finding 3] In genres with high Kindle Unlimited availability and high AI diffusion, books without any AI text lose sales dominance, suggesting that AI-generated content disrupts traditional competitive advantages.

## Methodology  
The authors analyzed 14,419 self-published genre-fiction books sold on Amazon from 2023 to June 2026. They used full-text AI detection tools to estimate the proportion of AI-generated text in each book and matched this data with daily sales records over the same period. Crucially, none of the books disclosed whether they contained AI content, allowing for a blind assessment of commercial impact. The study focused on genre-level trends, comparing performance across different availability models (e.g., Kindle Unlimited) to isolate the effect of AI diffusion.

## Results  
Books with detected AI text made up a large portion of the catalog but accounted for fewer sales than books without AI content. Over time, these AI-generated books gained market share and occupied top-rank positions previously held by non-AI books. However, revenue per selling book declined across most genres due to rapid growth in volume outpacing income. Notably, AI-generated books showed higher overlap with language from existing works, suggesting they draw heavily on copyrighted material. The impact was strongest in Kindle Unlimited titles and high-diffusion genres.

## Significance  
This research demonstrates that generative AI is not merely a tool for low-quality content but a systemic force reshaping commercial publishing through scale-driven competition. By flooding the market with affordable, detectable AI books, it erodes revenue per unit sold and weakens traditional quality-based advantages. These findings have direct implications for copyright law, particularly fair use defenses, as they show how AI-generated works can achieve significant market presence despite potential infringement concerns.
