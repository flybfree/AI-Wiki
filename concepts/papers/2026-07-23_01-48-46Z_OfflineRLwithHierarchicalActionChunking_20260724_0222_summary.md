## Summary  
Offline goal‑conditioned reinforcement learning seeks to learn general‑purpose policies from static datasets without any online interaction, but long‑horizon tasks suffer from the “curse of horizon” where value estimates become biased through successive Bellman backups. The paper introduces Hierarchical Implicit Q‑Chunking (HiQC), a method that merges high‑level latent planning with low‑level action chunking to compress both the planning and execution horizons. By conditioning the low‑level critic on temporally extended action sequences, HiQC produces unbiased k‑step value backups, thereby alleviating horizon bias at two levels of decomposition. This dual‑decomposition yields tighter theoretical error bounds than standard hierarchy or flat chunking alone.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: The low‑level critic is conditioned on temporally extended action sequences to produce unbiased k‑step value backups.  
- Finding 2: HiQC jointly decomposes the task into high‑level latent goals and low‑level action chunks, compressing the horizon at both levels.  
- Finding 3: Theoretical analysis shows that this dual decomposition yields a tighter bound on value error under bounded per‑backup error than standard hierarchy or flat chunking.

## Methodology  
The authors propose Hierarchical Implicit Q‑Chunking (HiQC), an offline goal‑conditioned RL algorithm. First, they learn a high‑level latent planner that selects action chunks based on the current task goal. Then, they train a low‑level critic using the extended sequences of actions generated by the planner. By conditioning the critic on these longer sequences, each backup incorporates information from multiple steps, which mitigates horizon bias and enables compression of both planning and execution horizons.

## Results  
HiQC achieves the highest aggregate performance across the OGBench benchmark suite, with its largest gains observed on long‑horizon navigation tasks such as humanoid‑giant. The theoretical bound demonstrates lower variance in value estimates compared to baseline methods. Empirically, the compression of horizons translates into better sample efficiency and greater stability than flat or standard hierarchical approaches.

## Significance  
This work addresses a fundamental limitation of offline RL—value error accumulation over long horizons—by providing a principled, dual‑decomposition approach that improves both theory and practice. It offers a scalable framework for learning complex, goal‑driven policies from static data without online interaction, potentially enabling more reliable and efficient policy generation in real‑world applications.
