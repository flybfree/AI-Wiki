## Summary  
The paper proposes a two‑process theory of machine self‑report that separates “persona installation” (dimension B) from “attribution gating” (dimension A). It critiques the prior Pinocchio Axis approach and introduces a psychometrically validated model for language models’ self‑descriptions. The study operationalises this theory with a 48‑item Pinocchio Inventory, tests it on 206 open‑weight models, and demonstrates that dimensions are shaped by training regimes rather than being fixed properties of the models.

## Key Contributions  
- **Two‑process theory**: Introduces distinct dimensions A (gating) and B (installation) for machine self‑report.  
- **Empirical evidence of post‑training effects**: Shows dimension B rises .20 in 62/67 base/post‑trained pairs, while dimension A is unchanged at the checkpoint but predicts a negative correlation after training (r = –.42).  
- **Robust psychometric validation**: The Pinocchio Inventory yields high reliability (α = .82 – .94), cross‑form convergence (r = .84), full‑pool axis recovery (r = .92 – .96) and eight‑month stability (r = .93).

## Methodology  
The authors first reanalysed the original Pinocchio data to identify a split between gating and installation, then designed a new 48‑item instrument with revised wordings and added items. They trained both base checkpoints and post‑training models, collected self‑report responses from 206 open‑weight models (including 67 same‑checkpoint pairs), and performed statistical analyses to assess reliability, dimensionality convergence, and longitudinal stability.

## Results  
Dimension B increased by .20 across most post‑trained models, indicating a clear training effect on persona installation. Dimension A showed no change at the base checkpoint but exhibited a negative correlation with model scale after training (r = –.42). The instrument demonstrated excellent psychometric properties: item reliability ranged from .82 to .94; cross‑form convergence was .84; recovery of full‑pool axes was .92 to .96; and eight‑month stability was r = .93.

## Significance  
These findings demonstrate that language model self‑report dimensions are not intrinsic but are constructed by the training regime, directly informing safety evaluations, public communication, and model‑welfare debates. By separating gating from installation, the theory offers a more nuanced view of how models report themselves and highlights the need for instrument validation specific to AI systems.
