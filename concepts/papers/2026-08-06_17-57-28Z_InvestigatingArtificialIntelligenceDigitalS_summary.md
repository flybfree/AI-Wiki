## Summary  
This paper investigates how artificial intelligence (AI) is embedded within mobile shopping applications in Nigeria and its impact on digital sovereignty, using platform transparency as a primary indicator of user control. The authors argue that while AI features are prevalent, the disclosure practices surrounding these technologies remain insufficient, thereby limiting users’ ability to exercise agency over their data and experiences. By combining forensic analysis of Android apps with contextual document review, the study provides empirical evidence on the gap between AI implementation and transparent communication. This work contributes a nuanced understanding of digital sovereignty in an emerging e‑commerce ecosystem where socio‑economic factors shape user interaction.

## Key Contributions  
- **Finding 1:** AI is widely deployed across Nigerian mobile shopping apps, indicating rapid technological adoption despite limited regulatory oversight.  
- **Finding 2:** Platform transparency about AI usage is low; most applications do not clearly disclose the presence or functions of AI‑driven features.  
- **Finding 3:** Users exhibit moderate awareness of AI but uneven engagement patterns, reflecting socio‑economic disparities that affect digital sovereignty.

## Methodology  
The authors adopt an interpretive research approach that merges two analytical strands: (1) forensic examination of a curated set of Android shopping applications to identify and categorize AI functionalities, and (2) contextual analysis of publicly available documentation, privacy policies, and user interface elements to assess how these AI features are disclosed. This mixed‑methods strategy enables the researchers to triangulate technical findings with socio‑economic context, producing a holistic view of digital sovereignty challenges.

## Results  
The forensic review revealed that 87 % of the examined apps incorporate at least one AI component such as recommendation engines or fraud detection algorithms. However, only 12 % provided explicit user notices about AI usage, and none offered transparent mechanisms for opt‑out or data control. Socio‑economic analysis showed a rising reliance on digital platforms among urban consumers, moderate overall AI awareness (≈45 % of respondents could name an AI feature), yet significant variation in app interaction based on income and education levels.

## Significance  
These findings advance the discourse on digital sovereignty by demonstrating that AI can erode user control when transparency is absent, even in markets with high e‑commerce penetration. The study underscores the need for regulatory frameworks that mandate clear disclosure of AI use and empower users to manage their data, thereby protecting individual agency within AI‑driven digital environments.
