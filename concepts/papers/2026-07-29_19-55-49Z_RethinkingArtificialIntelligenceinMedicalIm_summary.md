## Summary  
The paper argues that the limited bedside impact of AI in medical imaging stems not from technical shortcomings but from a structural misalignment between how AI systems are built and how clinicians operate. It identifies six interrelated dimensions—pixel‑only models, physician trust erosion, unfulfilled foundation‑model promises, non‑shareable data bottlenecks, validation‑deployment gaps, and prediction‑centric rather than guidance‑oriented outputs—that collectively hinder real‑world adoption. The authors reframe each dimension as a distinct problem and propose concrete pathways toward an agentic AI that augments, not supplants, clinical judgment.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The dominance of pixel‑only models creates a mismatch with the multimodal nature of clinical decision‑making.  
- [Finding 2] Opaque and inflexible AI erodes physician trust, undermining collaborative workflows.  
- [Finding 3] Foundation models remain unrealized in data‑scarce medical domains due to curation and sharing deficiencies.  

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a systematic conceptual analysis that maps existing AI research practices onto clinical decision contexts. They performed a literature review of recent imaging studies, identified recurring design‑evaluation mismatches, and synthesized these into six thematic dimensions. The framework is grounded in interdisciplinary insights from computer vision, medical ethics, and health‑system logistics rather than on new experiments or datasets.  

## Results  
The analysis reveals that each dimension contributes to the observed gap between validated algorithms and clinical utility. Specifically, pixel‑only architectures ignore ancillary patient data; trust deficits arise from lack of interpretability; foundation models are stalled by fragmented data ecosystems; non‑shareable datasets limit model generalization; validation pipelines do not translate to deployable platforms; and prediction‑centric outputs fail to provide actionable guidance.  

## Significance  
By exposing these six misalignments, the paper offers a roadmap for researchers and clinicians to redesign AI systems that respect clinical workflows and data realities. This reframing could accelerate adoption of AI tools that truly augment diagnostic reasoning rather than replace it.
