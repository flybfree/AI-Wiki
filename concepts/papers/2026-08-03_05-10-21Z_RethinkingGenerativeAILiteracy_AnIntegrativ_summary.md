## Summary  
This paper addresses the growing “GenAI literacy lag” in K‑12 teacher education by proposing a comprehensive framework that integrates technical, critical, collaborative, contextual, ethical and agency dimensions of generative AI. The Responsible AI Literacy in Education (RAIL‑Ed) model is built on six interdependent pillars grounded in critical, pragmatist, sociocultural and human‑centered traditions, and it is organized as a developmental three‑level rubric plus a dialectical perspective that shows how the same generative affordance can deepen or displace learning depending on teacher literacy. By treating ethics, equity and agency as constitutive rather than supplementary, RAIL‑Ed offers a theoretically grounded basis for curriculum design, teacher preparation and policy.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The RAIL‑Ed framework comprises six interdependent pillars (Technical Fluency, Critical Evaluation, Human‑AI Collaboration, Contextual Awareness, Ethical Reasoning, Empowered Agency) each marked by three commitments.  
- [Finding 2] A developmental rubric with Emerging, Competent and Advanced levels maps the maturation of each pillar across the K‑12 teacher‑preparation continuum.  
- [Finding 3] The dialectical lens argues that generative AI’s impact on learning is contingent on the teacher’s literacy, not merely on tool adoption.

## Methodology  
The authors conducted a systematic review and qualitative framework analysis of 67 studies published between 2023‑2025. They triangulated findings from critical, pragmatist, sociocultural (Vygotsky) and human‑centered (Freire, Dewey, Shneiderman) traditions to derive the six pillars and their commitments.

## Results  
The framework is presented as a conceptual model with explicit propositions: (1) any missing pillar yields pedagogical failure; (2) each pillar follows the three developmental stages; (3) generative affordances can either deepen or displace learning based on teacher literacy. These propositions are intended for empirical validation.

## Significance  
RAIL‑Ed aligns with UNESCO’s AI Competency Framework for Teachers and OECD/European Commission AILit, providing an evidence‑based model that can guide curriculum development, teacher education programs and policy implementation to close the GenAI literacy gap.
