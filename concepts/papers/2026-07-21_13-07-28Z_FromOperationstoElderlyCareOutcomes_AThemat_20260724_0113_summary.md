## Summary  
This paper conducts a thematic review of 30 seminal studies that intersect industrial engineering and operations research (OR) with elderly care, focusing on three domains: home healthcare operations, polypharmacy management, and clinical chronotherapy. The authors highlight a methodological shift from static, deterministic optimization models toward dynamic, stochastic frameworks augmented by artificial intelligence (AI). Despite these advances, the literature remains dominated by process‑level improvements that fail to translate into measurable patient outcomes. Their contribution is a conceptual framework that moves research attention from isolated operational tasks to integrated, multi‑level decision‑making systems.

## Key Contributions  
- The review categorizes the OR‑elderly care literature into home healthcare operations, polypharmacy management, and clinical chronotherapy, revealing distinct but interrelated problem spaces.  
- It documents an evolution from static deterministic models to dynamic stochastic frameworks that incorporate AI, such as digital twins and large language models.  
- The authors expose a critical translational gap: current OR work optimizes staff routing and resource allocation without linking these efficiencies to actual clinical outcomes or holistic care transitions.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a systematic thematic synthesis of 30 seminal papers published at the intersection of industrial engineering and elderly care. They extracted core themes, mapped methodological trends over time, and identified recurring analytical techniques. This literature‑based analysis was complemented by a conceptual modeling exercise to illustrate the gap between operational metrics and patient‑level outcomes.

## Results  
The synthesis demonstrates that early OR studies relied on deterministic linear programming for staff scheduling, while recent works employ stochastic simulation and AI integration to model uncertainty in care delivery. The primary theoretical result is the identification of three distinct research gaps: (1) lack of outcome‑oriented metrics linking process optimization to health results; (2) absence of holistic models that bridge hospital and community care; and (3) underutilization of human‑inclusive design principles in AI‑driven systems.

## Significance  
Understanding these gaps is crucial because it informs the development of resilient, smart healthcare ecosystems. By emphasizing robust systems analysis, human‑centered design, and the smartification of care through emerging digital technologies, this work guides future research toward interventions that actually improve elderly health outcomes rather than merely streamlining processes.
