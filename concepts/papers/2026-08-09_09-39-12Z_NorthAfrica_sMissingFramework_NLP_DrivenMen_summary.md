## Summary  
The paper proposes a conceptual framework for applying Natural Language Processing (NLP) to mental‑health care in Algeria, identifying four structural barriers that limit current solutions: the language‑of‑care gap, geographic inequities in access, stigma‑related help‑seeking barriers, and the absence of research and digital infrastructure. By mapping existing NLP capabilities to each barrier, it offers a tailored research‑policy roadmap for low‑resource, multilingual settings. This work fills a notable gap in NLP mental‑health literature that has historically focused on high‑income English contexts.

## Key Contributions  
- Identification of four structural barriers: language‑of‑care gap, geographic inequities, stigma‑related help‑seeking barriers, and lack of digital infrastructure.  
- Mapping existing NLP capabilities to each barrier with potential applications and implementation constraints.  
- Proposing a research‑policy roadmap that prioritizes data resources, multilingual language technologies, evaluation frameworks, and regulatory capacity.

## Methodology  
The authors employed narrative synthesis across three sources: global NLP mental‑health literature, Algerian healthcare policy documents, and low‑resource NLP methodological studies. They performed a systematic mapping to align NLP capabilities with the identified barriers, then synthesized these findings into a conceptual framework and actionable roadmap.

## Results  
The analysis reveals that current NLP tools are limited by English‑centric language models, insufficient Arabic/Algerian linguistic resources, and inadequate digital health infrastructure. The proposed roadmap outlines steps: (1) collect Arabic mental‑health corpora, (2) develop domain‑specific multilingual models, (3) establish evaluation metrics, and (4) create regulatory pathways for deployment.

## Significance  
This framework enables culturally appropriate AI‑driven mental health services in underrepresented regions, offering a scalable model that can be adapted to other post‑colonial, low‑resource settings. It bridges the gap between NLP research and real‑world health delivery, fostering responsible AI use where it is most needed.
