## Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of accurate long-term forecasting for large-scale multivariate time series generated by heterogeneous IoT sensors, where traditional foundation models struggle due to their reliance on static parametric knowledge and lack of dynamic external context. The authors propose CrossRAG, a novel retrieval-augmented generation framework designed to overcome specific hurdles in time series applications, such as magnitude variations across different sensor sources and the frequent mismatch between historical similarity and future consistency. By integrating Shape-Aware Memory with RevIN normalization, the system achieves magnitude-robust shape-level retrieval, ensuring that relevant patterns are identified regardless of scale differences. Furthermore, the framework employs Future-Consistent Contrastive learning to filter out hard negatives and utilizes Cross-Attention Temporal Fusion to effectively integrate retrieved historical references into the model's representations.

## Key Contributions
- The introduction of Shape-Aware Memory (SAM) combined with RevIN normalization, which enables robust retrieval of historical patterns by focusing on shape rather than magnitude, thereby solving the issue of heterogeneous sensor scales.
- The development of Future-Consistent Contrastive (FCC) learning, a novel mechanism that distinguishes informative reference pairs from hard negatives that share similar historical trajectories but divergent future outcomes.
- The design of Cross-Attention Temporal Fusion (CATF), which fuses retrieved historical-future reference pairs directly into the backbone’s representations at the feature level, enhancing the model's predictive accuracy without altering its core architecture.

## Methodology
The authors approached the problem by first identifying the limitations of existing retrieval-augmented methods in time series forecasting, specifically their sensitivity to magnitude shifts and inability to ensure future consistency. To address this, they constructed a framework that begins with normalization techniques to stabilize input magnitudes, allowing for shape-based similarity searches within a memory bank. During the retrieval phase, FCC learning is applied to contrast positive pairs (historical sequences with similar futures) against negative pairs, ensuring that only temporally consistent references are selected. Finally, CATF mechanisms are employed to dynamically weight and fuse these retrieved contextual embeddings with the current input sequence, allowing the backbone model to leverage external historical knowledge during inference.

## Results
Extensive experiments conducted on seven public benchmarks demonstrate that CrossRAG consistently outperforms both parametric-only baselines and existing retrieval-augmented forecasting methods. The results indicate significant improvements in long-term forecasting accuracy, validating the effectiveness of combining shape-aware retrieval with future-consistent contrastive learning. The framework shows robustness across diverse datasets, confirming its ability to handle the complexities of heterogeneous IoT data sources effectively.

## Significance
This research is significant because it bridges the gap between static foundation models and dynamic real-world requirements for predictive maintenance and resource scheduling in IoT ecosystems. By providing a method to dynamically access and utilize external historical patterns while mitigating common pitfalls like magnitude variance and irrelevant similarity, CrossRAG offers a scalable solution for improving forecasting reliability in complex, multi-source environments.
