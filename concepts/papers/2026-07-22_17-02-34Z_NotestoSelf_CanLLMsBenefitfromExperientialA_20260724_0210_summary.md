## Summary  
The paper investigates whether Large Language Models can benefit from experiential abstractions—human‑like distilled knowledge such as strategies and reminders—that improve problem solving. By analyzing the solution traces of LLMs on the MATH dataset, the authors extract natural‑language abstractions into a reusable library and test two usage modes: inference‑time retrieval and reinforcement learning with augmentation prompts. The study finds that these abstractions boost performance on mathematical and logical reasoning tasks. Crucially, self‑extracted abstractions match those generated by teachers, suggesting LLMs can generate useful knowledge independently.  

## Key Contributions  
- Experiential abstractions extracted from LLM solution traces improve performance on benchmarks of mathematical and logical reasoning.  
- Self‑generated abstractions produced by the model are comparable to teacher‑extracted ones, indicating that LLMs can create valuable knowledge without human supervision.  
- The abstraction usage framework is transferable to other datasets and models beyond MATH.  

## Methodology  
The authors first compile solution traces from the MATH training set, which contain both correct answers and intermediate reasoning steps. Using these traces, they automatically generate natural‑language abstractions that capture recurring strategies or cautionary insights. Two experimental modes are evaluated: (1) inference‑time retrieval, where retrieved abstractions are injected into the model’s prompt during generation; and (2) reinforcement learning with augmentation prompts, where abstraction‑enhanced prompts guide the reward function. The framework is then applied to a suite of reasoning tasks to measure impact.  

## Results  
Experiments show that incorporating extracted abstractions yields measurable gains on standard math and logic benchmarks, often outperforming baseline LLMs without retrieval or RL cues. When self‑extracted abstractions are used, their performance aligns closely with teacher‑extracted ones, demonstrating that the model’s own knowledge distillation is effective. The framework also transfers to other datasets, confirming its versatility across different problem types and model sizes.  

## Significance  
These findings suggest that LLMs can benefit from experiential abstractions much like humans do when learning from experience, opening a path toward more efficient, self‑improving AI systems that continuously distill knowledge into reusable insights. The work bridges human cognition research with LLM training, highlighting potential for continual learning and adaptation without external supervision.  

## Related Concepts  
- Experiential abstraction: the process of turning raw experience into reusable mental models.  
- Teacher‑student knowledge transfer: using expertly generated abstractions to improve student performance.  
- Reinforcement learning with prompts: guiding model behavior via augmented instruction signals.  
- Retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG): combining external knowledge retrieval with language generation.  
- MATH dataset: a collection of mathematical problems used for reasoning evaluation.  
- Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning: the ability of LLMs to solve complex logical and mathematical tasks.
