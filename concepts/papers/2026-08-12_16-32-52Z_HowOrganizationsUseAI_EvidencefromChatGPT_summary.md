## Summary  
This paper investigates how organizations adopt and use frontier generative AI by linking ChatGPT Enterprise account data to worker roles, task classifications, and public‑company financial information through March 2026. The authors combine these datasets in a privacy‑preserving manner to produce a large‑scale view of adoption patterns, revealing four key empirical findings about the speed, breadth, purpose, and organizational characteristics of AI integration. Their work provides the first systematic evidence that firms differ markedly in how quickly they adopt ChatGPT, which roles are most active users, and what kinds of knowledge tasks the AI supports.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] ChatGPT Enterprise usage has grown rapidly due to both new firm adoption and increasing intensity among existing adopters.  
- [Finding 2] U.S.-based public‑company adoption is concentrated among larger, more valuable firms that are R&D‑intensive and have high SG&A expenses.  
- [Finding 3] Active use within adopting firms spans job functions and seniority levels, with especially high usage intensity among early‑career workers.

## Methodology  
The authors construct a privacy‑preserving analysis by aggregating ChatGPT Enterprise account records—containing timestamps, user IDs, and message metadata—with publicly available firm financial data (e.g., market capitalization, R&D spending, SG&A) and internal task classifications derived from the messages themselves. By matching usage logs to worker roles and classifying each message’s purpose (writing, technical work, communication, synthesis), they generate a sample of over 17 million messages across 1,500 organizations within six months. This linked dataset enables statistical inference without exposing individual user data.

## Results  
The empirical results confirm rapid adoption growth: the number of firms using ChatGPT Enterprise increased sharply after its launch. Public‑company adopters are disproportionately large and financially robust, suggesting that AI investment aligns with existing R&D and administrative budgets. Usage is not limited to senior staff; early‑career employees generate a disproportionate share of messages, indicating that the tool is being used for learning and routine tasks. Moreover, the task mix is broad, covering creative writing, technical problem solving, internal communication, and information synthesis.

## Significance  
These findings matter because they illustrate that enterprise AI adoption is heterogeneous: firms differ in speed, scope, and strategic purpose. The concentration of public‑company usage among high‑value organizations hints at a market segmentation driven by financial capacity rather than pure technology readiness. Finally, the early‑career heavy usage signals that organizations are still experimenting with how to embed generative tools into daily workflows.
