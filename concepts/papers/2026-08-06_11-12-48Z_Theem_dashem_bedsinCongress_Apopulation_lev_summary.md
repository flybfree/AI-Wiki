## Summary  
The paper investigates whether the unspaced em‑dash (U+2014) appears more often in U.S. congressional press releases as large‑language‑model (LLM) writing becomes widespread, using a preregistered analysis of 146 239 releases from 2021–2025. It finds a statistically significant population‑level increase in this typographic trace, with the density doubling and the share of affected releases rising to roughly one quarter, just above the pre‑specified 1.5× threshold. The authors argue that the rise is not confined to a single office or a specific LLM launch but reflects broader diffusion as models mature.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: The unspaced em‑dash density per 1,000 characters rose from ~0.10–0.12 in 2021‑2024 to 0.217 in 2025, more than twice the baseline.  
- Finding 2: The proportion of releases containing such dashes increased from ~13 % to 24.8 %, and the primary frequency ratio (2023‑2025 vs. 2021‑2022) was 1.55 with a 95 % CI of 1.28–1.93, exceeding the registered cut‑off of 1.528.  
- Finding 3: The increase is net‑new (hyphen density unchanged), persists across all 262 continuous offices (p ≈ 1e‑16) and a closed panel of 224 offices, survives three placebo falsification tests, and shows no step at the ChatGPT cut‑off but an acceleration after it.

## Methodology  
The authors employed a preregistered design (OSF: 10.17605/OSF.IO/U5NEY) to analyze 146 239 cleaned press releases from 480 House and Senate offices spanning 2021‑2025. They computed the density of unspaced em‑dashes per 1,000 characters using Poisson and negative‑binomial models with a length offset, then clustered results by office. A segmented regression examined possible step changes at the ChatGPT launch (November 2022) and the 2024/2025 boundary. Falsification tests included three placebo cut‑offs to rule out spurious effects.

## Results  
The empirical density of unspaced em‑dashes reached 0.217 per 1,000 characters in 2025, a 117 % increase over the four‑year baseline. The share of releases exhibiting this mark jumped to 24.8 %. The primary frequency ratio for 2023‑2025 versus 2021‑2022 was 1.55 (95 % CI 1.28–1.93), just above the pre‑specified threshold of 1.528, indicating a statistically significant rise. Net‑new hyphen density remained stable across offices; 75.6 % of continuous offices showed the increase, and all offices in the closed panel exhibited it. No step was detected at the ChatGPT cut‑off, but a clear post‑period acceleration appears.

## Significance  
The findings suggest that the unspaced em‑dash may serve as an early, population‑level marker of LLM assistance in congressional communications, supporting the exploratory claim that stylistic traces become detectable as models mature. While the study does not establish causality, it highlights a potential methodological avenue for detecting AI influence without requiring per‑release authorship attribution.
