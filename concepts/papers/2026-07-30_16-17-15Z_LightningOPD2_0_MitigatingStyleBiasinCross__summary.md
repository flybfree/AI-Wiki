## Summary  
On‑policy distillation (OPD) relies on dense token‑level supervision from a teacher model, but its performance can degrade when the teacher and the SFT reference are generated by different sources or have mismatched styles. The authors show that this mismatch creates a recurring “style‑token bias” that is not captured by standard OPD updates. Their contribution, Lightning OPD 2.0, introduces a cross‑fitted style residualization technique that isolates and subtracts this bias, enabling effective distillation even in cross‑teacher settings where teacher consistency is absent.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Raw teacher–reference disagreement contains both useful context‑specific evidence and a recurring component of style‑token bias.  
- [Finding 2] Lightning OPD 2.0 employs rollout‑level cross‑fitting to estimate this recurring component as an operational proxy for style‑token bias and subtracts it before constructing token‑level OPD updates.  
- [Finding 3] Lightning OPD 2.0 consistently outperforms the baseline Lightning OPD in cross‑teacher scenarios, achieving 82.4 % on AIME 2024 and 63.0 % on LiveCodeBench v5.

## Methodology  
The authors address the problem by first performing a rollout‑level cross‑fit between the SFT reference model and the distillation teacher to capture the systematic differences in wording, formatting, and reasoning cadence. This yields a residual vector that approximates the style‑token bias. During token‑level OPD update, this residual is subtracted from the teacher’s output, leaving only the informative signal. The process is fully cross‑fitted, so it works even when the two models were trained on unrelated data or generated by separate pipelines.

## Results  
Experiments on mathematical reasoning (AIME 2024) and code generation (LiveCodeBench v5) demonstrate that Lightning OPD 2.0 improves over the original Lightning OPD baseline across both tasks. The gains are statistically significant, with the new method reaching 82.4 % on AIME 2024—well above prior OPD performance—and 63.0 % on LiveCodeBench v5, a notable increase from the earlier version’s results.

## Significance  
By decoupling teacher consistency as a prerequisite, Lightning OPD 2.0 makes cross‑teacher distillation practical in real‑world pipelines where SFT data may be generated by different models or sources. This relaxation enables more flexible model selection and reduces reliance on perfect alignment of training and distillation corpora.

## Related Concepts  
- On‑policy distillation (OPD) – token‑level supervised fine‑tuning using a teacher’s output.  
- Teacher consistency – the assumption that the teacher and SFT reference share provenance and style.  
- Style bias / token‑level bias – systematic differences in wording, formatting, or reasoning cadence between models.  
- Cross‑teacher settings – scenarios where teacher and reference are generated independently.  
- Residualization – extracting and removing a component to isolate the signal of interest.  
- Rollout‑level cross‑fitting – aligning two model outputs at the rollout level to estimate shared biases.
