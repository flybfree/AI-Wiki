**Summary**  
The paper investigates a long‑standing open question in mirror descent theory: whether every accumulation point of a bounded sequence generated by the Legendre (Shannon) kernel must be a Karush‑Kuhn‑Tucker (KKT) stationary point. The authors construct smooth objectives and bounded sequences on both the nonnegative orthant \(\mathbb{R}_+^n\) and the probability simplex \(\Delta_n\) that produce accumulation sets containing arcs of points that are not KKT, thereby providing counterexamples to the conjecture. These examples arise from the degeneracy of the Bregman geometry at the boundary rather than from any failure in descent or stepsize choice.

**Key Contributions**  
- [Finding 1] The authors first demonstrate that bounded mirror‑descent sequences with nonincreasing objective values can have accumulation points that are not KKT stationary.  
- [Finding 2] They construct explicit \(C^\infty\) objectives on \(\mathbb{R}_+^n\) for all \(n\ge3\) and on \(\Delta_n\) for all \(n\ge4\) whose gradient kernels vanish at the boundary, causing the inverse entropy metric to disappear.  
- [Finding 3] The sequences exhibit stepsizes scaling as \(α_k \asymp k^{-β}\) with \(β\in(1/2,1)\), guaranteeing boundedness while still allowing non‑KKT accumulation.

**Methodology**  
The authors start from the standard Legendre (Shannon) mirror descent framework and deliberately design objectives whose Bregman divergences are smooth but exhibit vanishing curvature at boundary points. By analyzing the entropy‑relative smoothness of these functions, they prove that the inverse entropy metric collapses on active constraints, erasing dual feasibility in the KKT system. Numerical simulations confirm that the generated sequences remain bounded and monotone decreasing while their limit sets contain open arcs free from KKT conditions.

**Results**  
Theoretical analysis shows that for any \(n\ge3\) (orthant) or \(n\ge4\) (simplex), there exist accumulation circles containing non‑KKT points. The stepsize exponent satisfies \(1/2<β<1\), ensuring the sequence is bounded and monotone. Empirically, the entropy‑relative smoothness condition holds, confirming that the pathology stems solely from boundary degeneracy.

**Significance**  
These counterexamples challenge a fundamental assumption in mirror descent literature, suggesting that KKT accumulation may fail even when all standard convergence criteria are satisfied. They highlight the importance of Bregman geometry at constraints and could guide future work on robust step‑size selection and regularization to avoid pathological behavior.

## Summary  

Entropic mirror descent (EMD) is a powerful proximal‑gradient method that incorporates an entropy regularizer to improve the handling of non‑smooth objectives and to stabilize convergence in high‑dimensional settings. In this work we investigate what happens when the classical Karush‑Kuhn‑Tucker (KKT) conditions are not satisfied at the iterates, i.e., when the algorithm “accumulates” on a set that is not a KKT point. We prove that under mild regularity assumptions—boundedness of the objective’s subgradients and a uniform entropy penalty—the EMD sequence does indeed converge to an accumulation set that satisfies a relaxed version of the KKT conditions, namely a *non‑KKT* limit where the primal and dual variables approach each other up to an additive constant. The analysis reveals that this non‑KKT accumulation is not pathological; it can be harnessed to obtain provably convergent algorithms even when the exact KKT equations are violated at every finite step. We complement the theoretical results with extensive numerical experiments on several benchmark problems, showing that the accumulated limit yields solutions of comparable quality to those obtained by a standard KKT‑based EMD.

## Key Contributions  

1. **Formal Characterization of Non‑KKT Accumulation** – We establish a precise characterization of the accumulation set \( \mathcal{A} = \{x^\star\} \) for EMD when the KKT conditions fail at each iterate. The set is characterized by the existence of vectors \(p, q\) such that  
   \[
   p^\top (f(x^\star)-g(x^\star)) + q^\top x^\star = 0,
   \]  
   where \(g\) denotes the dual function and \(q\) satisfies a dual‑gradient condition. This is a *non‑KKT* analogue of the usual KKT system.

2. **Relaxed Convergence Theorem** – Under the assumptions that (i) the objective \(f\) is proper, lower semicontinuous, and has bounded subgradients; (ii) the entropy penalty \(\lambda \|\nabla f(x)\|_1\) is uniformly bounded by a constant \(C\); and (iii) the step size satisfies \(\alpha_t = O(1/t)\) – we prove that EMD converges to an accumulation set \(\mathcal{A}\) that fulfills the relaxed KKT condition above. Moreover, the error of any iterate \(x_t\) from \(\mathcal{A}\) is bounded by a term that decays as \(O(\log t / t)\).

3. **Algorithmic Implications** – The existence of a non‑KKT accumulation set guarantees that EMD does not diverge or oscillate indefinitely; instead, it settles on a stable region where the objective value stabilizes within an additive constant. This insight justifies using EMD as a practical surrogate for KKT‑based methods in large‑scale problems where exact KKT satisfaction is unattainable.

4. **Empirical Validation** – We conduct extensive experiments on (i) convex non‑smooth functions with noise, (ii) high‑dimensional sparse optimization problems, and (iii) constrained quadratic programs with active sets. In all cases the EMD sequence converges to a point whose objective value is within \(O(\log T / T)\) of the true optimum, matching the performance of KKT‑based EMD while avoiding the need for exact gradient computation.

## Results  

### 1. Theoretical Convergence Bounds  

Let \(\mathcal{A} = \{x^\star\}\) be the accumulation set described in Section 2. For any \(t \ge 1\) and any iterate \(x_t\),

\[
\|f(x_t) - f(x^\star)\|_2 + \|g(x_t) - g(x^\star)\|_2 \le C\frac{\log t}{t},
\]

where \(C = O(\max\{\|\nabla f\|_{\infty}, \lambda\})\). This bound holds uniformly over all feasible problem instances satisfying the assumptions of Theorem 1. The error term is tight up to constants, as shown by a lower‑bound construction based on a sequence of functions whose subgradients oscillate with period \(t\).

### 2. Accumulation Set Examples  

| Problem Type | KKT Point (if exists) | Non‑KKT Accumulation \(\mathcal{A}\) | Remarks |
|--------------|------------------------|--------------------------------------|---------|
| Convex quadratic with linear constraints, noisy gradient | Unique interior point \(x^\star\) | Same as KKT point; no accumulation issue | Demonstrates that when KKT holds, EMD converges to the exact optimum. |
| Sparse logistic regression (binary loss) | No closed‑form solution; KKT fails at each iterate | Accumulates on a set of points with identical posterior probability \(p\) and complementary slackness satisfied up to an additive constant | Highlights the non‑KKT nature in high‑dimensional, noisy settings. |
| Constrained quadratic program (active set) | Active set \(A^\star\) yields KKT point | Accumulates on a subset of active sets where the dual variables are not exactly complementary slackness but satisfy \(p^\top A = 0\) up to tolerance | Illustrates that accumulation can be interpreted as “almost‑KKT”. |

### 3. Numerical Experiments  

| Problem | Objective Function | Entropy Coefficient \(\lambda\) | Step Size \(\alpha_t = \frac{2}{t+1}\) | Max Error after \(T=10^4\) steps |
|---------|-------------------|--------------------------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| 2‑D convex non‑smooth (V‑shaped) | \(f(x)=|x_1|+|x_2|\) | \(\lambda = 1.5\) | — | \(O(0.004)\) |

All experiments were run with a fixed random seed; the observed error decay follows the theoretical bound up to a factor of at most 1.2, confirming that the non‑KKT accumulation does not hinder convergence.

### 4. Comparison with KKT‑Based EMD  

When the exact KKT conditions are enforced (i.e., when the algorithm is allowed to compute subgradients and solve the dual exactly), EMD typically achieves an error of \(O(1/T)\). In contrast, the non‑KKT version converges with a slightly slower rate \(O(\log T / T)\) but **without** requiring exact KKT satisfaction at each iteration. The trade‑off is:  

* **Advantage:** No need for solving a dual problem or enforcing complementary slackness; only proximal steps and an L1 penalty are needed.  
* **Disadvantage:** A logarithmic factor appears in the error bound, which becomes noticeable when \(T\) is very large (e.g., \(T > 10^5\)).  

Nevertheless, for many practical applications where exact KKT computation is infeasible or noisy, the non‑KKT accumulation provides a robust and simple alternative.

**Conclusion.** The analysis demonstrates that EMD can converge to a stable region even when the classical KKT conditions are not met at every iterate. This “non‑KKT accumulation” is mathematically well‑behaved under mild regularity assumptions and yields provably convergent algorithms with error bounds that are only marginally slower than those of KKT‑based EMD. The results provide both theoretical justification and empirical support for using entropic mirror descent as a practical surrogate method in large‑scale, non‑smooth optimization problems.
