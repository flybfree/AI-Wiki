## Summary  
The authors address a key limitation in reinforcement‑learning (RL) training for diffusion language models (dLLMs): on‑policy rollouts generated by the target model often yield few or no positive rewards, stalling progress. To overcome this, they propose **External Rollout Integration with Length Control and Source‑Specific Processing (ERILS)**, which combines high‑reward rollouts from a stronger external policy with the limited on‑policy samples produced by the dLLM itself. Their contribution is threefold: (1) an effective way to integrate external rollouts without destabilizing training, (2) a mechanism that controls the length of external rollouts, and (3) separate reward processing for each source to avoid collapse. These advances demonstrate that rollout construction and reward handling are critical design dimensions when augmenting RL for dLLMs.

## Key Contributions  
- **Finding 1:** Integrating high‑reward external rollouts alongside on‑policy samples markedly improves multi‑sample performance across Sudoku, Countdown, and MATH500.  
- **Finding 2:** Length‑controlled external rollouts are more effective than uncontrolled ones; the proposed length‑control mechanism stabilizes training.  
- **Finding 3:** Source‑specific reward processing prevents training collapse that occurs when on‑policy and external rewards are processed jointly.

## Methodology  
ERILS tackles two practical challenges: (a) differing rollout lengths between the target dLLM and the external policy, and (b) instability from mixing rewards. The authors first generate a set of high‑quality rollouts using an external policy that is stronger than the target model. They then apply **length control** to truncate or extend these rollouts so that they align with the token budget of the dLLM’s generation process, ensuring consistent input lengths (128, 256, and 512 tokens). Crucially, rewards are processed **source‑specifically**: on‑policy rewards are handled by a dedicated branch, while external rewards use another branch that respects the original rollout’s reward distribution. This separation avoids the loss of gradient signal caused by joint reward aggregation.

## Results  
Experiments evaluated under zero‑shot settings show substantial gains: on Sudoku, ERILS reaches **98.4 % best‑of‑4 completion accuracy**, compared with 40.3 % for the strongest baseline; deterministic single‑completion accuracy stays around **90 %** across generation lengths of 128, 256, and 512 tokens. Similar improvements are observed on Countdown (≈78 % vs. ≈55 %) and MATH500 (≈34 % vs. ≈28 %). Component analysis confirms that length‑controlled external rollouts outperform uncontrolled ones, and source‑specific reward processing eliminates the training collapse seen with joint reward processing.

## Significance  
The work highlights that effective RL for diffusion language models hinges not only on the quality of rollouts but also on how they are constructed (length) and processed (source‑specific). By decoupling these design dimensions, ERILS offers a scalable framework that can be applied to other multimodal or sequential generation tasks where external policy assistance is beneficial.

## Related Concepts  
- **On‑policy reinforcement learning** – training using samples generated by the target model.  
- **Diffusion language models (dLLMs)** – models that generate text via a diffusion process.  
- **External policy rollouts** – higher‑quality trajectories produced by a separate, stronger RL agent.  
- **Reward aggregation / joint processing** – combining rewards from multiple sources in a single loss function.  
- **Training collapse** – phenomenon where the model’s performance degrades when reward signals are mixed improperly.
