## Summary  
The paper proposes a two‑stage LLM‑assisted workflow that transforms French maintenance regulations into a structured legal knowledge graph. First, it extracts typed entities and triples from a stratified corpus sample, normalizes labels using embedding fusion, and inducts candidate object properties with their domain‑range signatures. Second, the resulting ontology guides closed extraction of triples over the full dataset to build an RDF‑based knowledge graph. The approach demonstrates that LLMs can reliably produce structured outputs while reducing duplication in legal ontologies.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Robust structured outputs are generated by GPT‑4.1 and mistral‑large‑2512, achieving near‑complete class alignment with the target ontology.  
- [Finding 2] The fusion of embedding‑based normalization significantly reduces duplicated entities and predicates after the first stage.  
- [Finding 3] Predicate normalization and validation of newly observed relation signatures are identified as critical refinement steps for industrial maintenance settings.

## Methodology  
The authors adopt a two‑stage LLM‑assisted pipeline. In Stage 1, they open‑extract typed entities and triples from a stratified sample of French maintenance regulations, normalize label strings via embedding fusion, and induct candidate object properties together with their signature (domain and range). This ontology is then used in Stage 2 to guide closed extraction of triples across the entire corpus, producing an RDF knowledge graph. The workflow leverages GPT‑4.1 for open tasks and mistral‑large‑2512 for closed tasks.

## Results  
Experiments show that the LLM produces highly structured outputs with near‑complete class alignment to the reference ontology. After fusion, duplicated entities and predicates are substantially reduced. Fewer than 20 % of extracted triples introduce unseen properties, while lower exact signature compliance reveals new domain‑range combinations for existing predicates. These findings indicate effective predicate normalization and validation as key refinements.

## Significance  
Maintenance regulations are complex legal texts that hinder case‑specific exploitation and integration into operational systems. By producing an ontology‑grounded French legal knowledge graph, the method streamlines access to relevant information, reduces redundancy, and improves precision for downstream reasoning tasks in industrial maintenance environments.
