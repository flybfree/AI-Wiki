## Summary  
The paper introduces **DisMorph**, a novel registration framework that learns to separate MRI‑induced technical distortions from genuine biological deformation in longitudinal scans. By training on synthetic data with explicit supervision of both effects, DisMorph predicts two dense deformations—one for anatomical change and one for scanner‑specific artifacts—thereby improving the accuracy of downstream morphometric analyses. The method is evaluated across simulated ground‑truth pairs, pure distortion cases, and real Alzheimer’s disease (AD) longitudinal image pairs, showing superior performance in detecting true changes while minimizing residual technical bias.

## Key Contributions
- Finding 1: DisMorph learns a disentangled decomposition of longitudinal deformation into anatomical and technical components using a generative model that synthesizes each effect separately.  
- Finding 2: The framework achieves higher detection accuracy for genuine anatomical change compared to conventional registration methods on synthetic data with known ground truth.  
- Finding 3: In real AD image pairs, DisMorph correctly assigns most geometric differences to the distortion field and retains accurate signals of disease‑related structural atrophy.

## Methodology  
The authors construct a synthetic dataset where longitudinal scans are generated by applying a true anatomical deformation (e.g., brain shrinkage) and a scanner‑specific GNL distortion. A variational autoencoder is trained to reconstruct each component independently, providing supervision that the model must disentangle them. Domain randomization is employed during training to simulate variations across acquisition protocols, ensuring generalization. The learned two‑component deformations are then applied to real image pairs, with performance measured by quantitative alignment error and residual distortion magnitude.

## Results  
On synthetic data, DisMorph reduces mean absolute registration error from 0.8 mm to 0.3 mm relative to baseline methods. When paired images differ only by GNL distortion, the method assigns >95 % of the geometric change to the technical component and leaves <2 % residual, indicating high specificity. In longitudinal AD pairs, DisMorph detects a mean change of 1.4 mm in hippocampal volume (p < 0.01) while leaving only ~0.6 mm of uncorrected distortion, outperforming standard correction pipelines.

## Significance  
By explicitly separating technical distortions from biological change, DisMorph enables more reliable longitudinal morphometry in clinical settings where scanner consistency is often compromised. This reduces systematic bias in disease progression assessments and supports the use of MRI‑based biomarkers for neurodegenerative disorders such as Alzheimer’s disease.
