## Summary  
The paper proposes a reference‑free evaluation framework for open‑ended question answering that focuses on the multi‑step reasoning traces generated by large language models (LLMs). By decomposing each trace into logical segments, the authors label premise‑target relations using Natural Language Inference (NLI) and organize these labels in a hypergraph. A deterministic backward AND‑OR search then assigns segment‑level audit labels that indicate how well each part of the response is grounded within the generated answer. This approach provides a reliable reference‑free signal without relying on final answers or LLM‑as‑judge baselines, enabling more trustworthy assessment in high‑stakes domains.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A hypergraph‑based audit framework that captures local premise‑target relations within reasoning traces.  
- [Finding 2] Deterministic backward AND‑OR search yields segment‑level audit labels that are more reliable than LLM‑as‑judge baselines.  
- [Finding 3] The method improves detection of flawed reasoning on both Hard2Verify (mathematical) and UroReason (medical), outperforming current QA evaluation methods.

## Methodology  
The authors first split a generated reasoning trace into discrete segments, each representing a step or sub‑argument. For every segment they apply NLI to infer whether the premise is entailed by the target statement, producing binary relation labels. These relations are then placed in a hypergraph where premises are nodes and targets are edges. A deterministic backward AND‑OR search traverses this hypergraph from the final answer outward, assigning audit labels that indicate how each segment is grounded within the overall response. The entire process does not require any external reference data or additional LLM verification.

## Results  
On Hard2Verify, the hypergraph audit reduces false positive detections by roughly 15 % and increases the identification of flawed reasoning compared to a baseline where an LLM judges the final answer. On UroReason, the framework uncovers problematic reasoning segments that top‑performing LLMs overlook, achieving higher precision in flagging weakly grounded claims. Overall, the reference‑free hypergraph evaluation provides a more robust signal than direct answer comparison or LLM‑as‑judge approaches.

## Significance  
This matters because current QA evaluation often overestimates model quality by focusing on fluent final answers while ignoring the underlying logical structure of reasoning traces. By grounding evaluation in how inferential relations compose across a trace, the framework enables trustworthy assessment for critical applications such as medical diagnosis, where shallow but fluent responses could be dangerous.
