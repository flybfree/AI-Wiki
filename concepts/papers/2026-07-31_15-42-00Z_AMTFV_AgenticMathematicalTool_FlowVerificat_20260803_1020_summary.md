## Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of reliably verifying candidate answers generated by Large Language Models (LLMs) in complex mathematical reasoning tasks. The authors identify significant limitations in existing self-correction methods, noting that natural-language reflection often lacks computational precision while direct code generation prematurely couples high-level modeling with low-level implementation details. To resolve this, they introduce Agentic Mathematical Tool-Flow Verification (AMTFV), a novel framework that decouples verification logic from concrete execution through an interrupt-execute-resume interface known as Mathematical Tool Flow (MTF). By leveraging a dedicated mathematical toolbox agent, AMTFV enables exact computation and robust adjudication of candidate answers, significantly enhancing the reliability of LLM self-correction mechanisms across diverse reasoning complexities.

## Key Contributions
- **Decoupling Verification Logic from Execution**: The primary contribution is the introduction of Mathematical Tool Flow (MTF), an interface that separates the conceptual modeling of verification from the actual computational execution. This allows the system to handle complex mathematical objects without being constrained by immediate implementation details, thereby reducing errors caused by premature coupling of logic and code.
- **Agentic Workflow for Exact Computation**: The authors propose a multi-agent architecture where a verification agent constructs a workflow and encodes computational intent into MTF requests. A separate mathematical toolbox agent then parses these requests, generates executable calls, and dispatches them to backend tools for exact computation, ensuring high-fidelity results that natural language alone cannot provide.
- **Superior Performance on Complex Reasoning Tasks**: Extensive evaluations demonstrate that AMTFV consistently outperforms representative baselines across five challenging mathematical reasoning datasets. Notably, the framework achieves up to an 8.3 percentage point improvement in average accuracy over the strongest baseline, with particularly significant gains observed in samples requiring medium and high verification complexity.

## Methodology
The authors developed AMTFV as a multi-agent system designed to enhance LLM self-correction through precise tool use. The process begins with a verification agent that analyzes the LLM's candidate answer and constructs a specific verification workflow. Instead of generating raw code or relying solely on text, the agent encodes the necessary mathematical objects and computational intent into an MTF request. This request is sent to a specialized mathematical toolbox agent, which acts as an intermediary. The toolbox agent parses the request, translates it into executable tool calls, and dispatches them to a backend for exact computation. The resulting tool outputs are then used to adjudicate the correctness of the candidate answer, revise incorrect responses, or update the verification workflow itself if necessary. This iterative loop ensures that mathematical reasoning is grounded in exact computational results rather than probabilistic text generation.

## Results
The study evaluated AMTFV on five challenging mathematical reasoning datasets using seven different model configurations from major providers including DeepSeek, GPT, and Gemini. The experimental results indicate that AMTFV outperforms all representative baselines evaluated in the study overall. In terms of specific metrics, the framework improved average accuracy by up to 8.3 percentage points compared to the strongest baseline under individual model configurations. The performance gains were not uniform; they were particularly pronounced on samples characterized by medium and high verification complexity, suggesting that the method is especially effective for difficult problems where standard self-correction mechanisms typically fail due to computational inaccuracies.

## Significance
This research matters because it tackles a fundamental bottleneck in deploying LLMs for rigorous mathematical tasks: the lack of reliable verification. By decoupling modeling from execution, AMTFV provides a more robust and scalable approach to self-correction that minimizes the hallucination and calculation errors inherent in pure text-based reasoning. This advancement is crucial for applications requiring high precision, such as scientific computing, education, and automated theorem proving, where approximate answers are insufficient.
