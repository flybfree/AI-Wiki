## Summary  
The paper introduces **FILLER**, a feature‑imputation framework that deliberately explores the two‑dimensional latent space generated by a generative model to fill missing values in corrupted test samples. By training G‑NeuroDAVIS on fully observed data, FILLER retrieves appropriate entries from this latent space, thereby balancing scalability and structural consistency—a key challenge in real‑world machine learning. The authors also provide a mathematical proof of convergence for the iterative search process. Experimental results on several image datasets demonstrate that FILLER outperforms existing state‑of‑the‑art strategies in terms of RMSE, PSNR, SSIM, and downstream classification/clustering performance.

## Key Contributions  
- **FINDING 1:** FILLER integrates latent location exploration with retrieval to produce accurate imputations.  
- **FINDING 2:** G‑NeuroDAVIS serves as a generative model that can be trained on complete data and used for consistent imputation.  
- **FINDING 3:** The authors prove the convergence of the iterative search algorithm, guaranteeing stability under repeated queries.

## Methodology  
The methodology follows two main stages: first, G‑NeuroDAVIS is trained on a dataset where every observation is complete; this training produces a 2‑D latent space that captures the underlying distribution of features. Second, for each corrupted test sample with missing entries, FILLER queries the learned latent space using retrieval mechanisms to select candidate values and iteratively refines them until convergence. The iterative process leverages the structure of the latent space to ensure that imputed values preserve the global consistency of the data manifold.

## Results  
Experiments were conducted on multiple image datasets with both random and structured missingness patterns, varying in the proportion of missing features. Compared with state‑of‑the‑art methods, FILLER achieved lower RMSE (≈ 5–8 % reduction), higher PSNR (≈ 3–5 % improvement) and better SSIM scores (≈ 2–4 % gain). A Wilcoxon signed‑rank test confirmed that these gains are statistically significant. Downstream tasks—such as classification accuracy and clustering quality metrics—also improved, indicating that the imputed features retain discriminative power.

## Significance  
Feature imputation is a critical preprocessing step for many ML pipelines; however, existing approaches often sacrifice either scalability or structural fidelity. FILLER addresses both concerns by providing a mathematically sound, scalable method that maintains the integrity of the data manifold. The convergence proof adds theoretical assurance, while empirical results demonstrate practical superiority across diverse scenarios.
