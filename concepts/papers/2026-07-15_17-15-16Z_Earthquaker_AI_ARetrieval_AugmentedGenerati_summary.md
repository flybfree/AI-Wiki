## Summary  
Earthquaker-AI is a retrieval‑augmented generation framework that augments the award‑winning STEM robotics project Earthquaker with an AI assistant to teach primary school students earthquake safety through progressive cognitive tasks. It combines hands‑on Lego WeDo2 simulations, rubric‑based feedback, and RAG‑driven conversational responses to promote self‑regulated learning under emergency conditions.

## Key Contributions  
- The framework introduces a hybrid robotics‑AI system that maps student queries via semantic retrieval to official safety guidelines, reducing hallucinations while maintaining high accuracy.  
- It implements a tiered rubric system—two‑dimensional for early grades, three‑axis for middle grades, four‑dimensional for upper grades—that aligns assessment with cognitive development stages.  
- Experimental results demonstrate that the RAG module achieves >90% groundedness and low hallucination rates across all grade levels.

## Methodology  
The authors built upon the existing Earthquaker robotics platform by integrating a conversational AI assistant powered by Retrieval‑Augmented Generation. The system uses Lego WeDo2 to simulate seismic response, while the RAG module retrieves relevant safety protocols from an official database and generates responses. Assessment is performed using multi‑dimensional rubrics that evaluate multiple dimensions of student output at each developmental stage.

## Results  
In controlled classroom trials with 120 primary school students across three grade bands, the AI assistant provided correct safety guidance in 93% of interactions. Rubric scores showed consistent improvement from early to upper grades, with average clarity scores rising from 2.4/4 to 3.7/4. The low hallucination rate (≈5%) and high groundedness (>90%) confirmed the system’s reliability.

## Significance  
Earthquaker‑AI advances educational AI by merging tangible robotics learning with cognitive‑aware conversational support, fostering early crisis‑management skills and responsible digital literacy among young learners.
