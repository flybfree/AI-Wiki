## Summary  
The paper addresses the challenge of applying vision‑language models to time‑series anomaly detection while maintaining efficiency and interpretability. It introduces VisAnomBench, a benchmark that pairs public time‑series data with high‑quality natural‑language explanations generated by large VLMs. By fine‑tuning a parameter‑efficient VLM on this dataset, the authors develop VisAnomReasoner, which localizes anomalies more accurately than existing baselines. The method achieves substantial gains in precision and F1 scores across multiple benchmarks.  

## Key Contributions  
- Construction of VisAnomBench, a curated benchmark integrating time‑series data with high‑quality anomaly rationales from large VLMs.  
- Development of VisAnomReasoner, a parameter‑efficient vision‑language model fine‑tuned for time‑series anomaly detection that produces interpretable decisions.  
- Demonstration that VisAnomReasoner improves precision and F1 by at least 21.23% and 23.87% on VisAnomBench and further gains of 9.57% and 13.39% on TSB‑AD‑U.  

## Methodology  
The authors address the gap between raw VLM outputs and interpretable anomaly explanations by first compiling a dataset where each time‑series segment is annotated with natural‑language rationales derived from multiple large vision‑language models, ensuring task‑specific relevance. They then fine‑tune a lightweight VLM using these paired inputs, leveraging reward signals that prioritize both detection accuracy and explanation fidelity. The training process is parameter‑efficient, employing knowledge distillation or adapter modules to minimize additional parameters while preserving model capacity.  

## Results  
Experimental evaluation on VisAnomBench shows VisAnomReasoner surpasses all baselines in anomaly localization with precision gains of 21.23% and F1 scores of 23.87%. On the TSB‑AD‑U benchmark, it further improves precision by 9.57% and F1 by 13.39%, confirming cross‑benchmark generalization. Ablation studies indicate that the inclusion of natural‑language rationales is critical to achieving these gains.  

## Significance  
This work bridges the interpretability gap in multimodal anomaly detection, enabling models to produce human‑readable justifications that are essential for trustworthy deployment in safety‑critical domains. By demonstrating that a small, parameter‑efficient VLM can outperform larger baselines when guided by curated explanations, it offers a scalable path toward efficient and explainable time‑series monitoring.
