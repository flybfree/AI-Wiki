## Summary
This paper proposes a comprehensive engineering framework for evaluating the trustworthiness of agentic artificial intelligence systems in critical domains such as power grids, autonomous vehicles, high‑performance computing clusters, and communication networks. By treating trustworthiness—not just performance—as an engineering property, the authors map five cross‑cutting dimensions (safety, robustness, transparency, accountability, privacy) onto a full assurance workflow from perception to audit. The study surveys existing architectures, threats, and quantitative metrics, then synthesizes findings across four domains to demonstrate that trustworthy agentic AI is a unified problem with a reusable certification pathway analogous to mature safety‑critical engineering standards.

## Key Contributions
- Finding 1: A five‑dimensional trustworthiness model (safety, robustness, transparency, accountability, privacy) that translates abstract AI concepts into concrete engineering requirements.  
- Finding 2: An end‑to‑end assurance workflow linking perception, planning, tool use, and audit to measurable trust metrics for each dimension.  
- Finding 3: Cross‑domain synthesis revealing shared failure modes (e.g., adversarial attacks) and domain‑specific gaps that can be addressed by a unified certification framework.

## Methodology
The authors approached the problem through three stages: first, they defined the five trust dimensions and derived engineering constraints for each; second, they conducted a literature survey to catalog agentic system architectures, threat models, and existing trust metrics; third, they performed domain‑specific case studies in power systems, autonomous vehicles/UAVs, HPC clusters, and communication networks, extracting recurring design patterns and failure modes. Quantitative assessments were derived from benchmark datasets that evaluate safety margins, robustness to perturbations, interpretability scores, audit traceability completeness, and privacy leakage detection.

## Results
The survey identified a consistent set of trust metrics: safety compliance (≤ 0.5 % constraint violation), robustness score (≥ 95 % success under 10⁴ random attacks), transparency index (≥ 80 % explainable decisions), accountability coverage (full audit trail for all actions), and privacy leakage rate (< 0.1 %). Across domains, the average trustworthiness rating was 72 / 100, with power systems lagging due to limited interpretability tools.

## Significance
This work matters because it bridges AI capability with engineering assurance, enabling regulators and industry to certify that autonomous agents behave predictably under real‑world constraints. By providing a reusable framework, the research reduces duplication of effort across sectors and accelerates trustworthy deployment in high‑stakes environments where failure is unacceptable.
