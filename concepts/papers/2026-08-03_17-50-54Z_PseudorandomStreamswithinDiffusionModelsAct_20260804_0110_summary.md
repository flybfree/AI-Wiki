## Summary  
The paper investigates how pseudorandom streams used in diffusion models can serve as learnable inputs that influence generation quality, showing that the stochastic nature is not merely a distributional artifact but a model‑dependent structured input. It demonstrates that replacing real images with online random tensors while preserving the diffusion architecture leads to measurable degradation in loss and image quality on MNIST and CIFAR‑10. The authors also introduce a probe to measure predictability of orbit sequences via an MLP, linking it to rank correlations with degradation. These findings suggest that stochasticity within diffusion models is a learnable component affecting training dynamics.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Pseudorandom streams in diffusion models act as learnable inputs whose concrete values affect both loss and gradient.  
- Finding 2: A lightweight MLP predicts the next orbit value from recent history, capturing sequence predictability that correlates with degradation.  
- Finding 3: After controlling for marginal statistics and finite‑precision issues, the probe loss and real‑data loss follow empirical power laws with dataset‑specific exponents.

## Methodology  
The authors replace the stochastic input of diffusion models with an online random tensor generated by a deterministic pseudorandom rule. They train a small multilayer perceptron to predict subsequent values from recent history, measuring sequence predictability as a proxy for learnable information. A diffusion probe replaces real images with these random tensors while keeping the same architecture and objective, allowing them to compare loss and generation quality between stochastic and deterministic inputs. To isolate model‑dependent effects, they control marginal distributions, screen out clear dynamical or precision failures, and normalize results against an i.i.d. baseline.

## Results  
The probe loss and real‑data diffusion loss exhibit strong rank correlations with overall degradation on both MNIST (exponent ≈ 2.3) and CIFAR‑10 (exponent ≈ 1.8). Local rankings differ, indicating varying sensitivity across datasets. Normalized to the i.i.d. baseline, both losses follow power laws, confirming that stochasticity is not merely a noise source but contributes structured information.

## Significance  
This work reveals that stochasticity in diffusion models is a learnable component influencing training dynamics and generation quality, challenging the view of randomness as purely distributional. It opens avenues for controlling stochastic inputs to improve robustness or efficiency.
