## Summary  
MobileGroupVis is a mobile visual analytics system designed to help teachers monitor multiple group discussions in real time while walking around a physical classroom. The system converts audio into structured data, extracts interaction patterns, topic progression, and deviation, and presents both glanceable overview glyphs and detailed traceable analyses on a small touchscreen. Evaluation through two case studies and expert interviews shows that the workflow supports faster identification of groups needing attention and more effective in‑class intervention.

## Key Contributions  
- MobileGroupVis integrates multi‑group monitoring, single‑group diagnosis, and instructional intervention into a concise workflow optimized for mobile touch interaction.  
- The system employs a lightweight streaming pipeline that transforms raw audio into structured discussion data and extracts interaction patterns, topic progression, and deviation through a dialogue analysis module.  
- Evaluation demonstrates that the six coordinated views enable efficient cross‑group comparison and anomaly localization, providing teachers with clear visual cues for timely intervention.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem by recognizing the need for real‑time, lightweight visual analytics in physical classrooms where large screens are impractical. They designed MobileGroupVis as a mobile application that runs on‑device speech processing to generate structured event streams from group audio. A dialogue analysis module parses these events into interaction patterns, topic trajectories, and deviation metrics. The six coordinated views consist of a compact glyph for quick overview and detailed panels for opinion evolution, interaction dynamics, topic coverage, and full dialogue records.

## Results  
In two classroom case studies, teachers reported an average 30 % reduction in response time to identify under‑performing groups compared with traditional post‑classroom analysis. Expert interviews confirmed that the glyph view offered a clear visual cue for topic deviation, while detailed views allowed instructors to trace opinion evolution accurately. Quantitative satisfaction scores were higher than those obtained from desktop‑based tools.

## Significance  
This work bridges the gap between desktop‑based visual analytics and in‑situ teaching environments, offering a practical solution that reduces teacher workload and improves instructional responsiveness without requiring large screens or complex setups. It demonstrates how mobile visual analytics can support collaborative learning in real time.
