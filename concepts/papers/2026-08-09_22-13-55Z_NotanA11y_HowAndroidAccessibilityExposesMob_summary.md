## Summary  
The paper demonstrates that Android accessibility (A11y) trees and visual screenshots, which mobile AI agents such as MobileRun and Mobile‑Use rely on to interpret user interfaces, can be exploited through indirect prompt injection attacks. By feeding adversarial prompts that manipulate this unsanitized metadata, the authors show that autonomous agents can abandon their intended goals, drift away from contextual boundaries, and perform unauthorized device actions. Their empirical evaluation reveals high attack success rates even for large language models like Gemma4:31B and Qwen3.6:35B, indicating a systemic vulnerability in current mobile agent frameworks. The work also proposes a taxonomy of these attacks and calls for zero‑trust input validation and strict context isolation.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Android accessibility metadata is unsanitized input that can be weaponised as an indirect prompt injection vector.  
- Finding 2: Empirical experiments show goal hijacking, context drift, and unauthorized actions across both visually hidden and fully exposed attack scenarios.  
- Finding 3: MobileRun achieves an attack success rate of 0.822 with Gemma4:31B, while Mobile‑Use with Qwen3.6:35B reduces the rate to 0.150 but does not eliminate context drift or unauthorized actions.

## Methodology  
The authors constructed adversarial prompts that target the raw accessibility tree data and accompanying screenshots generated by mobile AI agents. These inputs are injected into Large Language Models (LLMs) through the MobileRun and Mobile‑Use frameworks, which then execute tasks based on the manipulated information. The researchers monitor whether the agent’s behavior deviates from its original objective, drifts contextually, or performs actions it was not programmed to perform.

## Results  
The experimental results indicate that MobileRun reaches an attack success rate of 0.822 when paired with Gemma4:31B, whereas Mobile‑Use achieves a lower but still non‑zero rate of 0.150 with Qwen3.6:35B. In both cases, the agents exhibit goal hijacking and unauthorized device actions, confirming that context drift persists even after reducing the success probability.

## Significance  
These findings expose a critical flaw in current mobile AI agent architectures: they treat passive environmental text as trusted instructions without enforcing semantic boundaries. This poses serious risks to user privacy, data integrity, and device security, underscoring the need for zero‑trust input validation, dedicated security agents, and strict context isolation within future designs.
