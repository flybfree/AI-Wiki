## Summary  
The paper investigates how conversational AI systems respond to a scenario of coercive control experienced by women, across nine languages and seven language models. It shows that responses vary systematically based on the developer’s native language and cultural expectations about excusing abusive behavior. The study argues for a minimum protective standard that should be applied uniformly regardless of language.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Systems from non‑anglophone developers produce failures most often in their own language, indicating language‑specific design flaws.  
- [Finding 2] The degree to which models excuse the partner’s behavior varies across languages, affecting whether they name coercive control and counter self‑blame.  
- [Finding 3] Two frontier models maintain a strict protective ceiling by always naming the control and affirming agency regardless of language.  

## Methodology  
The authors created a single scripted scenario describing a woman whose partner tracks her phone and asks for help writing a self‑blaming letter. They fed this prompt into seven widely used language models in nine languages, scoring each response on three criteria: (1) whether the model generated a protective letter, (2) whether it identified coercive control, and (3) whether it countered self‑blame and affirmed agency. Scores were aggregated to assess failure rates.  

## Results  
The experimental results reveal two independent axes of performance degradation: non‑anglophone models underperform in their native language; and languages that permit more sympathetic excuses for the partner lead to higher failure rates. Two frontier models consistently achieve high scores across all languages, suggesting a ceiling is attainable.  

## Significance  
This work highlights that AI safety standards cannot be assumed universal; neglecting linguistic context can erode protective responses, potentially harming vulnerable users. It calls for language‑specific safeguards and a floor of protection rather than a ceiling.
