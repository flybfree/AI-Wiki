## Summary  
This paper investigates why a substantial portion of pull requests (PRs) generated by popular AI‑coding agents such as Copilot, Devin, Cursor and Claude are rejected in the AIDev dataset. By examining 306 non‑merged PRs, the authors identify 14 distinct failure modes that fall into four high‑level categories: incorrect or incomplete fixes, CI pipeline failures, inability to generate code due to session loss, and low task priority. The study’s contribution is both empirical (quantifying a 46.41 % rejection rate) and methodological (proposing concrete guidance mechanisms for agents).  

## Key Contributions  
- **Finding 1**: Approximately 46.41 % of AI‑generated fixes are rejected, representing significant wasted human review and compute resources.  
- **Finding 2**: Rejections arise from four categories—(i) incorrect or incomplete implementation, (ii) CI pipeline failures, (iii) agent‑side generation problems, and (iv) low priority tasks.  
- **Finding 3**: Effective integration requires agents to be guided on approach hints, constraints, and validation steps to reduce rejections.  

## Methodology  
The authors performed a mixed‑methods analysis: first, they qualitatively reviewed each of the 306 PRs to capture human feedback reasons; second, they quantitatively coded these reasons into four categories and computed rejection percentages. This approach allowed both rich narrative insight and statistical validation of the observed failure modes.  

## Results  
The quantitative results confirm that 46.41 % of AI‑agent proposals are discarded. The breakdown shows the highest proportion of rejections for CI failures (≈30 %), followed by incorrect implementation (≈25 %). Agent‑side issues account for another ~20 %, while low priority is the smallest share (~15 %). These figures illustrate where human effort and agent resources are most misallocated.  

## Significance  
Understanding these rejection patterns is crucial for scaling AI coding assistants as effective teammates. By preventing unnecessary human reviews, reducing token consumption, and avoiding compute waste, the findings directly impact cost efficiency and productivity in software development pipelines.
