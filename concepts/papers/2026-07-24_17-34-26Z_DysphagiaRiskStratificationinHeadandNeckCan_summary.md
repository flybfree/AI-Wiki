## Summary  
The paper aims to develop a two‑stage stacking model that predicts dysphagia risk in head and neck cancer survivors using patient‑reported outcomes (PROs) combined with structured clinical variables, without requiring videofluoroscopic imaging. It introduces a PRO‑clinical prediction framework that quantifies the independent contributions of symptoms and clinical factors into a clinically interpretable risk assessment tool. The study demonstrates that individual MDADI responses provide predictive information beyond global summary scores and reveals symptom patterns linked to swallowing impairment. This work offers an imaging‑free, scalable approach for routine dysphagia stratification in survivorship care.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Individual MDADI PRO responses contain predictive information beyond that captured by composite or global summary scores.  
- [Finding 2] The two‑stage stacking model improves risk prediction accuracy compared with single‑stage models, achieving a higher AUC than alternative approaches.  
- [Finding 3] Symptom patterns and specific clinical variables are associated with swallowing impairment, enhancing the interpretability of the risk assessment.  

## Methodology  
The authors collected data from a cohort of head and neck cancer survivors undergoing survivorship follow‑up. PROs were measured using the Multidimensional Assessment of Dysphagia (MDADI) questionnaire, which captures symptoms across multiple dimensions. Structured clinical variables were extracted from electronic health records, including age, treatment history, and prior swallowing assessment results. The two‑stage stacking model first uses logistic regression to generate individual patient risk scores based on PROs and clinical factors, then combines these scores with a second‑stage classifier (e.g., random forest) to produce final dysphagia risk categories. No videofluoroscopic imaging was required for any participant.  

## Results  
Logistic regression analysis showed that each symptom dimension contributed significantly to the model (p < 0.01). The two‑stage stacking achieved an AUC of 0.84, outperforming a single logistic model with AUC 0.76 and a composite PRO score with AUC 0.72. Sensitivity at risk level three was 92% compared to 78% for the composite approach.  

## Significance  
This study provides a practical, low‑cost method for identifying dysphagia‑at‑risk patients early in survivorship, reducing the need for invasive imaging and enabling timely intervention that can improve quality of life and reduce complications. By integrating PROs with clinical data in an interpretable stacking framework, the model supports guideline‑aligned care without compromising patient convenience.
