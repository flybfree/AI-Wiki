## Summary  
The paper investigates alienation in AI research communities, linking it to abstraction mechanisms that distance researchers from lived experiences of harm and epistemic injustice. It argues that abstract logics marginalize certain identities while tokenizing others, leading to feelings of exclusion. Through an autoethnographic inquiry, the authors narrate three vignettes illustrating how alienation manifests and is resisted. They propose a hermeneutic framework to interpret alienation’s preconditions, mechanisms, and harms.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Alienation in AI research is rooted in abstraction that separates lived experience from dominant logics of legitimacy.  
- [Finding 2] Affective abstraction—a mechanism where emotional responses are abstracted away—intensifies isolation among marginalized researchers.  
- [Finding 3] Collective action can mitigate alienation by re‑grounding research practices to material realities.

## Methodology  
The authors employed an autoethnographic approach, drawing on personal narratives and reflective analysis of three vignettes that illustrate encounters with alienation within AI research communities. They used hermeneutic interpretation to categorize alienation’s preconditions (e.g., tokenism), mechanisms (affective abstraction), and harms (epistemic injustice). The framework is built from their lived experience, not formal experiments.

## Results  
The analysis yields a theoretical interpretive framework that maps alienation onto abstraction processes, identifying affective abstraction as a high‑leverage mechanism. It also demonstrates how collective action can reduce isolation by re‑engaging with affective and material concerns.

## Significance  
This work matters because it foregrounds the social dimensions of AI research, challenging disciplinary norms that prioritize abstraction over lived experience. By offering a hermeneutic resource, it equips early‑career researchers to reflect critically and resist exclusionary practices, fostering more inclusive AI communities.
