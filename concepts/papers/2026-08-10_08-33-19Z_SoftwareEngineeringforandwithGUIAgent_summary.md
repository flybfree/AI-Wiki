## Summary  
The paper argues that GUI agents, despite rapid technological progress, lack a mature software‑engineering foundation and are vulnerable to real‑world deployment. It contributes a systematic review of 336 GUI‑agent papers (2018‑2026) that identifies three core findings: the field’s architectural imbalance toward perception‑reason‑act loops while recovery, human escalation, safety, and observability remain underdeveloped; evaluation practices are still task‑centric and incomparable across protocols; and existing work provides limited support for post‑release testing, maintenance, privacy, and governance. By linking these gaps to lifecycle‑centered engineering principles, the authors propose future research that couples dependable execution with reproducible testing, auditability, and human‑centred oversight to achieve deployable GUI‑agent systems.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The field’s architectural imbalance: modular perceive‑reason‑act loops dominate, but recovery, human escalation, safety enforcement, and auditability are underdeveloped.  
- [Finding 2] Evaluation practices remain task‑success focused and lack comparability across different GUI‑agent protocols.  
- [Finding 3] Existing studies provide limited support for testing beyond benchmarks and for maintaining agents after release.

## Methodology  
The authors performed a comprehensive literature review of 336 GUI‑agent papers published between January 2018 and April 2026. They answered five research questions covering the current landscape, architectural trends, evaluation methodologies, software‑lifecycle concerns, and future opportunities. This systematic analysis was used to surface recurring themes and gaps in the field.

## Results  
Since 2024 the GUI‑agent community has expanded sharply, with mobile and web environments still being the primary deployment contexts. Architectures increasingly adopt modular perceive‑reason‑act loops, yet recovery mechanisms, human escalation pathways, safety enforcement, and auditability are rarely addressed. Evaluation studies have become more interactive but continue to measure only task success, making cross‑protocol comparisons difficult. Moreover, research offers little guidance on post‑release testing, agent maintenance, observability, privacy engineering, or systematic human oversight.

## Significance  
Connecting dependable execution with lifecycle‑centered testing and reproducible evaluation is essential for building GUI‑agent systems that are secure, maintainable, and deployable in real environments. Integrating permission and privacy controls with cost‑aware, human‑centred governance will enable agencies to move beyond capability improvements toward robust, production‑ready solutions.
