## Summary  
This paper investigates how prosodic cues—particularly duration and intensity—correlate with the pragmatic functions of the Persian discourse marker hālā ‘now’, which is known for its multifunctional use. By analyzing 267 spontaneous speech examples, the authors demonstrate that hālā often serves multiple textual, interactive, and modal roles simultaneously, and they identify specific prosodic patterns that differentiate these functions. The study contributes empirical evidence linking acoustic properties to pragmatic interpretation in a non‑English language.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Duration is longer for temporal uses of hālā and shorter for textual uses, while intensity peaks for interactive functions.  
- [Finding 2] Textual functions dominate the marker’s usage (≈70 % of tokens), followed by interactive and modal roles, indicating strong multimodal flexibility.  
- [Finding 3] Prosodic cues reliably differentiate at least two pragmatic categories: textual uses show lower intensity, whereas interactive uses exhibit higher intensity.  

## Methodology  
The authors collected a corpus of 267 utterances from natural Persian conversations and recorded them with synchronized acoustic features (duration and intensity). They applied a mixed‑method approach: first, they coded each occurrence for its primary pragmatic function using a detailed annotation scheme; second, they extracted prosodic metrics per token; third, they performed statistical comparisons to test whether functional categories differ systematically in duration or intensity.  

## Results  
Statistical analysis revealed that temporal hālā tokens had significantly longer durations (p < 0.01) compared with textual tokens, while interactive tokens showed the highest average intensity (p < 0.05). Textual functions were associated with shorter durations and lower intensity, whereas modal functions displayed a mixed pattern but tended toward slightly reduced intensity. The findings confirm that prosodic cues are not merely decorative but serve as functional markers in Persian discourse.  

## Significance  
Understanding the link between prosody and pragmatics enhances models of cross‑linguistic speech comprehension, especially for low‑resource languages like Persian where acoustic information is a primary cue for meaning. The results also suggest that computational systems can improve hālā parsing by incorporating duration and intensity signals.
