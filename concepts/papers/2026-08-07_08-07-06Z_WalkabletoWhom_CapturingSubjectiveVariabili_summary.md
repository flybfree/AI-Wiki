## Summary  
This paper addresses a critical limitation in urban planning and accessibility research: the assumption that walkability perception is uniform across individuals. By introducing a multimodal deep learning framework, the authors capture subjective variability in how people perceive sidewalk environments, moving beyond aggregated scores to model diverse user experiences. The study integrates visual imagery with individual rater attributes to produce more inclusive assessments of pedestrian environments. This work represents a significant step toward person-centered urban design evaluation.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Walkability perception is highly subjective and varies significantly across individuals due to personal characteristics, experiences, and preferences, challenging the use of single aggregated scores in research.  
- [Finding 2] Sidewalk-view imagery yields higher walkability ratings than matched street-view images, revealing that image source critically influences perceptual judgments beyond visual content.  
- [Finding 3] A user-conditioned multimodal deep learning model improves rank agreement with observed ratings by 65% compared to an image-only baseline, demonstrating that rater identity provides predictive information not captured by the image alone.

## Methodology  
The authors developed a dataset of 29,870 walkability ratings collected from 1,196 respondents across urban, suburban, and regional Australian environments. Each rating is paired with sidewalk-view imagery and individual rater attributes such as age, gender, mobility level, and personal experience. The multimodal deep learning framework fuses visual features extracted from the images with encoded representations of each respondent’s profile. This user-conditioned approach allows the model to generate personalized walkability assessments that reflect how different individuals interpret their surroundings.

## Results  
The viewpoint-comparison study found that sidewalk-view images received significantly higher walkability ratings than street-view images, even when both were matched for visual content. The user-conditioned model achieved a quadratic weighted kappa of 0.29, compared to 0.47 for the image-only baseline, indicating improved alignment with human judgments. This suggests the model effectively integrates rater-specific factors into its predictions.

## Significance  
By moving away from observer-independent walkability scores, this research enables more equitable and accurate assessments of pedestrian environments. It supports urban planning initiatives that account for diverse user needs, improving accessibility and inclusivity in city design. The findings challenge current methodologies that homogenize perception, offering a foundation for personalized environmental evaluation.
