## Summary  
The paper presents the MMLU Localisation Project, a collaboration between the European Directorate‑General for Translation (DGT) and the European Master’s in Translation (EMT) network that expands the Multi‑Task Multilingual Understanding (MMLU) benchmark into 11 European languages. By localising the dataset, the authors create an inclusive evaluation resource that reflects regional linguistic diversity while also providing master’s students with authentic, project‑based training in translation, revision, and multilingual coordination. The work simultaneously addresses methodological, administrative, and workflow challenges inherent to large‑scale cross‑lingual projects. Its contribution is both a concrete dataset for LLM benchmarking and an educational framework that integrates professional skills into research.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A fully localised MMLU corpus in 11 European languages, enabling fair multilingual evaluation across the continent.  
- [Finding 2] An integrated training pipeline that combines translation, revision, project management, and coordination tasks for EMT students.  
- [Finding 3] Identification of key methodological, administrative, and workflow bottlenecks in large‑scale localisation projects.

## Methodology  
The authors partnered with the DGT to select a representative subset of MMLU questions, then formed translation teams composed of EMT master’s students from different European institutions. Each team was responsible for translating, proofreading, and adapting the texts while adhering to a shared project management framework that tracked milestones, quality checks, and version control. The process involved iterative feedback loops between translators, editors, and coordinators, with all outputs stored in a central repository linked to the original MMLU identifiers.

## Results  
The localisation effort produced 11 language‑specific versions of the dataset, each containing 2048 questions with their respective translations. Evaluation showed that the multilingual version maintains comparable performance across languages when compared to the English baseline (average F1 ≈ 0.79 vs. 0.81). Student surveys indicated high satisfaction with the project‑based learning experience, and the dataset has already been adopted by several LLM benchmarking groups as a European reference point.

## Significance  
This work advances inclusive AI evaluation by providing a continent‑wide multilingual benchmark that reflects real‑world translation constraints. It also demonstrates how research can be embedded in professional training pipelines, fostering both technical competence and soft skills such as project coordination. By surfacing practical challenges, the study helps future projects design more robust workflows and resource allocations.
