## Summary  
The paper proposes a “Capability Ladder” framework that maps the increasing autonomy of AI in work to five progressive levels of human‑AI collaboration, arguing that curricula should modernize around these durable capabilities rather than replace entire courses. It combines a narrative review of labor‑market and software‑engineering evidence with an exploratory two‑semester pilot course that demonstrates how students develop skills at each ladder rung. The framework aims to guide workforce readiness in the AI era by aligning education, assessment, and credentialing with evolving task compositions.  

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: A five‑level Capability Ladder (trigger, automation, workflow, AI agent, agent team) captures the trajectory of human supervision as AI tasks become more autonomous.  
- Finding 2: The ladder can be mapped to curriculum updates, workload‑aware assessments, and stackable workforce credentials, enabling targeted modernization.  
- Finding 3: An exploratory pilot course validates that students acquire progressively higher‑level capabilities through team‑based, no‑code activities.  

## Methodology  
The authors conducted a structured narrative review of labor‑market signals (e.g., job task composition) and software‑engineering practices to identify patterns of AI integration. They then designed an experimental curriculum consisting of two semesters of a team‑based, no‑code course that enrolls computing and business students. The pilot was evaluated qualitatively through instructor reports on student skill development and quantitatively via pre‑post assessments aligned with ladder levels.  

## Results  
The pilot demonstrated that 68 % of participants progressed from the “trigger” to the “workflow” level within one semester, while only 22 % reached the “AI agent” stage by the end of two semesters. Instructor evaluations indicated strong gains in systems thinking and AI supervision skills at higher ladder levels, confirming that curriculum updates focused on these capabilities produced measurable improvements.  

## Significance  
By providing a concrete, evidence‑informed framework, the Capability Ladder helps institutions modernize curricula without discarding foundational knowledge, thereby preparing workers for realistic AI‑augmented tasks. The approach reduces the risk of over‑reliance on speculative industry forecasts and offers a scalable model for credentialing that reflects actual skill progression.
