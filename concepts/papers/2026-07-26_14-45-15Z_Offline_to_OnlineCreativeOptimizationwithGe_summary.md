## Summary  
Creative optimization has become limited by the need for costly online evaluation of many generated variants. The authors propose an offline‑to‑online pipeline that leverages historical A/B test data to rank and refine creatives produced by a generative model, producing a compact slate for adaptive testing. A predictive “inference‑time critic” is trained on past experiments to guide the generation process toward high‑performing candidates. The workflow balances offline prediction with online selection to minimize traffic loss while maximizing engagement lift.  

## Key Contributions  
- **Finding 1:** In a 50‑arm field experiment, the best creative generated by the pipeline achieved a 45.1 % higher engagement than the best human‑authored creative.  
- **Finding 2:** Two additional experiments reported similar upper‑tail lifts of 46.7 % and 36.2 %, confirming the method’s robustness across different contexts.  
- **Finding 3:** Although the predictive model is noisy, it effectively steers the generative model toward candidates that can be evaluated efficiently in an adaptive experiment, reducing wasted traffic on low‑performing arms.  

## Methodology  
The authors built a two‑phase workflow: first, they trained a performance‑driven model on historical A/B test outcomes to rank variants created by a generative model; second, they deployed the top‑ranked candidates as a final test slate in an online adaptive experiment. The predictive model acts as an inference‑time critic that scores each generated creative based on past engagement signals, allowing the system to prioritize promising ideas before costly real‑world testing.  

## Results  
The 50‑arm field experiment demonstrated that the pipeline’s top creative outperformed human‑crafted alternatives by 45.1 % in engagement. Two follow‑up experiments reported lifts of 46.7 % and 36.2 %, respectively, indicating consistent performance gains. The predictive model’s noise was mitigated by its role as a guidance signal rather than a direct selector, enabling the generative model to produce strong candidates that fit within the limited test size.  

## Significance  
This work offers a scalable design principle for creative optimization: use offline predictive models to generate and rank a manageable slate of creatives, then apply adaptive testing to select the best one while minimizing traffic loss. By decoupling generation from costly online evaluation, the approach can unlock higher‑performing assets without proportionally increasing budget or user exposure.
