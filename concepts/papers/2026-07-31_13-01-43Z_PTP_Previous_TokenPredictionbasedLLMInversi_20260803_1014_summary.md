## Summary
This research paper introduces Previous-Token Prediction (PTP), a novel functional approach for inverting Large Language Models (LLMs) to reconstruct original prompts from their generated outputs. Unlike prior methods that rely on semantic reconstruction using fine-tuned sequence-to-sequence models or access to internal model weights, PTP operates in a black-box setting without auxiliary aids. The authors propose training an explicit inverse language model from scratch using data synthetically generated by the target LLM itself. By employing previous-token prediction analogous to the forward next-token prediction process, the method establishes a generative link that enables near-exact prompt reconstruction with high fidelity and transferability across different models.

## Key Contributions
- The authors develop a black-box inversion technique that does not require access to the target LLM's weights, logits, or external training datasets, relying solely on synthetic data generated by the model itself.
- They introduce a previous-token prediction mechanism for the inverse model, which creates a symmetric generative relationship with the forward process, allowing for diverse yet semantically consistent prompt reconstructions through sampling.
- The proposed method demonstrates superior performance across various token-based evaluation metrics compared to existing state-of-the-art approaches, while also exhibiting strong generalization and transferability capabilities across different LLM architectures.

## Methodology
The core methodology involves constructing an inverse language model entirely from scratch, trained specifically to reverse the auto-regressive generation process of a target LLM. Instead of relying on semantic similarity or external corpora, the authors generate synthetic training data by feeding various prompts into the target LLM and recording the resulting outputs. The inverse model is then trained using previous-token prediction, effectively learning to map the output tokens back to their preceding context. This approach treats inversion as a functional mapping problem rather than a semantic one, ensuring that all reconstructed prompts induce similar responses under the forward target LLM. The training process is self-contained, utilizing only the input-output pairs produced by the target model during its normal operation.

## Results
Experimental evaluations demonstrate that PTP outperforms prior work in both prompt and response reconstruction accuracy using token-based metrics. The method achieves near-exact reconstruction of original prompts, significantly surpassing previous techniques that often produce semantically plausible but structurally inaccurate outputs. Furthermore, the approach exhibits robust transferability, meaning the inverse model trained on one LLM can effectively reconstruct prompts for responses generated by different LLMs. The ability to generate diverse valid prompts through sampling also highlights the method's flexibility in exploring the solution space of possible inputs that lead to a specific output.

## Significance
This work is significant because it provides a practical and efficient solution for LLM inversion without the computational overhead or privacy concerns associated with accessing model weights or large external datasets. By functioning as a black-box tool, PTP enhances our understanding of LLM behavior and input-output mappings, which has implications for security, interpretability, and debugging of AI systems. The ability to accurately reconstruct prompts from outputs aids in reverse-engineering AI behaviors and ensuring compliance with usage policies.
