## Summary  
This paper investigates how UI/UX designers form design intent through iterative lexical oscillation when interacting with generative AI tools, rather than assuming prompts directly convey pre‑existing intent. It argues that the process is bidirectional, involving moves between vague vibe language and precise operational code. The study uses reflexive thematic analysis to map this oscillation across five expert designer interviews. Its contribution is a theoretical framework for viewing AI as a non‑neutral interlocutor that generates ambiguity as a resource.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Design intent emerges through lexical oscillation between high‑level vibe language (L1), domain‑specific terminology (L2) and low‑level operational code (L3).  
- Finding 2: Mismatches with AI outputs trigger designers to re‑interpret their own meaning, shifting engagement from instruction to consultation.  
- Finding 3: One non‑oscillating trajectory indicates conceptual misalignment that may serve as a boundary for future research.

## Methodology  
The authors recruited five UI/UX designers (mean experience 15.4 years) who created landing‑page hero sections using a generative AI tool. They recorded think‑aloud protocols and conducted retrospective interviews, then applied reflexive thematic analysis to identify patterns of lexical oscillation across three granularities: L1 vibe, L2 design‑domain language, and L3 operational code.

## Results  
Analysis revealed frequent back‑and‑forth moves from vague descriptive prompts to precise implementation details, with designers revisiting earlier ambiguities when AI outputs diverged. The non‑oscillating negative case showed a designer who interpreted the prompt as a fixed spec without revising meaning, resulting in a misaligned final design.

## Significance  
This work challenges the assumption that generative AI merely translates intent into code; instead it shows that designers co‑construct meaning through iterative language use. By treating ambiguity as a design resource and AI as an interlocutor, the study informs better prompt engineering, collaborative workflows, and research on human‑AI interaction in design.
