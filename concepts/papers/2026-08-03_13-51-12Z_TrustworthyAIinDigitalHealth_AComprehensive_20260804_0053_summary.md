## Summary  
This paper presents a comprehensive review of trustworthy AI in the context of digital health, focusing on two core dimensions—robustness and explainability—that are essential for safe and ethical deployment of machine‑learning systems. The authors organize their work into three parts: (1) an overview of the technical and ethical challenges surrounding trustworthy AI; (2) application‑specific considerations across intensive care, neonatal health, and metabolic health; and (3) recent methodological advances that address robustness under data scarcity/distributional shifts and various explainable AI techniques. By synthesizing these strands, the review aims to provide researchers and practitioners with a clear roadmap for building reliable, interpretable models in high‑stakes healthcare settings.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The authors introduce a structured framework that integrates robustness and explainability into a single trustworthy AI paradigm tailored specifically for digital health.  
- [Finding 2] They map application‑specific trust considerations across intensive care, neonatal health, and metabolic health, illustrating how each domain’s unique constraints shape robustness and interpretability requirements.  
- [Finding 3] The review highlights recent technical advances, including methods that improve model robustness when data are scarce or shift in distribution, as well as a spectrum of explainable AI techniques ranging from feature attribution to counterfactual explanations.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a systematic literature synthesis. First, they identified and categorized contributions into three thematic sections: (i) foundational pillars of trustworthy AI; (ii) domain‑specific applications in digital health; and (iii) cutting‑edge techniques for robustness and explainability. This classification enabled them to compare methods across subdomains and to evaluate how each technique contributes to overall system trust.

## Results  
The synthesis reveals persistent gaps: many studies focus on either robustness or explainability, neglecting their interplay in healthcare contexts. The review documents a growing body of work that employs data‑efficient regularization for robustness under scarcity and gradient‑based or counterfactual methods for interpretability. Evaluation metrics such as validity, fidelity, and diversity are discussed as quantitative proxies for trustworthiness.

## Significance  
This matters because AI systems in digital health must be both robust to real‑world variations and explainable to clinicians and patients; without these qualities, models risk causing harm, eroding confidence, and facing regulatory scrutiny. By consolidating advances and outlining practical pathways, the paper supports responsible innovation that aligns technical performance with ethical obligations.
