## Summary  
The paper introduces ASTELD, a six‑axis classification framework that systematically captures the design dimensions of autonomous AI agents—architecture pattern, security posture, tool integration model, execution paradigm, level of autonomy and human control, and deployment topology. By synthesizing existing agent taxonomies with observable platform properties and explicit assignment rules, ASTELD provides an operational method for comparing platforms, identifying unoccupied design regions, and guiding framework selection. The authors evaluate the framework on eight representative systems and then apply it to a detailed case study of OpenClaw, revealing three cross‑platform patterns: a security‑accessibility diagonal, strong execution‑architecture coupling, and capability convergence with persistent architectural differentiation.  

## Key Contributions  
- ASTELD is an operational six‑axis classification framework that synthesizes prior agent taxonomies into a reproducible set of axes for autonomous AI agents.  
- The evaluation separates eight platforms under their dominant configurations and uncovers three cross‑platform patterns: security‑accessibility diagonal, strong execution‑architecture coupling, and capability convergence with persistent architectural differentiation.  
- The OpenClaw case study supplies a six‑category vulnerability taxonomy, evidence from five institutional assessments, and adoption/governance analyses that link platform coordinates to observed risks, showing innovation concentrates on Security, Execution, and Deployment axes.  

## Methodology  
The authors constructed ASTELD by aggregating observable properties of autonomous AI agent platforms with explicit category‑assignment rules derived from earlier taxonomies. They first mapped eight representative frameworks onto the six axes to assess discriminative and explanatory utility, then performed an in‑depth case study on OpenClaw, classifying 50+ derivatives and extracting vulnerability evidence from five institutional assessments.  

## Results  
Profiles of all eight platforms were separated under their dominant configurations, confirming that ASTELD can uniquely identify each system’s configuration space. The analysis revealed three cross‑platform patterns: a security‑accessibility diagonal (higher security correlates with lower accessibility), strong execution‑architecture coupling (execution paradigms are tightly bound to architectural choices), and capability convergence (agents converge on similar capabilities despite differing architectures). Classification of 50+ OpenClaw derivatives showed that most innovation occurs along the Security, Execution, and Deployment axes. A notable “empty region” was identified: none of the evaluated systems combine local‑first deployment with enterprise‑grade security.  

## Significance  
ASTELD offers a reproducible method for comparing autonomous AI agent platforms, highlighting unoccupied design regions that could guide future research or product development. By exposing where ecosystem fragmentation occurs—particularly in Security, Execution, and Deployment—and by mapping platform coordinates to risk assessments, the framework helps stakeholders select appropriate configurations and organize empirical studies.
