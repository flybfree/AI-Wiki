## Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of discovering Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) from sparse and noisy observations by decoupling the continuous field reconstruction process from the symbolic equation selection phase. The authors identify that traditional coupled neural optimization paths often fail to consistently reveal the exact support of differential terms, leading to unstable discovery outcomes. To resolve this, they propose a novel "freeze-then-select" framework that utilizes a structured field adapter for robust interpolation and Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS) for rigorous term identification. This approach significantly enhances the reliability of PDE discovery across various challenging dynamical regimes, particularly where data is limited.

## Key Contributions
- The authors provide a detailed analysis of optimization paths in coupled neural PDE discovery, categorizing them into three distinct behaviors: persistent exact support, transient appearance, or complete failure to emerge, thereby highlighting the instability inherent in current methods.
- They introduce a novel methodology that separates field reconstruction from equation selection, utilizing a structured field adapter that factorizes fields into spatial features and temporal cubic splines, followed by SVWS to identify recurrent terms across independent weak-form systems.
- The proposed method demonstrates superior performance in recovering exact supports across six sparse regimes of the MDBench dataset, achieving the highest recovery rates among classical and neural baselines, with notable success in complex Kuramoto-Sivashinsky dynamics and unknown nonlinear diffusion functions via genetic programming.

## Methodology
The authors first analyze the optimization trajectories of existing coupled neural PDE discovery models to understand why support recovery is often inconsistent. Based on this analysis, they develop a two-stage "freeze-then-select" strategy. In the first stage, a structured field adapter is trained directly from sparse observations without relying on any PDE residual constraints. This adapter factorizes the continuous field into learned spatial features and temporal coefficients represented by cubic splines, ensuring a stable and accurate reconstruction of the underlying dynamics. Once the field is frozen, the second stage employs Stability-Validated Weak Selection (SVWS). This process identifies candidate differential terms by analyzing their recurrence across independent weak-form systems. The method then refits the supports for these candidates and selects the final equation using held-out weak-form systems to validate stability and accuracy. Additionally, the framework is extended beyond fixed libraries to include expressions generated by genetic programming, allowing for the discovery of complex forms like power-law nonlinear diffusion functions.

## Results
Experimental evaluations across six sparse regimes of the MDBench benchmark demonstrate that the proposed method achieves the highest exact support recovery rate compared to both classical and neural baselines. The most significant improvements are observed in challenging Kuramoto-Sivashinsky dynamics, where traditional methods often struggle due to high nonlinearity and sparsity. Furthermore, the application of the same principle to genetic programming-generated expressions successfully recovered the power-law form of an unknown nonlinear diffusion function from sparse, noisy observations, validating the method's flexibility and robustness in handling complex, non-standard functional forms.

## Significance
This work matters because it fundamentally shifts the paradigm of PDE discovery by decoupling interpolation from selection, thereby mitigating the instability issues prevalent in end-to-end neural approaches. By ensuring that the discovered equations are validated through stability checks on weak-form systems, the method provides a more reliable and interpretable tool for scientific machine learning. This is particularly crucial for applications involving sparse experimental data, where accurate model discovery can lead to better predictions and deeper physical insights.
