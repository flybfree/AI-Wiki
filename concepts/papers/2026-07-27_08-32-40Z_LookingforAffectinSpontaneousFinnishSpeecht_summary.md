## Summary  
The paper investigates how affective perception in spontaneous Finnish speech is shaped by acoustic and linguistic features, aiming to disentangle their relative contributions to valence and arousal judgments. By analyzing a newly released corpus, it shows that combining text‑ and audio‑based cues yields better predictions of emotional valence than either alone, while the complementary effect for arousal remains modest.  

## Key Contributions  
- The study provides an affective Finnish speech dataset with synchronized acoustic and textual annotations.  
- It demonstrates that multimodal feature integration improves regression performance on valence but not on arousal, highlighting modality‑specific effects.  
- The findings extend prior cross‑linguistic results by offering empirical evidence specific to Finnish spontaneous speech.  

## Methodology  
The authors collected a large sample of natural Finnish utterances recorded in real‑life contexts and paired each utterance with its transcript. They extracted acoustic features such as pitch variance, jitter, and spectral centroid, alongside linguistic features like lexical valence, syntactic complexity, and prosodic markers. A supervised regression model was trained to predict human ratings of emotional arousal and valence using both feature sets separately and in combination.  

## Results  
Separate models achieved modest gains on each task; however, the combined multimodal model significantly outperformed the single‑modal baselines for valence prediction (R² increase of ~0.12) while showing only a negligible improvement for arousal (ΔR² ≈ 0.03). The complementary effect was not substantial, suggesting that affective perception in Finnish is more driven by acoustic cues than by linguistic content.  

## Significance  
Understanding the relative influence of acoustic and linguistic features on affect perception informs the design of emotion‑aware speech technologies and improves cross‑linguistic models. This work bridges gaps between acoustic‑phonetic and text‑based approaches, offering a nuanced view of how Finnish speakers encode emotional states in spontaneous communication.
