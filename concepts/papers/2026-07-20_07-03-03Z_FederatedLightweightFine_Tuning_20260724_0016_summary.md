## Summary  
Federated fine‑tuning suffers from prohibitive bandwidth because conventional schemes transmit full model weights or pseudo‑gradients that scale linearly with the network size. The authors propose Federated Lightweight Fine‑Tuning (FLITE), a method that replaces weight updates with tiny, low‑rank latent vectors generated by a shared affine projection of a centrally pretrained base model. By averaging these latents instead of full weights, FLITE achieves communication reductions of thousands of times while preserving accuracy. The approach also introduces a delta formulation and an orthogonal classifier head to further shrink payloads without sacrificing performance.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] A low‑rank, seed‑regenerable factorisation of the affine projection reduces generator memory from ~80 GB to ~10 MB while enabling exact averaging of generated weights.  
- [Finding 2] The delta formulation \(θ = θ^{\mathrm{pre}} + U V^{\top} z\) learns an additive correction around a shared pretrained base, yielding federated fine‑tuning that matches full‑weight FedAvg within half a percent.  
- [Finding 3] An orthogonal classifier head removes the head from the payload and improves accuracy, demonstrating int4 latents can reach 648 bytes per round with unchanged performance.

## Methodology  
The authors map each client’s weights through a frozen affine transformation that compresses the model into a small latent space. This projection is factorised into low‑rank matrices \(U\) and \(V^{\top}\) that are regenerated from a seed, allowing exact averaging of the latent vectors across clients. The resulting latents encode both the pretrained base and an additive correction learned cooperatively. A frozen orthogonal classifier head is added to predict class labels without contributing to communication. FLITE iterates these steps over several rounds, aggregating only the tiny latents.

## Results  
On CIFAR‑100 with ResNet‑18+GroupNorm, FLITE reaches 74.67 % accuracy—about 0.5 pp below full‑weight FedAvg. Per client per round it communicates just 1,280 floats (~5 KB), an 8,718× reduction compared with conventional methods. The averaging identity holds to floating‑point precision (≈6 × 10⁻⁸). FLITE outperforms PowerSGD and top‑k on the bandwidth‑accuracy Pareto frontier and matches or exceeds full‑weight FedAvg under strong non‑IID skew. Int4 latents further shrink communication to 648 bytes per round while maintaining accuracy, whereas int4 full‑weight FedAvg collapses to chance.

## Significance  
FLITE demonstrates that federated learning can achieve near‑full‑weight performance with orders of magnitude lower bandwidth, crucial for large‑scale deployments where network constraints dominate. By decoupling communication from model size and exploiting low‑rank latent representations, the method opens doors to real‑world edge devices and heterogeneous client environments.
