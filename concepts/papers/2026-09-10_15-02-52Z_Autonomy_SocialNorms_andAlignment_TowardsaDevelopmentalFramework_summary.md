# Summary: Autonomy, Social Norms, and Alignment: Towards a Developmental Framework for Autonomous Artificial Agents

## Summary
This paper argues that embodied autonomous agents cannot be aligned reliably in dynamic environments through fixed rules and pre-existing human-feedback datasets alone. Agents need to learn through direct interaction, while higher-level mechanisms such as intrinsic motivation make exploration more capable but also harder to control. The authors propose a developmental approach in which agents acquire increasingly complex social norms through situated experience, cooperation, and supervised progression—analogous to how children learn norms.

## Key Takeaways
- Alignment in open-ended environments is an ongoing learning problem, not a one-time specification problem.
- Regulatory sandboxes are framed as pedagogical environments: agents should receive increasing degrees of freedom only as they demonstrate responsible behavior.
- Moral-agent status is treated as gradual and attributed based on demonstrated ability to manage autonomy responsibly, rather than assumed from capability alone.
- The proposal connects embodied learning, norm acquisition, governance, and accountability, but is primarily a conceptual framework rather than an empirical safety evaluation.

## Why It Matters
The developmental framing complements current containment and staged-release work. It suggests that permission expansion should be treated as an evaluated curriculum with explicit milestones, reversible scopes, and human accountability—not as a binary choice between unrestricted autonomy and permanent restriction.
