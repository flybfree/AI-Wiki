## Summary  
The paper aims to synthesize a thematic review of industrial engineering and operations research (OR) studies that address elderly care, highlighting how operational improvements translate into patient outcomes. It contributes by mapping the evolution from static deterministic models to dynamic stochastic AI‑enabled systems and proposing an integrated decision‑making framework that bridges process optimization with clinical results.  

## Key Contributions  
- The review identifies three core domains—home healthcare operations, polypharmacy management, and clinical chronotherapy—as focal points of OR research in aging populations.  
- It documents a methodological shift from deterministic, static models to dynamic stochastic frameworks integrated with artificial intelligence such as digital twins and large language models.  
- It proposes a conceptual framework that refocuses research on multi‑level decision making rather than isolated operational tasks.  

## Methodology  
The authors conducted a thematic analysis of 30 seminal studies published up to the present, grouping them into the three domains mentioned above. They examined each study’s modeling approach, data sources, and evaluation metrics, then synthesized these findings to illustrate trends in OR methodology applied to elderly care.  

## Results  
The synthesis reveals that most current OR work optimizes staff routing or medication scheduling but fails to link these efficiencies to measurable health outcomes such as reduced hospital readmissions. The authors’ theoretical contribution is the proposed conceptual framework that explicitly connects operational metrics with patient‑level outcomes, suggesting a need for robust systems analysis and human‑inclusive design.  

## Significance  
Understanding this gap is crucial because an aging population strains healthcare resources; without translating process improvements into real clinical benefits, OR innovations remain abstract. The paper underscores the importance of integrating smart technologies to create resilient, patient‑centered care systems that deliver tangible health gains.
