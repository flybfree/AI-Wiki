## Summary  
The paper tackles one‑dimensional local root anti‑concentration for piecewise‑Lipschitz functions under homogeneous feature curves and coefficients whose density on a symmetric convex body is bounded by a constant A. It establishes that the worst‑case interval‑hitting constant equals A multiplied by a section‑averaged projective incidence speed, which for cube‑supported coefficients is equivalent up to universal constants to the projective Lipschitz constant. This yields a sharp, dimension‑free bound and eliminates the previous √N loss. Moreover, it characterizes when monic degree‑d polynomials have finite interval‑hitting constants in terms of bounded densities of ordered real‑root laws.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The worst‑case interval‑hitting constant is A times a projective incidence speed, giving a sharp, dimension‑free anti‑concentration bound.  
- [Finding 2] For cube‑supported coefficients the projective incidence speed equals the projective Lipschitz constant up to universal constants.  
- [Finding 3] Finite interval‑hitting constants for monic degree‑d polynomials are equivalent to bounded densities of ordered real‑root laws, with a sharp factor‑d comparison.

## Methodology  
The authors employ projective incidence theory and ordered root laws to relate the speed at which roots intersect intervals to Lipschitz constants. They use conditional and joint coefficient‑space area formulas together with a two‑chart certificate to verify the criterion for dependent or singular coefficient laws, thereby providing a general framework that extends beyond uniform distributions.

## Results  
Theoretical results show that the interval‑hitting constant is Θ(A) under the stated assumptions, removing the √N dependence. For monic polynomials, finiteness of the hitting constant holds iff ordered root law densities are bounded, and the bound is tight up to a factor d. The two graph‑learning applications achieve expected regrets O((An²De^{BD}/ℓ+1)√T) for Gaussian‑RBF classifiers and O((qn²κ+1)√T) for common‑offset polynomial kernels, even with singular coefficient laws.

## Significance  
This work provides a sharp, dimension‑free anti‑concentration bound that resolves open questions in online optimization of piecewise‑Lipschitz functions. It also supplies a precise necessary and sufficient condition on root‑law densities for finite interval‑hitting constants, bridging theory and practical machine‑learning models.
