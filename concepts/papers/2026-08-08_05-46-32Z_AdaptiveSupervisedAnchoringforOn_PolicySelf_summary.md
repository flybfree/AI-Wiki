## Summary  
On‑policy self‑distillation (OPSD) learns a language model by distilling guidance from a frozen teacher on trajectories generated by the student, but its performance degrades when those trajectories drift away from the intended target distribution. The authors demonstrate that conditioning the teacher solely on off‑target rollout prefixes corrupts task‑relevant supervision, which they call “rollout‑conditioned signal degradation.” To mitigate this, they introduce a unified training framework that simultaneously applies two complementary supervision pathways: one preserves the student’s actual visited states via rollout‑conditioned matching, and the other uses supervised cross‑entropy on canonical ground‑truth contexts. Their method also employs token‑level alignment to adaptively strengthen or relax the canonical‑context anchor as rollout quality improves.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Rollout‑conditioned signal degradation occurs when off‑target prefixes condition teacher guidance, weakening task relevance.  
- [Finding 2] A unified framework separates rollout‑conditioned distribution matching from canonical context supervision to avoid incompatibility.  
- [Finding 3] Token‑level rollout‑target alignment dynamically adjusts the strength of the canonical‑context anchor based on rollout quality.  

## Methodology  
The authors first collect student rollouts and identify prefixes that deviate from the target trajectory, labeling those as “off‑target.” They then construct two supervision signals: (i) a distribution‑matching loss that rewards the teacher’s output aligning with the actual prefix of each rollout step, ensuring the guidance reflects visited states; and (ii) a supervised cross‑entropy loss on canonical contexts derived from the correct answer sequence. The token‑level alignment mechanism monitors how often rollouts match their targets and modulates the weight applied to the canonical‑context loss: it is high during cold start when rollouts are poor, gradually decreasing as rollout quality improves. This dual‑path training preserves both state fidelity and task relevance without forcing the teacher to condition on erroneous prefixes.  

## Results  
Experiments across multiple model scales (small, medium, large), two distinct task families (e.g., code generation and natural‑language reasoning), and general‑reasoning benchmarks reveal that the proposed adaptive supervised anchoring consistently outperforms baseline OPSD methods. The improvement is measured by higher task acquisition rates while maintaining comparable or better performance on unrelated reasoning tasks, indicating a favorable plasticity‑stability trade‑off. Ablation studies confirm that removing either the rollout‑conditioned path or the canonical‑context supervision degrades results, underscoring the necessity of both components.  

## Significance  
Identifying context quality as a central bottleneck in OPSD highlights a previously unaddressed source of instability. By decoupling rollout‑driven guidance from task‑specific supervision and dynamically aligning token contributions, the framework enables models to adapt more robustly to varying data distributions. This work provides a practical template for future self‑distillation pipelines that must balance exploration (rollouts) with exploitation (canonical knowledge), ultimately leading to smarter, more stable language model training.
