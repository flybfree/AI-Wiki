## Summary
This paper addresses the critical challenge of accurate long-term forecasting for large-scale multivariate time series generated by heterogeneous IoT sensors, a task essential for effective resource scheduling and predictive maintenance. The authors identify a significant limitation in existing time series foundation models: while they offer strong generalization capabilities, they rely on static parametric knowledge and lack dynamic access to external historical patterns during the inference phase. To overcome this, the study proposes CrossRAG, a novel retrieval-augmented forecasting framework designed to bridge the gap between static model parameters and dynamic historical data. By integrating shape-aware memory mechanisms with future-consistent contrastive learning, the proposed method aims to retrieve relevant historical references that are not only similar in shape but also consistent with future outcomes, thereby enhancing forecast accuracy across diverse and noisy sensor data streams.

## Key Contributions
- The introduction of Shape-Aware Memory (SAM) combined with RevIN normalization, which enables magnitude-robust retrieval by focusing on the structural similarity of time series shapes rather than raw values, effectively handling the scale variations inherent in heterogeneous IoT sources.
- The development of Future-Consistent Contrastive (FCC) learning, a novel technique that distinguishes informative historical references from hard negatives—specifically those cases where historical patterns appear similar but lead to divergent future trajectories—thus improving the relevance of retrieved data.
- The design of Cross-Attention Temporal Fusion (CATF), a mechanism that fuses retrieved historical-future reference pairs directly into the backbone model's representations at the feature level, allowing for dynamic adaptation during inference without retraining the core parameters.

## Methodology
The authors approached the problem by first analyzing the limitations of standard Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in the context of time series forecasting, noting issues with magnitude variations and historical-future mismatch. They then constructed the CrossRAG framework, which operates in three distinct stages. First, SAM utilizes RevIN normalization to extract shape features from a memory bank of historical data, ensuring that retrieval is robust to amplitude differences common in heterogeneous sensor networks. Second, FCC learning is employed during the training phase to optimize the embedding space, pushing apart references with similar histories but different futures while pulling together those with aligned future trends. Finally, CATF integrates these retrieved vectors into the forecasting backbone via cross-attention mechanisms, allowing the model to dynamically attend to the most relevant historical patterns for each specific prediction instance.

## Results
Extensive experiments were conducted on seven public benchmarks representing various time series domains. The results demonstrate that CrossRAG consistently outperforms both parametric-only baselines and existing retrieval-augmented forecasting methods. Specifically, the framework showed significant improvements in long-term forecasting accuracy, validating the effectiveness of combining shape-aware retrieval with future-consistent contrastive objectives. The study highlights that the proposed method is particularly effective in scenarios with high heterogeneity and noise, where traditional static models fail to capture dynamic contextual dependencies.

## Significance
This research matters because it provides a scalable solution for real-world IoT applications where accurate long-term forecasting is crucial for operational efficiency. By enabling dynamic access to relevant historical patterns, CrossRAG enhances the adaptability of foundation models to new or shifting data distributions without the computational cost of frequent retraining. This advancement supports more reliable predictive maintenance and resource allocation in complex industrial environments.
