## Summary  
The paper seeks to map the cognitive capability gaps that persist in generative and agentic AI systems, which are still limited by fragmented reasoning, weak autonomy, poor self‑monitoring, inadequate environment interaction, and limited learning. By organizing recent research into five core dimensions—persistent state modeling, goal‑directed autonomy, self‑monitoring and control, environment interaction, and learning and adaptation—the authors present a taxonomy that unifies disparate studies and highlights unresolved challenges. The work also introduces the Adaptive Cognitive Intelligence Architecture (ACIA) as a conceptual framework for integrating these capabilities and proposes a cognition‑centric evaluation paradigm to assess long‑term reliability. Together, these contributions aim to provide a roadmap toward more adaptive, reliable, and cognitively capable AI systems that could eventually approach Artificial General Intelligence.

## Key Contributions  
- The taxonomy categorizes cognitive gaps into five distinct dimensions, offering a clear, reusable classification for researchers.  
- The Adaptive Cognitive Intelligence Architecture (ACIA) is proposed as an integrated design blueprint linking the five dimensions into a coherent system.  
- A cognition‑centric evaluation framework is introduced to measure long‑term reasoning, adaptive decision‑making, and continual learning beyond standard benchmarks.

## Methodology  
The authors approached the problem through a comprehensive literature review that clusters existing work around the five cognitive dimensions identified in prior surveys. For each dimension they systematically extracted reported capabilities, noted recurring limitations, and distilled open research questions. This synthesis was then used to formulate the taxonomy and to outline the ACIA architecture, ensuring that the framework is grounded in empirical observations rather than speculative assumptions.

## Results  
The results are theoretical but highly systematic: persistent state modeling gaps manifest as weak memory retention; goal‑directed autonomy is hampered by brittle task selection; self‑monitoring and control suffer from lack of introspection mechanisms; environment interaction remains shallow due to limited embodied perception; learning and adaptation are constrained by static training pipelines. These findings collectively illustrate where current generative or agentic models fall short of sustained cognitive function.

## Significance  
This work matters because it moves the AI community away from isolated performance metrics toward a holistic view of cognition, enabling targeted research on each gap. By providing a unified taxonomy and an architectural blueprint, the paper guides engineers and theorists in designing systems that can maintain state across time, pursue goals autonomously, self‑regulate, interact meaningfully with environments, and continuously improve—key prerequisites for advancing toward Cognitive AI and AGI.
