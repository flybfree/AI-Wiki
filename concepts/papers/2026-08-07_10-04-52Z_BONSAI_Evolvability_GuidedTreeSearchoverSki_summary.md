## Summary  
BONSAI is a novel skill‑optimization framework that treats the evolution of viable document variations—rather than only their current fitness—as the guiding principle for search. By modelling evolvability, the method builds a Monte Carlo tree where each node represents a mutated skill and selects children based on both local performance and the potential of surrounding regions to keep generating improvements. The algorithm returns the single best‑scoring document it discovers without incurring extra cost beyond its accept‑if‑better loop. This approach yields substantial gains over baseline skill‑free agents and comparable optimisation baselines.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] BONSAI is a skill‑optimization framework that leverages evolvability—a property of documentspace—to guide tree search, moving beyond the limitation of optimizing only current fitness.  
- [Finding 2] The algorithm constructs a Monte Carlo search tree where every child is a mutation of its parent and employs an exploitation‑exploration selection rule that blends the node’s own score with the scores of its neighbourhood to concentrate budget on improving regions while retaining weak branches alive.  
- [Finding 3] BONSAI outputs the best document found during the search, replacing the skill‑free baseline at no additional cost beyond the accept‑if‑better loop.

## Methodology  
The authors treat a skill as a frozen natural‑language document whose weights cannot be updated; any capability missing must be supplied in prose. Optimising such a skill is framed as improving its textual score. BONSAI builds a search tree via Monte Carlo sampling: each node’s children are generated by mutating the parent’s text, and the selection rule computes an upper‑confidence bound that combines the node’s own fitness with the average fitness of its neighbours. The exploitation term rewards high scores, while the exploration term keeps low‑scoring branches alive to explore promising regions. The search terminates when the best document is selected for replacement.

## Results  
BONSAI lifts heldout accuracy by 2313 points over a skill‑free agent and improves GEPA (+387) and SkillOpt (+397) on three benchmark datasets. These gains demonstrate that evolvability‑guided search can achieve far better performance than naïve optimisation while using the same budget.

## Significance  
By integrating evolvability into tree search, BONSAI shows that focusing on the capacity of a region to generate future improvements can outperform blind fitness maximisation. This is especially valuable for frozen agents where skill evolution cannot be directly updated, offering an efficient way to enhance performance without costly re‑training.
