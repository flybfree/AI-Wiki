## Summary  
The paper proposes MEMENTO, a memory‑guided memetic framework that evolves executable code‑as‑policy for long‑horizon embodied tasks. By treating policies as programs that can be inspected and revised after rollout evaluation, MEMENTO combines an evolution loop with a sequential local‑improvement phase guided by structured feedback metrics generated from LLMs. This approach enables selection among independently created variants while also allowing the best‑performing policy to drive further refinements. The framework is evaluated on two long‑horizon manipulation domains: Robosuite’s Tower‑of‑Hanoi and AI2‑THOR household interaction.

## Key Contributions  
- **Memory‑guided single‑elite memetic framework** for code‑as‑policy evolution, integrating rollout fitness with structured feedback metrics.  
- **Sequential local improvement phase**: the evolved evaluator conditions generation via hill‑climbing, macro‑mutation, and crossover, allowing the best policy to steer subsequent proposals.  
- **Demonstration of success on two long‑horizon tasks** (Robosuite Tower‑of‑Hanoi and AI2‑THOR) and successful sim‑to‑real deployment of the evolved Robosuite policy on a physical Franka robot.

## Methodology  
The authors first evolve a rollout evaluator that maps each policy’s execution trace into a scalar fitness score and richer feedback metrics. Fitness selects which candidates are retained as elites, while the feedback metrics condition the generation process—using memory‑guided hill‑climbing, macro‑mutation, and crossover—to produce new code variants. MEMENTO operates in a single‑elite memetic loop: an elite policy is evaluated, its fitness determines acceptance, and the evaluator’s structured feedback guides the next generation of policies generated by LLMs.

## Results  
MEMENTO outperforms Eureka and REvolve—baselines adapted as code‑as‑policy evolutionary methods—in both task success rates and generalization to held‑out Robosuite object configurations and unseen AI2‑THOR scenes. Ablation studies show that zero‑shot generation or using an unevolved evaluator cannot solve either domain, confirming the necessity of the memory‑guided search. Removing the policy‑search branch further degrades performance. The best‑evolved Robosuite policy is then deployed on a physical Franka robot, achieving sim‑to‑real transfer.

## Significance  
MEMENTO introduces a novel evolutionary paradigm that couples rollout‑based fitness with memory‑driven local improvement, enabling sequential refinement of code‑as‑policy solutions. This bridges the gap between simulation and real robotics, showing that evolved programs can be directly executed on hardware. The framework advances memetic AI by providing a systematic way to generate, evaluate, and iteratively improve executable policies for long‑horizon tasks.
