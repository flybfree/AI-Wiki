## Summary  
The paper addresses a limitation of semi‑autoregressive speculative decoders such as DSpark, which treat draft continuations as single linear chains and thus lose performance when early tokens diverge from the target model. By exploiting the conditional knowledge already encoded in the Markov head, we propose Parent‑Conditioned Drafting Tree (PCTree), a tree‑structured inference that evaluates multiple child continuations per parent without retraining or extra backbone passes. This approach converts DSpark’s linear draft into a parallelizable tree while preserving its one‑pass forward pass and demonstrates measurable speedup gains across diverse models and benchmarks.  

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The conditional structure learned by the semi‑autoregressive drafter can be leveraged to generate multiple parent‑consistent continuations, enabling a branching inference model without architectural changes.  
- [Finding 2] PCTree allocates a fixed verification budget per path, selecting the most probable branches while still evaluating alternatives, thus converting linear drafting into an efficient tree traversal.  
- [Finding 3] The method yields up to 29.5 % relative speedup over autoregressive decoding on Qwen‑3 models, surpassing DSpark’s gains and improving mean acceptance length from 9.41 to 11.16 tokens.  

## Methodology  
PCTree builds on DSpark’s backbone by treating each token block as a parent node with several child continuations generated by the lightweight Markov head. During inference, the model scores all children independently for their concrete parent, then allocates a single verification budget to the highest‑scoring path while still considering others for fallback. This parallel evaluation replaces the sequential chain processing of DSpark, preserving the one‑pass forward pass and enabling efficient GPU utilization.  

## Results  
Across Qwen3‑{4B,8B,14B} and nine benchmarks at $B=7$, PCTree achieves speedup gains ranging from 3.1 % to 29.5 % relative to autoregressive decoding, outperforming DSpark’s maximum of 29.5 %. On Qwen3‑4B GSM8K with $B=16$, mean acceptance length rises from 9.41 to 11.16 tokens and three‑run AR speedup increases from 6.14× to 6.60×, confirming both higher acceptance rates and faster inference.  

## Significance  
PCTree demonstrates that existing conditional capacity in semi‑autoregressive drafters can be fully exploited for end‑to‑end inference gains through an inference‑only modification, eliminating the need for retraining or additional passes. This insight opens a path to further efficiency improvements by converting linear drafting into parallel tree traversal, which is crucial as models scale and latency becomes a bottleneck.
