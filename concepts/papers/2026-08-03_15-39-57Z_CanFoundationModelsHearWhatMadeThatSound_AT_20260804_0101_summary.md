## Summary  
The paper presents a comprehensive benchmark for closed‑set sound source identification that compares eleven audio classification methods—task‑aware large language models (LLMs) and traditional fixed‑vocabulary classifiers—across 23 fine‑grained classes within 11 coarse categories. It introduces a tiered evaluation framework because the models differ fundamentally in how they receive input and are scored, moving beyond a single leaderboard to report macro Precision, Recall, F1, and false‑negative rate per tier. The study evaluates these methods on 2 242 audio clips generated by the authors’ own dataset. Findings show that some models excel at coarse classification but struggle with fine‑grained discrimination.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] Gemini‑3.1‑Pro‑Preview achieves the highest category‑level F1 (85.6 %) and fine‑grained F1 (56.7 %), outperforming all other LLMs in this closed‑set task.  
- [Finding 2] Kimi‑Audio reaches a respectable 67.5 % category‑level F1 but fails on approximately 1.6 % of samples, indicating limited reliability despite its smaller size.  
- [Finding 3] SSLAM and CLAP match or exceed Gemini’s performance at the coarse level without seeing candidate lists, demonstrating strong zero‑shot capability.

## Methodology  
The authors constructed a closed‑set sound source identification task with two levels of granularity: 11 category labels (e.g., “car,” “dog”) and 23 fine‑grained sub‑labels (e.g., “sedan,” “beagle”). Eleven models were grouped into four evaluation tiers based on how they process the input and are scored. For each tier macro Precision, Recall, F1, and false‑negative rate were computed. Gemini’s chain‑of‑thought responses were collected for 8 968 samples to analyze reasoning patterns.

## Results  
The best model, Gemini‑3.1‑Pro‑Preview, attains 85.6 % category‑level F1 and 56.7 % fine‑grained F1. Kimi‑Audio reaches 67.5 % category‑level F1 but suffers a 1.6 % false‑negative rate. SSLAM and CLAP match or exceed Gemini at the coarse level yet lag on fine‑granular accuracy, with confusion matrices revealing systematic misclassifications of similar‑sounding classes. Overall macro metrics show that tiered reporting better captures trade‑offs between model size, zero‑shot performance, and fine‑grained discrimination.

## Significance  
This tiered benchmark clarifies the practical implications of using large language models versus traditional classifiers for audio classification. By separating coarse‑set and fine‑set performance, it guides practitioners to select a method that balances accuracy across both levels—critical for applications such as smart speakers or medical diagnostics where precise sound identification matters.
