## Summary  
This paper introduces the Human‑AI Substitution Principle (HASP), a formal analytical model that predicts when and why human workers will be replaced by artificial intelligence within hierarchical organizations. The authors derive a precise condition—grounded in an economic asymmetry between skill acquisition and AI capability scaling—that determines the timing, location, and structural triggers of replacement. By extending this principle, they show how risk heterogeneity, organizational depth, and strategic adaptation can produce abrupt workforce shifts or hybrid human‑AI structures without imposing a minimum‑human‑fraction rule. The work unifies automation economics, organizational design, AI governance, and workforce planning into a single theory of AI‑driven transformation.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The Human‑AI Substitution Principle provides a mathematically explicit condition under which AI replaces human labor, formalizing the asymmetry between skill acquisition rates and AI capability growth.  
- [Finding 2] The model demonstrates that AI adoption can create abrupt workforce transitions or hybrid organizations where risk heterogeneity sustains both human and AI roles, eliminating the need for a minimum‑human‑fraction constraint.  
- [Finding 3] Middle‑management positions are identified as especially vulnerable to automation when organizational depth is high, baseline costs are low, and risk differentials are pronounced.

## Methodology  
The authors construct a hierarchical organization model where tasks are allocated between humans and AI agents over time. They embed an economic asymmetry assumption—human skill acquisition follows a diminishing‑returns curve while AI capability scales linearly with investment. Using this framework, they compute risk‑adjusted costs for each allocation decision, incorporate organizational depth (number of management layers), deployment scale, strategic adaptation rates, and heterogeneous risk profiles to simulate substitution events.

## Results  
The theoretical analysis yields three core outcomes: (1) a condition that AI replaces humans when the cost of acquiring additional human skill exceeds the marginal benefit of AI capability; (2) scenarios where hybrid structures persist because risk heterogeneity prevents a clean binary switch; (3) elevated vulnerability of middle‑management roles under conditions of high organizational depth and low baseline costs. Simulations confirm that flattening hierarchies can mitigate substitution pressure, while deeper layers amplify it.

## Significance  
Understanding when AI will supplant human workers is crucial for strategic workforce planning, policy design, and ethical governance. The HASP framework offers a predictive tool that integrates economic incentives with organizational structure, enabling managers to anticipate transition points and mitigate unintended displacement. This unifies disparate fields into actionable insight.
