## Summary  
The paper investigates whether the ACL Responsible NLP Checklist is merely a box‑ticking exercise by analyzing 73,922 checklist responses from EMNLP 2025. It reveals patterns of superficial compliance, logical contradictions, and poor justification quality across both Main and Findings tracks.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: Authors largely isolate ethics questions from the bulk of their papers, treating them as afterthoughts.  
- Finding 2: 44.9 % of justifications are brief or empty, indicating poor or bad‑faith responses.  
- Finding 3: 6 % of checklists contain logical contradictions between parent and child checklist items.

## Methodology  
The authors curated two datasets: (a) all checklist responses together with author justifications from EMNLP 2025 Main and Findings tracks; (b) links to the sections of each paper that address the corresponding checklist item. They then performed a large‑scale textual analysis, counting response lengths, classifying justification quality, detecting contradictions, and mapping responses to paper content.

## Results  
In the Main track, 44.9 % of justifications are poor or empty; 6 % contain logical contradictions; 53 % of authors dismiss potential risks that should be addressed. The Findings track shows similar trends: high proportion of brief justifications and a comparable rate of contradictory items. Overall, only ~10 % of checklists demonstrate substantive engagement.

## Significance  
These findings suggest the checklist may encourage performative compliance rather than genuine responsible practice, highlighting design flaws such as lack of mandatory depth and insufficient scrutiny of risk mitigation. The paper calls for concrete improvements like minimum word counts and stricter evaluation of appliance risks.
