## Summary
This paper presents a web framework that integrates user‑provided annotations with continuous physiological streams from wearables to create interactive visualizations of stress events and interventions. In a four‑week pilot, the tool identified specific benefits: social interaction lowered heart rate by about 4–5 bpm, deliberate rest cut Garmin stress scores by roughly 10 points, and mindfulness reduced average HRV by around 6–7 ms.

## Key Takeaways
- Social interaction reduces average heart rate by approximately 4.35 to 5.0 beats per minute, showing that brief group activities can quickly lower physiological arousal.
- Deliberate rest lowers average Garmin stress scores from 10.03 to 13.83 points, indicating a measurable relaxation effect after intentional breaks.
- Mindfulness practices decrease average HRV by about 6.61 to 13.22 milliseconds, reflecting improved autonomic balance through focused breathing.

## Context
The rapid rise of consumer wearables has generated vast amounts of personal health data yet few methods translate this raw stream into actionable insights for everyday stress management. This work bridges that gap by combining machine‑learned pattern detection with user‑driven annotations, offering a practical AI‑assisted interface for self‑reflection.

## Implications
For researchers, the framework demonstrates how collaborative annotation can improve data interpretability and guide intervention design. For industry, it suggests a pathway to commercial products that turn passive monitoring into personalized wellness coaching, potentially reducing stress‑related health risks in workplaces and schools.
