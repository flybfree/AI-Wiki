## Summary
The paper introduces SpecCal, a training-free candidate calibration method that re-ranks and expands the molecule candidates generated by existing infrared-to-molecule models to better resolve ambiguous spectral data. Experiments show that SpecCal improves top-k reconstruction accuracy for both SMILES strings and scaffold representations across multiple benchmarks compared with standard base models.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework operates on candidate outputs of any base model without retraining, making it plug-and-play and model‑agnostic.
- By re‑ranking candidates using spectral consistency, SpecCal mitigates the ambiguity caused by similar functional groups producing nearly identical IR responses.
- Calibrating candidate sets under spectral ambiguity consistently yields higher reconstruction quality at both SMILES and scaffold levels.

## Context
Infrared spectroscopy provides valuable molecular fingerprints but suffers from inherent ambiguities where multiple structures share overlapping vibrational patterns. Current AI models generate ranked candidates, yet their selection is often biased by training preferences rather than true spectral fit. This work addresses that gap by providing a post‑hoc calibration step that refines predictions based on the raw spectrum.

## Implications
The results suggest that simple calibration techniques can significantly boost performance of IR‑based molecular reconstruction systems without complex model redesigns, offering practical benefits for cheminformatics pipelines and drug discovery workflows. Practitioners can integrate SpecCal into existing tools to obtain more reliable structural hypotheses from limited spectroscopic data.
