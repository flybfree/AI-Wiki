## Summary
This paper introduces PEARL, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation system designed to deliver contextualized gameplay support for a parallel programming puzzle game by combining semantic knowledge retrieval with structural board-state matching. Qualitative evaluations reveal that players generally preferred traditional visualization tools over the AI agent due to frustration, generic responses, and trust deficits, though some users found value when actively initiating interactions. The study positions PEARL as a design probe to highlight failure modes and establish a concrete agenda for future AI gameplay support systems.

## Key Takeaways
- PEARL integrates dual input streams (natural language queries and board topology) to retrieve both conceptual explanations and peer-generated board states, offering capabilities that standard LLMs with game state access cannot provide alone.
- In a qualitative study of ten participants, half minimized or abandoned the AI tool during gameplay, primarily due to proactive but unsolicited delivery, generic responses, and a lack of trust in the system's recommendations.
- Despite overall disengagement, four participants found PEARL complementary to visualization tools when they proactively sought help, demonstrating that context-aware, on-demand scaffolding can still hold value in educational gaming environments.

## Context
The integration of generative AI into interactive learning environments represents a rapidly evolving frontier in human-computer interaction and educational technology. While large language models have shown promise in tutoring and adaptive feedback, grounding them in structured, domain-specific game data remains a significant technical and pedagogical challenge that this research directly addresses.
