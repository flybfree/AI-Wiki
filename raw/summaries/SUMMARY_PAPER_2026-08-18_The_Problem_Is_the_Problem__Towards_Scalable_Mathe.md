## Summary
The paper proposes a new human-AI discovery paradigm for mathematics that shifts the role of humans from selecting isolated problems to providing research directions. It introduces Find, Attempt, and Recommend (FAR) pipeline that searches literature, filters conjectures, and recommends items for review, achieving 77 items out of 598 potential resolutions in a combinatorics pilot.

## Key Takeaways
- The FAR pipeline recovers 6,453 candidate conjectures from 5,245 papers, showing that large corpora can generate many open problems.
- It filters to 4,717 well‑posed and still‑open conjectures, demonstrating effective pre‑screening of literature.
- Human review focuses on 77 items, highlighting the system’s ability to surface promising discoveries such as results on Davies–Jenssen–Perkins–Roberts, Erdős–Straus, Ikenmeyer–Pak–Panova, and Lund–Saraf–Wolf.

## Context
Current AI‑for‑math workflows treat human effort as a bottleneck at problem selection and final review. This paper addresses the inefficiency by integrating search capabilities into the discovery loop, aligning with trends in recommender systems that personalize content based on user interest.

## Implications
The approach could accelerate mathematical research by continuously feeding new conjectures to AI tools, reducing reliance on manual literature mining. Practitioners may adopt similar pipelines to maintain a steady flow of open problems and solutions, enhancing collaboration between experts and machines.
