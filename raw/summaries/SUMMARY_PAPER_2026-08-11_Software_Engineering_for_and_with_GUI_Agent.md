## Summary
This paper reviews 336 GUI‑agent papers published between 2018 and April 2026 to understand the current state of GUI agent research. It finds rapid growth, especially in mobile and web settings, but notes that many systems lack robust software engineering practices such as recovery, safety enforcement, and auditability.

## Key Takeaways
- Architectures increasingly use modular perceive‑reason‑act loops yet fail to integrate recovery mechanisms or human escalation pathways.  
- Evaluation focuses on task success rates and is limited in comparability across different interaction protocols.  
- Observability, privacy engineering, and systematic human oversight remain underdeveloped despite advancing capabilities.

## Context
GUI agents are moving toward closed‑loop software systems where perception, reasoning, and execution interact continuously. This trend mirrors broader AI research that emphasizes integration with real‑world interfaces, yet most work remains isolated from established software lifecycle standards.

## Implications
For practitioners, the gap between capability and deployment readiness creates risks of brittle, unmaintainable agents in production environments. Addressing these gaps will enable more reliable, secure, and scalable GUI‑agent systems across industries.
