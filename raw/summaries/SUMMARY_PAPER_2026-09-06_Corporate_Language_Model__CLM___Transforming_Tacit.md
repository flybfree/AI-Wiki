## Summary
The paper proposes the Corporate Language Model (CLM) as a framework that integrates tacit and unstructured enterprise knowledge into an ontology‑grounded foundation for AI, enabling reasoning that is both firm‑specific and scalable. It demonstrates how this architecture enables sovereign, auditable, and executable corporate intelligence.

## Key Takeaways
- CLM creates a Neurosymbolic Mesh coupling generative models with a knowledge graph to ground reasoning in firm‑specific ontologies, ensuring that AI decisions are traceable to organizational logic.
- The Skill Graph typifies reusable tactics, personas, objections, and goals as typed entities, enabling compositional explainability of AI actions by composing these building blocks.
- A Spec‑as‑Code paradigm translates grounded intent into executable artifacts, supporting dynamic capability building where new business rules can be encoded directly.

## Context
Enterprise AI often stalls because models lack the organizational context that drives decision making. This work addresses the gap between generic LLMs and firm‑specific knowledge by providing a structured substrate that encodes tacit expertise.

## Implications
The CLM framework could become a standard for regulated industries where explainability and data sovereignty are required, offering a blueprint for compliance. It encourages organizations to embed AI within living digital twins that evolve with business processes, fostering continuous learning.
