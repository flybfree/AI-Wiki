## Summary
The paper introduces a Workforce Readiness Level framework that maps AI‑driven manufacturing competency onto nine progressive stages and four pillars. It evaluates the framework using eight capstone projects over four semesters, showing a readiness index between 5.2 and 6.4 and highlighting gaps in cyber‑physical and data‑driven decision making.

## Key Takeaways
- The WRL framework adapts the Technology Readiness Level scale into nine competency stages and uses a four‑pillar rubric that includes digital AI literacy, cyber‑physical systems fluency, human‑machine collaboration, and data‑driven decision making.  
- A composite stage score and cohort‑level index reveal that strong analytics do not guarantee high readiness because cyber‑physical and data‑driven gaps persist.  
- Advancement to the highest stages depends on industry‑embedded experience rather than additional coursework.

## Context
The rapid integration of AI, IoT, robotics, and advanced robotics into manufacturing creates a skills mismatch that traditional curricula cannot address. This paper offers a diagnostic tool that aligns educational outcomes with industry needs in an era where technical fluency is insufficient without contextual experience.

## Implications
Educators can use the WRL to diagnose curriculum deficiencies and prioritize experiential learning. Accreditation bodies may adopt it as evidence of workforce readiness, while regional workforce systems can benchmark progress toward AI‑enabled manufacturing standards.
