## Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models can produce an "infinite poem" by generating couplets within a strict anaphoric, litany structure where the only variation lies in the poetic relationship between end words. Through two experiments—one involving manual prompting and co-creation, the other using automated semantic similarity methods—the authors demonstrate that while AI can produce interesting word pairings, it consistently falls short of human-level creativity and genuine understanding of poetic relationships. The paper also provides a survey of computational poetry experiments and argues that the couplet form is particularly well-suited for probing AI's poetic capabilities.

## Key Takeaways
- Experiment 1 relies on manual prompting and human-AI co-creation to extend an original poem, which is then developed into an animated webpage installation and interactive artwork, highlighting the collaborative dimension of AI-assisted poetry rather than fully autonomous generation.
- Experiment 2 achieves automated output reaching a 1000-couplet benchmark through an alternative method combining an LLM with semantic similarity scoring, with Method 2 demonstrating a higher capacity for both semantic coherence and quantitative poetic output compared to the first method.
- Despite these technical achievements, the authors conclude that current LLMs exhibit fundamental limitations at the word level in generating truly creative poetic relationships, suggesting that AI cannot yet replicate the depth of human authorial intention or the nuanced understanding of what makes a poetic pairing meaningful.

## Context
This research sits at the intersection of computational linguistics, creative AI, and literary studies, contributing to a long-standing debate about whether algorithmic systems can produce art that carries genuine authorial intention. The paper distinguishes between "natural" and "artificial" poetry, a distinction that has been central to computational poetry experiments since their earliest iterations. By choosing the couplet form with strict anaphoric constraints, the authors create a controlled experimental framework that isolates the model's ability to generate meaningful semantic relationships at the word level, making it a more rigorous test than open-ended poetry generation.

## Implications
For practitioners in AI-assisted creative writing, this research suggests that poetry will not be replaced by LLMs but will instead "respond and revive" by incorporating AI workflows as collaborative tools, pointing toward hybrid human-AI creative pipelines rather than fully autonomous generation. For the broader AI research community, the findings underscore that current models remain limited in fine-grained semantic creativity at the word level, which has implications for any application requiring nuanced linguistic artistry, from literary generation to advertising copywriting. The paper also calls for continued critical engagement with questions of authorial intention and the ontological boundaries between human and machine creativity as AI systems become increasingly embedded in artistic practice.
