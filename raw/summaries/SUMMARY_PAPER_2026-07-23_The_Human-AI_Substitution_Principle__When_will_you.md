## Summary
The paper introduces a formal analytical model for Human--AI Task Allocation that quantifies the economic asymmetry between human skill acquisition and AI capability scaling. It derives a precise condition—the Human--AI Substitution Principle—that predicts when AI will replace human labor within an organization. The analysis shows that replacement depends on risk-adjusted costs, organizational depth, deployment scale, strategic adaptation, and structural hierarchy.

## Key Takeaways
- The model reveals that abrupt workforce transitions can occur even without imposing a minimum human‑fraction constraint because risk heterogeneity can sustain both AI and human roles.
- Middle‑management positions are especially vulnerable to automation when the organization has deep hierarchies and high baseline costs relative to AI capabilities.
- Highly skilled workers face replacement only if their skill threshold exceeds the asymmetry defined by organizational depth, cost structure, and risk differentials.

## Context
Organizations worldwide grapple with rapid advances in artificial intelligence that promise efficiency gains but also raise concerns about job displacement. This work bridges automation economics with organizational design, offering a unified framework to predict where and how AI will take over tasks. By formalizing the asymmetry assumption, it provides a testable lens for strategic workforce planning.

## Implications
Practitioners can use the model to anticipate which roles are most at risk and to design hybrid structures that mitigate abrupt layoffs.
