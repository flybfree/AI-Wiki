## Summary
The paper investigates how labeling a prompt with “Victorian” or “Zero‑Style” influences the output of three large language models generating novel‑length texts. It finds that Victorian prompts cause systematic shifts in GPT and Qwen toward nineteenth‑century grammar, while Llama shows weaker alignment. The Period Alignment Score quantifies this effect using topic‑reduced grammatical features.

## Key Takeaways
- Victorian prompts produce consistent historical‑direction shifts in GPT and Qwen but not robustly in Llama.
- Using Victorian‑only recalibration or harder comparison corpora strengthens the GPT and Qwen effects.
- The measurable target is the broader nineteenth century rather than the Victorian period per se.

## Context
This work addresses a growing tension between AI’s ability to generate text and our desire for historical authenticity. By treating literary periods as constraints, it explores how generative models can be steered by narrative framing, a concept relevant to both scholarly analysis and creative applications.

## Implications
For researchers, the study suggests that period labels can be used as design parameters in prompt engineering. For industry practitioners, integrating such constraints could produce more thematically coherent outputs, though model capabilities vary across systems.
