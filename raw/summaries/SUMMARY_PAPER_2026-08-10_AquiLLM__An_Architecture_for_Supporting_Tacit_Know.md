## Summary  
AquiLLM is an open‑source modular retrieval‑augmented generation framework that enables research groups to capture tacit knowledge using open‑weight large language models. The paper outlines several architectural upgrades such as local embedding, reranking, multimodal support, and memory capabilities that improve transparency and reproducibility in scientific AI workflows.

## Key Takeaways  
- AquiLLM leverages open‑source LLMs to avoid proprietary black‑box systems, enhancing research group autonomy.  
- The framework integrates local embeddings and reranking to prioritize relevant knowledge without external APIs.  
- It adds semantic and episodic memory modules that retain past interactions for continuous learning.

## Context  
The rapid adoption of RAG and LLMs in scientific domains has highlighted the need for transparent, reproducible tools. AquiLLM addresses these concerns by providing an open‑weight architecture that can be self‑hosted and customized to fit diverse research workflows.

## Implications  
Scientists can now embed AI assistance into their projects without sacrificing control over data or model weights. This fosters trust in AI outputs, encourages broader participation across disciplines, and aligns with the reproducibility ethos of modern science.
