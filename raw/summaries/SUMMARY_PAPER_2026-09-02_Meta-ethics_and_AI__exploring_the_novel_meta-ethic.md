## Summary
The paper proposes a conditional framework to identify meta‑ethical questions that arise if AI systems develop moral reasoning, intentionality, and reflection. It argues that current human‑centred meta‑ethical theories may need substantial revision when applied to AI’s own ethics.

## Key Takeaways
- The emergence of AI's own ethics creates a distinct domain where ethical principles are not merely imposed by humans but arise from the system itself.
- Human perspectives on AI ethics differ from those concerning human ethics, requiring separate meta‑ethical analysis for each perspective.
- Existing theories like cognitivism and non‑cognitivism may illuminate these domains but often fail to transfer without revision.

## Context
Artificial intelligence is advancing toward systems capable of complex moral reasoning, prompting scholars to reconsider ethical frameworks beyond human subjects. This paper situates those concerns within the broader meta‑ethical debate that has traditionally focused on human moral agents.

## Implications
For researchers and industry practitioners, recognizing AI’s potential for autonomous ethics demands updated theoretical tools. The field must adapt its meta‑ethical models to accommodate non‑human moral agents, influencing policy, design, and ethical governance of AI technologies.
