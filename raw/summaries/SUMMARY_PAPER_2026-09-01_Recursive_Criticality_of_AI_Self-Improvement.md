## Summary
This paper investigates when AI research and development feedback becomes self‑amplifying by deriving a recursive reproduction number that compares feedback strength to the difficulty of further progress. The model shows that amplification can occur before visible acceleration, and that higher baseline productivity or shared improvements across organizations can trigger it even without individual breakthroughs.

## Key Takeaways
- The recursive reproduction number $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{AI}}$ determines whether AI improvements compound across cycles; when it exceeds one the system is in a self‑amplifying regime.  
- The transition between regimes depends on feedback strength relative to increasing research difficulty, not on a fixed level of model capability.  
- Multiple research actors can collectively create an ecosystem that is self‑amplifying even if no single actor achieves it.

## Context
Rapid advances in AI are driven by iterative cycles of research, where each cycle builds upon the previous one. Traditional models focus only on speed of progress, but this paper highlights a hidden dynamic: feedback loops can amplify or dampen those cycles independently of how quickly new capabilities emerge.

## Implications
Understanding $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{AI}}$ helps researchers and industry stakeholders gauge whether an AI development effort is entering a self‑reinforcing loop that could lead to runaway growth. Practitioners can use measurable properties such as feedback strength, propagation efficiency, cycle duration, and rising difficulty to differentiate true recursive amplification from ordinary progress.
