## Summary
This paper introduces Ideation Arena, a battle‑style platform that pits research ideas generated by fourteen frontier large language models and five research agent architectures against one another through pairwise human assessments. The study collects over six thousand double‑blind comparisons from computer science researchers and produces an Elo rating leaderboard that reflects expert preferences under a shared closed‑context protocol.

## Key Takeaways
- Ideation Arena demonstrates substantial variation in ideation quality among different frameworks, with some agents improving on their base models while others provide little benefit or even fall short.  
- The leaderboard remains stable across changes in annotator composition and domain coverage, indicating robust interrater agreement for the evaluation metric.  
- Current LLM judges cannot reliably reproduce expert preferences, achieving only a 72.56% soft accuracy on overall quality.

## Context
The rapid proliferation of large language models has led to an explosion of automated research idea generation, yet there is no standardized way to gauge their scientific merit. This work addresses that gap by providing a human‑centric benchmark that captures nuanced expert judgments beyond simple metrics.

## Implications
For researchers and industry practitioners, Ideation Arena offers a practical tool to compare autonomous ideation systems in real time, guiding development efforts toward models that truly enhance creativity and relevance. The findings highlight the need for more sophisticated evaluation protocols as AI assists scientific discovery.
