## Summary
This paper introduces a methodology for validating semantic mappings between ontologies by embedding metaphysical commitments into the mapping process. The authors illustrate their approach with a test case comparing IES and BFO ontologies, emphasizing cardinality constraints on mapped elements. They also demonstrate how these principles are applied via SPARQL queries to validate mapping pipelines.

## Key Takeaways
- The methodology treats ontology foundations as metaphysical choices that shape validation rules, especially regarding the number of matched instances.
- Cardinality constraints derived from these metaphysical commitments are enforced through SPARQL queries to ensure mapping integrity.
- The framework bridges formal ontology theory with practical data integration workflows using query‑based validation.

## Context
In AI and semantic web research, aligning diverse ontologies is essential for interoperable knowledge representation. Traditional validation often ignores the underlying philosophical assumptions that drive each ontology’s structure, limiting its effectiveness in real‑world applications.

## Implications
Practitioners can leverage this framework to create robust mapping pipelines that respect both technical specifications and philosophical foundations. This leads to more reliable data integrations across domains such as healthcare, legal, and AI research.
