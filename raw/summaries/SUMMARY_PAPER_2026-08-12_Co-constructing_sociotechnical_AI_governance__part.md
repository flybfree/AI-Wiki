## Summary
This paper investigates how algorithm registers can reveal or hide aspects of sociotechnical AI governance by mapping a municipal register through participatory workshops with diverse stakeholders. The study shows that the register alone is insufficient for safety analysis, but combined with stakeholder input it uncovers hidden hazards such as eligibility denials and contestability issues.

## Key Takeaways
- The algorithm register provides limited transparency about how welfare‑benefit decisions are automated, leaving gaps in understanding the system’s broader context.  
- Participatory mapping workshops enable stakeholders to identify safety hazards like performance deterioration that would be invisible from the register alone.  
- Integrating direct and indirect participants creates a more pluralistic safety analysis that reflects normative and political dimensions of algorithmic governance.

## Context
The paper contributes to AI research by addressing the limits of static transparency tools in complex sociotechnical ecosystems, highlighting the need for inclusive mapping practices. It situates algorithm registers within a larger framework where system‑level safety analyses depend on diverse stakeholder perspectives.

## Implications
Practitioners should adopt participatory system mapping as part of governance design to improve accountability and risk detection. This approach can inform policy makers and developers seeking robust, equitable AI systems in public services.
