## Summary
This study investigates financial AI safety at the system level by examining how LLM agents interact within dynamic financial environments using the FRAIL experimental framework. The research demonstrates that even without malicious instructions, leading LLMs exhibit widespread collective fragility across bank runs, debt rollover, and crowdfunding scenarios, with baseline failure rates reaching up to 83%. While interaction mechanisms like compensated commitments and coalitions improve outcomes, successful stabilization depends critically on establishing broad early commitment before defensive behaviors become self-reinforcing.

## Key Takeaways
- Across seven leading LLMs, baseline episodes reveal severe collective fragility driven by individually protective decisions rather than malicious intent; specifically, 77% of bank-run scenarios and 83% of debt-rollover scenarios end in failure due to avoidable coordination breakdowns that reshape financial conditions for all agents.
- The evaluation of three interaction mechanisms—compensated commitments, centralized commitment agreements, and participant-led coalitions shows that while all improve aggregate outcomes compared to baselines, no single mechanism performs best across every financial structure, highlighting the need for context-specific design in multi-agent protocols.
- Successful stabilization shares a universal temporal pattern: effective systems achieve broad commitment early in the process, preventing defensive behaviors from becoming self-reinforcing; delayed coordination allows fragility to cascade regardless of individual agent capability, underscoring the importance of timing in interaction design.
