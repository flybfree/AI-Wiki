## Summary
This paper introduces a framework to audit value pluralism in the clinical ethics of large language models, using clinician‑verified ethical dilemmas as benchmarks and an attribution method that extracts model decision priorities. The results show that while frontier LLMs can articulate competing values during reasoning, their actual outputs are highly deterministic across repeated sampling, revealing systematic value preferences rather than the distributional pluralism seen among physicians.

## Key Takeaways
- Models exhibit near‑deterministic decisions on benchmark cases, indicating committed ethical preferences rather than genuine pluralistic deliberation.  
- The majority of model priorities align with typical inter‑physician variation, but a notable subset systematically underweights patient autonomy.  
- Deploying such models without attention to their value biases could amplify those biases across all patients served.

## Context
The rapid integration of AI into medical decision support introduces new ethical challenges that differ from human clinicians’ inherently pluralistic practice. Understanding how LLMs encode and express clinical ethics is crucial for ensuring responsible deployment in a field where values often conflict.

## Implications
If left unchecked, the consistent value orientation of LLMs could replace nuanced clinical pluralism with a uniform, potentially biased decision pipeline. Practitioners must therefore design safeguards that balance model priorities with patient autonomy to preserve ethical diversity.
