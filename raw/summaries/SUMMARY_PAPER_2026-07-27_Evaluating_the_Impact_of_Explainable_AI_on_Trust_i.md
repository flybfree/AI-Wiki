## Summary
This study investigates how explainable AI (XAI) influences developers’ trust in automated code‑review systems. Using a within‑subjects experiment with 34 participants, it compares three LLM‑based review setups that vary in the amount of explanation provided. The results show that full explanations boost perceived trust but not agreement, while moderate explanations maximize agreement.

## Key Takeaways
- Full explanations (Condition A) produce the highest trust rating (M = 3.99/5) but do not lead to the strongest agreement with AI recommendations.
- Moderate explanations (Condition B) achieve the highest agreement rate (89.22%), suggesting that more explanation may prompt developers to question AI decisions.
- No explanations (Condition C) result in the lowest trust and agreement, indicating a clear negative impact of omitting XAI.

## Context
Explainable AI aims to make automated systems transparent for human users, yet its role in software development tasks like code review remains underexplored. This paper contributes to that gap by empirically linking explanation depth to user trust and acceptance in a real‑world coding environment.

## Implications
Developers and system designers should balance the amount of XAI provided to avoid over‑explaining while still fostering confidence in AI feedback. The findings guide the creation of trustworthy code‑review tools that enhance both developer collaboration and system reliability.
