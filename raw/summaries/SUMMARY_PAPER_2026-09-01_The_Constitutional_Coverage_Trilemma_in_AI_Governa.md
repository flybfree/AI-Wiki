## Summary
This paper investigates whether the variety of constitutional values encoded in frontier large language models matches human preferences. It finds that demand for safety, helpfulness, honesty, autonomy and equity is widely spread but only a small fraction of model archetypes cover these needs, leaving many users without a suitable model.

## Key Takeaways
- Demand spans all five values with the largest group under one‑third, while supply covers only about 2 % of demand under conservative estimates.  
- No archetype places helpfulness or autonomy first; roughly 37 % of users are constitutionally homeless and the trade‑offs shift monotonically across model families.  
- Adding just two vertices (honesty and autonomy) improves mean regret by 47 %, outperforming the full set of 23 archetypes.

## Context
Frontier AI systems act as implicit constitutional institutions that rank core values, yet their diversity is limited compared to human value preferences. The study bridges this gap with empirical data from a large US sample and an audit of model defaults.

## Implications
Practitioners must curate or augment model families to cover under‑served values before deploying them at scale. Ignoring the trilemma risks widening welfare gaps for vulnerable users.
