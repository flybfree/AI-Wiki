## Summary
This paper presents the first measurement study of online conflict over generative AI, tracing the pipeline from toxic discourse in Reddit communities to concrete anti-AI actions such as crawler tarpits and data-poisoning services. By analyzing 931,640 posts across 25 communities and tracing the output of three deployed anti-AI tools, the authors quantify how online hostility translates into technical interventions that directly affect AI training data pipelines.

## Key Takeaways
- Toxicity in AI-conflict communities is dramatically elevated: 30.0% of posts in communities devoted to the pro/anti-AI conflict are toxic, compared to only 4.4% in general AI communities, indicating that the discourse surrounding generative AI is disproportionately hostile and polarized.
- Anti-AI actions primarily target AI crawling and model training data through mechanisms like image cloaking and data poisoning. The authors identified 3,312 such actions and traced their outputs, revealing that 66.5% of code samples generated by a data-poisoning service contain fabricated facts, misused APIs, or broken logic, yet 93.6% of these poisoned samples still pass a public code-curation pipeline, nearly matching the 95.0% survival rate of genuine source code.
- Anti-AI tool outputs have already infiltrated major training corpora: crawler tarpit and data-poisoning output has entered Common Crawl, a widely used public web archive, and crawler tarpit output has also entered FineWeb, a released pretraining dataset. A simple modification—adding a period to the end of each paragraph—increases the curation survival rate of crawler tarpit output from 16.4% to 91.9%, demonstrating how easily adversarial content can slip into training pipelines.

## Context
As generative AI models are trained on vast web-scale corpora, the integrity of training data has become a critical concern for model quality and reliability. Online communities have begun organizing not just rhetorically but technically, deploying tools that actively interfere with data collection pipelines. This paper fills a gap in understanding how grassroots opposition to AI manifests in measurable, technical actions that directly perturb the data supply chain feeding large language models, bridging the study of online discourse with the engineering of AI training infrastructure.

## Implications
For AI practitioners and data engineers, these findings highlight that adversarial content is already embedded in widely used pretraining datasets, meaning model developers must account for contamination from anti-AI interventions when curating training data. For content moderation and platform governance, the elevated toxicity in AI-conflict communities signals a need for targeted moderation strategies. For the broader field, this work underscores the importance of auditing public web archives and pretraining corpora for adversarial insertions, and it calls for collaboration between social media researchers and AI training pipeline engineers to monitor and mitigate data poisoning at scale.
