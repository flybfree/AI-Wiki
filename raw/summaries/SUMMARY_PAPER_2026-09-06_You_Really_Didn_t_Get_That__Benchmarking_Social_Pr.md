## Summary
This paper introduces a benchmark to evaluate how large language models recover social pragmatic meanings from indirect and playful Chinese online comments. The authors construct 4,735 human‑validated items from over 200,000 public interaction records and test eight LLMs in a leave‑writer‑out setting; the best model reaches 81.42% accuracy versus human performance of 90.8%, while the average across models is 68.7%.

## Key Takeaways
- The strongest model achieves 81.42% leave‑writer‑out accuracy, which is close to but still below human accuracy.
- Models frequently detect broad irony or playfulness yet often misidentify the specific mechanism or interactional move involved.
- Human evaluators score 90.8%, highlighting a notable gap between current LLM performance and human pragmatic understanding.

## Context
Evaluating LLMs on situated pragmatic inference is crucial because social media language relies heavily on indirect cues that are not captured by predefined categories. This benchmark provides a real‑world dataset to measure how models handle nuanced, context‑dependent meanings in everyday discourse.

## Implications
Improving LLM performance on pragmatic tasks will benefit both research and industry, as it enables systems to better understand user intent and improve conversational quality. Practitioners should prioritize developing models that can interpret subtle social cues beyond simple keyword matching.
