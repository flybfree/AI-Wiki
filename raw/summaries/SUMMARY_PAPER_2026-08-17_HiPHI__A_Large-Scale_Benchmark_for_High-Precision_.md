## Summary
This paper introduces HiPHI, a large‑scale benchmark of over six hundred hours of high‑fidelity human motion and object interaction data. The dataset is generated with optical motion capture to achieve sub‑millimeter spatial accuracy for full‑body markers and mesh‑level object trajectories, and it is organized using FrameNet primitives to maximize coverage of the human motion and interaction manifold.

## Key Takeaways
- HiPHI provides a 600+ hour collection that far exceeds existing motion datasets while preserving millimeter‑scale precision in both body and object data.  
- The dataset systematically covers a wide range of whole‑body motions and grounded interactions, guided by FrameNet’s linguistic primitives to ensure manifold diversity.  
- A benchmark suite evaluates motion‑space diversity, interaction grounding, object consistency, and physical AI applicability, demonstrating that HiPHI expands coverage without sacrificing high‑fidelity quality.

## Context
The gap between internet‑scale video data—rich in content but lacking precise physics—and laboratory datasets with narrow behavioral coverage hampers scalable humanoid policy learning. HiPHI bridges this gap by delivering a physically grounded, high‑resolution resource that can be used for training and evaluating embodied AI systems.

## Implications
For researchers, HiPHI offers a reproducible foundation to train models on diverse human motions and interactions, improving generalization in real‑world tasks. Practitioners can leverage the dataset to develop more robust motion prior models in computer graphics and robotics, accelerating progress toward truly intelligent humanoid agents.
