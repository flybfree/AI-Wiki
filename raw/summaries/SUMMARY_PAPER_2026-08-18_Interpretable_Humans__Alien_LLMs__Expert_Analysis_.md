## Summary
The paper investigates whether latent factors governing large language model performance map onto human‑interpretable cognitive constructs by applying Exploratory Factor Analysis to responses from humans and six LLMs across quantitative reasoning and chemistry assessments. Blind Subject‑Matter Experts could interpret most of the human‑derived factors but none in quantitative reasoning and only half in chemistry, revealing that LLM mechanisms are statistically opaque.

## Key Takeaways
- Human EFA yields factor graphs that SMEs readily label with pedagogical meaning, indicating clear cognitive constructs.  
- The same analysis on LLMs produces factor graphs that lack any interpretable labels, especially in quantitative reasoning where no factors were assigned.  
- In chemistry, only half of the LLM factors received a meaningful interpretation, suggesting partial but not complete alignment.

## Context
This study matters because it challenges the assumption that AI performance can be understood through familiar human cognitive models, highlighting a gap between statistical similarity and substantive meaning in model evaluation. It underscores the need for methods that reveal underlying mechanisms beyond simple correlation.

## Implications
For researchers, this work calls for developing assessment frameworks that expose latent structures rather than relying on pre‑set rubrics. Practitioners should treat LLM outputs as statistically distinct from human reasoning, avoiding overinterpretation of their performance metrics.
