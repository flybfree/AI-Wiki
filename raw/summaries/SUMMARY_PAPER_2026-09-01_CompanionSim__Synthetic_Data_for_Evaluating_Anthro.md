## Summary
The paper introduces CompanionSim, a synthetic data framework that generates 2,240 human‑chatbot dialogues across 16 chatbot behaviors and seven use cases to study how companionship behaviors affect human perceptions. Experiments with over 3,800 participants show that such behaviors lower likability, perceived humanlikeness, and trust in AI chatbots.

## Key Takeaways
- Companionship‑oriented chatbots are judged less likable by a majority of participants, especially women and older respondents.  
- The same behavior reduces the sense that an AI is humanlike, leading to lower trust ratings across all age groups.  
- These negative perceptions persist in both U.S. and international samples, indicating a cross‑cultural impact.

## Context
Human‑AI companionship research faces data scarcity because real interactions are rare and often unstructured. This work addresses the gap by creating a large, reproducible synthetic dataset that can be used for controlled experiments on AI social dynamics.

## Implications
Researchers can now benchmark chatbot designs against human expectations using standardized synthetic scenarios. Practitioners should consider reducing overt companionship cues if they aim to maintain trust and perceived neutrality in AI systems.
