## Summary
This study investigates whether financial news is priced in before it is released and how rumors versus confirmed stories affect market reactions. Using a large language model classifier on millions of articles across 3,000 US stocks, the authors find that price moves concentrate at publication, underreaction to numbers occurs, and volatility widens then narrows after release.

## Key Takeaways
- The cumulative move in the news direction by the close of publication day is 2.8 times its value 20 days later, indicating most impact happens before or on the day of release.  
- For rumor‑flagged events the entire price change occurs on the rumor day while follow‑up confirmation adds no additional effect.  
- Markets underreact to fundamental numbers and overreact to story‑driven news, with volatility rising before publication and falling once the news is out.

## Context
The research leverages AI‑generated event tags to quantify how quickly markets absorb public information, highlighting a gap between human intuition about timing and empirical market behavior. This approach provides a data‑driven framework for testing news impact models in quantitative finance.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that news‑conditioned forecasts should weight pre‑publication signals heavily and treat rumors as fully priced events. The table of drift per event tag can serve as priors to improve predictive models in AI‑driven trading systems.
