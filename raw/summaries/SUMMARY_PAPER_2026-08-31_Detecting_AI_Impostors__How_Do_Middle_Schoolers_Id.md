## Summary
This paper investigates how middle schoolers detect AI‑generated impersonators in a cooperative social deduction game called DoppelBot, revealing that their detection accuracy improves over time as they shift from focusing on linguistic cues to using shared social and contextual signals. The study also shows that students reflect on privacy issues and acknowledge the embodied limitations of LLMs.

## Key Takeaways
- Students’ detection accuracy rises with repeated exposure because they move away from analyzing word patterns toward recognizing group dynamics and situational context.  
- Participants explicitly discuss data‑privacy concerns, indicating an emerging awareness of AI’s societal impact beyond mere technical flaws.  
- The research demonstrates that middle schoolers can identify AI agents not only through text but also by interpreting non‑linguistic cues such as peer behavior.

## Context
The rapid adoption of generative AI among adolescents creates a need to understand how young users perceive and trust these tools in real‑world interactions. This work bridges the gap between technical detection methods and human social cognition, offering insights into age‑appropriate communication strategies for AI developers.

## Implications
For educators and designers, the findings suggest that fostering collaborative environments can enhance students’ ability to spot AI impersonation without relying solely on linguistic analysis. Practitioners should also incorporate privacy discussions early in AI integration to cultivate responsible attitudes toward data use.
