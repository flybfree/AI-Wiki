## Summary
This paper investigates whether adding more agents to a single LLM improves its ability to verify facts by comparing a three‑agent panel to a fixed single‑agent baseline across six verification datasets. The results show modest gains on two benchmarks, a loss on one, and no clear effect on the others.

## Key Takeaways
- Relative accuracy changes range from +8.5 to -4.4 percentage points, indicating that debate can both help or hurt performance depending on the task.
- Gains are tied to differences between model variants used in reference and panel, not solely to multi‑agent interaction.
- Three datasets show statistically inconclusive results, suggesting limited evidence for a universal benefit.

## Context
The study addresses a growing trend of using ensemble models to boost reasoning capabilities. By focusing on groundedness verification—a task where factual accuracy is critical—it highlights the gap between theoretical promise and empirical performance in AI systems.

## Implications
For developers, the findings caution against assuming that more agents automatically yield better outcomes without careful benchmarking. Practitioners should evaluate system‑level effects rather than attributing improvements solely to debate dynamics.
