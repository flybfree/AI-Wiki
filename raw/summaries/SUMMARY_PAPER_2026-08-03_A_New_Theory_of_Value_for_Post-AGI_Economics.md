## Summary
This paper introduces Flourishing Value Theory (FVT) to address the economic challenges posed by post‑AGI automation, arguing that traditional measures like GDP and profit are insufficient. It proposes a multidimensional value framework centered on durable human and planetary flourishing rather than scarcity‑driven competition.

## Key Takeaways
- FVT redefines value as the counterfactual contribution of systems to people’s ability to flourish within ecological limits, emphasizing distribution sensitivity.
- The theory separates value creation from value capture, treating price, profit, productivity, and GDP as only partial signals rather than final outcomes.
- It shifts economic dynamics toward governed abundance, transformation over transaction, and infinite‑game cooperation that preserves agency.

## Context
The rapid advancement of artificial general intelligence threatens existing scarcity‑based models of labor and expertise, prompting a need for alternative value metrics. FVT builds on capability theory, ecological economics, and AI governance to align economic incentives with long‑term human well‑being.

## Implications
Policymakers and firms must adopt Flourishing Metrics and Return on Flourishing accounting to guide investment away from short‑term output maximization toward sustainable, equitable flourishing. This shift could reshape corporate strategy, public finance, and AI regulation in the post‑AGI era.
