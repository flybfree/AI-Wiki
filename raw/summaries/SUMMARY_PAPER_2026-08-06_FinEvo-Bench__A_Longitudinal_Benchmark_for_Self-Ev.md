## Summary
This paper introduces FinEvo-Bench, a longitudinal benchmark that evaluates self‑evolving agents across 120 real‑case financial tasks organized in six professional domains. The study compares four agent scaffolds using the Qwen3.7-Max backbone and shows that evolving conditions improve scores by up to 19 points while reducing compliance issues by up to 0.44 per task.

## Key Takeaways
- Evolving agents achieve higher evolved scores (up to 91.65) and fewer compliance violations than non‑evolving controls, indicating effective use of prior experience.
- The largest self‑evolution gain is observed in Codex (+19.37 points), suggesting that skill‑only evolution outperforms memory‑only or combined approaches.
- Rubric feedback yields better task quality and compliance than reference‑answer feedback across all scaffolds.

## Context
The paper addresses a gap in AI evaluation by measuring how agents transfer experience between tasks within professional workflows, moving beyond isolated benchmarks. It highlights the importance of longitudinal assessment for autonomous agents that must adapt continuously.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, FinEvo-Bench provides a practical framework to benchmark self‑evolving agents and guide design choices such as feedback mechanisms. For researchers, it sets a new standard for evaluating real‑world professional competence in AI systems.
