## Summary
The paper presents Agaram 2.5B, a 2.5-billion-parameter Tamil-English language model trained from scratch on approximately 50 billion tokens (58% Tamil) using eight H100 GPUs over six days, with a total project cost of roughly USD 13,300. The model supports Tamil script, romanized Tamil (Tanglish), and English, includes tool-calling capability, and outperforms Sarvam-1, a 2B Indic base model, across all six FLORES and IN22 translation directions, while nearly doubling its base model's performance on native-Tamil instruction-following benchmarks. However, the model remains weak on factual knowledge, with reading-comprehension and knowledge benchmarks near chance level.

## Key Takeaways
- The model was trained from scratch on 50 billion tokens with a custom tokenizer that preserves line breaks, followed by supervised fine-tuning on 134,428 conversations generated by an open-weight teacher model and three targeted training rounds, producing a chat model that correctly selects the right tool 23 out of 25 times and produces exact expected arguments 13 out of 25 times on tool-calling prompts whose schemas appeared in training data.
- Translation performance surpasses Sarvam-1 across all six FLORES and IN22 directions, achieving chrF scores of 54.3 into Tamil and 55.2 into English compared to Sarvam-1's 49.2 and 44.2, and native-Tamil IndicIFEval performance nearly doubled from 10.0% to 19.3%, demonstrating meaningful gains for under-represented language capabilities.
- Knowledge remains a critical weakness: on 50 held-out everyday questions authored by a native Tamil speaker, an LLM judge rated only 46% of replies as usable across three evaluation passes (with a 95% confidence interval of 33–60% for 23 of 50 questions), and nearly every failure was attributed to a wrong fact rather than formatting or language errors. Two additional SFT experiments altered answer format but did not improve knowledge measures.

## Context
This work addresses a persistent gap in large language model development: the under-representation of Dravidian languages like Tamil in foundational training data and evaluation pipelines. Most existing Indic language models are fine-tuned from English-centric base models, limiting their native-language fluency and cultural alignment. Agaram 2.5B demonstrates that a small team with modest compute resources (one eight-GPU node, approximately USD 13,300 total) can train a language model from scratch with a custom tokenizer and achieve competitive multilingual translation performance, offering a replicable playbook for other low-resource language communities. The paper also candidly documents the failures of its predecessor, Agaram 1.5B, and the iterative design decisions those failures informed.

## Implications
For practitioners working on under-represented languages, this paper provides a concrete, budget-conscious blueprint covering tokenizer design, data composition ratios, SFT strategies, and evaluation methodology that can be adapted to other linguistic communities without requiring frontier-scale compute. The honest reporting of knowledge deficiencies and judge-score variability across evaluation passes sets a transparency standard for small-model research. For industry, the release of GGUF files for llama.cpp and open weights on Hugging Face lowers the barrier for deploying Tamil-capable models in local or edge settings, though the factual-accuracy limitations mean such models are currently better suited for translation, formatting, and tool-orchestration tasks than for knowledge-intensive applications.
