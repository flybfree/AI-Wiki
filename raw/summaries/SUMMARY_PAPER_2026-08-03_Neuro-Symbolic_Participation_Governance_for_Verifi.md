## Summary
This paper introduces a neuro‑symbolic decentralized governance framework that enables verifiable AI agents to participate safely in open digital twin ecosystems. The approach combines probabilistic neural reasoning with deterministic institutional policies, allowing trustworthy collaboration while preventing unauthorized actions. Experiments on a clinic‑digital‑twin‑wearable prototype show the system can enforce policies with minimal overhead.

## Key Takeaways
- Multi‑layer semantic profiles represent agents, merging neural inference with formal ontologies to produce machine‑interpretable capabilities that respect policy constraints.
- Blockchain‑based smart contracts validate credentials issued by organizational authorities, providing auditable participation without revealing sensitive data.
- The framework successfully blocks unauthorized interactions and enforces institutional policies in a prototype involving clinic, digital twin, and wearable agents.

## Context
Current AI systems rely heavily on large language models that lack built‑in mechanisms for identity verification or policy enforcement. In decentralized environments where multiple institutions share digital twins, this gap creates trust risks and compliance challenges. This work addresses those gaps by integrating symbolic reasoning with blockchain to create a verifiable governance layer.

## Implications
The neuro‑symbolic governance model offers a scalable solution for safe human‑machine collaboration across institutional boundaries, reducing the need for manual audits. Practitioners can adopt this framework to build trustworthy AI agents that comply with regulations while preserving data privacy and operational efficiency.
