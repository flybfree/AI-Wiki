## Summary
This paper proposes ARISMA, an AI Reporting and Integration standard for systematic reviews, scoping reviews, and mapping studies. It aims to provide a complete operational framework that guides when artificial intelligence should be used, how it must be validated, which decisions stay human‑led, and how involvement is reported. The framework includes a lifecycle taxonomy, process guidance, governance model, tool support, reporting checklist, and validation matrix.

## Key Takeaways
- AI is entering review practice across many steps but empirical evidence remains uneven and task‑dependent, so unconstrained automation is not justified.  
- Current standards such as PRISMA 2020 and related guides lack an end‑to‑end operational standard for integrating AI, leaving a gap in methodological clarity.  
- ARISMA treats AI as an inspected, benchmarked, logged, and reversible assistant rather than an autonomous reviewer, ensuring every consequential decision remains human interpretable, auditable, and accountable.

## Context
The rapid advancement of artificial intelligence, machine learning, and large language models is reshaping evidence synthesis workflows. While these technologies can automate tasks like search, screening, extraction, and reporting, the literature on their appropriate use in systematic reviews is fragmented. ARISMA addresses this gap by offering a unified guideline that balances innovation with methodological rigor.

## Implications
For researchers, practitioners, and institutions adopting AI tools, ARISMA provides a clear roadmap to integrate technology responsibly without compromising transparency or reproducibility. It also supports regulatory compliance and ethical use of data, fostering trust in AI‑enhanced evidence synthesis across the scientific community.
