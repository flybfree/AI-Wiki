## Summary
This paper presents NRCD, an openly available database of 128,963 collegiate running performances from 2004 to 2026, and introduces a unified performance standardization pipeline that reduces within‑athlete variability. The dataset includes detailed course metrics, weather data, and venue information for most meets, enabling rigorous longitudinal analyses.

## Key Takeaways
- NRCD provides the first large‑scale public collection of collegiate running results, covering 28,913 athletes across four sports with 36.3 % women, which expands prior research beyond ~500 performances and reduces gender bias.  
- The unified standardization framework lowers median within‑athlete cross‑meet variability by 51.0 % for women and 34.4 % for men compared to raw times, demonstrating the impact of distance, elevation, and heat adjustments.  
- The dataset is maintained as a live community‑governed resource with open submissions and expert approval, supporting continuous growth and reproducibility.

## Context
In AI research on sports analytics, large, standardized datasets are essential for training models that predict performance or identify patterns across diverse populations. NRCD addresses this need by delivering a high‑quality, gender‑balanced dataset enriched with environmental covariates, which can be leveraged to develop more equitable and accurate machine learning models.

## Implications
For researchers, NRCD enables longitudinal athlete modeling and environmental confounder studies without relying on limited or biased sources. Practitioners in sports analytics can integrate this data into AI pipelines to produce fairer predictions and actionable insights for collegiate running programs.
