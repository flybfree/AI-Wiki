## Summary
This paper presents EvolvingWorld, an open‑schema framework that enables co‑evolving character agents and world models within interactive literary simulations. By treating the simulation as a long‑horizon process with persistent updates, the authors demonstrate improved coherence across extended scenes compared to static persona imitation or isolated scene generation.

## Key Takeaways
- EvolvingWorld replaces fixed schemas with an open‑schema architecture that allows diverse literary worlds to be simulated without predefined constraints.
- The framework couples a Character Agent for multi‑character role‑play and persistent profile evolution with an LLM‑based World Model that maintains global state, location, and entity information across scenes.
- Evaluation is performed at trajectory level using 20 metrics across 10 dimensions, showing measurable gains in long‑horizon simulation fidelity.

## Context
The work addresses a gap in AI literature where interactive storytelling often decouples character behavior from world dynamics. By integrating persistent agents with a global world model, the study aligns with broader trends toward holistic agentic environments and long‑term planning in generative systems.

## Implications
EvolvingWorld offers practitioners a scalable method to create richly evolving narrative experiences that can be reused across multiple literary domains. Its open‑schema design encourages community contributions, fostering innovation while providing measurable performance improvements for AI‑driven storytelling applications.
