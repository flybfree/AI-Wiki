## Summary
The paper proposes a self‑explanation tutor called ESSE that uses a large language model to evaluate student explanations of programming worked examples and provides immediate feedback. Experiments show the LLM can reliably judge correctness and completeness, and deploying it in an introductory Java course improves student persistence, explanation depth, and learning outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- The LLM’s judgments align with both expert and crowd‑based reference standards, revealing systematic strengths and predictable errors that help design better feedback.  
- Students who receive the tutor’s feedback are more likely to revise a line of code rather than abandon it, indicating the system supports active revision cycles.  
- Across attempts, students’ explanations become richer in both detail and conceptual understanding, showing measurable learning progress.

## Context
Automated assessment of free‑text student work remains a bottleneck for scalable tutoring systems, especially as large language models become capable of reasoning about technical content. This work demonstrates that an LLM can serve as a functional tutor engine without requiring costly human oversight.

## Implications
For educators, the model offers a low‑cost way to provide instant, consistent feedback on student explanations. For industry, it suggests that LLMs can be integrated into learning platforms to enhance active learning experiences and reduce reliance on manual grading.
