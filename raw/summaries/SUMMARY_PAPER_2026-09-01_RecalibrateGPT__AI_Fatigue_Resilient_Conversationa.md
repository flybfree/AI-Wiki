## Summary
The paper introduces RecalibrateGPT, a system that adds five cross‑turn operators to reduce conversational AI fatigue by recalibrating LLM responses using full conversation history. Pilot studies with advanced users show reduced perceived cognitive workload and high usability scores. The approach targets retyping loops, scanning overload, decision paralysis, and context drift.

## Key Takeaways
- RecalibrateGPT introduces five operators (Anchor, Replay, Delta, Scope, Steer) that each address a specific fatigue type through a single‑click recalibration of the full conversation history. - The system reduces perceived cognitive workload by half according to NASA‑TLX scores from 2.7 at high SUS ratings, indicating significant improvement in user experience. - Pilot studies with twelve advanced LLM users demonstrate that the operators effectively mitigate retyping loops, scanning overload, decision paralysis, and context drift.

## Context
Conversational AI interfaces often suffer from cognitive load and task abandonment due to repetitive prompting patterns. Existing solutions focus on model quality or prompt engineering but rarely address flow‑level fatigue. RecalibrateGPT shifts attention to interaction design, treating fatigue as a systemic issue that can be mitigated by structured operators.

## Implications
This work shows that interface innovations can substantially lower user mental effort without altering underlying AI performance. For industry practitioners, adopting cross‑turn recalibration mechanisms could improve adoption rates of LLM tools and reduce support costs. The findings suggest a new design paradigm where fatigue is proactively managed through UI elements.
