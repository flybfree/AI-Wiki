## Summary
The paper introduces an online learning algorithm for controlling airflow in a multi-fan vertical wind tunnel to generate tailored profiles such as uniform, Gaussian, and parabolic. It achieves sample-efficient convergence and produces a passive soaring profile that improves the flight performance of a soaring robot.

## Key Takeaways
- The method uses a simplified physical model combined with iterative measurement-based learning to converge quickly to desired airflow distributions.
- It can produce complex profiles like uniform, Gaussian, and parabolic airflow, demonstrating versatility across different shapes.
- The algorithm generates a specific passive soaring profile that enhances the performance of a soaring robot.

## Context
This work advances AI-driven control in physical testbeds by integrating online learning with real-time sensor feedback. It illustrates how machine learning can replace trial-and-error tuning for aerodynamic experiments, reducing time and resource consumption.

## Implications
Practitioners can apply this approach to other wind tunnel configurations, enabling rapid prototyping of airflow conditions for UAV testing. The technique supports scalable fan setups, offering a robust framework for future autonomous flight optimization.
