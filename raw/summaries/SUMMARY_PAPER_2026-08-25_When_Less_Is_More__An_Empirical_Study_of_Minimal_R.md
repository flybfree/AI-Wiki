## Summary
The paper investigates minimal responses in counseling dialogues and compares their presence in human datasets versus large language model outputs. It finds that while humans frequently use brief, empathic utterances, these are scarce in LLM‑generated dialogues. It also notes that commercial LLMs can produce minimal answers when instructed, yet lack the ability to judge appropriateness of such brevity.

## Key Takeaways
- Human counseling data contain many minimal responses that convey attentive listening, but these are scarce in LLM‑generated dialogues.
- Commercial LLMs can produce minimal answers when instructed, yet lack the ability to judge appropriateness of such brevity.
- Synthetic‑data trained models tend toward verbose replies and underperform on minimal response tasks.

## Context
Understanding how brief utterances function in therapeutic settings is crucial for designing AI that respects human interaction norms. This study highlights a gap between theoretical empathy modeling and practical LLM behavior.

## Implications
Practitioners must evaluate AI counseling tools not only by content richness but also by their capacity to generate concise, supportive replies. Companies should prioritize training data that includes minimal utterances to improve alignment with therapeutic communication.
