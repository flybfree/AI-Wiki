## Summary
This paper presents a domain‑centric survey of face de‑identification that covers the entire data acquisition pipeline, from physical concealment to digital processing. It systematically examines current methodologies across three stages—physical, sensor, and digital—and reviews existing evaluation protocols while highlighting open problems for future research.

## Key Takeaways
- Early approaches operate in the digital domain by modifying pixel‑level or appearance‑level features after capture.
- Recent advances integrate privacy mechanisms directly into sensors during image acquisition, linking sensing systems to downstream vision algorithms.
- Physical‑domain methods explore wearable accessories and materials that conceal identity information before any image is taken.

## Context
Face de‑identification sits at the intersection of computer vision and privacy‑preserving AI, driven by growing regulatory demands for data protection. By mapping techniques onto each stage of the acquisition chain, this work clarifies how advances in one domain can enable progress across others, enriching the broader AI ecosystem focused on responsible model deployment.

## Implications
For researchers, standardized benchmarks are essential to compare cross‑domain solutions and ensure reproducibility. Practitioners should consider hybrid approaches that combine physical concealment with digital processing to meet privacy standards while preserving task utility in real‑world applications.
