## Summary
The paper introduces a framework of open, executable AI modules for power systems education, mapping core AI concepts onto practical tasks and lowering entry barriers. The framework consists of open Jupyter notebooks that run locally or on Google Colab and are delivered through an IEEE online course and PES webinar series.

## Key Takeaways
- 92% of surveyed researchers cite at least one barrier before running an AI model, highlighting a widespread need for accessible tools.  
- 94% express a desire for a power‑specific hands‑on course, indicating strong demand for practical learning experiences.  
- The webinar attracted over 590 live attendees and more than 344 repository visits within two weeks, demonstrating high engagement with the released modules.

## Context
Current AI research in power systems often focuses on isolated applications without providing reusable educational resources. This gap limits adoption among interdisciplinary learners who rely on large language models instead of building their own solutions.

## Implications
By delivering a progressive ladder of modules, the framework enables practitioners to apply AI techniques within established engineering rules, fostering confidence and competence in power‑system contexts. The success of the webinar suggests that such educational initiatives can scale quickly, encouraging broader integration of AI into industry practice.
