## Summary
The paper investigates how blind and low‑vision scientists use interactive question‑answering tools like ChatGPT and Gemini to explore multimodal scientific papers, comparing their practices with those of sighted colleagues. It finds that both groups rely on AI for visual exploration but abandon it when image descriptions are vague or AI answers are wrong.

## Key Takeaways
- Blind and low‑vision scientists often depend on static alt text while using AI tools to generate dynamic explanations of figures, which can improve engagement if the AI accurately interprets images.  
- Vague or incomplete image captions cause both groups to stop using AI, highlighting a critical need for precise visual descriptions in multimodal papers.  
- Incorrect AI outputs are equally disruptive for sighted researchers, suggesting that reliability is essential across abilities.

## Context
Artificial intelligence now enables scientists to query documents that combine text and images, moving beyond static accessibility solutions. This study adds empirical insight into real‑world usage patterns and highlights gaps in current multimodal QA systems.

## Implications
Future AI‑powered scientific QA must prioritize accurate visual interpretation and robust error handling to serve diverse abilities. Addressing these issues can broaden access to cutting‑edge research and improve collaboration across the STEM community.
