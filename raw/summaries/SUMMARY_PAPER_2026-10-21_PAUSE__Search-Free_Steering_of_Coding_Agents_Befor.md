## Summary
PAUSE is a framework that steers coding agents with a single, search-free planning intervention applied just before the agent's first source-code edit. By intercepting the edit request and injecting a repair plan generated by a separate Planner model, PAUSE improves task resolution rates on SWE-bench benchmarks by up to 16.8% without requiring learned monitors, checkpoint searches, or additional model calls beyond the single planning step.

## Key Takeaways
- PAUSE exploits the observation that coding agents reach their first source-code edit at a median of 60% of trajectory progress, after they have already performed exploration, bug reproduction, and testing. This timing provides a natural, high-information checkpoint where the agent's accumulated understanding of the problem can be leveraged by a Planner to produce a targeted repair plan, eliminating the need for periodic reviews or anomaly-detection monitors that existing online steering methods rely on.
- The framework is explicitly search-free: it requires no learned monitor to detect behavioral problems, no search over trajectory checkpoints, and no extra model call beyond the single Planner intervention. The Executor agent resumes with its full context and workspace intact, making the approach both operationally simple and cost-efficient. In SWE-agent, PAUSE outperforms LivePlan (a rule-triggered advisor) by 3.2% on Verified and 3.7% on Multilingual at similar Planner cost, and matches SICA's periodic overseer at roughly 11–15% of its Planner cost.
- Offline application of PAUSE recovers 35.4% of eligible failed Verified tasks, and resuming from a recorded trajectory prefix uses 33–41% fewer Executor tokens than restarting the agent from scratch, demonstrating that the pre-edit transcript contains substantial reusable signal that a single well-timed intervention can redirect toward a successful patch.

## Context
This paper addresses a persistent failure mode in autonomous coding agents: agents can invest heavily in repository exploration, bug reproduction, and testing yet still produce unsuccessful patches. Prior steering approaches either periodically review trajectories at high computational cost or invoke advisors only when a monitor flags anomalous behavior, which misses cases where the agent is confidently heading in the wrong direction. PAUSE reframes steering as a single, well-timed intervention at the moment of maximum accumulated understanding, bridging the gap between costly continuous oversight and purely reactive anomaly detection.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying coding agents in production software engineering pipelines, PAUSE offers a low-overhead steering mechanism that can be applied both online during live runs and offline to salvage failed trajectories, reducing token costs by a third or more while improving resolution rates. For the broader AI research community, the finding that a single planning intervention at a natural trajectory checkpoint can rival or exceed periodic overseers at a fraction of the cost challenges the assumption that continuous monitoring is necessary for agent reliability, and points toward more efficient, targeted intervention strategies for autonomous agents across domains.
