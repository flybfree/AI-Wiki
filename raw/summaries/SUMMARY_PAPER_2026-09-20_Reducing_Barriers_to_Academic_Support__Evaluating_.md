## Summary
This paper evaluates "Beacon," a course-specific Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system designed to mitigate disparities in student help-seeking by providing private, contextually grounded academic support. The study demonstrates that such systems can serve as an effective intermediary between independent study and human instruction, helping students overcome psychological barriers like anxiety or lack of confidence when seeking academic assistance.

## Key Takeaways
- Students frequently face significant hurdles when seeking help from instructors, including fear of judgment, anxiety about their own level of understanding, and uncertainty regarding expectations; these issues are particularly acute in complex, cumulative subjects like computer science.
- While students increasingly turn to general-purpose generative AI tools for assistance, these models often produce inaccurate or contextually irrelevant responses that do not align with specific module requirements or pedagogical goals.
- The "Beacon" system was found to be more trustworthy than unrestricted AI because it grounded its responses in approved teaching materials and provided scaffolded explanations rather than direct answers; this approach allows students to feel more confident in their learning process while maintaining academic integrity.

## Context
This research addresses a critical shift in the field of educational technology from general-purpose AI toward domain-specific, grounded systems that prioritize accuracy and pedagogical alignment. It contributes to the ongoing discussion about how large language models can be safely integrated into higher education to support diverse learner needs without compromising the quality of instruction.

## Implications
For educators and institutions, these findings suggest that RAG systems should be viewed as tools to broaden access to academic guidance rather than replacements for human instructors. By providing a "safe" first point of contact for students, these technologies can help bridge the gap between independent study and formal consultation, ultimately fostering a more inclusive and accessible learning environment.
