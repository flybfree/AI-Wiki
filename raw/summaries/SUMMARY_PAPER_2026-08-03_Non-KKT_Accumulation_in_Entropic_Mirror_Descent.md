## Summary  
The paper investigates whether accumulation points of bounded mirror descent sequences generated by a Legendre kernel must be Karush‑Kuhn‑Tucker (KKT) stationary, and it shows the answer is negative. It constructs smooth objectives on nonnegative orthants and probability simplices where the sequence converges to boundary circles containing arcs of non‑KKT points. The steps are α_k ~ k^{-β} with β in (1/2,1), objective values nonincreasing, and entropy‑relatively smooth.

## Key Takeaways  
- A bounded mirror descent sequence can converge to a set that includes points not satisfying KKT conditions even though the algorithm respects proper stepsizes.  
- The pathology originates from the vanishing inverse entropy metric at the boundary, which destroys dual feasibility in the KKT system rather than from step‑size issues or failure of descent.  
- These examples are the first known counterexamples for Bregman‑geometry based mirror descent with nonincreasing objective values.

## Context  
Mirror descent is a theoretical tool used to analyze and implement gradient‑based methods, especially in machine learning where entropy regularization appears. Understanding its behavior at boundaries is crucial because many optimization problems involve constraints that create sharp feasible sets.

## Implications  
The findings caution practitioners against assuming KKT accumulation guarantees convergence to optimal solutions when boundary effects dominate. They highlight the need for careful analysis of Bregman geometry and may influence algorithm design in constrained learning tasks.
