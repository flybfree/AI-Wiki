## Summary
The paper introduces ContractScrub, a benchmark for evaluating the performance of large language models in reviewing legal contracts. It demonstrates that frontier models struggle with routine contract scrubbing tasks despite excelling on broader benchmarks, achieving only modest recall rates.

## Key Takeaways
- Frontier LLMs perform poorly on contract scrubbing, reaching at most 0.75 macro average recall even though they excel on related general tasks.  
- The benchmark highlights that current models lack the necessary long‑context reasoning and consistency checking for legal text.  
- ContractScrub shows that domain‑specific evaluation is essential to reveal real‑world limitations of AI in legal work.

## Context
Legal contract review is a high‑volume, detail‑intensive task that aligns with frontier LLM capabilities such as long‑document understanding and named entity recognition. This study addresses the gap between theoretical performance on generic benchmarks and practical impact in specialized domains like law.

## Implications
ContractScrub underscores the need for tailored evaluation frameworks before deploying AI in legal practice, ensuring models meet domain‑specific reliability standards. Practitioners can use these insights to prioritize model improvements that address real contract errors rather than relying solely on general performance metrics.
