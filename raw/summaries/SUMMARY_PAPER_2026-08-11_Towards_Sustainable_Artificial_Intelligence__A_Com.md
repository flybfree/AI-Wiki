## Summary
The paper reviews Green AI and Green DL research, compares carbon measurement tools, and runs an experiment with six DL models on a multi‑label classification task to quantify emissions. It finds training dominates the footprint and that model complexity does not guarantee proportional accuracy gains. These findings highlight the urgency of aligning model efficiency with ecological responsibility.

## Key Takeaways
- The training phase accounts for the majority of carbon emissions in the evaluated models.
- Increased architectural complexity does not consistently lead to linear improvements in prediction performance, indicating a non‑linear relationship between size and accuracy.
- Carbon measurement tools vary widely, making standardized evaluation challenging across studies.

## Context
Artificial intelligence systems increasingly demand massive computational resources, raising concerns about their environmental impact. As organizations deploy larger models for real‑world applications, understanding the carbon cost becomes essential for sustainable development. The growing adoption of AI across sectors amplifies the need for transparent carbon accounting.

## Implications
Practitioners must prioritize energy‑efficient model selection and design to reduce emissions without sacrificing performance. Integrating sustainability metrics into AI workflows can guide research and industry toward greener solutions.
