## Summary
The paper introduces Rule of Thumb (RoT), a method for explainable artificial intelligence that selects the most relevant features to predict an AI system’s behavior for a single datapoint without requiring full model access. RoT demonstrates applicability in zero‑shot classification with large language models, auditing opaque systems, and scientific discovery tasks. It satisfies key regulatory requirements, offers rapid visualisations, and is model‑agnostic.

## Key Takeaways
- RoT identifies the most relevant features for predicting AI decision on a single datapoint, enabling explanations without full model access.
- The approach works in zero‑shot classification with large language models, auditing opaque systems, and scientific discovery tasks.
- RoT meets leading AI regulation requirements, provides familiar visualisations, is faster than alternatives, and is model‑agnostic.

## Context
Explainable AI remains a challenge as many modern systems use black‑box deep learning. Regulators demand transparency, yet providing full model explanations can be costly. This work offers a lightweight alternative that focuses on feature relevance rather than detailed model internals.

## Implications
For practitioners, RoT simplifies XAI workflows and reduces computational overhead. For industry, it supports compliance with AI regulations while maintaining speed. The method could become standard in auditing and scientific applications where full model access is unavailable.
