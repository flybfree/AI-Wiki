## Summary
This paper investigates whether foundation models (FMs) can be treated as explanatory cognitive and developmental models by outlining a four‑stage inferential framework. It argues that merely observing behavioral alignment is insufficient; meaningful alignment requires explicit theoretical commitments, diagnostic tasks, and systematic comparisons across candidate models.

## Key Takeaways
- Behavioral fit alone does not justify treating FMs as cognitive models because it lacks grounding in theory or comparison with alternative explanations.
- The four‑stage framework stresses the need to adapt human tasks to model formats, specify linking hypotheses that map model outputs to behavioral measures, and evaluate correspondence systematically.
- Alignment becomes scientifically meaningful only when embedded within explicit theoretical commitments, theory‑diagnostic tasks, and comparative analysis across models.

## Context
Foundation models are increasingly used as proxies for complex cognitive processes, but their role remains speculative. This work highlights the methodological gaps in treating these AI systems as candidates for cognitive explanation without rigorous validation.

## Implications
For researchers, the paper calls for a theory‑driven approach to evaluating FMs, ensuring that any alignment claims are diagnostic rather than coincidental. Practitioners should adopt systematic contrastive evaluation to distinguish genuine cognitive insights from superficial performance matches.
