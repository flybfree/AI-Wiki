## Summary
This paper introduces a behavioural measurement framework that links consumer intent with delegated decision authority to quantify how much financial decision‑making power users grant to AI assistants like ChatGPT and Gemini. Applied to 1.5 million real‑world interactions across the United States and India, it reveals that while AI is widely used for information retrieval and shaping judgment in finance, actual delegation of execution remains uncommon.

## Key Takeaways
- Consumers primarily rely on AI to fetch financial data and influence their thinking rather than to act directly on decisions.  
- The study documents a clear gap between informational use and the rare instances where users explicitly hand over decision authority.  
- The framework provides a baseline metric for tracking how human‑AI authority is distributed as AI becomes more agentic.

## Context
The rapid integration of conversational AI into everyday life has sparked research on how humans allocate control to machines, especially in high‑stakes domains such as finance. This work contributes to that conversation by grounding the discussion in large‑scale empirical data from popular chat platforms.

## Implications
Understanding this authority gap helps designers create systems where users feel comfortable delegating execution without losing oversight. For industry stakeholders, it signals a need for transparent mechanisms that clarify when and how AI can act autonomously in financial services.
