## Summary
The paper investigates whether neural models trained exclusively on modern morphological data can recover cross‑lingual lexical structures that align with historical reconstructions of Proto‑Bantu. Using BantuMorph v7 and encoder embeddings for noun and verb lemmas across 14 Eastern and Southern Bantu languages, the authors identify many cognate candidates that match established reconstructed forms.

## Key Takeaways
- Ten of the top eleven noun candidates (90.9%) align with previously reconstructed Proto‑Bantu forms such as *-ntU 'person' in eight languages.
- Twelve verb cognates correspond to reconstructed Proto‑Bantu roots like *-bon- 'see' and *-jIm- 'stand', attested across wide geographic ranges.
- Cross‑model validation using NLLB‑600M confirms these patterns are consistent with Guthrie zone classifications (p < 0.01).

## Context
This work shows that modern AI models can infer deep historical linguistic relationships, providing a computational bridge between contemporary language data and ancient reconstructions.

## Implications
For AI researchers, the study demonstrates that neural embeddings can serve as effective proxies for lost lexical structures. For language preservation efforts, these tools offer new pathways to reconstruct extinct forms from present‑day corpora.
