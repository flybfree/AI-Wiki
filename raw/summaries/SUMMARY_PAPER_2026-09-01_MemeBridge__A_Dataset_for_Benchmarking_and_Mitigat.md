## Summary
This paper introduces MemeBridge, a dataset that captures how Chinese and U.S. participants interpret U.S.-originated memes from both expression and perception perspectives. The study finds that while large language models can improve with culturally grounded data, they often misinterpret nuanced meanings, highlighting the difficulty of bridging cultural gaps in AI.

## Key Takeaways
- The dataset documents two complementary viewpoints: Chinese interpretations of U.S. memes and U.S. expectations about how those memes might be misunderstood by others, revealing asymmetries in cultural understanding.
- Human agreement checks and GPT‑based classification verification ensure high annotation quality, providing rich supervision for downstream tasks such as sentiment analysis and emotion detection.
- Fine‑tuning models on MemeBridge yields better cross‑cultural performance than training with generic meme data, demonstrating the value of culturally specific resources.

## Context
The work addresses a growing need in AI research to evaluate language models across diverse cultural contexts rather than assuming universal comprehension. By focusing on memes—a highly context‑dependent medium—it contributes to broader efforts that aim for fair and inclusive AI systems.

## Implications
For developers, MemeBridge offers a practical benchmark to test and improve cross‑cultural model performance, informing product design in global markets. Practitioners can leverage the dataset to create more culturally aware AI tools, reducing misunderstandings and enhancing user trust worldwide.
