## Summary
The paper presents CAIRN, a machine‑learning nowcasting framework that links routine weather data to real‑time hydrogen sulphide readings at a European landfill, enabling early public‑health alerts. By training the model on measured emission timescales it predicts gas levels without engineered features and transfers across sites and co‑emitted methane.

## Key Takeaways
- CAIRN identifies wind‑borne transport of HS as a fast component and multi‑hour weather changes as a slow component, matching observed emissions dynamics.  
- The framework operates solely with routine meteorological variables and the calendar, producing tiered alerts that align with WHO odour guidance.  
- Validation across two monitoring stations and co‑emitted methane demonstrates robustness and transferability of the nowcasting approach.

## Context
This work advances AI applications in environmental health by demonstrating how causal inference can be built into a nowcasting pipeline without manual feature engineering, highlighting the synergy between meteorological data and emission monitoring. It underscores the potential for real‑time exposure prediction to complement traditional sensor networks in urban planning.

## Implications
Public‑health authorities gain a validated, graded trigger that can initiate interventions before community exposure escalates, reducing health impacts. Industry stakeholders benefit from a scalable AI tool that translates weather forecasts into actionable emission alerts, improving operational efficiency and regulatory compliance.
