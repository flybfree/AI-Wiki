## Summary
The paper investigates how generative AI floods the fiction market and dilutes revenue per book, using a dataset of self‑published Amazon books from 2023 to 2026. It finds that AI‑generated books account for many titles but capture less sales than those without AI, while still climbing in rankings despite lower profit.

## Key Takeaways
- Books with over 25% AI text make up a large share of the catalog yet generate fewer sales than those without AI, indicating market saturation rather than quality loss. - The number of selling books grew 19.2‑fold while quarterly revenue rose only 8.9‑fold, showing that scale outpaces profit and compressing revenue per book. - In high‑AI‑diffusion genres where Kindle Unlimited is common, titles without AI lose the most market share, especially in top‑rank positions.

## Context
Generative models can now produce full‑length narratives at minimal cost, raising questions about originality and copyright infringement. This study provides empirical evidence that AI‑generated content reshapes commercial dynamics beyond simple quality judgments. The findings highlight a shift from scarcity of unique works to abundance of derivative material.

## Implications
For authors and platforms, the paper suggests that reliance on AI may erode perceived value and competitive advantage in crowded markets. It also informs legal arguments about fair use, as widespread AI‑produced books could blur lines between transformation and copying. Practitioners should consider how AI content affects sales metrics and market positioning.
