## Summary
The paper introduces CORA, an agentic retrieval‑augmented large language model designed to test how source‑linked clinical assistance influences physician decision making. In a study of 46 physicians, CORA raised accuracy from 70.8% unaided to 82.6%, while also revealing that citations can both boost correct advice uptake and reduce resistance to wrong answers.

## Key Takeaways
- Source‑linked citations increased the proportion of physicians who adopted correct LLM advice from 34% to 76.9%, showing a strong positive impact when evidence is presented.
- When an incorrect answer was citation‑supported, physician resistance dropped dramatically from 92% to 34.8%, indicating that source linkage can mask errors and reduce skepticism.
- Despite the safety risk, CORA maintained benchmark performance and delivered larger gains on cases published after its training cutoff, highlighting both benefit and limitation.

## Context
Retrieval‑augmented LLMs aim to ground AI responses in real medical literature, a trend that promises more reliable clinical support. This study adds empirical evidence about how clinicians interpret such sources, revealing a gap between technical accuracy and human behavior.

## Implications
Clinicians may become overly dependent on citation‑backed LLM outputs, potentially overlooking genuine errors if the source is trusted. Practitioners should be cautious in deploying these tools without safeguards to prevent false reliance and maintain clinical judgment integrity.
