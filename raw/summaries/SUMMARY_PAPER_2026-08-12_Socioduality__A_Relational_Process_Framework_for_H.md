## Summary
The paper introduces socioduality, a relational process framework that captures how human and AI contributions are shaped by the conditions created by each other’s responses. It defines nested units such as moves and confirmed episodes, showing that response contingency and return contingency are essential for sociodual episodes. Empirical testing on three natural human-AI records demonstrates that move reconstruction aligns with actual interaction in two cases, differing only in a single multimodal decision.

## Key Takeaways
- Socioduality requires both response contingency and return contingency to define a confirmed episode, highlighting the reciprocal nature of contributions.
- The framework classifies episodes as sociodual, non‑sociodual, or indeterminate before coding response orientation and substantive re‑formation, providing a structured coding scheme.
- Empirical results show that move reconstruction converges with actual interaction in two cases, with disagreement concentrated at return‑contingency boundaries.

## Context
Human‑AI research often focuses on performance metrics rather than the relational dynamics of dialogue. This paper’s emphasis on history‑conditioned formation and pathway information addresses a gap by offering a bounded construct that preserves interaction structure beyond endpoint evaluation.

## Implications
Socioduality can guide researchers in designing AI systems that respect the evolving conditions of human input, leading to more adaptive interfaces. Practitioners may use its coding protocol to evaluate real‑world interactions without discarding valuable intermediate data.
