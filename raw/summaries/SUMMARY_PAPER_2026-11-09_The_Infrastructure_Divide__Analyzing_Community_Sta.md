## Summary
This paper introduces an LLM-powered opinion-mining framework that systematically analyzes community sentiment toward data center development by mining 798 Reddit posts and over 102,000 comments spanning a decade. The study finds that opposition dominates discourse volume rather than outright disapproval, with anti-data-center posts outnumbering pro posts by 2.6:1 and generating 8.5 times more comments, while opposition arguments cluster into four reproducible themes centered on resource consumption, contested siting processes, political opposition, and corporate accountability.

## Key Takeaways
- The researchers developed a Chain-of-Stance prompting framework using an open-source large language model guided by three domain-specific labeling rules, achieving 83% classification accuracy relative to the human inter-reviewer agreement ceiling, demonstrating that LLMs can reliably extract community stance at scale from unstructured social media data.
- Opposition discourse is quantitatively dominant: against posts outnumbered favor posts 2.6:1 and generated 8.5 times more comments, and this engagement gap widened significantly over 86 weeks, indicating that public resistance intensifies over time rather than stabilizing, with an estimated $64 billion in U.S. data center projects blocked or delayed between May 2024 and March 2025.
- Topic modeling resolved opposition arguments into four reproducible clusters: resource consumption and scarcity (42.8%), contested siting processes and secrecy (34.2%), political opposition to data centers (13%), and political and corporate accountability (8%), revealing that the majority of community resistance stems from tangible resource and transparency concerns rather than abstract ideological opposition.
- Engagement analysis showed that posts with emotional or provocative political framing attracted the most engagement, while key technical concerns such as water and power usage were dispersed across lower-engagement comment threads, suggesting a mismatch between what communities discuss publicly and what they actually care about substantively.

## Context
The rapid expansion of AI-driven data center construction has created a significant gap between infrastructure development timelines and meaningful public engagement, as traditional methods like surveys and public hearings are introduced late in the planning process and fail to capture community sentiment at scale. This paper addresses a critical methodological gap by being the first study to systematically mine social media data to measure community stance on data center development, leveraging LLM-based classification to transform unstructured public discourse into quantifiable, continuously updatable evidence that can inform planning decisions before opposition hardens into costly delays or legal challenges.

## Implications
For local leaders, developers, and policy makers, this framework provides a quantified and continuously updatable basis for prioritizing community concerns and identifying effective policy levers before projects reach the point of costly opposition, enabling a shift from reactive conflict management to proactive, negotiated partnerships. For the AI infrastructure industry, the findings underscore that addressing transparency in siting processes and resource consumption concerns—rather than relying on late-stage public hearings—could reduce the billions of dollars in project delays currently attributable to community resistance, while the methodology itself offers a replicable template for measuring public sentiment on other large-scale infrastructure projects.
