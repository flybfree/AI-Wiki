## Summary
The paper investigates the design of proactive thought partners for writing, AI agents that anticipate writers' needs and offer customizable support at appropriate moments. In a one‑week study with 16 participants, users created partners by configuring roles and proactivity levels, and the system delivered suggestions during ideation and revision phases. Participants found the assistance valuable when it was lightweight and framed non‑directively.

## Key Takeaways
- Users configured proactive support through prospective planning, shaping how agents intervene based on their writing goals.
- Suggestions were used for both idea generation and self‑monitoring, indicating dual utility of the system.
- Participants preferred visual representations alongside non‑directive rhetorical framing to keep interventions non‑intrusive.

## Context
Current AI writing tools often provide generic autocomplete or post‑hoc feedback, which does not adapt to individual cognitive workflows. Proactive assistance promises a more seamless integration that aligns with when and how writers need help, reflecting broader trends toward personalized and context‑aware agents.

## Implications
Designing such assistants requires careful attention to customization depth, timing of interventions, user engagement, and representation style. Practitioners can leverage these insights to build tools that feel supportive rather than intrusive, advancing the field of human‑AI collaboration in creative tasks.
