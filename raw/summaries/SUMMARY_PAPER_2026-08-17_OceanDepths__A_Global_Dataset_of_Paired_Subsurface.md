## Summary
The paper OceanDepths presents a global, high‑resolution dataset that pairs satellite sea surface temperature, salinity and height observations with co‑located subsurface temperature and salinity profiles from EN4, using GLORYS12 reanalysis for validation. The dataset covers 2000 to 2024 at 0.1°×0.1° resolution and weekly time steps, providing over nine million paired depth records across 50 standardized levels.

## Key Takeaways
- OceanDepths is the first open AI‑ready dataset that couples surface satellite products with in‑situ subsurface profiles on a global scale.
- It offers extreme sparsity of subsurface observations, roughly one observation per depth level per grid cell, challenging existing AI training regimes.
- The data are regridded to 0.1°×0.1° and available at weekly temporal resolution for the entire globe.

## Context
In oceanic AI research, reliable joint surface‑subsurface data are scarce because most resources rely on model outputs or single variables. This dataset bridges that gap by delivering real measurements in a format suitable for machine learning pipelines.

## Implications
The availability of OceanDepths will accelerate development of observation‑based forecasts and enable new methods to infer subsurface states from sparse measurements, benefiting climate monitoring agencies and commercial ocean services.
