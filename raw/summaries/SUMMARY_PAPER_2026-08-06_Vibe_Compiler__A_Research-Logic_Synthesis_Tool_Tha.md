## Summary
The paper introduces Vibe Compiler, a research‑logic synthesis tool that transforms vague ideas into coherent logic without requiring elaborate prompts. It demonstrates that the system’s success depends on providing AI with a structured ontology rather than complex prompting strategies. The authors show that compilation failures trigger reflective questions to preserve human agency.

## Key Takeaways
- Compilation failures signal missing logical components, prompting researchers with reflective questions instead of auto‑filling gaps.
- The framework identifies four origin types based on cognitive function (Synthesis vs Analysis) and executing agent (human vs AI).
- Effective AI assistance relies on knowledge structure supplied to the AI rather than sophisticated prompt engineering.

## Context
Generative AI is increasingly used as a research assistant, yet it can undermine human epistemic agency by promoting uncritical acceptance of its outputs. This work addresses that risk by embedding metacognitive checks into the synthesis process. The proposed model aligns with broader efforts to design AI tools that augment rather than replace human reasoning.

## Implications
Researchers will benefit from a tool that encourages critical oversight, reducing reliance on prompt engineering and fostering agency. For industry, Vibe Compiler exemplifies how structured ontologies can guide AI collaboration, potentially improving productivity and quality in knowledge production.
