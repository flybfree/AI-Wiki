## Summary
This paper investigates how generative AI reshapes software engineering by producing abundant, low‑cost code, and it examines a 12‑week case where an expert used AI coding agents to build an accessibility remediation system. The study reveals that the real challenge is making this high‑velocity, agentic work inspectable, correctable, and maintainable, leading to a theory of governance conversion.

## Key Takeaways
- High‑velocity agentic implementation surfaces recurring structural failure classes that are only visible during the development process.
- Engineering judgment must convert these failures into durable governance mechanisms rather than imposing pre‑existing controls.
- The model explains how governance is discovered from failures, not derived from known obligations.

## Context
Generative AI is moving software engineering toward a paradigm where code generation is cheap and abundant, altering traditional assumptions about effort and quality. This shift forces researchers to reconsider how oversight and reliability are maintained when the primary bottleneck is not human labor but the lack of governance structures.

## Implications
For practitioners, the paper suggests building feedback loops that capture emergent failures as opportunities for governance rather than treating them as bugs. For research, it opens a testable framework linking AI‑mediated velocity to observable structural outcomes, guiding future studies on maintainable agentic development.
