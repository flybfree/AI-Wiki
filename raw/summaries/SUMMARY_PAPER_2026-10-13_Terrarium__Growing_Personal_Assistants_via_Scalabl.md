## Summary
Terrarium is a framework designed to overcome the scalability bottleneck in training general-purpose personal assistants by synthesizing executable "App Worlds" from real-world application information and generating training tasks with evaluator-side rubrics for outcome-based reinforcement learning. The authors construct a training suite of 275 App Worlds and 13,344 tasks, demonstrating that Qwen3 models trained with Terrarium outperform their base counterparts by an average of 18.34 percentage points on the τ²-bench and 10.94 percentage points on the AppWorld bench across 4B, 8B, and 14B parameter scales.

## Key Takeaways
- Terrarium addresses the core practical barrier to training assistants at scale: real-world applications restrict access and make state difficult to reset. By organizing public web information and in-application observations into structured app specifications, the framework constructs self-contained runtimes with callable operations and mutable, resettable state, enabling reproducible and scalable training environments without requiring direct access to production applications.
- The task synthesis mechanism grounds tasks in App World state and pairs them with evaluator-side outcome rubrics that assess success without prescribing reference trajectories. This design choice supports outcome-based reinforcement learning, allowing agents to discover their own strategies for task completion rather than being constrained to mimic a single prescribed solution path, which is critical for generalization across diverse application ecosystems.
- The empirical results demonstrate consistent gains across three model scales (4B, 8B, and 14B parameters), with an average improvement of 18.34 percentage points on τ²-bench and 10.94 percentage points on AppWorld bench. This scalability across model sizes suggests that the synthetic training data generated by Terrarium provides transferable learning signal rather than merely overfitting to a specific model architecture.

## Context
This work sits at the intersection of reinforcement learning for language agents and the growing demand for unified natural-language interfaces to application ecosystems. Prior approaches to training tool-using assistants have relied on hand-curated task suites or limited access to production APIs, both of which constrain training scale and diversity. Terrarium's approach of synthesizing executable app specifications from public information represents a novel pathway to generating large-scale, diverse training environments without requiring partnerships with application vendors, addressing a fundamental data bottleneck in the field of agentic AI.

## Implications
For practitioners building personal assistants or agentic systems, Terrarium offers a reproducible methodology for generating large-scale training data that can be adapted to new application domains without bespoke engineering effort, potentially reducing the cost and time required to train domain-specific assistants. For the broader industry, the framework suggests a path toward general-purpose assistants that can operate across hundreds of applications simultaneously, moving beyond single-domain copilots toward truly unified assistant ecosystems. The use of outcome-based rubrics rather than trajectory matching also signals a shift in evaluation philosophy that could influence how future agent benchmarks are designed.
