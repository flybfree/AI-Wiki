## Summary
The paper introduces the concept of governed execution for agentic workflows and demonstrates that a provenance‑based layer called Matrix can preserve institutional integrity without sacrificing correctness. While both governed and direct workflows often achieve identical outcomes, only the governed path reliably records authority, validates completion evidence, and limits recovery to dependent tasks.

## Key Takeaways
- Governed execution requires inspectable provenance for decisions, completions, and responses to change.  
- Matrix records authority‑fact dependencies and can invalidate work that violates these constraints.  
- A deterministically enforced completeness contract caused a role‑separated transfer challenge by over‑blocking synthetic packets.

## Context
Institutional AI systems must ensure that autonomous agents act under accountable authority, not merely produce correct results. This paper addresses the gap between functional correctness and verifiable governance in complex workflows.

## Implications
For practitioners, Matrix offers a tool to audit agentic decisions and maintain trust across distributed tasks. Its emphasis on provenance integrity could become a standard practice as AI systems scale into regulated environments.
