## Summary
The paper evaluates how language models infer city characteristics from incomplete prompts, revealing systematic assumptions about urban scale and development. It quantifies these inferences across ten checkpoints using 40 indicators and seven domains.

## Key Takeaways
- Larger developed area is favored because it signals a more complete representation of the city.
- Faster recent growth correlates with higher ratings as models associate rapid expansion with vitality.
- Less sparse form, i.e., greater non‑residential capacity, leads to better profiles because it reduces ambiguity.

## Context
This work addresses the hidden biases in generative AI that shape perceptions of place without explicit geographic data. It contributes to understanding how model training data implicitly encodes urban stereotypes.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that city descriptions generated by language models reflect underlying development metrics rather than neutral geography. This insight can guide more balanced prompt engineering and evaluation frameworks.
