## Summary
This paper introduces MultivationBench, a benchmark that evaluates multimodal large language models on sequential motivation reasoning within story-driven visual narratives. The study finds that all tested models fail to maintain consistent motivation across time steps, highlighting a gap between static perception and dynamic reasoning.  

## Key Takeaways
- All tested models struggle to preserve coherent motivation across multiple frames, indicating limited capacity for cumulative reasoning.  
- Existing benchmarks focus on isolated visual or textual snapshots rather than the ongoing narrative context required for motivation inference.  
- The benchmark demonstrates a significant disconnect between static recognition capabilities and the dynamic reasoning needed for human‑like social understanding.  

## Context
Multimodal large language models aim to emulate human social intelligence by integrating text, images, and video into a single output. However, most prior work evaluates these systems in static or single‑frame scenarios, ignoring how motivations evolve over time within sequential stories. This paper addresses that limitation by constructing a benchmark that mirrors the temporal dynamics of real‑world behavioral drivers.  

## Implications
The findings suggest that current multimodal AI systems lack the ability to sustain motivation reasoning, which is essential for applications like interactive storytelling and social robotics. Practitioners must prioritize research into dynamic context integration to bridge the gap between perception and sustained reasoning.
