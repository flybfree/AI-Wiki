## Summary
The paper proposes a consensus anomaly detection framework to analyze monthly malaria surveillance data from Ghana between 2014 and 2023. It identifies atypical transmission patterns that are highly structured in both space and time, revealing regional hotspots such as Tamale, Kumasi, and Accra. The study distinguishes between cumulative case burden during anomalous periods and the frequency of unusual behaviour.

## Key Takeaways
- Ashanti and Northern Regions account for most recurrent anomalies with persistent hotspots at Tamale, Kumasi, and Accra.
- High-burden areas are not necessarily those with the highest anomaly rates; Tamale shows the greatest cumulative cases during anomalies while Ashanti districts have the highest anomaly frequencies.
- Anomalous months form a statistically distinct group with much higher case counts (Cohen's d = 3.252) and large seasonal deviations (d > 1.2) compared to normal months.

## Context
This work advances AI applications in public health surveillance by demonstrating how unsupervised consensus methods can uncover hidden patterns in complex spatiotemporal data. The approach integrates machine learning consensus across multiple detectors, highlighting the value of ensemble models for robust anomaly detection in limited labeled datasets.

## Implications
Practitioners can use these insights to prioritize investigations in regions where transmission deviates most from expected norms rather than focusing solely on high case numbers. Targeted control strategies are more effective when aligned with anomalous behaviour patterns identified by this framework, improving resource allocation and surveillance efficiency.
