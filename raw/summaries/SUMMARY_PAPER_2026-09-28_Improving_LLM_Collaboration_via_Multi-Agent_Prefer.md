## Summary
This paper addresses the challenges of constructing reliable rewards in multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) for large language model collaboration by introducing Multi-Agent Preference Learning (MAPL). The authors formulate preference-based multi-agent systems from both decentralized and centralized perspectives, proposing a framework that iteratively improves solutions through comparative feedback. Experimental results across diverse tasks demonstrate that MAPL enhances collaboration quality and efficiency while approaching the performance of MARL with fixed rewards, though method sensitivity varies based on data coverage and model configurations.

## Key Takeaways
- The proposed Multi-Agent Preference Learning (MAPL) framework enables iterative refinement by comparing current solutions against decentralized or centralized alternatives generated by various agents, effectively bypassing the need for complete and accurate reward metrics that are often unavailable in practice.
- MAPL is instantiated using MARL from Human Feedback (MARLHF) with a learned reward model and Multi-Agent Direct Preference Optimization (MADPO), where experiments indicate that MARLHF generally outperforms MADPO across collaborative writing, coding, tool use, and travel planning tasks.
- While effective, the performance of preference-based approaches remains sensitive to critical factors including data coverage, the choice of agent and comparator models, and the underlying MARL algorithms, highlighting implementation dependencies for practitioners.

## Context
As large language models increasingly operate in collaborative settings, optimizing their joint behavior remains a significant challenge due to the complexity of defining global objectives and reward functions. While preference learning has revolutionized single-agent alignment through techniques like RLHF, its extension to multi-agent environments is less mature, leaving a gap in scalable methods for improving group dynamics
