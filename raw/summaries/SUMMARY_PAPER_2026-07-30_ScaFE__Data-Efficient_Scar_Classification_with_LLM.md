## Summary
The paper introduces ScaFE a method for classifying keloid versus hypertrophic scars using locally executable feature programs generated by an LLM instead of sending images to a remote VLM. On 600 photographs from three hospitals the approach achieves 81 percent site‑macro balanced accuracy, outperforming BiomedCLIP by ten points and maintaining strong performance with only ten percent of the data. Iterative refinement also raises the executable program rate from sixty six point seven percent to ninety five percent while providing verified evidence for ninety one point seven percent of features.

## Key Takeaways
- ScaFE transfers LLM knowledge into deterministic feature programs that run locally, keeping raw images and patient outputs private.
- The method reaches 81 percent site‑macro balanced accuracy on multi‑hospital data, a ten‑point gain over the best baseline.
- Iterative refinement improves program execution from sixty six point seven percent to ninety five percent with high verification rates.

## Context
Current scar classification relies heavily on deep image models that require large labeled datasets and cannot be deployed across institutions due to data governance constraints. This paper demonstrates an alternative where clinical knowledge is encoded as executable code, enabling reproducible and auditable assessments without external model calls.

## Implications
Clinics can implement ScaFE within their own servers, complying with privacy regulations while gaining performance comparable to state‑of‑the‑art models. The approach also offers a transparent pipeline for feature engineering that can be iteratively improved as more local data becomes available.
