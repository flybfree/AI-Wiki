## Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models can effectively comprehend the poetic logic of modern Chinese poetry, a domain that relies on holistic reasoning rather than simple semantics. The authors introduce Peony, a benchmark designed to evaluate four tasks at stanza, line, and imagery levels across six mainstream LLMs in both non‑thinking and thinking configurations. Their results show that current models struggle with this specialized reasoning, confirming the need for dedicated evaluation.

## Key Takeaways
- Poetic logic involves multi‑level reasoning (stanza, line, imagery) that goes beyond surface semantics.  
- Existing LLM evaluations ignore this holistic dimension, leading to misleading performance metrics.  
- Peony demonstrates that LLMs perform poorly on these tasks even in “thinking” modes, highlighting a gap in their literary comprehension.

## Context
Understanding literary texts is crucial for advancing natural language understanding beyond narrow NLP benchmarks. Modern Chinese poetry’s unique structure challenges models trained primarily on factual or conversational data, underscoring the need for domain‑specific reasoning frameworks within AI research.

## Implications
For researchers, this work calls for creating task‑aligned benchmarks that capture literary nuance to guide model development. Practitioners should consider these limitations when deploying LLMs in creative or interpretive applications where poetic insight matters.
