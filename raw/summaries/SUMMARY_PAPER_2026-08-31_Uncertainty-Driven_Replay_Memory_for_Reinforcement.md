## Summary
This paper introduces Uncertainty-Driven Replay Memory (UDRM), a new way to update an experience replay buffer in reinforcement learning based on uncertainty estimates generated by the agent’s model during training. The proposed scheme prioritizes storing transitions that are less certain, which is believed to improve generalization and reward performance compared with standard methods. Experiments show higher rewards for agents using UDRM than those using other uncertainty‑aware approaches.

## Key Takeaways
- Uncertainty estimates guide replay buffer updates, favoring the inclusion of uncertain state‑action pairs over confident ones.  
- The method reduces training time and leads to greater long‑term reward gains by encouraging exploration of unknown regions.  
- Experimental results demonstrate that UDRM outperforms existing uncertainty‑aware RL frameworks in both reward and sample efficiency.

## Context
Uncertainty estimation is a growing focus in reinforcement learning, aiming to balance exploration and exploitation more effectively than traditional temporal difference methods. By integrating model confidence into memory management, this work extends the replay buffer concept beyond error‑based updates, offering a principled way to shape the data that drives learning.

## Implications
For practitioners, UDRM provides a simple yet effective mechanism to improve sample efficiency without complex modifications to existing RL pipelines. In industry settings, such improvements can translate into faster model training and better performance on safety‑critical applications where exploration is crucial.
