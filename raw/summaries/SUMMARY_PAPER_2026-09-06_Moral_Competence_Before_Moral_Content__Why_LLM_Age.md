## Summary
The paper argues that AI alignment depends on a coherent moral policy rather than specific moral content. It shows that frontier LLM agents fail to meet this structural requirement across simulated deployments.

## Key Takeaways
- The four conditions (verdict stability, monotonicity, decisiveness, Pareto viability) define a form of moral competence measurable from behavior alone.
- LLM agents exhibit verdict-rate shifts up to 99% due to surface-form perturbations, indicating lack of coherence across scenarios.
- A model's success on one scenario does not predict its competence on another.

## Context
AI alignment research seeks systems that reliably follow human values. This work highlights a gap: current large language models cannot produce stable, consistent moral judgments because their outputs are sensitive to minor wording changes and lack systematic policy mapping.

## Implications
For practitioners, the paper suggests rethinking alignment strategies around structural competence rather than content. It warns that deploying LLM agents for moral decision making may be premature until they meet these behavioral criteria.
