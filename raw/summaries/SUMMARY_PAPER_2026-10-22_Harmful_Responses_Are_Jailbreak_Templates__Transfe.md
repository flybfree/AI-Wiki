## Summary
This paper identifies a novel jailbreak attack surface unique to diffusion large language models (dLLMs), where a harmful response to a query can itself serve as a jailbreak template when its tokens are masked and fed back into the model for bidirectional reconstruction. The authors propose RMT-Jailbreak, a framework that transfers existing autoregressive jailbreak attacks to dLLMs by converting AR-generated harmful outputs into high-mask-ratio templates, demonstrating strong cross-paradigm transferability of jailbreak vulnerabilities across fundamentally different model architectures.

## Key Takeaways
- The core insight is that harmful responses generated by autoregressive models can be repurposed as jailbreak templates for diffusion models: by masking a subset of tokens in a harmful response and providing it to a dLLM, the model's bidirectional reconstruction capability naturally regenerates the harmful content, effectively bypassing safety alignment without requiring adversarial prompt engineering.
- RMT-Jailbreak introduces a two-stage refinement process: first, uniform token retention converts an AR-generated harmful response into a high-mask-ratio template, and second, prompt-based safety filtering removes any residual visible harmful content from the template before it is presented to the target dLLM, ensuring the jailbreak operates through reconstruction rather than direct exposure.
- Extensive experiments spanning multiple autoregressive jailbreak methods and several target dLLMs confirm that the framework generalizes across architectures and attack strategies, revealing that jailbreak vulnerabilities are not confined to a single generation paradigm but transfer across autoregressive and diffusion-based models.

## Context
As diffusion language models emerge as a promising alternative to autoregressive transformers, their bidirectional masked generation mechanism introduces qualitatively different safety vulnerabilities that existing jailbreak research has not adequately addressed. This paper matters because it bridges two rapidly evolving research threads—jailbreak robustness in large language models and the architectural transition from autoregressive to diffusion-based generation—showing that safety evaluation frameworks built for one paradigm cannot be assumed to transfer to the other without explicit adaptation.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying dLLMs in production, this work signals that safety guardrails tuned against autoregressive jailbreaks may not suffice, and that new evaluation protocols must account for reconstruction-based attack vectors unique to masked generation. For the broader AI safety community, the demonstrated cross-paradigm transferability suggests that jailbreak robustness should be studied as a model-agnostic property rather than an architecture-specific one, potentially accelerating the development of unified safety benchmarks as the field moves toward diverse generation architectures.
