## Summary
The paper introduces a framework called Stereotypes-to-Decisions that measures regional bias in large language models across all 34 Chinese provinces using warmth and competence ratings plus paired-choice tasks in education, occupation, and social interaction. The study finds substantial regional differences in both model outputs and human stereotypes, which align with economic and digital development indicators.

## Key Takeaways
- Regional scores for competence and occupation decisions are consistently high across models, reflecting a systematic bias toward certain provinces.
- Warmth ratings vary widely by region, showing mixed human-like stereotypes where some areas are seen as friendly but not capable.
- These patterns remain stable when prompts are in Chinese or English, indicating the bias is embedded rather than prompt-dependent.

## Context
Assessing regional bias in AI systems is crucial because language models influence public perception and policy decisions. This work fills a gap by linking abstract stereotypes to concrete social outcomes across diverse administrative regions.

## Implications
Practitioners must design regionally aware evaluation protocols and consider mitigation strategies that account for these biases. Ignoring such disparities could lead to unfair algorithmic impacts in hiring, education, and social services.
