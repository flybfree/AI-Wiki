## Summary  
The paper investigates the design of proactive thought partners that provide customizable AI assistance during writing tasks. It demonstrates a technology probe where participants configure agents and observe how they intervene with suggestions over a week. The study finds that users benefit from timely, non‑intrusive support that supports both idea generation and self‑monitoring.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Participants created partners through prospective planning, allowing them to tailor roles and proactivity levels for each writing moment.  
- Suggestions were used for both generating new ideas and monitoring progress, showing dual utility of the AI’s input.  
- Users preferred lightweight visual cues combined with non‑directive framing, indicating a need for subtle, respectful interventions.  

## Context  
Current AI writing tools often rely on reactive autocomplete or post‑hoc feedback, which can disrupt flow. Proactive assistance promises to anticipate needs and act in the background, aligning with cognitive science insights about optimal timing of support. This work contributes to that promise by exploring user‑centered design principles within a real‑world setting.  

## Implications  
Designing proactive writing assistants requires careful balancing of customization, timing, engagement, and representation to avoid overwhelming users. For industry practitioners, these findings suggest moving beyond generic text suggestions toward agents that learn from prospective planning and deliver gentle, visual cues that enhance rather than hinder the writing process.
