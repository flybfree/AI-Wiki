## Summary
The paper presents FaceVid-Forensics-100K, a large dataset of 100,000 deepfake videos annotated with forensic explanations generated by multi-model MLLMs. It also introduces a multi-agent reasoning framework that uses four specialized agents to analyze forgery cues and a judge agent to combine their reports into a final prediction.

## Key Takeaways
- FaceVid-Forensics-100K spans 33 synthesis methods including recent generators like Seedance 2.0 and provides fine-grained textual annotations aligned with forensic verdicts.
- The multi-agent framework employs agents for texture, lighting, motion, and physics perspectives to capture subtle artifacts that single models miss.
- Experiments on out-of-domain test sets show the framework outperforms both open-source MLLMs and closed-source GPT and Gemini models across all metrics.

## Context
Deepfake detection remains a critical challenge as generative AI creates increasingly realistic forgeries. Existing benchmarks often lack up-to-date coverage and reliable annotations, limiting model generalization to new synthesis techniques. This work addresses those gaps by creating a comprehensive dataset and a novel reasoning approach.

## Implications
The framework demonstrates that modular, multi-agent systems can surpass large proprietary models in specialized tasks, offering a template for future AI safety tools. Practitioners can leverage such architectures to build robust forensic pipelines without relying on costly closed-source APIs.
