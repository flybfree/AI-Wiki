## Summary
The paper introduces the zbMATH Open Knowledge Graph, a large‑scale RDF knowledge graph that spans more than two and a half centuries of mathematical scholarship. It integrates expert‑curated semantic content such as reviews, keywords, subject classifications, software references, and disambiguated authorship into a machine‑readable format.

## Key Takeaways
- The KG merges domain‑specific semantic data—including reviews, keywords, subject classifications, software references, and disambiguated authorship—into RDF triples that go beyond mere bibliographic metadata.  
- It covers 250 years of mathematical research, comprising 34 million entities and 168 million RDF triples, enabling fine‑grained temporal analysis of scholarly concepts and relationships.  
- The paper demonstrates query‑driven, historically grounded exploration use cases that reveal patterns and connections invisible in traditional bibliographic or citation data alone.

## Context
This work advances AI research by providing a richly annotated semantic knowledge graph for mathematics, supporting tasks such as historical trend detection, concept linking, and interdisciplinary integration. By adhering to FAIR principles within a scholarly domain, the KG serves as a benchmark for temporal knowledge representation in artificial intelligence systems.

## Implications
For researchers, the KG enables automated discovery of cross‑century mathematical connections, improving literature mining and citation analysis. Industry stakeholders can leverage it for AI‑driven research curation, enhancing data interoperability across academic platforms and fostering new insights from centuries of scholarly output.
