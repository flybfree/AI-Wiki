## Summary
This paper introduces an explainable AI‑powered framework that automatically evaluates cataract surgery videos using ten motion‑based metrics derived from computer vision and signal processing. The system was tested on 83 recorded surgeries, showing strong agreement with expert assessments through the Capsulorhexis Skill Assessment System (CSAS), achieving up to 87 % accuracy in skill classification.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework processes video data to generate ten quantitative indicators that correlate closely with subjective surgeon ratings.  
- Expert surgeons using CSAS provided objective scores, which were compared to the AI‑derived metrics, revealing robust alignment across all ten measures.  
- The model’s explainability allows clinicians to understand and trust each metric, moving beyond opaque classification toward transparent skill assessment.

## Context
Automated surgical training is gaining traction as a means to address workforce shortages and standardize education. Traditional methods rely heavily on manual scoring, which can be inconsistent and time‑intensive. AI tools that combine computer vision with explainable outputs offer a scalable alternative for real‑time feedback in high‑stakes procedures.

## Implications
The results suggest that objective metrics can complement or even replace subjective evaluations in surgical training programs. By providing transparent performance indicators, the framework could streamline competency verification and support regulatory compliance across institutions worldwide.
