## Summary
The paper argues that inferential capability does not dictate the legal scope of EU digital law, presenting a two‑level framework where inference serves both constitutive and protective functions. It shows that absence of inference does not grant immunity, highlighting how agentic architectures intensify these effects.

## Key Takeaways
- Article 3(1) of the AI Act treats inference as a constitutive feature, while GDPR regulates it protectively based on personal data impact regardless of AI classification.  
- Protective function operates via identificatory, attributive and decisional pathways; composition is not a separate pathway but an architectural dimension with reach, persistence and reviewability.  
- The compositional‑effects test assigns the burden of proof to the controller and ties documentation duties to inference chains.

## Context
The EU AI Act and GDPR create overlapping regimes around inference, yet their definitions diverge, leaving legal interpretation ambiguous for developers building single‑shot versus agentic systems.

## Implications
Practitioners must design architectures that respect both constitutive and protective aspects of inference, ensuring compliance through clear documentation of inferential chains and adherence to the compositional‑effects test.
