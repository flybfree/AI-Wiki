## Summary
This study investigates whether passive exposure to external content within a large language model's context can systematically steer decision-making, even when such information offers no justification for altering the outcome. The authors demonstrate that introducing additional text significantly shifts model responses across various open-weight and closed-weight architectures, with decision changes reaching nearly 50 percentage points in some cases. These findings reveal that LLMs are highly susceptible to contextual noise, which can lead to violations of explicit user constraints and increased acceptance of false claims regardless of the content's relevance or accuracy.
