## Summary
The paper introduces BEAR‑Bench, a bilingual English‑Russian benchmark for reasoning over dense enterprise and academic texts, and evaluates 16 multimodal large language models on it. The study shows that even top models have room to improve and that hallucination detection methods can be evaluated against these failures.

## Key Takeaways
- BEAR‑Bench provides 1000 human‑annotated questions spanning English and Russian business and scientific documents, creating a self‑contained benchmark for MLLMs. - The evaluation includes both proprietary and open‑weight models such as Gemini 3.1 Pro and Qwen3.5‑397B, revealing clear headroom even for the strongest systems. - Results also demonstrate that existing hallucination detection methods can be assessed by measuring how often failures occur and their reliability on this benchmark.

## Context
Multimodal large language models excel at visual tasks but lack systematic assessment of reasoning over text‑dense professional content in multiple languages, especially Russian. This work fills a gap by creating a comprehensive bilingual benchmark that supports cross‑lingual evaluation and highlights the need for robust hallucination detection.

## Implications
For researchers, BEAR‑Bench offers a reusable resource to benchmark MLLMs across languages, guiding future model development. For industry practitioners, it signals that reliable reasoning in enterprise documents is still an open challenge, prompting investment in better detection tools and multilingual capabilities.
