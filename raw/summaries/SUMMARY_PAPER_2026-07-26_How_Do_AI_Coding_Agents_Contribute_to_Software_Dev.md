## Summary  
This paper investigates how AI coding agents influence software development by analyzing agentic pull requests alongside human‑generated ones using the AIDev dataset. It finds that merge rates and task distributions differ across development stages, revealing a dynamic role for AI agents in the lifecycle of code contributions. The study also compares key characteristics such as complexity and defect likelihood between the two types of PRs.

## Key Takeaways  
- Agentic pull requests show lower merge rates early in the cycle but increase over time, suggesting growing acceptance as developers adapt to their output.  
- AI agents are most frequently applied to routine coding tasks like boilerplate generation, while human contributions dominate complex refactoring work.  
- The complexity and defect scores of agentic PRs tend to be higher initially, improving gradually with more experience.

## Context  
The rise of large language models has accelerated the integration of AI into software engineering workflows, prompting a need for empirical insights into their real‑world impact. This research fills that gap by providing longitudinal data on how AI agents interact with human developers throughout project phases.

## Implications  
For practitioners, understanding these dynamics can inform policies on when to rely on AI assistance versus manual coding. For industry, the findings suggest opportunities to refine tooling and training programs to mitigate early‑stage quality issues associated with agentic contributions.
