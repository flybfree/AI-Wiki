## Summary
The paper introduces AICOME, an AI‑based framework that uses respondent‑level measures to recover both individual and group effects in contextual models. Validation on the China Family Panel Studies shows that AI‑derived variables can capture much of the survey information when rich job characteristics are available, especially for weekly hours where associations with satisfaction are strong.

## Key Takeaways
- AI contextual measurement can recover between‑ and within‑occupation associations that traditional surveys alone cannot explain.
- The method works best when multiple related concepts (e.g., computer use, foreign language use) are jointly modeled rather than treated as separate unobserved variables.
- Performance drops sharply if only occupation and basic demographics are considered, limiting the ability to recover contextual effects.

## Context
This work advances AI applications in social science by treating measurement reconstruction as a modeling problem rather than simple prediction. It demonstrates that rich respondent data can be leveraged to fill gaps left by conventional survey instruments, highlighting a potential synergy between machine learning and traditional observational research.

## Implications
Practitioners can use AICOME to extract actionable insights from existing datasets without redesigning surveys. The approach offers a cost‑effective way for organizations to assess job satisfaction and related outcomes while preserving the nuanced between‑group dynamics that matter in occupational studies.
