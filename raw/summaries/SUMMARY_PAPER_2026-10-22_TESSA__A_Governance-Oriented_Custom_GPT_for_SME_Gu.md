## Summary
This paper presents TESSA, a governance-oriented Custom GPT designed to help small and medium-sized enterprises navigate compliance requirements under the European Union AI Act. Using a design science research methodology, the authors evaluated TESSA against baseline ChatGPT across 100 benchmark scenarios, finding that TESSA achieved higher governance alignment rates (90% vs. 83% in preliminary assessments) and maintained greater consistency across repeated executions, suggesting that purpose-built governance grounding can enhance AI-assisted compliance guidance for SMEs.

## Key Takeaways
- TESSA was developed as an educational artifact within the OpenAI Custom GPT environment, specifically tailored to AI governance and regulatory compliance for SMEs. The design followed a design science research approach, incorporating a human-validated regulatory update mechanism to ensure the system stays current with evolving EU AI Act provisions, addressing a critical gap where generic LLMs may provide outdated or misaligned regulatory guidance.
- The evaluation employed a multidimensional framework combining LLM-assisted governance alignment assessment, independent human expert review by an AI regulation law practitioner, and consistency testing across repeated executions. While TESSA showed higher alignment scores (90% vs. 83%) in automated assessments, the independent legal practitioner validation yielded more modest differences (71% vs. 70%), highlighting the importance of multi-method evaluation and the gap between automated scoring and expert judgment in regulatory contexts.
- The study demonstrates that governance-oriented grounding in Custom GPTs can produce structured, practical, and stable compliance guidance, with TESSA maintaining high consistency across repeated executions. This consistency is critical for SMEs that need reliable, repeatable guidance rather than variable outputs, and the contribution includes a reusable evaluation framework applicable to other governance-oriented AI artifacts.

## Context
The EU AI Act represents one of the most comprehensive regulatory frameworks for artificial intelligence globally, yet its complexity poses significant compliance challenges for SMEs that lack dedicated legal or AI governance expertise. As generative AI adoption accelerates across European businesses, there is a growing need for accessible, affordable tools that can translate dense regulatory language into actionable guidance. This paper sits at the intersection of AI governance education, regulatory compliance tooling, and design science research, contributing to the emerging field of AI-assisted regulatory navigation.

## Implications
For SME practitioners and compliance officers, this research suggests that purpose-built, governance-grounded AI tools can offer more reliable and consistent regulatory guidance than general-purpose chatbots, potentially reducing compliance costs and legal risk for smaller enterprises. For the AI governance research community, the multidimensional evaluation framework and the human-validated regulatory update mechanism provide transferable methodologies for assessing domain-specific AI artifacts. However, the divergence between automated alignment scores and expert legal validation underscores that human oversight remains essential in regulatory AI applications, and practitioners should treat such tools as supplementary aids rather than authoritative compliance determinations.
