## Summary  
The paper presents a design science research project that creates an AI artifact capable of detecting toxic language and rewriting it into respectful paraphrases while preserving meaning. The system combines fine‑tuned transformer classifiers such as DistilBERT with a generative detoxification model mT0‑XL‑Detox‑ORPO, achieving high detection accuracy and strong semantic preservation in the rewritten messages.  

## Key Takeaways  
- The integration of both detection and generation components enables a complete workflow that not only flags harmful speech but also produces constructive alternatives.  
- Detection accuracy is reported as high while the generative model maintains semantic equivalence to the original text, ensuring conversation flow remains uninterrupted.  
- The approach emphasizes fairness by producing paraphrases that are non‑offensive and contextually appropriate.  

## Context  
In recent years, AI systems have been deployed for content moderation, yet most solutions focus solely on suppression rather than remediation. This gap leaves digital workplaces without tools that can both identify toxic communication and restore a respectful tone, highlighting the need for more nuanced interventions.  

## Implications  
For practitioners, this research offers design principles that balance accuracy with meaning preservation, encouraging the development of AI artifacts that support collaborative environments. The methodology could inform future systems aiming to foster inclusive discourse across platforms and organizations.
