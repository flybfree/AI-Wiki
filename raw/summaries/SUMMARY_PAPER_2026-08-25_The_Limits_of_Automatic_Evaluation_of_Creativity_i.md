## Summary
The paper investigates whether existing automatic evaluation methods can reliably capture human judgments of creativity in large language model generated text. It compares human evaluations across dimensions with automated metrics and LLM-as-a-Judge results, finding strong misalignment. The study concludes that current computational approaches fail to reflect the multidimensional nature of creativity.

## Key Takeaways
- Human judges consistently rate AI-generated stories higher than human-authored ones despite lower scores on unpredictability and other creative dimensions.
- Automated metrics show near-zero correlation with human judgments for both types of stories, indicating they miss key aspects of creativity.
- LLM-as-a-Judge evaluations systematically favor stylistic traits of AI output over the inherent randomness of human writing.

## Context
Creativity in text generation is a core challenge for AI systems that aim to produce novel and engaging content. Existing evaluation frameworks often rely on narrow, quantitative metrics that cannot capture subjective qualities like originality or surprise. This paper highlights a gap between automated assessment and human perception.

## Implications
Researchers must develop more nuanced evaluation protocols that incorporate qualitative judgments rather than relying solely on objective scores. Practitioners should be cautious about trusting automated creativity rankings when they do not align with human evaluations.
