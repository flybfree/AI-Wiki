## Summary
This paper introduces EuroExec, a benchmark of 413 open‑ended European executive tasks created by domain experts, to evaluate frontier language models. The results show that the best model solves only about 57 % of tasks, while expert‑written reference answers achieve near‑perfect performance and are preferred in most rankings.

## Key Takeaways
- The strongest model solves only 56.9 % of EuroExec tasks, indicating a substantial gap between model capability and professional standards.
- Expert‑written reference answers judged blindly are solved at near‑ceiling levels and are preferred over every model response in 74 % of direct rankings.
- Automatic measurements also fall short when evaluating this real‑world open‑ended problem with subjective ground truth.

## Context
Frontier LLMs are often assessed on standard benchmarks that do not reflect the complexity and subjectivity of real executive decision tasks. This study highlights a mismatch between typical evaluation practices and the demands of high‑stakes professional work, underscoring the need for more realistic assessment frameworks.

## Implications
Relying solely on automated metrics can mislead stakeholders about model performance in practical settings. Practitioners must incorporate human evaluators and rigorous statistical checks to obtain trustworthy insights into AI capabilities for complex executive decision making.
