## Summary
The paper investigates how the concentration of frontier AI models in a few states creates new geopolitical risks. It finds that access to these models can be revoked and that sovereignty is limited for most nations.

## Key Takeaways
- The United States imposed licences on advanced model releases, leading to worldwide withdrawal, showing that legal power translates into operational control.
- Frontier AI changes cyber attack economics, making autonomous AI‑run espionage feasible and prompting governments to treat model access as part of defence strategy.
- Sovereign capability is only realistic for a few states; most must rely on open‑weight models or regional pooling, which are both more capable and politically exposed.

## Context
The rapid evolution of large language models has shifted the balance of power in cyber operations. As AI becomes central to both offensive and defensive tools, traditional security frameworks struggle to keep pace.

## Implications
For practitioners, this means that managing model access is as critical as securing data. Companies must anticipate regulatory shifts and consider open‑weight alternatives while building resilience against AI‑driven attacks.
