## Summary  
The paper investigates whether newer generative models improve at pastiching contemporary artworks and examines the consistency of multidimensional stylistic evaluation across different large language models. It compares AI‑generated pastiches to twelve artists’ originals using five computer vision models that capture texture, color, semantics, composition, and perceptual features via cosine distances in embedding spaces.  

## Key Takeaways  
- The newer image generation model produces pastiches with improved semantic alignment and greater diversity compared with the previous model.  
- It is slightly less performant on shallow features such as color, texture, and perceptual adherence.  
- Quantitative results confirm that artistic style is inherently multidimensional and cannot be reduced to any single spatial architecture.  

## Context  
This work contributes to ongoing debates about the capabilities of generative AI in reproducing artistic styles. By quantifying stylistic similarity across multiple dimensions, it provides empirical evidence that challenges simplistic metrics and supports a more nuanced understanding of model performance.  

## Implications  
For researchers, the findings suggest that evaluating AI art should consider both deep semantic content and superficial visual cues. Practitioners may need to balance aesthetic fidelity with creative diversity when deploying generative models for artistic applications.
