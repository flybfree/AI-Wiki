## Summary
The paper argues that legitimacy of AI governance is a separate regulatory goal from alignment and that it depends on public perception of authority rather than performance. It identifies three failure points—opacity, private power, administrative automation—and proposes thin and thick legality as legal mechanisms to secure recognition.

## Key Takeaways
- Opacity blocks audiences from forming justified beliefs about AI decisions, leading to legitimacy crises even when systems perform well.
- Private power allows firms to set public-facing rules without clear authorization, creating a legitimacy gap between authority and public trust.
- Administrative automation undermines reason-giving, participation, and review processes within state institutions, eroding accountability.

## Context
AI already governs speech, attention, and applications, raising questions about who legitimately sets objectives. The paper contends that legitimacy is sociological, rooted in belief of rightful exercise of power, not merely technical alignment.

## Implications
Policymakers must embed AI rule‑setting in established public venues to avoid thin legality that only looks fair. Embracing thick legality with public authorship can restore genuine trust and mitigate political crises over autonomous systems.
