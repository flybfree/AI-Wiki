## Summary
This paper introduces a friction‑aware persona modeling approach that incorporates perceived transaction cost (PTC) to simulate tenant responses to energy‑policy interventions using large language models. By integrating survey data on practical frictions such as information burden and administrative effort, the authors demonstrate that PTC‑based personas improve LLM performance across prompt‑only and fine‑tuned settings.

## Key Takeaways
- The study shows that adding PTC‑derived persona details consistently boosts model accuracy when simulating tenant reactions to energy‑efficiency renovation proposals.  
- Both supervised fine‑tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) benefit from the inclusion of perceived transaction costs, indicating a robust bridge between theory and practice.  
- The approach leverages real survey data from 1,068 Dutch citizens to create nuanced personas that capture both demographic attributes and cost‑perception variables.

## Context
Large language models are increasingly used for policy simulation, yet most methods ignore the cognitive and social frictions that affect human behavior. This work addresses that gap by grounding persona design in empirical transaction‑cost metrics, offering a more realistic representation of decision‑making processes.

## Implications
For AI researchers, this framework provides a practical tool to align model outputs with real‑world policy impacts. Practitioners can use PTC personas to evaluate intervention effectiveness and reduce unintended consequences, making LLM simulations more trustworthy and actionable.
