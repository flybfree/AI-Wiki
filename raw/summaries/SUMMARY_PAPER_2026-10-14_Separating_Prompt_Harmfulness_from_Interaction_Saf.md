## Summary
This paper challenges the prevailing approach of LLM safety guardrails that classify prompts in isolation and reject them before any generation occurs. The authors demonstrate that prompt harmfulness and interaction safety are fundamentally distinct properties, showing that prompt-level labels and response-level labels agree on only 50.46% of items (Cohen's κ = 0.06). They propose a partial-generation approach where the system begins generating, monitors the partial output, and halts only if the response turns harmful, achieving strong classification performance (best AUPRC 0.885, F1 0.807) using only about one-third of response tokens.

## Key Takeaways
- Prompt harmfulness and interaction safety are not equivalent: across 41,944 prompt-response pairs generated by seven open-weight LLMs from three families (Llama, Gemma, and Qwen), prompt labels and response labels agree on only 50.46% of items with a Cohen's κ of 0.06, indicating near-random agreement. Furthermore, of prompts that cause any model to produce a harmful response, only 3.5% do so across all seven LLMs, meaning harmfulness is highly model-dependent rather than an intrinsic property of the query.
- Partial generation is sufficient for safety classification: fine-tuned classifiers trained on partial responses achieve strong performance (best AUPRC 0.885, F1 0.807), and approximately one-third of response tokens recover nearly the full-response AUPRC. This means safety decisions can be made early during generation rather than waiting for a complete response, enabling a "generate-and-monitor" paradigm instead of a "reject-before-generate" paradigm.
- The current prompt-only guardrail paradigm imposes significant costs on users and systems: by treating harm as a property of the query alone, systems reject many prompts that would have received safe responses, reducing the utility of conversational and generative search systems as these become increasingly common.

## Context
This work sits at the intersection of AI safety, LLM deployment, and information retrieval systems. As generative search and conversational AI systems scale, the tension between safety guardrails and user utility becomes a central engineering challenge. Existing safety frameworks largely operate at the input level (prompt classification) or the output level (full-response evaluation), but this paper identifies a critical gap: the assumption that a prompt's harmfulness predicts the safety of the model's interaction with it. By empirically demonstrating that this assumption fails across multiple model families, the authors contribute to a growing body of work questioning the validity of input-only safety filtering in multi-model, multi-turn generative systems.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying LLM-based information systems, this research suggests that prompt-only guardrails are both overly restrictive and poorly calibrated, potentially denying users safe and helpful responses at scale. The partial-generation monitoring approach offers a practical path toward reducing false rejections while maintaining safety, potentially lowering computational costs and improving user experience in conversational search and generative assistants. For the broader field, the finding that only 3.5% of harmful-response prompts are harmful across all seven tested models highlights the need for model-aware, interaction-level safety evaluation rather than universal prompt-level classification, which could reshape how safety benchmarks and guardrail architectures are designed for future LLM deployments.
