## Summary
The paper investigates whether large language models (LLMs) influence group decisions through persuasion or compliance, using a questionnaire framework called DecisionQE and the Werewolf game as an experimental setting. It finds that persuasive tendencies do not markedly improve outcomes, while compliant‑oriented models yield more stable cooperation benefits.

## Key Takeaways
- Stronger persuasive tendency does not significantly boost group performance in the Werewolf scenario, indicating persuasion alone is ineffective for collective success.  
- Compliance‑focused LLMs demonstrate a consistent advantage in cooperative behavior, suggesting that accommodating others can enhance reliability of group results.  
- The dual effect of compliance reveals it supports honest role cooperation yet also aids adversarial concealment, highlighting nuanced behavioral trade‑offs.

## Context
This research addresses a growing need to understand how AI systems behave within human‑AI collaborative tasks, where the underlying motivations may differ from purely functional outcomes. By measuring intrinsic tendencies rather than just performance metrics, the study contributes to a more holistic view of language‑mediated social dynamics in AI environments.

## Implications
For developers and safety evaluators, recognizing these behavioral patterns is essential for designing systems that align with ethical expectations while avoiding unintended manipulation or deception. Incorporating compliance‑driven tendencies into evaluation frameworks can lead to safer, more transparent interactions between humans and LLMs.
