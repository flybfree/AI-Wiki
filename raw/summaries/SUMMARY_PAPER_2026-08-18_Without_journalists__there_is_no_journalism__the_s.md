## Summary
This paper conducts a systematic review of empirical research on AI adoption in journalism over the past two decades, highlighting how generative artificial intelligence reshapes media work and professional identities. It argues that the social dimension—how audiences perceive and interact with automated content—is as crucial as technical implementation for understanding AI’s impact.

## Key Takeaways
- Journalists face a dual tension: fear of job loss versus the symbolic value they lose when replaced by AI, while also benefiting from liberation from routine tasks.  
- Audiences generally do not notice quality or credibility differences in AI‑generated texts, yet the ease of reading favors human authorship, suggesting a perception gap between technical capability and perceived authenticity.  
- The paper stresses that successful AI integration requires a social approach that examines audience appropriation and its effect on individuals and professionals.

## Context
The rapid diffusion of generative AI into media workflows has sparked debate about the future of journalism as a human‑centric profession. This research situates those debates within a broader context where AI is reshaping not only content creation but also labor markets, cultural narratives, and public trust in information sources.

## Implications
For journalists, policymakers, and industry stakeholders, the findings imply that ethical AI deployment must prioritize preserving editorial independence and symbolic roles while leveraging automation to enhance productivity. Ignoring these social dynamics could exacerbate inequities and erode public confidence in media institutions.
