## Summary  
This paper introduces CaRGo‑T (Causal Reasoning Graph‑of‑Thought), a framework that captures the subtle causal and contextual links between image and text elements in multimodal humor. By modeling these interactions as a lightweight graph, the method serializes the reasoning into code that can be interpreted by any VLM, enabling zero‑shot or in‑context learning. Experiments on four diverse humor datasets show that CaRGo‑T improves understanding scores by up to 20% and detection scores by about 3% compared with state‑of‑the‑art baselines.

## Key Takeaways  
- The framework represents multimodal humor as a causal context graph, providing a structured way to encode entity interactions.  
- The graph is converted into serializable code that any VLM can read and use for reasoning, supporting both zero‑shot and in‑context scenarios.  
- Mutual information analysis reveals that CaRGo‑T’s representations contain significantly more relevant information than those generated by baseline reasoning methods.

## Context  
Understanding humor in multimodal settings remains a challenge because it relies on complex, non‑linear relationships across visual and textual cues. Traditional prompting or linear chain‑of‑thought approaches often fail to capture these intricate dependencies, limiting the performance of large vision‑language models. This work addresses that gap by introducing a graph‑based reasoning layer that explicitly models causal links.

## Implications  
The ability to generate interpretable reasoning graphs could enhance the interpretability and robustness of multimodal AI systems beyond humor detection, such as in content moderation or dialogue generation. Practitioners can leverage CaRGo‑T to build more reliable models that better handle subtle human expressions across modalities.
