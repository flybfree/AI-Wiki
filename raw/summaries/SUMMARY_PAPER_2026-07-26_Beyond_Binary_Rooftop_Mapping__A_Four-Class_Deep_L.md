## Summary
The paper introduces a deep convolutional neural network that classifies Swiss rooftops into four distinct categories using open geospatial data. Applied to Bern it distinguishes existing green roofs, suitable for greening, solar‑panel covered roofs and flat unsuitable roofs. The model is fully open source and can be transferred globally.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework integrates aerial orthophotos with 3D roof slope derived from swissSURFACE3D to produce a four‑class rooftop map.
- It leverages only publicly available Swisstopo datasets, making the pipeline reproducible without proprietary inputs.
- Results show realistic opportunities for green roof expansion and clear delineation of unsuitable flat surfaces.

## Context
This work advances AI applications in urban climate adaptation by demonstrating how convolutional networks can fuse high‑resolution imagery with topographic data to generate actionable land‑use insights. It highlights the potential of open‑source models to replace labor‑intensive manual mapping for green infrastructure planning.

## Implications
Planners and city officials can use the model as a decision support tool to prioritize sites where greening yields climate benefits while avoiding conflicts with solar installations. The open nature encourages replication in other Swiss municipalities and beyond, fostering scalable urban sustainability strategies worldwide.
