## Summary
The paper studies how ML engineers, data scientists and software engineers describe soft skills on their CVs using a large corpus and LLM extraction. It finds that candidates rely heavily on narrative to convey leadership, coordination and mentoring rather than keyword lists. The study tests 13 hypotheses about role signatures and seniority progression.

## Key Takeaways
- Candidates disclose soft skills through narrative rather than keyword lists by roughly three to one, especially for leadership, coordination and mentoring (88‑96% narrative).  
- Seniority nearly triples the odds of articulating leadership, indicating that this competency is not universally expressed.  
- Technical candidates do articulate soft skills but a keyword‑based screening systematically misses them.

## Context
In AI hiring, employers rely on demand‑side data to define required competencies, yet candidate self‑presentation remains unexamined. This work bridges the gap by analyzing how professionals actually describe their abilities in CVs, providing evidence beyond simple keyword matching.

## Implications
For HR and talent acquisition teams, narrative cues may be more reliable than keyword filters for identifying soft skill proficiency. Recruiters should consider seniority and role‑specific expectations when interpreting candidate statements to avoid missing valuable candidates.
