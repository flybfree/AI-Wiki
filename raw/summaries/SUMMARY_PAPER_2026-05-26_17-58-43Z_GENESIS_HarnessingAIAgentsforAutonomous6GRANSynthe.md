## Summary
The paper introduces GENESIS, an agentic AI framework that automates the six labor‑intensive steps of cellular R&D by converting intents into validated over‑the‑air solutions stored in a persistent knowledge base. Experiments show it reduces manual engineering effort and improves interoperability compared with traditional methods.

## Key Takeaways
- GENESIS transforms specification clauses, telemetry anomalies, or research hypotheses into concrete network implementations that are tested on real hardware via OTA experiments.
- The framework’s SYNAPSE knowledge layer serves as both ground truth source and repository for all artifacts, enabling compounding of capabilities across iterations.
- By replacing manual synthesis with AI‑driven generation, GENESIS cuts the typical months of R&D down to minutes while preserving interoperability.

## Context
Current AI research has demonstrated rapid compression of software engineering tasks using large language models, yet these models struggle with domain‑specific constraints like radio specifications. The limitations create a bottleneck in 6G RAN development where errors propagate quickly across components.

## Implications
This work offers a scalable path for autonomous network design, allowing operators to prototype and validate new functionalities without extensive manual coding. As 6G standards evolve, GENESIS could become the backbone of continuous, AI‑driven R&D pipelines.
