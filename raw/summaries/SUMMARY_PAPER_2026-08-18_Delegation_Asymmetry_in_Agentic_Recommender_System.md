## Summary
This paper investigates how users perceive delegating conversation to autonomous LLM agents on a dating platform, focusing on the asymmetry between sending and receiving agent‑mediated messages. Using large surveys it finds that willingness to send an agent’s message is distinct from willingness to receive one, with deployment requiring lower receptivity than engagement.

## Key Takeaways
- The model shows a systematic delegation asymmetry: deploying one’s own agent needs a threshold of -0.38 while engaging a counterpart’s agent requires +0.32 and full engagement +1.39, meaning deployment is far less likely.
- Only 4‑13 % of dyads combine both actions, highlighting a significant gap between sending and receiving agent communication.
- Reciprocity requirements cut interaction volume by half or more because they exclude nearly two‑thirds of potential deployments.

## Context
Autonomous LLM agents are increasingly used to streamline user interactions on social platforms, yet their adoption hinges on unexamined user receptivity. This study provides the first quantitative measurement of that receptivity in a real‑world matching context, offering a methodological template for similar AI‑driven services.

## Implications
Designing agentic recommenders must therefore incorporate both sending and receiving mechanisms to avoid bottlenecks. Receptivity‑aware matchmaking can boost engagement by routing contacts based on measured openness, potentially tripling per‑contact interaction rates and improving overall system performance.
