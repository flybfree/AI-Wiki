## Summary
This paper presents the first comprehensive empirical study of fuzz testing for reinforcement learning agents, systematically evaluating five state‑of‑the‑art methods and random testing across three progressively complex environments. The benchmark reveals that throughput‑oriented approaches such as MDPFuzz excel in crash discovery while exploration‑focused strategies like SeqDivFuzz uncover diverse behaviors. Crucially, crashes generated by fuzzing can meaningfully improve agent robustness and support reliable safety monitoring with strong cross‑method generalization.

## Key Takeaways
- Throughput‑oriented methods achieve superior effectiveness and efficiency in crash detection, as measured by higher crash rates per unit time compared to other techniques.  
- Exploration‑driven strategies generate a wider variety of crash scenarios, increasing diversity and reducing redundancy among discovered failures.  
- The crashes produced by fuzzing are directly usable for improving agent robustness and for monitoring safety performance, showing consistent results across different fuzzing methods.

## Context
The rapid deployment of reinforcement learning agents in safety‑critical applications creates a pressing need for reliable testing tools that can uncover hidden failure modes without exhaustive simulation. Existing RL fuzz tests often lack standardized evaluation frameworks, leading to fragmented research and limited practical adoption. This study bridges that gap by providing a unified benchmark across diverse environments.

## Implications
For researchers, the findings suggest that combining complementary fuzzing strategies yields more robust coverage of failure cases. For industry practitioners, integrating these insights can enhance safety monitoring pipelines and accelerate deployment confidence in autonomous systems.
