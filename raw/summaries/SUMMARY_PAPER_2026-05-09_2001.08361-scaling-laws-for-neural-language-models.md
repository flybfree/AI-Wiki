# Summary: 2026-05-09 2001.08361-Scaling-Laws-For-Neural-Language-Models


## Summary
The paper investigates how language model performance on cross-entropy loss scales with model size, training data volume, and compute. It discovers a power‑law relationship where loss depends primarily on the number of tokens seen during training rather than total computation.

## Key Takeaways
- The cross‑entropy loss follows a power law that grows with model capacity, dataset token count, and computational resources.
- Large models are constrained by the amount of data they can ingest, not by how much compute is allocated to them.
- Empirical results show that doubling tokens halves loss, indicating a strong linear scaling effect.

## Context
Understanding these scaling laws helps researchers predict performance gains without proportional increases in cost. The findings align with earlier work on model efficiency and highlight the importance of data availability in training large AI systems.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, focusing on token‑rich datasets can yield better models for less money. Practitioners should prioritize data collection over raw compute when scaling language models to improve real‑world outcomes.
