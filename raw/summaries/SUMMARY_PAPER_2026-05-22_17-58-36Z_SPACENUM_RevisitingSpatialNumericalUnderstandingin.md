## Summary
This paper introduces SpaceNum, a framework that tests whether vision-language models truly ground numbers in spatial meaning by evaluating bidirectional tasks Num2Space and Space2Num across dynamic transitions and static layouts. The experiments reveal that current VLMs often generate outputs close to random guessing, indicating shallow understanding of numerical values in spatial contexts.

## Key Takeaways
- Models lack stable coordinate‑aware representations and fail to map visual spatial structures onto precise numerical labels.  
- Error analysis shows reliance on superficial cues rather than genuine spatial reasoning.  
- Explicit reasoning yields only marginal improvement while parameter tuning can partially boost performance.

## Context
Understanding how neural models integrate numbers with spatial perception is crucial for embodied AI systems that must act in the physical world. This work highlights a gap between language generation and true spatial numeracy, prompting deeper investigation into multimodal grounding mechanisms.

## Implications
Practitioners must move beyond surface‑level cues to develop robust training strategies that foster genuine spatial numerical comprehension. Advances here could improve robot navigation, augmented reality, and any system requiring precise spatial judgments.
