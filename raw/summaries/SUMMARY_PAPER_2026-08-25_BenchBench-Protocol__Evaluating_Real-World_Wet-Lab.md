## Summary  
The paper introduces BenchBench‑Protocol, a benchmark of protocol‑modification tasks derived from real wet‑lab work. It evaluates large language models on 149 tasks that capture differences between published protocols and the versions scientists actually use.

## Key Takeaways  
- The benchmark uses 149 tasks across nine biology domains, each rated by domain experts to ensure quality.  
- Claude Opus 5 scores highest at a normalized rubric of 59.2%, while other models range from 34.1% to 47.1%.  
- Even with ten attempts the best model does not achieve a perfect score, showing that current methods still have room for improvement.

## Context  
This work addresses the gap between open‑ended life‑science benchmarks that rely on expert‑curated tasks and those grounded in actual experimental changes. By reconstructing modifications from real wet‑lab practice, BenchBench‑Protocol provides a more realistic test of model reasoning.

## Implications  
For researchers, the benchmark shows that current models still struggle with nuanced protocol adjustments, highlighting the need for continued training on domain‑specific data. For industry, it underscores the importance of evaluating AI tools in practical scientific workflows before deployment.
