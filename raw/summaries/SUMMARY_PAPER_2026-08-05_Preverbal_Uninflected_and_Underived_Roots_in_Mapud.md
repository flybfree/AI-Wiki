## Summary
The paper investigates preverbal uninflected and underived roots in Mapudungun, focusing on wuno 'return/re-'. It shows that existing classifications of auxiliaries, modal prefixes, and particles are insufficient, revealing three profiles: stable V1 compounds, volatile orthographic shifts, and a true particle.

## Key Takeaways
- The diachronic corpus identifies V2 attestations for kim and kupa confirming they function as full lexical verbs rather than auxiliary forms.
- Orthographic changes such as pepi to wuno reflect phonological erosion that early missionaries recorded as spaces, suggesting fossilized prosodic pauses.
- Zúñiga's radical concatenation model is supported as the grammatical explanation for apparent variable binding.

## Context
This research contributes to AI by demonstrating how diachronic linguistic data can be used to train models that understand historical orthographic evolution and syntactic reconstruction in under-documented languages, a challenge for natural language processing systems lacking pre-contact corpora.

## Implications
Understanding these root types informs the development of multilingual models that respect phonological fossilization and avoid misclassifying particles as auxiliaries, improving accuracy in low-resource language AI applications.
