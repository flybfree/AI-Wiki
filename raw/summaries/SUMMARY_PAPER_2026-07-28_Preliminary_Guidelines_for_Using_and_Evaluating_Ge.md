## Summary
The paper presents a rapid review of existing work on using generative AI and large language models to support systematic literature reviews, identifying gaps in evaluation methods. It proposes the GUEST framework that outlines process recommendations for planning, conducting, and reporting SLRs that incorporate GenAI. The authors conclude that while GenAI cannot replace human oversight in unsupervised systematic studies, it can assist with repetitive tasks and provide validation of complex analyses.

## Key Takeaways
- Researchers must consider process issues when evaluating GenAI tools for SLRs, including how the AI is integrated into search, screening, and synthesis steps.  
- The paper highlights that current GenAI outputs lack the rigor, reliability, and transparency required by systematic literature reviews, necessitating human oversight.  
- GUEST recommendations aim to help both conduct trustworthy SLRs using GenAI and design rigorous independent evaluation studies.

## Context
Generative AI has rapidly advanced, offering capabilities such as text generation and summarization that are attractive for academic research tasks. However, many studies in software engineering have explored these tools without addressing the specific demands of systematic literature reviews, which rely on reproducible, transparent, and validated methods. This paper bridges that gap by focusing on the unique challenges of SLRs.

## Implications
For researchers, adopting GUEST can improve the credibility of their systematic literature reviews and make them more defensible in peer review processes. For industry practitioners evaluating AI tools, the framework provides a clear checklist to assess performance and limitations, supporting responsible adoption of GenAI in evidence‑based work.
