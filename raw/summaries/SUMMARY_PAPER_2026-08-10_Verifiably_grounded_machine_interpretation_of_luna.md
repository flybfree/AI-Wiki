## Summary
This paper introduces a machine‑learning system that interprets lunar basaltic mare stratigraphy by integrating topographic, spectral, and geologic map data into a vision‑language model. The authors show that the model can produce verifiably grounded geological interpretations when it combines local visual evidence with open‑book retrieval of published chronologies.

## Key Takeaways
- The system balances established geological priors with locally observed features to describe stratigraphy, yet its age estimates default to memorized priors without external data.  
- An open‑book retrieval mechanism allows the model to cite actual scientific chronologies, making its interpretations verifiable and grounded in published records.  
- The architecture separates visual interpretation of site evidence from quantitative historical context, highlighting a necessary division for automated geologic inference.

## Context
Automated geological reasoning is a frontier in AI where vision‑language models must align local observations with global scientific knowledge. This work contributes to that effort by demonstrating how retrieval can close the gap between raw data and established chronologies, a challenge recognized across planetary science and natural language processing.

## Implications
For planetary geologists, this approach offers a scalable way to generate consistent stratigraphic narratives without manual annotation. In industry, similar architectures could be applied to other domains where integrating local sensor data with external databases improves decision quality.
