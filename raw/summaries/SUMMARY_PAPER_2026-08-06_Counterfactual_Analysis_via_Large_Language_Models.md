## Summary
This paper explores the use of large language models, specifically GPT‑3.5, to perform counterfactual analysis in online lending by predicting return on investment under alternative interest rates. The study compares the model’s performance with traditional machine learning methods and demonstrates that prompt engineering can boost predictive accuracy, aligning closely with gradient‑boosted regression results.

## Key Takeaways
- Prompt engineering improves GPT‑3.5’s R‑squared from 1.97% to 2.84%, bringing it near the performance of advanced algorithms like gradient‑boosted regression.  
- The model generates logical and coherent counterfactual ROI predictions when presented with alternative interest rate scenarios, indicating strong causal reasoning capabilities.  
- These findings suggest that LLMs can serve as effective tools for counterfactual analysis in lending contexts.

## Context
Counterfactual analysis is essential for evaluating hypothetical outcomes in financial decision‑making, yet traditional methods often struggle to capture nuanced causal relationships. The integration of generative AI offers a novel approach to augment or replace conventional statistical models with human‑like reasoning.

## Implications
For lenders and data scientists, LLMs can provide rapid, interpretable counterfactual insights without extensive feature engineering. This capability may expand the scope of predictive modeling across sectors where causal inference is critical, encouraging broader adoption of AI in risk assessment.
