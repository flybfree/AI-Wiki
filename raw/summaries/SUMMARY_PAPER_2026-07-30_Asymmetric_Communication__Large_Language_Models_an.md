## Summary
The paper argues that AI discourse misattributes human communicative properties to language models, revealing a category mistake; it defines asymmetric communication where the model only outputs and humans bear norms; three conditions are identified: correctness is enforced exclusively by the receiver, accountability is borne by human participants alone, and practical standing of any output depends entirely on human uptake.

## Key Takeaways
- correctness is enforced exclusively by the receiver.
- accountability is borne by human participants alone.
- practical standing of any output depends entirely on human uptake.

## Context
This work situates large language models within Wittgensteinian language games, emphasizing that communication completion occurs on the receiver's side and that algorithmic contingency does not confer agency; it challenges prevailing AI narratives about sentience and alignment by showing that model outputs are not cognitively responsible but merely communicative artifacts. It also highlights that more powerful models do not change these structural conditions.

## Implications
The framework reframes AI governance as institutional constraint engineering rather than technical alignment, stressing that responsibility remains with human institutions; this suggests policy should focus on accountability mechanisms and normative frameworks rather than expecting machines to internalize goals.
