## Summary
The paper introduces ResNet, a residual learning framework that allows the construction of very deep neural networks by adding skip connections between layers. This design mitigates the vanishing gradient problem and enables training of networks with hundreds or thousands of layers, which were previously impractical.

## Key Takeaways
- The core innovation is the use of identity (skip) connections that add the output of a previous layer to the output of the current layer, preserving information flow.  
- Empirically, ResNet achieves significantly higher accuracy on ImageNet compared with standard deep networks without residuals.  
- The architecture can be trained efficiently using SGD and momentum, demonstrating robustness across various depths.

## Context
Deep neural networks have become a cornerstone of computer vision, but training them beyond 20–30 layers was hindered by gradient vanishing issues. ResNet resolves this by rethinking layer composition, aligning with the broader goal of scalable deep learning models that can process complex visual data.

## Implications
ResNet has reshaped industry practices, enabling faster prototyping and deployment of high‑performance vision systems in autonomous vehicles, medical imaging, and retail analytics. Practitioners now routinely adopt residual architectures as a default choice for deep image recognition tasks.
