## Summary
The paper proposes a privacy‑preserving federated learning framework that uses multimodal breast cancer data to predict tumour progression while respecting transparency, scalability, security and fairness. It compares the federated model’s performance with a centralised baseline trained on aggregated data. The study demonstrates that federated learning can achieve comparable predictive accuracy while keeping patient data local.

## Key Takeaways
- Federated learning enables training on sensitive clinical, imaging and demographic data without moving raw records to a central server.
- Performance of the multimodal model matches that of a centralised approach despite privacy constraints.
- The framework addresses fairness across patient subgroups by ensuring updates do not bias subgroup predictions.

## Context
Federated learning is gaining traction as a method to balance model utility with patient confidentiality in healthcare. Multimodal integration adds complexity but also enriches prediction power, making the paper relevant for future AI‑driven clinical decision support systems.

## Implications
These results suggest that privacy‑preserving federated models can be deployed at scale across hospitals, supporting personalised treatment planning and digital twins without compromising data security.
