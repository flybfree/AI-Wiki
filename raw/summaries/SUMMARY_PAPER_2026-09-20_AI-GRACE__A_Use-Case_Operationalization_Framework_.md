## Summary
The paper introduces AI-GRACE (Agentic Intelligence-Governance, Risk, Assurance, Controls, and Evidence), a comprehensive framework designed to bridge the gap between high-level organizational governance and the technical implementation of agentic artificial intelligence. It provides a structured methodology for determining what must be validated, controlled, and observed to ensure that autonomous agents deliver intended outcomes while remaining compliant with institutional obligations.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework begins by establishing clear objectives and obligations, which are then subjected to a risk assessment across seven domains, including critical areas like mission success and value realization.
- It produces specific, actionable outputs such as an Agent Operating Envelope—which defines the boundaries of permitted actions and escalation conditions—and Risk-Aligned Independence Levels (RAIL), which quantify the degree of autonomy allowed based on specific risks.
- The methodology provides a traceable path for organizations to identify capability gaps, allowing them to determine exactly what infrastructure must be built versus what is already supported by their current systems.

## Context
As agentic AI moves from experimental prototypes into production environments, the primary challenge shifts from model performance to operational safety and governance. This paper addresses a critical gap in how organizations can systematically map abstract corporate policies to concrete technical controls for autonomous agents that may take independent actions.

## Implications
For industry leaders and AI practitioners, this framework provides a repeatable blueprint for deploying agentic systems while maintaining human oversight and accountability. By creating a traceable path from high-level goals to logical architecture, it allows organizations to mitigate risks proactively and ensure that AI behavior remains aligned with organizational values.
