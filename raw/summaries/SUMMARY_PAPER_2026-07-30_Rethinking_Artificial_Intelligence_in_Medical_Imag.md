## Summary
The paper argues that the limited bedside impact of AI in medical imaging stems from structural misalignments between model design and clinical practice. It identifies six dimensions of this misalignment and proposes reframing each to create agentic, physician-aligned AI.

## Key Takeaways
- The dominance of pixel-only models ignores multimodal data sources that are essential for real-world diagnosis.
- Opaque and inflexible systems erode physician trust by providing rigid predictions without actionable guidance.
- Foundation models promise value in data‑sparse domains but currently fail to deliver because they lack domain adaptation.

## Context
Medical imaging AI has been a focal point of clinical research, yet performance gains have not translated into routine use. This paper situates the issue within broader AI challenges of interpretability and deployment.

## Implications
Clinicians must be involved in model design to ensure relevance; industry should prioritize multimodal integration and explainable outputs. The vision is an AI partner that augments judgment rather than supplants it.
