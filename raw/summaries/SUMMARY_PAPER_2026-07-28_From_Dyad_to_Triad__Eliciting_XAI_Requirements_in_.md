## Summary
This paper introduces a video‑based scaffolding protocol for eliciting explainable AI (XAI) requirements from stroke survivors and their caregivers. The study found that four different scaffolding techniques surfaced diverse, sometimes contradictory XAI needs, while also exposing three facilitator biases that can distort responses.

## Key Takeaways
- Analogical bridging mapped AI states to familiar systems, revealing participants’ need for intuitive analogies despite aphasia challenges.
- Projective personas depersonalised sensitive topics, allowing patients to discuss algorithmic transparency without self‑focus.
- Binary forcing reduced cognitive load, yet it limited expression of nuanced preferences and introduced conflicting needs.

## Context
Explainable AI is crucial for brain‑computer interfaces in rehabilitation, but standard elicitation methods assume intact communication and conceptual understanding. This work highlights the gap between theoretical XAI design and real‑world patient engagement, making methodological adaptation essential.

## Implications
Practitioners must adopt flexible scaffolding to avoid normative bias and hypothesis confirmation, ensuring authentic requirements capture. Ignoring these biases could lead to untrustworthy interfaces that fail to meet actual user needs in stroke rehabilitation.
