## Summary
This paper introduces an agentic workflow that automates the translation of theoretical quantum protocols into experiments on neutral‑atom processors while keeping human experts in the loop for validation. The agents handle protocol design, compilation, simulation, and cloud execution across three case studies in many‑body physics and optimization. A secondary agent classifies 633 Rydberg‑array arXiv papers, showing that nearly half are implementable today with identified hardware upgrade requirements.

## Key Takeaways
- The agentic workflow reduces the manual effort from theory to QPU campaign, completing a full pipeline overnight on two Pasqal QPUs.  
- Human reviewers were essential to catch errors such as an inadequate observable selection and a faulty hardware diagnosis that would otherwise go unnoticed.  
- Classification of 633 arXiv papers reveals that about half are feasible with current hardware, while the rest require specific upgrades.

## Context
The integration of quantum computing into high‑performance cloud environments demands seamless pipeline orchestration beyond raw hardware access. This work bridges AI‑driven automation with quantum algorithm development, illustrating how machine agents can act as co‑pilots for researchers lacking deep expertise in protocol compilation or hardware diagnostics.

## Implications
For the scientific community, this approach democratizes quantum experimentation by lowering implementation barriers and enabling rapid prototyping of complex protocols. Industry stakeholders benefit from accelerated validation cycles that translate research ideas into tangible QPU campaigns, fostering faster innovation and investment in neutral‑atom technology.
