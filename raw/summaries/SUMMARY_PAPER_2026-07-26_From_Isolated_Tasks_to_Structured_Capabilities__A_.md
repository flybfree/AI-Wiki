## Summary
The paper proposes a multilayer taxonomy of 14 capability domains and 91 subskills that organizes LLM evaluation beyond isolated tasks. It demonstrates that the framework uncovers coverage gaps and highlights high‑impact capabilities such as Language‑Semantic Competence and Reasoning. The study maps these capabilities across 31,505 papers from major conferences.

## Key Takeaways
- The taxonomy groups capabilities into Primitive, Constructed, and Integrative layers, providing a structured view that goes beyond task‑based evaluation.
- Direct research attention is concentrated on Language‑Semantic Competence (22.3% of papers) and Reasoning (21.3%), indicating these are the most studied subskills.
- Six capability domains appear in fewer than 2% of papers, revealing significant coverage gaps that could be addressed.

## Context
AI research often evaluates models through discrete benchmarks rather than underlying cognitive abilities, leading to fragmented literature. This taxonomy aligns evaluation with human developmental stages and functional support, offering a more coherent framework for understanding model capabilities.

## Implications
For researchers, the structured capability view enables targeted hypothesis testing and training strategies that address identified gaps. For industry practitioners, it provides a common language to assess LLM performance across diverse applications, improving reliability and trustworthiness.
