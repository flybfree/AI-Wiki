## Summary
The paper argues that as large language models are used to settle mathematical conjectures, new norms should be established requiring disclosure of prompts and software setups and formal verification of results. It also stresses the need for human authors to provide intuitive explanations. The study suggests these practices will improve reproducibility and trust.

## Key Takeaways
- LLM‑derived proofs must include a complete record of the exact prompts, model version, hardware configuration, and code used, so that others can reproduce or verify the computation.
- Formalization is required; results should be expressed in a formal proof system that machines can check, ensuring correctness beyond human interpretation.
- Human authors retain responsibility for creating clear, intuitive explanations of LLM‑generated findings to aid understanding.

## Context
The rapid integration of LLMs into mathematical research has produced surprising breakthroughs but also raised concerns about transparency and reliability. Existing community norms focus on peer review and citation but ignore the technical provenance of AI‑assisted proofs. This paper addresses those gaps by proposing concrete procedural standards that align with reproducibility principles in computational mathematics.

## Implications
These recommendations will shape how journals, conferences, and research groups evaluate LLM‑based results, encouraging a shift toward verifiable, explainable outputs. Practitioners can adopt the norms to reduce risk of false positives, foster trust, and ultimately advance the credibility of AI‑assisted scientific discovery.
