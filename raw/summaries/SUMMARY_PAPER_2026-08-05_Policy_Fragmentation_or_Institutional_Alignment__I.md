## Summary
This paper investigates how higher education institutions in the United States implement artificial intelligence policies, focusing on differences between university‑level and school‑level governance as well as business‑school specific frameworks. Using natural language processing to analyze 34 state‑wide AI policy documents, the authors reveal a clear divergence: university policies prioritize data security and risk mitigation, while school policies emphasize pedagogical use of tools. Business schools often lack distinct AI policies, creating misalignment with their learning objectives.

## Key Takeaways
- University‑level AI policies concentrate on safeguarding student data and minimizing operational risks rather than curriculum design.  
- School‑level policies, when present, are oriented toward practical tool usage and classroom integration of AI technologies.  
- Business schools frequently adopt university frameworks without tailoring them to their unique disciplinary goals.

## Context
The rapid adoption of artificial intelligence across high‑skill professions demands that higher education institutions keep pace with evolving workforce needs while maintaining rigorous academic standards. This study contributes by providing empirical evidence of policy fragmentation within a single institution, highlighting the need for coherent governance structures that bridge institutional and departmental responsibilities.

## Implications
For faculty and students, misaligned policies can hinder access to AI tools and create uncertainty about data handling practices. Accreditation bodies may also face challenges when evaluating institutions whose AI strategies do not reflect discipline‑specific learning outcomes. Aligning policy across levels will improve educational quality and support industry readiness.
