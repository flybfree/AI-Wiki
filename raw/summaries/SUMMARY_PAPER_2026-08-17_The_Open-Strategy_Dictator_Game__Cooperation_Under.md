## Summary
The paper introduces the Open-Strategy Dictator Game (OSDG) where players submit natural‑language strategies visible to all participants and a language model decides whether the dictator shares or takes an endowment based on those texts. Experiments show conditional cooperation dominates unconditional always‑share or always‑take approaches, indicating robust evolution of cooperation under mutual transparency.

## Key Takeaways
- Conditional strategies consistently dominate over unconditional ones because they adapt to the recipient’s behavior.
- The analysis uses softmax equilibrium frequencies to show that cooperative payoffs become more attractive when cooperators are prevalent.
- Sensitivity testing reveals that even with modestly low cooperation values, conditional rules still outperform static rules.

## Context
This work extends classic game theory into AI‑driven environments where decision procedures are encoded in text and evaluated by large language models. It highlights how algorithmic transparency can shape strategic outcomes a concern for systems relying on interpretable behavior.

## Implications
For developers of collaborative AI agents, the findings suggest designing strategies that condition actions on observed others’ choices to improve long‑term stability. Practitioners should consider these dynamics when building transparent interaction protocols across platforms.
