## Summary
The paper investigates how Artificial Intelligence in Nigerian mobile shopping applications affects digital sovereignty by focusing on platform transparency as a measure of user awareness and control. It finds that AI features are common but disclosure practices are limited, indicating low user control over the technology. A socio‑economic analysis also reveals rising dependence on consumer digital platforms with moderate AI awareness.

## Key Takeaways
- AI is widely implemented in the applications yet transparency about its use remains limited, showing a gap between functionality and user understanding.
- The study documents an increasing dependence on consumer digital platforms across Nigeria, which raises concerns about long‑term control of personal data.
- Uneven patterns of interaction suggest that awareness varies by socio‑economic status, affecting the effectiveness of AI safeguards.

## Context
Artificial Intelligence in mobile commerce globally raises questions about user autonomy and corporate responsibility. Nigeria’s rapid e‑commerce growth underscores the need for transparent practices to protect digital sovereignty, making this study relevant to broader discussions on responsible AI deployment.

## Implications
For researchers, the findings highlight a framework that links transparency metrics with individual digital sovereignty. For industry practitioners, improving disclosure mechanisms can enhance user trust and mitigate fraud risks in AI‑driven shopping apps.
