## Summary
This paper introduces PeerMathDial, a dataset of authentic middle school math dialogues that captures peer‑to‑peer problem solving. The study shows how students coordinate, negotiate strategies, and benefit from teacher interventions within real classroom settings.

## Key Takeaways
- The dataset comprises 55 dialogues involving 27 students, totaling 6,406 turns, providing a large-scale resource for analyzing collaborative discourse in mathematics.
- Teacher interventions are tracked over time to measure their impact on dialogue evolution and problem solving outcomes.
- Dialogue act predictions by LLMs align with student‑reported traits such as confidence and leadership, linking observable behavior to self‑perception.

## Context
The paper addresses a gap in AI research where most educational dialogue datasets focus on teacher‑student interactions rather than peer collaboration. This limitation hampers the development of models that understand how students negotiate knowledge without adult guidance.

## Implications
For educators, PeerMathDial offers concrete examples of effective peer strategies to inform classroom design. For developers, the dataset enables training AI models to simulate realistic student dialogue, supporting tools that enhance collaborative learning experiences.
