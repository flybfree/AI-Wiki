## Summary
The paper investigates how formal variation in novels generated by language models compares to human-written works across different styles and sources. It finds that AI-generated collections exhibit compressed sentence structure, readability, and lexical diversity relative to human literature.

## Key Takeaways
- The study shows that repeated AI generation compresses sentence structure more than human novels, reducing variability in average sentence length and punctuation patterns.
- Lexical variation is similarly limited except for Qwen Zero‑Style MATTR, which retains higher lexical entropy compared to other models.
- This compression manifests as both variational overclosure (limited formal range) and correlational overclosure (individual AI novels resemble human style while the collection does not).

## Context
Large language models are increasingly used to produce long-form narratives, yet their stylistic consistency remains poorly understood. Understanding the degree of formality variation helps assess authenticity, creative control, and potential biases in automated writing.

## Implications
For publishers and educators, this research suggests that AI‑generated novels may be perceived as less diverse than human works, affecting reader expectations and educational value. Practitioners should monitor both stylistic consistency and lexical richness when deploying LLMs for narrative production.
