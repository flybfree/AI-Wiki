## Summary
The paper investigates how multilingual supervision of proverbs improves figurative language identification, using 742 proverb concepts across seven languages and five models. It finds that around half the translated data is enough for near‑optimal performance and that combining all four figurative forms yields best results.

## Key Takeaways
- Approximately 50% of the multilingual training data suffices to reach peak performance, indicating efficient use of supervision.
- The Culture Specific form shows the largest gains under multilingual supervision, suggesting its importance even when rare.
- Moral/Advisory and Culture Specific forms drive instruction‑tuned LLMs’ figurative identification, highlighting their impact on model behavior.

## Context
Multilingual AI models often rely on homogeneous data, limiting cross‑lingual transfer. This study bridges that gap by showing how diverse translation supervision can boost performance across languages, a step toward more inclusive language understanding.

## Implications
Researchers and practitioners should adopt multidimensional annotation frameworks for figurative language tasks to capture multiple meanings. The findings support scaling multilingual figurative models with modest data, encouraging cost‑effective deployment in global applications.
