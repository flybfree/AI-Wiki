## Summary
The paper introduces Compass, a system that continuously aligns social media feeds with users' reflective preferences by prompting brief in‑situ reflections and adjusting feed content accordingly. In a ten‑day field study of fifteen YouTube Shorts viewers, Compass increased reflective consumption, iterative preference updates, and feed alignment without compromising the casual browsing experience.

## Key Takeaways
- Compass uses lightweight notifications to surface in‑situ reflections that help users articulate their preferences while they browse. 
- The system periodically simulates behavioral signals and directly manipulates feed content to keep the feed aligned with reflective preferences rather than immediate impulses. 
- In the study, Compass promoted more purposeful feed consumption, iterative preference adjustment, and stronger alignment without making browsing feel forced.

## Context
Social media recommendation systems typically rely on real‑time behavioral data that reflect users' current impulses, often diverging from deeper‑held preferences. Existing approaches require manual user input or configuration pages, which are rarely used because they demand effort. Compass addresses this gap by embedding reflection directly into the browsing flow, offering a seamless way to align feeds with evolving preferences.

## Implications
For AI researchers, Compass demonstrates that continuous, in‑situ feedback loops can improve alignment between system outputs and user intent without disrupting engagement. For industry practitioners, the approach offers a scalable method to enhance personalization while preserving the informal nature of social media consumption.
