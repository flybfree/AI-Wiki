## Summary
The paper investigates how large language models represent national cultural change using two decades of World Values Survey data and compares their outputs to the Inglehart‑Welzel cultural map. It finds that while LLMs often place countries near recent surveyed positions, these representations lag several years behind true trajectories, capture only part of observed change, introduce artificial movement, and rarely reproduce reversals.

## Key Takeaways
- Models generally align with the most recent World Values Survey snapshot but their temporal representation lags by multiple years. 
- The magnitude of cultural shifts is underrepresented; models show less extreme changes than historical data indicate. 
- Artificial movements are introduced where none occurred, and reversals in cultural trajectories are rarely reproduced.

## Context
Understanding how AI systems model cultural evolution is crucial for evaluating the representational fidelity of language models beyond static snapshots. This work highlights a gap between snapshot accuracy and dynamic cultural awareness that affects trust and usability of LLMs in cross‑cultural contexts.

## Implications
These findings suggest that current evaluation metrics focusing on present‑day alignment may mislead developers and regulators about a model’s ability to capture historical change. Practitioners must adopt longitudinal assessment frameworks to avoid reinforcing outdated or inaccurate cultural representations in AI systems.
