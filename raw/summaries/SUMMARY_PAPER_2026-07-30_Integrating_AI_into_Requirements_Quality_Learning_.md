## Summary
The paper investigates a TPACK‑guided integration of a multi‑agent AI tool into a master‑level requirements engineering assignment, examining how the structured design shapes students’ use of the tool and their understanding of user story quality criteria. It finds that students employed the AI mainly as support for analysis and evaluation rather than for automation, improved alignment on concrete dimensions such as value articulation and testability, while negotiability showed mixed effects. Students reported conditional trust in the AI, actively refined its outputs, became more aware of quality criteria, yet faced moderate usability challenges.

## Key Takeaways
- Students employed the AI tool primarily as support rather than automation, focusing on analysis and evaluation tasks.
- Alignment improvements were most evident for concrete quality dimensions such as value articulation and testability, whereas negotiability showed mixed effects.
- Students expressed conditional trust in AI, engaged actively in refining outputs, became more aware of quality criteria while encountering moderate usability challenges.

## Context
The rapid adoption of generative AI in software engineering has created a demand for pedagogically sound frameworks to integrate such tools into education, especially in conceptually complex domains like requirements engineering. This study contributes by applying TPACK—a framework linking technology, pedagogy, and content knowledge—to guide the design of AI‑enhanced assignments.

## Implications
The findings suggest that TPACK‑guided scaffolding can align AI affordances with RE learning objectives, supporting responsible integration in curricula. Practitioners should adopt similar frameworks to balance AI support with student agency, ensuring tools enhance rather than replace critical thinking and quality judgment.
