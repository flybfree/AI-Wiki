## Summary
This paper introduces the 2026 PNPL competition, which focuses on word classification and demonstrates efficient cross‑subject generalisation using the LibriBrain100 dataset. The Deep track achieves high within‑subject performance, while the Broad track shows that models can perform well with only a few minutes of per‑subject data, moving closer to practical brain‑computer interfaces.

## Key Takeaways
- The competition’s Deep track reaches F1‑macro scores around 95 % on word classification, showing state‑of‑the‑art performance when using abundant within‑subject MEG data.  
- The Broad track proves that models can maintain comparable accuracy with as few as 10 minutes of per‑subject fine‑tuning, highlighting the importance of cross‑subject generalisation.  
- LibriBrain100 expands the original LibriBrain dataset by adding 32 subjects and roughly 80 hours of data, enabling systematic evaluation of both intra‑ and inter‑subject capabilities.

## Context
The work advances AI research in neurotechnology by pushing word classification beyond typical language models that rely on large text corpora. By leveraging MEG recordings, the study bridges signal processing with deep learning, illustrating how compact datasets can still support high‑level linguistic tasks. This aligns with broader trends toward efficient, data‑light machine learning for real‑world applications.

## Implications
For clinicians and BCI developers, these results suggest that communication devices could be feasible with minimal user training time, reducing the burden on users of profound paralysis. Industry stakeholders may adopt this paradigm to design scalable, low‑cost neurointerfaces without requiring extensive per‑subject data collection.
