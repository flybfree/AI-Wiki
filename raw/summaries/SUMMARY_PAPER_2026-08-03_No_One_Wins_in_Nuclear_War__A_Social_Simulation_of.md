## Summary
The paper presents WOPR, a deterministic social‑simulation environment that models high‑stakes decision making through the nuclear war card game. By layering structured communication channels on a replay‑validated rules engine, it demonstrates how collective command‑and‑control systems can generate strategic outcomes. The authors show that explicit agent decisions and private‑channel negotiations shape the probability of conflict.

## Key Takeaways
- WOPR provides a verifiable rules engine with private‑channel negotiation that makes every strategic choice an explicit agent decision.
- The environment layers communication channels from silence to single‑recipient messages, allowing varied social dynamics within a fixed rule set.
- All code, configurations and replay data are publicly released, enabling reproducibility across other social‑simulation frameworks.

## Context
This work addresses the need for transparent, auditable models of military decision making in AI research. By separating simulation mechanics from persona fidelity, it offers a reusable contract that can be applied to any high‑risk scenario beyond nuclear war.

## Implications
For practitioners, WOPR demonstrates how private‑channel contracts can be embedded into AI agents to produce verifiable outcomes. The framework may inform policy simulations and ethical testing of autonomous decision systems in contested environments.
