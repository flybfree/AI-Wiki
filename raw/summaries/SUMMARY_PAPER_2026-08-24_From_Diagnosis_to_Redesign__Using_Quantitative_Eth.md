## Summary
This paper introduces a quantitative ethnographic (QE) method to diagnose and redesign multi‑agent large language model systems by analyzing the discourse generated during agent interactions. Applied to automated essay scoring, the study shows that correct decisions are linked to rubric‑grounded justification while incorrect ones feature prolonged proposition‑challenge exchanges. After prompt revisions based on these findings, exact scoring accuracy rose from 27.78 % to 40.28 %, and interaction patterns aligned more closely with successful outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- Correct scoring decisions are characterized by rubric‑grounded justification, agreement, and elaboration in the initial system.
- Incorrect scoring decisions involve extended proposition‑challenge‑response exchanges that lack consistent alignment with rubric criteria.
- Prompt redesign based on QE analysis improved exact scoring accuracy from 27.78 % to 40.28 % and made correct and incorrect debate patterns nearly indistinguishable.

## Context
Multi‑agent LLM systems aim to enhance reasoning by delegating tasks across specialized agents, yet their coherence remains a challenge. This work demonstrates that discourse analysis can reveal hidden performance drivers, offering a concrete bridge between qualitative insights and quantitative improvements in AI reasoning pipelines.

## Implications
The findings suggest that systematic dialogue monitoring can guide prompt engineering for multi‑agent systems, reducing reliance on trial‑and‑error optimization. Practitioners can leverage QE to systematically improve model reliability, making such approaches valuable across industries where accurate decision‑making is critical.
