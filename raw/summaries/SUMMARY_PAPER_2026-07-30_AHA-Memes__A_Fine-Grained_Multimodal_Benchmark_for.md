## Summary
The paper introduces AHA‑Memes, the first large‑scale Arabic hateful meme benchmark with fine‑grained multi‑label annotations. It presents a taxonomy of attack strategies and evaluates models on text‑only, image‑only, late‑fusion multimodal tasks as well as few‑shot ICL and zero‑shot VLM settings.

## Key Takeaways
- The dataset comprises 5K manually annotated memes using a hate type taxonomy that captures attack strategies.  
- It also provides ~66K silver‑labeled memes for future research.  
- Benchmarking includes text‑only, image‑only, late‑fusion multimodal models and few‑shot ICL/VLMs under zero‑shot and fine‑tuning, establishing strong baselines.

## Context
Arabic hateful meme detection remains underexplored compared to high‑resource languages, limiting the development of culturally aware AI systems. Existing resources focus on propaganda or coarse harmful‑content labels rather than nuanced hate types. This work addresses that gap by offering a richly annotated benchmark for Arabic.

## Implications
This benchmark will guide researchers in building models that understand nuanced cultural contexts and attack strategies. Practitioners can leverage it to improve detection accuracy while respecting ethical considerations.
