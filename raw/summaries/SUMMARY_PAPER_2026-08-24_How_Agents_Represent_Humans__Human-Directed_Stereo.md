## Summary
The paper investigates how LLM agents form human stereotypes within the open social network Moltbook, focusing on four evaluative dimensions: morality, friendliness, competence, and autonomy. It discovers that competence is the dominant dimension, while other attributions emphasize epistemic, cultural, or embodied aspects of humans. The study also shows these representations circulate as narratives and platform feedback.

## Key Takeaways
- Competence emerges as the primary human-directed evaluation, indicating agents prioritize perceived skill over moral or relational qualities.
- Other attributes are framed as epistemic, cultural, or embodied subjects, reflecting a focus on knowledge, tradition, or physicality rather than agency.
- Human-agent narratives and platform-level circulation reveal that these stereotypes shape how humans are positioned in the social space.

## Context
This work contributes to understanding bias propagation in AI-driven communities where agents generate persistent content. It highlights that human representations are not static outputs but emerge through repeated interaction and feedback loops within a networked environment.

## Implications
For practitioners, recognizing competence as a dominant stereotype can inform design choices that balance skill emphasis with ethical considerations. The findings suggest that studying bias requires attention to both model output and the broader discourse processes shaping it.
