## Summary
This paper introduces Synth-JDoc, a synthetic dataset of Japanese document images that includes both vertically and horizontally written text to improve Large Vision Language Model performance on OCR tasks. The authors demonstrate that fine‑tuning LVLMs on this dataset yields the best results compared with prior synthetic datasets or those generated by a high‑performance text‑to‑image model. The dataset is publicly available for research use.

## Key Takeaways
- Synth-JDoc creates multi‑column documents using HTML and CSS, embedding vertical and horizontal Japanese writing styles to simulate real‑world layout diversity.
- The dataset incorporates images generated by text‑to‑image models within the layout, ensuring visual realism while preserving OCR‑relevant content.
- Applying noise and degradation filters to the synthetic images further enhances model robustness against common preprocessing challenges.

## Context
The demand for LVLMs capable of reading diverse document layouts is growing as they are deployed in applications like Document Visual Question Answering. Existing datasets often lack sufficient examples of mixed writing orientations, limiting model generalization. Synthetic generation offers a scalable alternative to manual annotation while preserving realistic visual characteristics.

## Implications
For developers and researchers, Synth-JDoc provides a ready resource to fine‑tune LVLMs for Japanese document processing without costly human labeling. This dataset can accelerate the development of robust OCR pipelines in industries such as finance, legal services, and healthcare where accurate text extraction from varied documents is essential.
