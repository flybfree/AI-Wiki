## Summary
This paper fills a gap in the literature by integrating AI adoption, cybersecurity governance, and public‑sector constraints into a single analytical framework. It proposes a seven‑domain typology of ten specific AI‑driven cyber governance failures and demonstrates that current frameworks lack coverage for emerging issues such as Shadow AI, speed asymmetry, and governance vacuums.

## Key Takeaways
- The typology identifies ten concrete causes where AI implementation leads to cybersecurity governance breakdowns in government agencies.  
- A three‑pathway failure model shows how accountability, operational resilience, and compliance failures reinforce one another.  
- Five major governance frameworks are tested against the typology, revealing that none address Shadow AI, speed asymmetry, or the operational specificity required for public sector use.

## Context
Artificial intelligence is rapidly being integrated into public‑sector services to improve efficiency and decision‑making. However, existing cybersecurity standards were designed before such AI‑specific risks emerged, leaving a regulatory vacuum that can undermine both security and accountability.

## Implications
Practitioners must adopt the new typology and framework to design maturity models that account for AI‑driven threats in government contexts. The work offers a design specification that helps organizations anticipate and mitigate governance failures before they occur.
