## Summary
The paper explores how sustainability information is presented in recommendation explanations and its effect on user choices. It finds that simply stating sustainability facts does not change behavior, but framing the information or invoking social norms does increase sustainable selections. The study used LLM-generated explanations across coffee and hotel booking contexts.

## Key Takeaways
- Disclosing sustainability data alone improves explanation ratings but fails to shift purchase decisions.
- Framing sustainability as a benefit or referencing a descriptive norm significantly boosts eco‑friendly choices in both low‑ and high‑involvement settings.
- The research demonstrates that generative AI can produce theory‑grounded explanations at scale while highlighting the gap between perception and action.

## Context
Explainable AI seeks to make algorithmic outputs understandable, yet real‑world impact on user behavior remains understudied. This work bridges that gap by testing how narrative framing influences both perception and action in everyday recommendations.

## Implications
Practitioners can leverage LLM explanations not only for transparency but also as behavioral nudges when combined with effective framing strategies. Designing adaptive, socially aware explanations could drive sustainable consumption at scale across industries.
