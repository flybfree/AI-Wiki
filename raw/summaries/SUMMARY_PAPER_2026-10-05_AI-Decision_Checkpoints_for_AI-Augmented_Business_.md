## Summary
This paper proposes a structured framework called "AI-decision checkpoints" to integrate artificial intelligence as a first-class design element within Business Process Management (BPM) education and practice. The author introduces explicit decision points in a process development trajectory where developers evaluate AI-candidate sub-processes, assess impacts on time, cost, quality, and flexibility, and document reasoned adoption or rejection decisions. A fictitious customer onboarding process serves as a teaching case embedded in a six-module lifecycle framework combining process modeling, simulation, AI-agent workflow execution, and process mining. Preliminary formative reflections from instructor observations and student artifacts suggest the approach helped learners distinguish task-level automation from process-level value creation.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper identifies a critical gap in current BPM curricula and frameworks, which treat AI as an add-on technology rather than a core design element of end-to-end business processes, leaving graduates unprepared to reason about AI integration at the process architecture level.
- AI-decision checkpoints are defined as explicit moments where process developers must identify AI-candidate sub-processes, evaluate expected effects across four dimensions (time, cost, quality, and flexibility), consider legal and organizational constraints, and document a reasoned decision to adopt, constrain, or reject specific AI components such as LLMs, RAG systems, or AI agents.
- The framework is instantiated through a six-module lifecycle-driven teaching case spanning process modeling, simulation, workflow execution with AI agents, and process mining, where each module's output feeds into the next, creating a coherent educational pipeline. Preliminary observations from learning-management-system logs and submitted artifacts indicate the approach supported clearer conceptual distinctions between automating individual tasks and generating genuine process-level value.

## Context
As LLMs, RAG pipelines, and autonomous AI agents become increasingly embedded in operational workflows across industries, the gap between AI capability and process design methodology has widened significantly. BPM has historically provided mature frameworks for modeling, simulating, and optimizing workflows, but these frameworks were not designed with AI as a native architectural component. This paper matters because it bridges BPM pedagogy and AI system design, offering a structured decision-making scaffold that could influence how universities train future process engineers and how organizations govern AI adoption within their operational processes.

## Implications
For BPM educators and curriculum designers, the checkpoint framework offers a concrete pedagogical tool for teaching students to evaluate AI integration systematically rather than reactively, potentially reshaping how process management programs incorporate AI literacy. For industry practitioners and process architects, the structured decision points provide a governance mechanism for evaluating whether AI components genuinely improve process outcomes or merely automate tasks without delivering strategic value. The approach also signals a broader need for BPM standards and certification bodies to update their frameworks so that AI is treated as a first-class design concern rather than an afterthought bolted onto existing process models.
