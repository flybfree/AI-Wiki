## Summary
This paper addresses the software engineering challenges of deploying AI‑driven control systems for building operations, where failures have tangible physical consequences. It identifies three missing SE4AI perspectives that hinder successful deployment and proposes a foundation for safety‑critical AI in buildings. The authors draw on two interdisciplinary research projects to illustrate these gaps.

## Key Takeaways
- A single bad control decision can waste energy irreversibly, violate occupant comfort, or accelerate equipment wear, making software failures far more costly than in typical digital environments.  
- Although actual safety‑critical failures are rare, the inherent fault tolerance of building automation systems means that minor faults still produce lasting physical impacts, demanding a different SE approach.  
- The paper proposes a foundation for SE4AI specifically tailored to systems where failure has real‑world consequences, which must be validated through further research.

## Context
The integration of AI into building operations is gaining momentum as cities seek smarter energy management solutions. However, most SE literature assumes failures are merely user‑experience issues, overlooking the irreversible physical effects in critical infrastructure like buildings.

## Implications
Engineers and practitioners must adopt a safety‑first mindset when designing AI for building automation to prevent costly damage and ensure occupant well‑being. This shift could set a new standard for cyber‑physical systems across various industries.
