## Summary
This paper introduces a dataset of 951 argumentative critiques paired with 442 position texts across diverse topics such as AI safety and decision theory. Six expert raters evaluate each critique on dimensions like centrality, strength, correctness, and clarity, producing ratings that enable model benchmarking. The study demonstrates that models can reliably score these contextualized arguments, aligning their performance with general capability rankings.

## Key Takeaways
- The dataset comprises 951 critiques of 442 position texts, covering interdisciplinary domains where ground‑truth answers are unattainable.
- Expert raters provide detailed scores on centrality, strength, correctness, and clarity, creating a multi‑dimensional evaluation framework.
- Model performance on this benchmark correlates with broader capability rankings, indicating that argument scoring is a viable proxy for general reasoning ability.

## Context
Philosophical questions and AI safety debates often lack clear, verifiable answers, making them unsuitable for standard verification tasks. This work addresses the gap by focusing on evaluating the quality of arguments rather than final conclusions, reflecting a shift toward assessing reasoning processes in AI models.

## Implications
For practitioners, this dataset offers a practical tool to stress‑test argument generation and critique capabilities within LLMs. The findings suggest that improving argument evaluation can indirectly boost overall model performance across complex, non‑verifiable domains.
