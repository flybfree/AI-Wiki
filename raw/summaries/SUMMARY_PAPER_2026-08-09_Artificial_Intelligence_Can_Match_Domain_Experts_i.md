## Summary
This study evaluates large language models’ ability to extract evidence and appraise research papers on microbial oncogenesis, using a benchmark of 24 papers with MMTV‑LV and breast cancer as the case study. Domain experts created a structured template of 77 questions across multiple formats, and LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Pro/Flash, GPT‑5, GPT‑5 Nano) answered them. The results show that GPT‑5 and GPT‑5 Nano performed as well as or better than human experts on structured tasks.

## Key Takeaways
- LLM responses aligned closely with expert answers across all question types, indicating strong evidence extraction and critical appraisal capabilities.
- Gemini models were more permissive in applying microbial oncogenesis criteria, suggesting potential over‑inclusion of non‑relevant content.
- Methodological appraisal and contradiction identification within full texts remained the most persistent weaknesses of LLMs.

## Context
The rapid advancement of AI research has prompted interest in using large language models for systematic literature synthesis, especially in complex biomedical domains where human effort is limited. This paper contributes to that conversation by providing empirical evidence on LLM performance relative to domain experts in a specific cancer‑microbe research area.

## Implications
Automated evidence synthesis powered by LLMs could streamline the discovery of novel microbial oncogenes and accelerate therapeutic development, reducing reliance on manual review. Addressing identified weaknesses will be crucial for deploying these models reliably in clinical decision support systems.
