## Summary
This paper proposes a reproducible workflow that maps farmland extent and visible boundaries from 1 m NAIP RGB imagery using a residual U‑Net trained with Dice loss combined with text‑prompted SAM 3 refinement. The model achieves high segmentation performance (Dice 0.92, IoU 0.86) on diverse agricultural scenes, producing coherent regional masks via sliding‑window stitching.

## Key Takeaways
- The residual U‑Net with Dice‑dominant loss delivers test accuracy of 0.88 and recall of 0.9794, significantly improving over baseline methods.
- Fusing frozen SAM 3 with the prompt “agricultural farmland field” via logical OR boosts Dice scores from 0.858 to 0.955 on orchard rows and from 0.804 to 0.903 on fragmented parcels.
- Sliding‑window stitching of 256 × 256 patches yields regional masks with Dice values around 0.9, enabling seamless coverage across scenes.

## Context
The integration of deep learning segmentation networks with vision transformers like SAM demonstrates how hybrid models can overcome the limitations of single‑task approaches in remote sensing. This work aligns with efforts to automate land‑use mapping where manual annotation is costly and data scarcity limits model training.

## Implications
Farmland maps generated by this pipeline provide a reliable, up‑to‑date reference layer for crop monitoring and production accounting without relying on proprietary or outdated cadastral data. Practitioners can use the semantic farmland‑extent layer to support decision‑making in precision agriculture and land‑conversion analysis.
