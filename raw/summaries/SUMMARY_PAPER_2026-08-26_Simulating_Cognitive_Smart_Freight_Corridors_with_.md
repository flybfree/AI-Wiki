## Summary
The paper presents an agent-based modeling framework that integrates a physical infrastructure layer, a connectivity (V2X) layer, and a decision layer combining reinforcement learning and multi-agent reinforcement learning to simulate smart freight corridors. It evaluates three scenarios—baseline, assisted, cognitive—and finds the cognitive scenario yields higher throughput and lower congestion while offering energy savings per kilometer through platooning. The framework also quantifies congestion and emissions, providing a holistic view of corridor performance.

## Key Takeaways
- The Cognitive scenario achieves higher throughput and lower congestion than the baseline.
- The Assisted scenario delivers meaningful energy savings per kilometer through platooning.
- Sensitivity analysis shows that the throughput advantage widens under high demand, and MARL coordination extracts greater utilization from fixed charging capacity than rule-based assignment. High demand conditions amplify the benefits of cognitive coordination, making smart corridors especially valuable in congested networks.

## Context
This work advances AI-driven simulation of complex transportation systems by integrating physical infrastructure with adaptive decision-making. It demonstrates how reinforcement learning can replace static control policies to capture emergent behaviors in platooning and charging coordination. Such simulations are critical for testing AI policies before real-world deployment, reducing costly physical trials.

## Implications
Practitioners can leverage these findings to design smarter freight corridors that balance efficiency, sustainability, and robustness. The approach offers a template for future AI research on coordinated multi-agent systems in logistics. Industry stakeholders can prioritize investments in V2X infrastructure where MARL yields the greatest operational gains.
