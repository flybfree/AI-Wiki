## Summary
This paper presents an educator-guided large language model pedagogical agent that delivers scaffolded feedback for conceptual database design within an entity--relationship diagram editor. The system architecture decouples hidden, artifact-grounded diagnosis from a four-stage workflow controlling the disclosure level of support, ensuring alignment with rubrics and instructional resources. Evaluation across 383 episodes showed that 71.1% of target-level changes incorporated diagnostic targets, while qualitative analysis highlighted benefits of staged disclosure in managing accuracy and fostering student agency.

## Key Takeaways
- The agent employs a four-stage progressive workflow ranging from concept checks to localized clarification, separating the hidden diagnostic process from student-facing output to control feedback granularity based on educator-authored rubrics and assignment requirements.
- Empirical results from 383 stateful episodes demonstrated high efficacy, with 71.1% of observed target-level changes fully or partially incorporating the agent's diagnostic goals, including significant uptake following early scaffolding stages that prompted guided application.
- Qualitative findings indicated that staged disclosure effectively withheld inaccurate details and supported selective error recovery, though user surveys noted preferences for delayed disclosure alongside concerns regarding indirectness and repetition in the feedback interactions.
