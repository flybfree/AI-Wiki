## Summary
The paper argues that AI agents, while increasing autonomous capabilities, undermine the human oversight mechanisms they rely on. It finds that current designs degrade both oversight effectiveness and the cognitive skills of overseers through skill atrophy. The authors propose integrating human needs into design to preserve critical judgment.

## Key Takeaways
- Human oversight is hindered by design choices that limit real‑time interaction with AI agents, causing slow decision making.
- Extended use of automation leads to cognitive skill decay in overseers, reducing their ability to evaluate outputs critically.
- The paper calls for treating human cognitive requirements as equally important as AI capabilities when designing agent systems.

## Context
AI research increasingly focuses on autonomous agents that operate without constant supervision. Yet the literature often overlooks how these systems affect the mental stamina of humans who must monitor them, creating a feedback loop of diminishing oversight quality.

## Implications
If developers ignore the human cognitive impact, AI agents will continue to erode the supervisory skills they depend on, risking system failures and loss of trust. Addressing this gap is essential for responsible deployment and long‑term reliability.
