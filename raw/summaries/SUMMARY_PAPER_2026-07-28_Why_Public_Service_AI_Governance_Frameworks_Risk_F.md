## Summary
The paper argues that general‑purpose AI (GPAI) threatens the core safety concepts used in public service governance, using policing as a case study to show how traditional mitigations become ineffective when outputs are unbounded and free‑text. It concludes that current frameworks risk failure because they were designed for narrow AI, not GPAI.

## Key Takeaways
- Accuracy cannot be reliably quantified over the unbounded range of GPAI outputs, which can produce varied text rather than fixed predictions.
- Bias assessment collapses when outputs are free‑text judgments instead of categorical results, making disaggregated bias analysis impossible.
- Explainability is replaced by superficial explanations, and accountability erodes as models optimize for persuasion rather than transparent decision‑making.

## Context
General‑purpose AI leverages large language models that can be steered with simple prompts to perform diverse tasks at low cost. This shift challenges the assumptions of existing public service AI governance frameworks that assume bounded, purpose‑built systems where safety metrics are well defined.

## Implications
For policymakers and practitioners, the paper warns against deploying GPAI in high‑stakes domains like policing until robust evidence shows it can meet safety standards. It calls for a clear taxonomy separating narrow from general‑purpose AI to guide appropriate governance measures.
