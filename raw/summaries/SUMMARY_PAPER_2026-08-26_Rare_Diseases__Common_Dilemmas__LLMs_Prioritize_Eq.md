## Summary
This paper creates a benchmark of 208 rare disease vignettes to test how large language models evaluate ethical trade‑offs in clinical decision making, discovering that all evaluated LLMs consistently prioritize justice and equal resource allocation over beneficence or patient autonomy. The study also reveals an authority‑framing effect: when decisions are framed as committee choices, models favor justice; only when clinicians or patients make the final call do they shift toward other principles.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs overwhelmingly prioritize equal resource distribution, ignoring differences in clinical severity or situational context.  
- The authority‑framing effect shows that model behavior changes with how decision authority is presented, favoring justice in committee contexts but beneficence and autonomy when clinicians or patients decide.  
- These results suggest that institutional pressures on rare disease resource use may be encoded into LLM‑driven decision support systems.

## Context
Evaluating AI ethics in high‑stakes medical scenarios is crucial as LLMs become part of clinical workflows; this work fills a gap by applying the same benchmark to rare diseases, where scarce resources and complex ethical dilemmas are common. The findings highlight how model training data can reflect broader societal inequities.

## Implications
These results warn that deploying LLM‑based decision support without scrutiny may amplify existing resource allocation biases in healthcare settings. Practitioners should design systems that explicitly balance justice with patient need to ensure equitable care.
