## Summary
The paper investigates how police officers in virtual reality simulations treat a Black male character, focusing on the deference of their language. It finds that most officers speak less deferentially to this character than to others, especially White, biracial, or multiracial female officers and when the character is flagged as a suspect. The average treatment effect can shift tone by two to several points on a 0‑10 scale.

## Key Takeaways
- Officers show reduced deference toward Black male characters, with larger differences for certain gendered groups, especially in suspect contexts.
- The marginal ATE from the character alone is substantial, adding tone changes beyond initial perception effects.
- LLM‑based ATE estimation using mixed‑effects models with iptw weighting offers a promising methodological framework.

## Context
