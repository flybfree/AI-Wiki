## Summary
This paper presents a case study examining the transition of a large embedded systems company toward an AI-first organizational model, focusing on the shift from AI assistants to autonomous agentic AI capable of planning and executing development tasks. Through a mixed-method analysis involving 40 stakeholders, the research identifies that agentic AI will significantly impact team structures, required competencies, organizational strategies, and developer roles. The study concludes by proposing a concrete roadmap for sustainable adoption, emphasizing federated AI team formation and human-in-the-loop practices tailored to the rigorous demands of embedded software engineering.

## Key Takeaways
- Agentic AI is expected to fundamentally reshape embedded software development by altering team structures, necessitating new competencies, and redefining developer roles as systems gain autonomy in planning, executing, and evaluating tasks.
- The transition poses unique challenges for embedded organizations due to strict requirements for quality, traceability, verification, and long-term maintainability, requiring specific strategies like human-in-the-loop practices to ensure compliance and reliability.
- The authors provide an actionable roadmap for becoming an AI-first organization, highlighting the importance of federated AI team formation and sustainable adoption frameworks that balance autonomy with the critical oversight needed in safety-critical domains.

## Context
As artificial intelligence evolves from passive coding assistants to autonomous agents capable of end-to-end software lifecycle management, the industry must address how these technologies integrate into domains with high barriers to entry and strict regulatory standards. This research addresses a critical gap by exploring AI adoption in embedded systems, where the consequences of errors are severe and traceability is paramount, offering insights distinct from general-purpose software engineering trends.
