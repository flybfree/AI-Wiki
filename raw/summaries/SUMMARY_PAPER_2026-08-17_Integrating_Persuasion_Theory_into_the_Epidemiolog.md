## Summary
The paper introduces a hybrid model that combines epidemiological dynamics with psychological signals to predict how health misinformation spreads on social media. The ELM‑SIRMMM framework outperforms standard SIR models by reducing prediction error and capturing realistic peaks in spread. On the FibVID dataset it improves accuracy, delays the peak, and raises prevalence.

## Key Takeaways
- The six‑compartment model (MS MI MR) better captures misinformation lifecycle dynamics than a simple SIR structure.
- Integrating ELM signals such as sentiment polarity, engagement metrics, and cognitive effort dynamically adjusts transmission rates, leading to higher predictive accuracy on FibVID.
- Minimal behavioural variation in the Monant dataset yields only modest gains, highlighting that functional realism requires meaningful psychological inputs.

## Context
This work bridges AI‑driven epidemiological simulations with behavioral science, offering a template for modeling complex information diffusion where individual cognition matters. It demonstrates how machine learning can incorporate psychometric variables to refine simulation outcomes beyond static parameters.

## Implications
For public health officials and platform operators, the model suggests that interventions must account for psychological drivers of sharing, not just structural spread patterns. Embedding such dynamic signals could improve early warning systems and targeted mitigation strategies in real‑world misinformation crises.
