## Summary
This study investigates how narrative transitions in films generate measurable EEG signatures that reflect comprehension. By analyzing continuous recordings from participants watching short movies, the authors show that transition‑related potentials (TRPs) can be extracted with a deep neural network and reproduce context‑dependent effects observed in traditional ERP paradigms.

## Key Takeaways
- TRPs exhibit an ERP‑like temporal pattern linked to processing of visual cuts, indicating significant information flow during narrative changes.  
- Group‑averaged continuous EEG enables automatic detection of these signatures across different films and subjects without manual annotation.  
- The extracted TRPs mirror the context‑dependent modulation seen in scrambled versions, revealing that narrative background shapes brain responses.

## Context
The paper contributes to AI research by demonstrating a machine‑learning approach that parses real‑time neural data from naturalistic stimuli, bridging the gap between lab‑based ERP studies and everyday viewing experiences. This aligns with broader efforts to integrate continuous EEG into affective computing and human‑computer interaction systems.

## Implications
For researchers, the method offers a semi‑automated tool for dissecting how viewers interpret narratives in real time, potentially informing content design and educational media.
