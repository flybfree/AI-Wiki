## Summary
This paper argues that Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is not a novel paradigm but an evolution of ideas from early information retrieval and question answering research. By tracing the intellectual lineage back to the 2000s, it shows that RAG’s core mechanisms have been studied long before large language models emerged.

## Key Takeaways
- The fundamental concepts of integrating retrieval with language generation, knowledge augmentation, answer verification, and iterative query refinement were already explored in IR and QA research decades ago.  
- Community fragmentation, shifting terminology, and a recency bias have obscured this continuity, leading to the mistaken belief that RAG is brand new.  
- The paper proposes viewing LLMs as an interface layer atop a long‑standing QA architecture rather than its origin point.

## Context
Understanding RAG’s historical roots helps demystify current AI practices and highlights that many solutions are rediscoveries of older work. This perspective encourages a more holistic view of how retrieval, generation, and validation have evolved together in the field.

## Implications
For researchers, industry, and practitioners, recognizing this lineage can reduce redundant effort and foster genuine cross‑community integration. It opens avenues to leverage underutilized prior research for designing smarter, more robust RAG systems.
