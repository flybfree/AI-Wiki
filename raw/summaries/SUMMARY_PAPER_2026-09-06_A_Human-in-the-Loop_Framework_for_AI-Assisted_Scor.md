## Summary
This paper introduces a human‑in‑the‑loop framework that uses large language models to score short written responses in a national assessment while preserving quality and reducing manual grading. The model’s scores are compared with human raters across several rubric dimensions, showing moderate to high agreement, and the workflow identifies cases where expert review is most valuable.

## Key Takeaways
- The AI‑generated scores align moderately to highly with human evaluations on most rubric criteria, indicating reliable assistance for large‑scale grading.  
- The decision flow prioritizes human review for ambiguous or borderline cases, allowing experts to focus their effort efficiently.  
- Successful integration of AI scoring requires a carefully designed oversight process that monitors model‑human alignment throughout the assessment cycle.

## Context
The rapid adoption of LLMs in education has prompted calls for scalable yet accurate grading solutions. This study contributes by grounding such technology in a real national test scenario, demonstrating how human oversight can mitigate risks while preserving assessment integrity.

## Implications
For educators and policymakers, this framework offers a practical pathway to integrate AI into high‑stakes assessments without compromising fairness or quality. Practitioners should adopt similar workflows that balance automation with expert judgment to future‑proof grading systems.
