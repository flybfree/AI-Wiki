## Summary
The paper proposes a unified decision framework that treats proactive service as a partially observable sequential decision process where agents infer opportunities from environmental and user signals rather than waiting for explicit instructions. It defines initiative, timing, content, delivery, and option values within a structured action cycle and evaluates methods across multiple domains.

## Key Takeaways
- Proactive service is defined by the ability to estimate state and need, gate interventions, construct actions, and adapt feedback in a single decision unit, emphasizing that waiting has an opportunity value and questions have a decision value.
- The framework normalizes evidence across streaming dialogue, screen, video, software engineering, and human‑agent collaboration using three‑axis descriptors, showing that offline classification alone cannot predict deployment benefit and long‑term memory is not essential for proactivity.
- Reliable proactive service requires calibrated incremental intervention value, verifiable authorization, recoverable execution, and counterfactual evidence to measure safety and policy value.

## Context
The work addresses a gap in AI research where most large language model agents remain reactive, awaiting user prompts. By formalizing initiative and decision processes, the paper contributes a common metric for measuring proactive behavior across heterogeneous resources, aligning with trends toward autonomous service delivery.

## Implications
For practitioners, this framework enables systematic design of agents that can act without constant prompting, improving efficiency and user experience in customer support, software assistance, and collaborative environments. For industry adoption, it provides measurable criteria to evaluate safety, cost, and value, guiding responsible deployment of proactive AI systems.
