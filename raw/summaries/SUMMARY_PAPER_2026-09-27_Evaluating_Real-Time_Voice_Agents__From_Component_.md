## Summary
This paper addresses the fragmented evaluation landscape of real-time voice agents by synthesizing insights from speech modeling, psycholinguistics, and agentic benchmarks into a unified taxonomy based on 38 primary sources. The authors argue that current metrics fail to capture production viability, proposing instead that architecture choices are deployment constraints rather than definitive solutions, evaluation must prioritize grounded state verification over component quality, and models must evolve beyond dyadic assumptions to handle multiparty interactions. To formalize these shifts, the study introduces the TRG reporting standard, which characterizes agent performance through timing, recovery capabilities, and state-verified outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- Architecture selection functions primarily as a deployment constraint rather than a settled technical verdict; recent evidence indicates that no fully self-hostable end-to-end system currently satisfies production requirements, yet chunked cascade architectures demonstrate that state-of-the-art duplex behavior can be achieved
