## Summary
This paper reviews 42 primary study families on large multimodal agents for intelligent transportation systems published between January 2023 and August 2026, mapping their architectures, empirical performance, and deployment readiness. It shows that while many models achieve high functional capability (C3) in semantic interpretation and integration, validation remains limited to early evidence levels (E2), and no study fully reconciles provenance, challenge handling, comparison, and outcome.

## Key Takeaways
- 19 families directly evaluate transportation semantics (P1) and multidimensional integration (P3), with only one covering both.  
- Evidence reconciliation (P2) is unresolved because no family demonstrates the complete provenance‑challenge‑handling‑comparison‑outcome chain.  
- Fourteen families reach C3, but 13 remain at E2; none achieve E4 validation.

## Context
The rapid rise of AI agents in transportation highlights a gap between research prototypes and real‑world accountability. This review maps the current evidence landscape to clarify which tasks are suitable for agent integration versus those requiring specialist oversight.

## Implications
Practitioners should adopt bounded orchestration rather than full replacement, using staged deployment protocols that pair LMAs with verifiable human or system authority. The provided evaluation protocol and repository enable transparent comparison of multimodal agents across ITS domains.
