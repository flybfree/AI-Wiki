## Summary
The paper introduces the Function--Evidence--Validation (FEV) framework to evaluate agentic bioinformatics workflows by focusing on function, evidence, and validation rather than architecture or final output alone. It maps 109 systems across multiple domains and finds that planning and tool-mediated execution have advanced faster than provenance, external validation, and prospective empirical testing.

## Key Takeaways
- The Function--Evidence--Validation (FEV) framework separates workflow operations, traceable support for actions, and use‑case specific validation as distinct units of analysis.  
- Across domains, planning and tool‑mediated execution have progressed more rapidly than replayability, provenance, robust scientific assessment, external validation, and prospective empirical testing.  
- This indicates that current agentic bioinformatics is judged by final answer correctness rather than workflow integrity.

## Context
The rapid integration of large language models into biological research has created a need for rigorous evaluation beyond benchmark scores. Existing reviews treat agents as black boxes, overlooking the importance of traceable, auditable workflows in scientific credibility.

## Implications
Practitioners and researchers must prioritize building transparent, reproducible pipelines that generate verifiable evidence before claiming results. This shift will guide responsible deployment of AI tools in genomics, drug discovery, and computational biology, ensuring accountability and trust.
