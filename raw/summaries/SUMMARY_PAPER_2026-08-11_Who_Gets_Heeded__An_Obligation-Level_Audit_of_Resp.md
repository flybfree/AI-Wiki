## Summary
The paper proposes an obligation-level responsiveness audit to assess whether EPA commenters' engagement translates into changes to specific regulatory duties. Using AI-assisted matching of comments to rule obligations, the authors find that commenter involvement is linked to modest revisions, direction of support does not predict outcomes, and organizational-majority engagement focuses on editorial refinement.

## Key Takeaways
- Engagement is associated with revision at a modest within-docket magnitude.
- Support versus opposition direction does not clearly differentiate outcomes, indicating a null result contrary to simple preference aggregation.
- Organizational-majority commenter engagement concentrates in editorial-refinement rather than substantive-modification at the cross-docket level.

## Context
This research advances AI applications in regulatory analysis by moving beyond coarse aggregate measures to granular obligation mapping. It demonstrates how machine learning can identify subtle patterns in rulemaking that traditional text-similarity methods miss, highlighting a need for more precise measurement of agency responsiveness.

## Implications
Practitioners and policymakers should recognize that commenter capacity varies across populations, affecting which obligations are contested. The findings suggest designing outreach programs to improve substantive engagement rather than merely formal access, potentially leading to more effective rule revisions.
