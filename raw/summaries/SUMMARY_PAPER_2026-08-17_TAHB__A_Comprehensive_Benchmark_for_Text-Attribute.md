## Summary
This paper introduces TAHB, the first public benchmark that combines hypergraph structures with raw textual attributes to evaluate text-aware hypergraph representation learning. The benchmark demonstrates that integrating language models enhances performance and that joint use of structural and textual information yields the best predictive results across diverse domains.

## Key Takeaways
- TAHB provides a comprehensive set of 10 real‑world datasets spanning e‑commerce, academia, movies, and politics, enabling systematic evaluation of hypergraph learning with text attributes.  
- Experiments confirm that LLM‑enhanced textual semantics improve hypergraph representation compared to using only structural information.  
- The optimal setting for LLM‑based prediction is when both structural and textual cues are jointly utilized.

## Context
Hypergraphs extend pairwise models by capturing higher‑order group relationships, yet few resources exist to test their integration with language models that understand semantic attributes from text. This work addresses the gap by offering a unified benchmark that aligns hypergraph learning objectives with natural language processing capabilities.

## Implications
For researchers, TAHB establishes a common evaluation framework that can guide future research on multimodal graph learning. For industry practitioners, the benchmark enables more effective deployment of LLMs in applications requiring group‑wise reasoning from textual data.
