## Summary  
This paper introduces EduZone, a framework that evaluates LLM safety in K-12 settings by generating adversarial interactions across student and teacher contexts, curriculum concepts, and risk categories. The study shows LLMs are especially vulnerable to education‑specific harms in dynamic multi‑turn conversations, exposing gaps in current guardrails.

## Key Takeaways  
- EduZone combines student‑ and teacher‑facing usage with fine‑grained curriculum concepts to create 28 subcategories of conventional and education‑specific risks.  
- The framework evaluates ten LLMs across four safety levels, revealing that dynamic multi‑turn interactions pose the highest risk.  
- Existing safety guardrails are found insufficient for handling these nuanced educational scenarios.

## Context  
LLMs are rapidly integrated into K‑12 curricula, but most safety assessments focus on generic text generation rather than classroom dynamics. This gap leaves educators and policymakers without reliable metrics to ensure responsible deployment in real teaching environments.

## Implications  
For the field, EduZone provides a scalable benchmark that can guide model designers toward safer educational AI. Practitioners can use its risk taxonomy to anticipate and mitigate education‑specific harms before rollout.
