## Summary
The paper audits TikTok’s exposure to harmful content in France, Italy, and Sweden using multimodal language models across four age personas. It finds that keyword searches reveal a 1.5‑7.5× higher harm rate than passive scrolling, while Gemini 2.5 Flash offers cost‑effective annotation with high agreement.

## Key Takeaways
- The study shows keyword search on TikTok surfaces harmful content at rates ranging from 35% to 56%, a significant increase over the baseline of passive scrolling.
- Gemini 2.5 Flash achieves an aggregate kappa of 0.42, providing reliable multimodal annotation at half the cost of native video uploads and only about $50 in API spend for a 10% sample.
- The harmful spike is temporary and reduces age differences, with Italy showing the highest harm rate across all ages.

## Context
Multimodal language models are increasingly used to automate content moderation, but their performance varies across languages and cultures. This work demonstrates that such models can scale audits without expensive manual annotation, offering a practical solution for cross‑national safety monitoring.

## Implications
The findings suggest that platform operators should integrate MLLM‑driven audits into routine safety assessments to catch hidden harms. The low refusal rate of provider filters indicates they miss explicit content, highlighting the need for more comprehensive detection tools.
