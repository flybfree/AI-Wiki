## Summary
This paper surveys how foundation models and learned game-world models are transforming AI applications throughout the entire game development lifecycle. The authors categorize existing research into six distinct roles—playing, modeling, designing, building, runtime adaptation, and testing—and analyze what structural inputs games provide, what AI systems produce, and which capabilities successfully transfer across different settings. Ultimately, the review highlights that while cross-role synergies exist, significant setting-specific barriers remain, making context validation a central challenge for future research.

## Key Takeaways
- The literature is systematically organized into six functional roles where AI interacts with games: playing and acting, modeling players and game dynamics, designing games, building and maintaining games, generating and adapting content at runtime, and testing and evaluating games.
- Strong cross-role connections exist, such as using gameplay trajectories to train world models, leveraging learned environments to provide training data for agents, and allowing play or testing feedback to directly inform iterative design and development cycles.
- Despite these synergies, critical barriers like proprietary control schemes, engine-specific interfaces, unique state representations, and diverse player contexts limit direct transferability, meaning evaluation remains highly standardized only for bounded gameplay while other roles lack robust validation frameworks.
