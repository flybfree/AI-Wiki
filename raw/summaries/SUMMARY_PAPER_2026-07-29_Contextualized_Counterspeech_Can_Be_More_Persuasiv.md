## Summary
The paper investigates how generating counterspeech that is tailored to the specific conversation and user can outperform generic, one‑size‑fits‑all responses in reducing toxicity online. Experiments show that personalized contextualized counterspeech yields higher perceived adequacy and persuasiveness while maintaining quality scores across ROUGE BLEU and BERTScore.

## Key Takeaways
- Personalization using conversational context and user history improves both perceived adequacy and persuasiveness of AI‑generated responses.
- Several additional contextual factors degrade human perception of the generated text despite technical quality metrics.
- Lightweight strategies that combine limited contextual signals are more effective than complex or overly tailored approaches.

## Context
AI systems increasingly generate synthetic dialogue to counter harmful speech, yet most solutions ignore user characteristics and conversation flow. This work highlights a gap between algorithmic performance metrics and real‑world acceptance, underscoring the need for human‑centered design in moderation tools.

## Implications
For practitioners, integrating lightweight personalization can lead to more effective moderation without sacrificing computational efficiency. The findings suggest that future AI systems should balance technical quality with contextual relevance to foster trustworthy interactions.
