## Summary
This paper investigates whether large reasoning models and humans share similar thinking effort when performing abductive reasoning tasks. It finds that both exhibit comparable reaction times and error patterns, suggesting alignment in cognitive load. The authors also show that enabling the model to explore multiple reasoning paths improves this alignment.

## Key Takeaways
- Human reaction times and LRM reasoning traces align across tasks, indicating shared mental effort despite different architectures.
- Both humans and models make similar logical errors, supporting the claim of parallel reasoning processes.
- Decoding methods that allow exploration of alternative reasoning branches increase the match between human and model thinking costs.

## Context
Abductive reasoning is a non‑inferential process where conclusions are drawn from observed facts without relying on formal rules, making it a unique test for genuine search behavior. This study contributes to debates about whether AI can replicate cognitive effort beyond statistical pattern matching.

## Implications
The findings suggest that future alignment research should focus on modeling the underlying search mechanisms rather than surface performance metrics. Practitioners may design systems that encourage exhaustive reasoning paths to better approximate human cognition.
