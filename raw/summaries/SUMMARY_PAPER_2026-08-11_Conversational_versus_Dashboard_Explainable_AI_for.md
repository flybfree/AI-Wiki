## Summary
This study compares a conversational XAI interface powered by large language models with a traditional dashboard for analyzing UAV intrusion detection outputs. The findings show that participants found the conversational system more useful, but it also lowered their sense of self‑reliance and increased the risk of over‑trust when the AI was wrong.

## Key Takeaways
- The conversational interface enabled easier access to and synthesis of multimodal cyber‑physical data, making information feel more accessible than a static dashboard.  
- Despite higher perceived usefulness, the natural‑language responses reduced participants’ tendency to verify evidence, suggesting inappropriate reliance on potentially incorrect AI advice.  
- This creates a trade‑off where improved usability may undermine cognitive forcing functions that encourage independent verification.

## Context
Explainable AI remains a critical challenge in high‑stakes domains such as autonomous UAV operations where transparency influences operator trust and decision making. Prior work has focused on static visualizations, but the rapid evolution of large language models opens new avenues for interactive XAI solutions.

## Implications
Designers must balance seamless interaction with mechanisms that prompt users to double‑check AI outputs, especially in safety‑critical applications like UAV intrusion detection. Ignoring this balance could lead to over‑reliance and reduced system robustness.
