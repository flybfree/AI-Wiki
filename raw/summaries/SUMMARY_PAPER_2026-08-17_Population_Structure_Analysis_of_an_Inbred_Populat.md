## Summary
This study examines the genetic population structure of an inbred group of 781 individuals from Norfolk Island, distinguishing descendants of English Mutineers from Polynesian ancestors. By applying quantitative shape phenotyping to stereo retinal photographs, the authors reconstruct three‑dimensional optic nerve head morphology and identify a set of hierarchical features that best separate the two groups. Stratified cross‑validation confirms high predictive accuracy for cluster assignment.

## Key Takeaways
- The multi‑scale stereo matching algorithm enables accurate reconstruction of 3D ONH shape from retinal images, providing a phenotype that is under genetic control.
- Deep learning decomposes these shapes into hierarchical features, and discriminant analysis selects the most informative ones to differentiate English versus Polynesian ancestry.
- Hierarchical clustering (k = 2–7) reveals distinct membership fractions, supporting heritability and linkage analyses of founder effects.

## Context
The integration of stereoscopic imaging with deep neural networks for phenotype extraction exemplifies a shift toward multimodal AI that combines visual data with genetic information. This approach moves beyond simple classification to uncover subtle morphological patterns linked to ancestry, illustrating how AI can enhance population genetics research.

For clinicians and researchers, this method offers a non‑invasive way to assess genetic risk associated with optic nerve head morphology, potentially informing early glaucoma screening.
