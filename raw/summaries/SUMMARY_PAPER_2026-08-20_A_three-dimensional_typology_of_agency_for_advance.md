## Summary
The paper introduces a three‑dimensional typology that categorizes agency in advanced AI systems along the dimensions of moral versus legal, individual versus collective, and human versus non‑human locus. It identifies eight possible instantiations classified as conventional, contested or controversial. The typology thus creates a conceptual map for evaluating AI agency in diverse contexts.

## Key Takeaways
- This separation prevents conflating moral blame with legal accountability, which is crucial as AI systems increasingly perform tasks that blur responsibility lines.
- Collective agency acknowledges that advanced AI may operate within networks of other agents, complicating the attribution of actions to a single entity.
- By allowing non‑human locus, the model respects the reality that AI can act as an autonomous decision‑maker rather than merely a tool.

## Context
Current AI research often conflates moral responsibility with technical capability, overlooking the legal and collective dimensions of agency. This typology provides a structured lens to dissect these complexities. The paper situates this typology within ongoing debates about AI alignment and responsibility frameworks.

## Implications
Industry practitioners can use the typology to design governance structures that differentiate between moral oversight and legal compliance, reducing ambiguity in liability disputes. For policymakers, it clarifies where liability may rest when AI pursues instrumental goals without human intent.
