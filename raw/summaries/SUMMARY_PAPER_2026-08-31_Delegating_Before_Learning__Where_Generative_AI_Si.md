## Summary
This study explores how twelve students use generative AI to draft professional emails, focusing on the highest level of involvement observed in email communication with instructors and administrators. The research reveals that AI can both generate messages entirely or assist in editing, while students evaluate outputs for AI detectability and personal voice. By comparing these AI‑mediated processes with unaided writing and a classic writing model, three distinct differences emerge.

## Key Takeaways
- The learning loop that builds writing skill is removed when AI writes the message outright, leaving students without practice in drafting professional language.  
- The resulting messages are no longer tailored to their specific recipient, reducing personalization and potentially undermining relational dynamics.  
- Confidence in successful communication shifts from the writer’s ability to the system’s performance, raising concerns about authenticity and trust.

## Context
Generative AI is rapidly entering professional workflows, prompting questions about its role in education and workplace communication. This paper situates student use of AI within a broader trend where technology mediates tasks that traditionally required human skill development and relational nuance. Understanding these dynamics helps contextualize how AI reshapes learning outcomes and social interactions.

## Implications
For educators, the findings suggest curricula must address both technical proficiency and ethical responsibility in AI‑assisted communication. For industry practitioners, the risks of diminished authenticity may affect trust and collaboration, necessitating policy frameworks that balance efficiency with human accountability.
