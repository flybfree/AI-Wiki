## Summary  
The paper introduces an outcome‑informed spatial pathology framework that combines AI‑generated recurrence risk heatmaps from H&E stained slides with spatial proteomics to uncover molecular niches in triple‑negative breast cancer. By integrating these modalities the study uncovers intratumoral heterogeneity beyond tissue compartment identity and demonstrates that localized molecular states drive recurrence.

## Key Takeaways  
- High scoring patches correlate with cell cycle and genome maintenance programs while low scores align with immune activation, indicating distinct molecular states behind recurrence risk.  
- AI heatmaps can be used to physically isolate tumor regions for spatial proteomics, revealing mitotic enrichment in high‑risk areas versus immune gene expression in low‑risk areas.  
- A 13‑protein composite derived from these contrasts predicts poorer recurrence‑free survival and improves out‑of‑bag C‑index from 0.679 to 0.739.

## Context  
Current AI models for cancer prognosis rely on global image features, limiting insight into localized biology. This work shows that outcome‑trained heatmaps can serve as experimental guides to interrogate spatially resolved molecular landscapes. The integration of deep learning with wet‑lab spatial assays represents a growing trend in precision oncology research.

## Implications  
This approach bridges morphology and molecular profiling, offering a new multiscale biomarker strategy that could guide personalized therapy in TNBC. It also provides a template for integrating AI predictions with spatial assays across cancer types.
