## Summary
This paper provides a narrative review of AI‑driven generative models for sound effect synthesis, analyzing how input modalities such as text, visual, audio, and multimodal data influence the quality, controllability, and contextual relevance of generated audio. The authors examined 30 peer‑reviewed studies from Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, and ACM Digital Library over five years and found that state‑of‑the‑art models now produce high‑fidelity, semantically aligned sound effects with improved temporal coherence.

## Key Takeaways
- The quality of generated sounds improves significantly when the model receives multimodal inputs, enabling more accurate mapping between visual or textual cues and audio semantics.  
- Despite advances, temporal synchronization remains a challenge for complex multi‑event scenarios, leading to occasional misaligned or disjointed sound sequences.  
- Objective evaluation metrics often diverge from human perception, highlighting a gap between quantitative performance scores and subjective listening quality.

## Context
The rapid growth of generative AI has transformed many creative domains, yet few studies have systematically compared how different input modalities affect audio synthesis outcomes. This review fills that gap by synthesizing recent research to clarify the current state of AI‑based sound generation and its practical limitations.

## Implications
For sound designers and interactive media developers, these findings suggest a need for multimodal integration strategies to achieve context‑aware effects while addressing synchronization issues. The insights also point toward more robust evaluation frameworks that align with human perception, guiding future research and industry adoption.
