## Summary
This paper explores how caregivers in the United States, Mexico, and Chile perceive four types of care robots: those that deliver supplies, help patients into bed, monitor vital signs, and assist with mobility. The mixed‑methods study shows that participants generally view robots positively when they handle logistical or physically demanding tasks but are less enthusiastic about those requiring close interpersonal contact. Qualitative insights reveal a shared set of ethical concerns while highlighting country‑specific priorities.

## Key Takeaways
- Caregivers across the three countries rate robots that perform logistical or physical duties as more acceptable than those that demand intensive personal interaction.
- Ethical concerns such as dependability, need for human oversight and potential job displacement are consistently mentioned but interpreted differently by each nation’s participants.
- The study demonstrates that acceptance is not uniform; it depends on task type and cultural context.

## Context
Care robot deployment in healthcare raises questions about user acceptance and ethical responsibility. Understanding caregiver attitudes helps bridge the gap between technical capability and real‑world implementation, informing design choices that align with human values.

## Implications
For designers, this research suggests tailoring robots to specific functional domains rather than assuming universal acceptance. Practitioners should anticipate varied stakeholder concerns and incorporate safeguards for autonomy and oversight to ensure responsible integration of care robotics.
