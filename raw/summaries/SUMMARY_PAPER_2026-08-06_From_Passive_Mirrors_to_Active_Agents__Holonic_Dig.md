## Summary
This paper introduces a framework called HDT‑Nets that enables real‑time physical AI inference by replacing passive mirrors with active agents. The authors demonstrate how hierarchical digital twins, governed by causal Markov blankets and category theory, can reason autonomously while coordinating across wireless networks to maintain shared spatiotemporal context.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework replaces passive mirroring with active reasoning, allowing each holonic agent to decide which beliefs to transmit based on their cognitive value.  
- Category‑theoretic transmission preserves semantic structure across heterogeneous agents that use incompatible representations.  
- Integrated information theory quantifies when collective intelligence surpasses independent operation and tracks how network intelligence evolves through coordinated learning.

## Context
Current AI systems struggle to operate reliably in physical environments because they lack robust world models for long‑horizon planning under uncertainty. Wireless networks offer a potential solution by providing pervasive sensing and communication, yet existing architectures prioritize throughput over real‑time coordination. This work addresses that gap with a novel networked digital twin approach.

## Implications
For robotics and autonomous vehicles, HDT‑Nets could enable safer, more adaptive behavior by allowing agents to share only the most informative beliefs. The method also offers a theoretical bridge between information theory and collective learning, potentially reshaping how distributed AI systems are designed and deployed.
