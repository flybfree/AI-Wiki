## Summary
This paper evaluates the compliance of 20 prominent Large Language Models (LLMs) against China's specific AI-Generated Content (AIGC) regulations to address growing content risks in a complex linguistic context. The study introduces a novel framework using over 2,300 questions across six dimensions and finds that international models exhibit high levels of compliance, particularly in areas related to ideological alignment.

## Key Takeaways
- The research addresses a significant gap in existing literature by acknowledging that previous studies often underestimated the complexity of Chinese language content compliance, which frequently focuses on English contexts.
- The authors developed a novel framework to assess 20 notable LLMs using 2,303 questions across six distinct dimensions, including a specific set of 203 self-constructed constitutional questions designed to test deep ideological alignment.
- The findings reveal that international models exhibit high levels of compliance even when subjected to standard Chinese questions, and the study establishes a unified regulatory benchmark for evaluating both domestic and foreign LLMs under legally grounded requirements.

## Context
As AI adoption accelerates globally, ensuring content safety and adherence to regional legal frameworks has become a critical priority for developers and policymakers alike. This paper is significant because it moves beyond generic safety metrics by providing a specific, evidence-based evaluation of how international models interact with China's unique regulatory landscape.

## Implications
For the AI industry, this research provides a clear benchmark to measure and improve model alignment with diverse regional regulations, helping to mitigate legal risks for global deployment. It also highlights the importance of understanding ideological nuances in content moderation, offering a roadmap for creating more culturally and legally aware AI systems.
