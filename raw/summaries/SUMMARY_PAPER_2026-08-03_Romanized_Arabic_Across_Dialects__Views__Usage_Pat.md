## Summary
Arabizi is the Latin script representation of Arabic, and this paper investigates how its prevalence varies across five North African dialects. By surveying speakers and analyzing their writing practices, the authors reveal systematic intra‑dialectal regularities while highlighting inter‑dialectal differences in Arabizi usage.

## Key Takeaways
- Survey participants consistently produce Arabizi that follows internal dialectal phonotactic patterns, indicating a stable mapping from spoken forms to Latin letters within each community.
- The Arabic script version of the same sentence often diverges across dialects, showing that transliteration norms are not uniform but reflect local linguistic identities.
- A manually curated parallel corpus demonstrates that Arabic speakers can reliably detect Arabizi at the sentence level, confirming its distinct visual identity.

## Context
Understanding Arabizi’s sociolinguistic patterns is crucial for AI models that process multilingual text, as they often assume a single script representation. This study contributes to the field by providing dialect‑specific resources and insights into user perception of script substitution.

## Implications
The findings guide developers in building Arabic NLP systems that respect regional writing conventions, reducing errors caused by mismatched transliterations. Practitioners can leverage these resources to improve cross‑dialect text analysis and enhance user experience in multilingual applications.
