## Summary
This paper introduces NariQ, a trellis-coded post-training quantization method designed specifically for long-horizon agentic tasks, addressing the critical gap between quantization methods calibrated on short web text and the demands of multi-step tool-calling at 100K+ token contexts. The method demonstrates that Qwen3.8-27B quantized to 3.0 and 2.5 bits per weight can match full-precision BF16 performance on long-horizon agentic benchmarks such as DeepSWE and Terminal Bench, a result the authors claim is unprecedented for sub-3-bit quantization.

## Key Takeaways
- Existing low-bit quantization methods (at or below 3 bits per weight) for Qwen3.8-27B match BF16 on shorter tasks like AIME but solve at most 72% as many tasks as BF16 on DeepSWE, a long-horizon agentic benchmark, revealing a fundamental calibration mismatch that makes naive quantization costly for agentic deployment.
- NariQ uses trellis-coded quantization calibrated on tokens generated by the BF16 model during actual agent sessions rather than short web text, directly aligning the quantization objective with the distribution of tokens encountered during multi-step tool-calling workflows.
- At 3.0 and 2.5 bits per weight, NariQ maintains parity with BF16 on DeepSWE, Terminal Bench, AIME, and RealWorldQA, while the 2.0-bit checkpoint trails this performance, establishing a practical threshold for lossless agentic quantization. The checkpoints are released as GGUF files with inference support through a custom llama.cpp fork.

## Context
The broader AI landscape is shifting from single-turn generation toward long-horizon agentic workflows where models orchestrate dozens or hundreds of tool calls across extended contexts. Quantization has long been the go-to strategy for deploying large models on constrained hardware, but the community has largely evaluated quantized models on perplexity and short-task benchmarks. This paper highlights that such evaluations systematically underestimate the degradation quantization causes in agentic settings, where errors compound across sequential decisions. NariQ represents a methodological correction by grounding calibration in the actual token distributions agents encounter during deployment.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying large language models on edge devices or cost-constrained infrastructure, NariQ demonstrates that sub-3-bit quantization no longer requires sacrificing agentic capability, potentially enabling on-device or low-cost serving of 27B-class models for software engineering and terminal-based tasks. For the research community, the finding that calibration data source matters more than bit-width alone suggests that future quantization work must adopt agentic calibration corpora rather than relying on static text datasets. The open release of GGUF checkpoints and a modified llama.cpp fork lowers the barrier for reproducible evaluation and practical adoption in production agentic pipelines.
