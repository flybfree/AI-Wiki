## Summary
The paper introduces a new paradigm for autonomous machine learning engineering that shifts focus from solution-centric search to an information-driven approach. Iris demonstrates this paradigm by achieving the highest any-medal rate on MLE-Bench within a 12‑hour budget, outperforming prior tree, graph, and chain based agents.

## Key Takeaways
- Iris uses epistemic actions to probe decision-critical unknowns without altering retained solutions.
- Task knowledge is built from synthesized observations into revisable claims with explicit scope and status.
- Cross-domain generalization across four tasks shows the system’s adaptability under limited budget.

## Context
Current LLM agents rely on fixed search structures that limit how information is gathered and integrated. The information paradigm offers a more flexible framework for evolving understanding in long‑horizon tasks.

## Implications
This approach can reduce wasted effort by focusing on uncertain variables rather than exhaustive solution exploration. Industries adopting autonomous ML engineering will benefit from higher efficiency and better generalization across diverse domains.
