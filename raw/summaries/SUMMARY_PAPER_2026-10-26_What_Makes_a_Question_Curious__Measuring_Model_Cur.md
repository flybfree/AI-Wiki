## Summary
This paper introduces a theory-grounded taxonomy for identifying "model curiosity" in LLM interactions, distinguishing curiosity-oriented questions from generic model questioning. The authors analyze four conversation datasets and find that curiosity-oriented questions constitute only 17–28% of all questions posed by models, yet they are associated with more exploratory and epistemically rich user responses. A supplementary human study confirms that model curiosity can encourage deeper exploration, particularly in advice, creative, and research contexts.

## Key Takeaways
- The authors define "model curiosity" as epistemic question-asking that surfaces knowledge gaps, assumptions, and alternatives during human-LLM interactions, and they develop a taxonomy grounded in curiosity theory to separate these from routine or generic model questions. This distinction is critical because not all questions a model asks serve the same epistemic function.
- Across four conversation datasets, curiosity-oriented questions remain rare, comprising only 17–28% of all questions generated by models. However, when such questions do appear, they are associated with more exploratory and epistemically rich subsequent user responses, though the specific patterns vary across datasets, suggesting that context and domain significantly shape how curiosity manifests.
- A dedicated human study evaluates the practical usefulness of model curiosity and finds that it can encourage exploration, especially in advice-giving, creative, and research-oriented contexts. This suggests that selective, curiosity-driven questioning by LLMs can meaningfully enhance the quality of human-AI collaboration rather than merely producing more questions.

## Context
As LLMs increasingly serve as conversational partners in everyday communication, the field has largely focused on how well models answer questions rather than how well they ask them. This paper addresses a gap in the literature by formalizing what constitutes a genuinely curious question versus a generic one, drawing on psychological and epistemic theories of curiosity. It matters because the ability to ask the right question is as important as providing an answer for productive human-AI collaboration.

## Implications
For practitioners building LLM-based assistants, these findings suggest that models should be designed to ask more selectively and strategically, prioritizing curiosity-oriented questions that surface assumptions and alternatives rather than flooding conversations with generic follow-ups. For the broader AI research community, the taxonomy provides a measurable framework for evaluating and improving model behavior in interactive settings, potentially guiding the development of LLMs that support deeper exploration and more meaningful human-AI collaboration in domains like education, creative work, and research assistance.
