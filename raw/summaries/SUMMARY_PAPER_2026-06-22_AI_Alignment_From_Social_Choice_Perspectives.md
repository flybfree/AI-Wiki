## Summary
The paper surveys recent AI alignment research that uses human feedback to shape language models, framing the aggregation of conflicting judgments as a social choice problem. It identifies how standard methods can produce undesirable outcomes and proposes principled approaches grounded in social choice theory.

## Key Takeaways
- Human feedback aggregates into an objective that may prioritize majority preferences over minority but valid values.
- The paper shows that disagreement among judges can lead to systematic bias if the aggregation rule is not designed with fairness constraints.
- Social choice lenses reveal design spaces where explicit trade‑offs between efficiency and equity are made transparent.

## Context
In AI alignment, aligning models with human intent often relies on aggregating subjective judgments. Traditional methods treat feedback as a black box, obscuring how disagreements are resolved. This work brings the social choice perspective to the forefront, offering clearer diagnostic tools for designers. These insights also encourage interdisciplinary collaboration between social scientists and AI engineers.

## Implications
Practitioners can adopt these insights to create more transparent and accountable alignment pipelines. By aligning aggregation with ethical principles, companies can reduce unintended model biases and improve public trust in AI systems. Such alignment will be essential as regulatory frameworks evolve to govern automated decision‑making.
