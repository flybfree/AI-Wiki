## Summary  
The paper catalogs antipatterns in AI‑assisted qualitative data analysis (QDA) that SE researchers often encounter, showing how seemingly beneficial practices can erode analytical rigor and produce low‑quality research. It groups these issues into three tiers — Dangerous Drivers, Operational Missteps, and Analytical Failures — to highlight the escalating impact of each risk.

## Key Takeaways  
- Uncritical AI use can compromise analytical rigor and validity by replacing human judgment with algorithmic shortcuts that ignore nuance.  
- The antipatterns are systematically classified into Dangerous Drivers (high‑risk assumptions), Operational Missteps (practical implementation errors), and Analytical Failures (consequences on results).  
- This catalog offers strategic guidance to SE researchers, helping them avoid common temptations that lead to low‑quality research output.

## Context  
AI tools are rapidly being integrated into qualitative research workflows across many disciplines, promising efficiency but also raising methodological concerns. In software engineering, where human‑centered analysis is crucial for understanding user experiences, the unchecked adoption of AI can blur the line between insight and automation. This paper situates those concerns within the broader AI landscape to inform responsible practice.

## Implications  
For SE researchers, recognizing these antipatterns will improve study credibility and encourage peer review to focus on methodological soundness rather than novelty alone. Practitioners in industry may adopt similar safeguards when using AI for user research, ensuring that AI augments rather than replaces human expertise.
