## Summary  
The paper introduces 402Pilot, a decision layer that lets autonomous agents choose among micropayment providers when their wallet is limited. It proposes PA‑DCT, a policy that adapts purchases and learns from post‑payment feedback. Experiments on 823 tasks show PA‑DCT spends only 39 to 43 percent of the wallet while keeping service quality competitive.

## Key Takeaways  
- PA‑DCT maintains competitive service quality while spending only 39 to 43 percent of the wallet and reallocates spending as market conditions change.  
- It attains the best non‑oracle PA‑gap/T under price shock and the best mean and worst‑case ranks across nine scenario‑metric combinations.  
- The results demonstrate that programmable payment must be complemented by buyer‑side decision‑making capable of learning service value.

## Context  
Autonomous agents often struggle to select which micropayment services to use when funds are scarce, a problem not resolved by existing programmable protocols. This work tackles the buyer‑side decision layer, enabling agents to learn and adapt their purchasing strategies in real time.

## Implications  
The findings suggest that future payment systems should integrate adaptive decision layers rather than relying solely on static rules. Practitioners can leverage such policies to improve ROI and reduce waste, fostering more efficient autonomous service ecosystems.
