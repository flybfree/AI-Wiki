## Summary
This paper introduces Generative Adaptive Behavioral Layer for Epidemics (GABLE), a framework that uses large language models to generate adaptive human behavior within mechanistic epidemic simulations. Applied to COVID‑19 in France, GABLE produced contact matrices that improved short‑term forecasts and enabled prospective policy evaluation beyond real‑world mobility data.

## Key Takeaways
- GABLE leverages LLMs to infer context‑dependent behavioral responses and translates them into age‑structured contact matrices for mechanistic epidemic models.  
- The generated matrices outperformed mobility‑driven matrices, especially at longer forecasting horizons, providing more accurate predictions of population mixing dynamics.  
- By simulating alternative policy timings and compositions before implementation, GABLE enables prospective evaluation of interventions within the same adaptive framework.

## Context
The integration of AI into epidemiological modeling addresses a longstanding limitation: static assumptions about human behavior that ignore real‑time adaptation to epidemic conditions. This work demonstrates how generative models can replace rigid behavioral parameters with flexible, context‑aware representations, aligning computational fluidity with scientific rigor in public health planning.

## Implications
For policymakers and health agencies, GABLE offers a tool to test interventions under plausible adaptive scenarios without waiting for real data, accelerating decision‑making. Practitioners can embed such AI‑driven behavioral layers into existing epidemiological pipelines, enhancing both forecasting precision and strategic foresight.
