## Summary
This paper introduces HARP, a platform that lets researchers run live human‑AI interaction studies with configurable AI agents and record detailed behavioral data. The authors demonstrate its use in a study showing how technical specificity and response length influence users’ memory of LLM output.

## Key Takeaways
- Researchers can control agent prompts, model parameters, and response characteristics while participants interact with the AI, enabling systematic manipulation of experimental conditions.
- HARP records fine‑grained metrics such as prompt composition time, response latency, deletions, and keystroke pauses to capture users’ thought processes in real time.
- The platform supports optional multimodal data collection (voice, facial expression, gesture) when ethically and legally permissible, enriching the dataset with affective information.

## Context
Current HCI research relies on static prototypes or post‑hoc analysis of transcripts, which miss dynamic user behavior during live AI conversations. This gap limits understanding of how design choices affect real‑world usability. HARP addresses this by providing a controlled environment for testing LLM interactions.

## Implications
HARP will help designers optimize LLM interfaces to improve retention and comprehension, reducing cognitive load for users. For industry practitioners, the platform offers a reproducible way to evaluate AI behavior across diverse scenarios, accelerating product development cycles.
