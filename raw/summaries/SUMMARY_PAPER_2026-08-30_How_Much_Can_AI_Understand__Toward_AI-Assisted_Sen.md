## Summary
This paper proposes an AI-Assisted Sensemaking Model for collaborative discussions that prioritizes the social history of a group, arguing that effective understanding requires integrating participants’ norms and relationships with the content itself. The authors demonstrate through two studies on Wikipedia editors that AI tools which ignore this context often misinterpret or fail to convey meaning, while their model can support sensemaking at varying levels of interpretive effort.

## Key Takeaways
- The model distinguishes between low‑level argument extraction and high‑level interpretation, where the latter reduces user burden but increases dependence on the system’s judgment.  
- AI that overlooks shared history risks misrepresenting discussions by treating them as isolated texts rather than embedded social events.  
- Making an intelligible system requires both contextual awareness and safeguards to prevent overreliance or bias amplification.

## Context
Current AI tools for collaborative dialogue focus on surface content, neglecting the relational dynamics that shape meaning in groups with established histories. This omission limits their utility in real‑world settings where social cues are critical, highlighting a gap between technical capability and practical sensemaking needs.

## Implications
For practitioners, integrating group history into AI systems can improve accuracy and user trust while managing dependency risks. The field must therefore develop frameworks that balance interpretive support with human oversight to ensure responsible deployment in collaborative environments.
