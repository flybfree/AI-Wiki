## Summary
The paper introduces a benchmark that combines the 45‑record Golden UML Modelset and an eight‑record reference archive to evaluate automated domain modeling approaches across varying levels of complexity. It provides a unified metric for comparing rule‑based and LLM‑driven methods on natural language inputs, demonstrating that more complex models can be distinguished by this standardized test.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark integrates the 45‑record Golden UML Modelset and the eight‑record reference archive, allowing evaluation of automated domain modeling approaches at different scales.  
- It supplies a single metric that objectively compares generated models to ground truth, enabling fair assessment between heuristic rule‑based and LLM‑driven strategies.  
- Results demonstrate that more complex models can be distinguished by the benchmark, underscoring the importance of standardized evaluation for research progress.

## Context
The rapid growth of automated domain modeling relies on diverse datasets, yet without common benchmarks progress is uneven. This work supplies a research artifact aligned with FAIR4RS to promote reproducibility and fair comparison across studies.

## Implications
For practitioners, the benchmark offers a ready‑to‑use test suite that can guide model selection and performance reporting. For researchers, it encourages reuse of the data and metric, fostering a more transparent landscape for future work.
