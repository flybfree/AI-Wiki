## Summary
The paper proposes a framework that links Flower and Hayes' cognitive process theory of writing to proactive writing support in large language models, aiming to infer user needs without explicit prompts. Through experiments with AToM CoWriter, the authors show that this approach improves expressiveness, idea exploration, and engagement.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework maps six cognitive processes to 14 support types, each tied to specific interaction behaviors.
- Proactive inference from writing interactions can enhance user expression and idea generation compared with manual prompting.
- Cognitive process inference leads to higher engagement with suggested assistance. The approach bridges theory and practice by using observable writing behavior to select appropriate support.

## Context
Current AI writing assistants rely on explicit prompts that hinder spontaneous creativity. This study demonstrates a more intuitive alternative that respects the natural flow of creative writing, offering a way to align system behavior with human cognitive processes.

## Implications
For researchers, this suggests cognitive models can guide support selection in real-time systems. For practitioners, it opens a path to less intrusive AI co-writing tools that adapt to user intent.
