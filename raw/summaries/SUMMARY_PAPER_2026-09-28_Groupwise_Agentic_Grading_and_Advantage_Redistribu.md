## Summary
The paper introduces GAGAR, a framework for quality-aware credit redistribution in code agent reinforcement learning that addresses the limitations of Group Relative Policy Optimization by incorporating groupwise agentic grading to differentiate between passing trajectories based on implementation quality. By employing an SFT-trained agentic grader to rank candidates within dynamic groups and applying a sum-preserving advantage rescaling mechanism, GAGAR shifts credit toward cleaner, more targeted solutions while maintaining stable training dynamics
