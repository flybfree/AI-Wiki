## Summary
The paper proposes a Company World Model to replace departmental structures in AI‑native biotech firms, testing whether AI agents should mimic human org charts or operate around a persistent asset‑to‑value state representation. Using a dry‑lab benchmark of 45 retrospective decision cases, the value‑conversion architecture outperformed traditional baselines and was preferred by judges focused on external success metrics.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark shows that an AI‑native value‑conversion architecture, built from Live Asset Value Record updated by Deal, Approval, Revenue, and Investment Arbiter loops, achieved the highest automatic score under a success function of BD approval and revenue discipline. - Stronger human org‑mimic baselines remained competitive in blind judging, indicating departments may still serve governance roles. - Stress tests revealed that only specific rooms—Revenue Room, Deal Room, Approval Room—carry useful work for the objective.

## Context
AI research increasingly explores organizational abstractions beyond static departmental models to capture dynamic value flows in scientific and commercial pipelines. This study contributes a systematic dry‑lab framework that isolates whether AI agents benefit from shared predictive state representations rather than human‑centric structures, offering a testbed for future work on autonomous biotech firms.

## Implications
Practitioners should consider building asset‑to‑value models as core operating primitives while using departmental views as supplementary governance tools. The findings suggest that future AI‑driven drug development may rely less on static org charts and more on continuously updated value representations to align scientific, regulatory, and commercial outcomes.
