## Summary
The paper investigates how large language models construct fictional worlds by measuring the use of five narrative space dimensions across AI‑generated stories and human texts. It finds that LLMs overproduce perceived space while humans favor action space, a pattern stable across time. The analysis spans English and German narratives using multiple models.

## Key Takeaways
- Human-authored fiction predominantly uses “action” space, which centers on embodied character‑environment interaction.
- LLMs systematically generate more “perceived” space, focusing on atmosphere and affect rather than physical action.
- This divergence is consistent across narrative time and varies with model architecture and language.

## Context
Understanding the spatial dimensions of storyworld construction helps researchers compare human creativity with machine generation. The study contributes to AI literature by operationalizing setting as a measurable variable and providing comparative metrics for model evaluation.

## Implications
For creators, this insight suggests that AI‑generated narratives may feel more atmospheric than interactive, potentially limiting immersive storytelling experiences. Practitioners should consider these spatial biases when designing prompts or evaluating AI outputs in creative applications.
