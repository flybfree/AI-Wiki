## Summary
The paper proposes a multilingual framework for analyzing social power in naturalistic dialogues using movie screenplays as data, and it evaluates six large language models on cross-cultural reasoning tasks. The analysis shows agreement on demographic attributes but disagreement on interpretive aspects of power such as asymmetry and intention alignment.

## Key Takeaways
- The constructed corpus includes 15,836 annotated instances from French and Egyptian Arabic scenes, demonstrating that the framework can scale to multilingual movie content.
- Human and model agreement is high for observable demographic cues but low for socially interpretive dimensions like power asymmetry and theory‑of‑mind reasoning.
- The framework integrates a social science schema with native speaker annotation pipelines, offering an extensible tool for cross‑cultural dialogue analysis.

## Context
Current AI research often treats language models as neutral predictors of human behavior, ignoring the cultural and relational nuances that shape social dynamics. This work bridges that gap by grounding model evaluation in a theory‑driven schema and real‑world multilingual data, highlighting the limits of existing benchmarks.

## Implications
For practitioners developing culturally aware AI, this framework provides a concrete method to test how models handle power asymmetries across languages. It also signals a need for richer datasets that capture relational depth beyond surface demographics, guiding future research in socially responsible machine learning.
