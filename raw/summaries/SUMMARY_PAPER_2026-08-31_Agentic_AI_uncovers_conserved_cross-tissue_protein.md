## Summary
This study introduces an LLM‑agent framework that enables large‑scale, evidence‑grounded comparison of protein co‑abundance networks across all possible pairs of human tissues and fluids. Applied to 820 pairwise combinations involving 41 tissues, the analysis uncovered 1,833 conserved clusters, revealing previously inaccessible relationships between seemingly unrelated organs.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework identified 1,833 conserved co‑abundance clusters across 406 tissue pairs, far exceeding the limited number of pre‑selected biological pairings.  
- Colon, synovial fluid, blood, cerebrospinal fluid, and bone marrow were the most broadly connected tissues, while bone marrow dominated the cluster‑richest pairs.  
- Non‑obvious relationships emerged, such as skin‑bone marrow clusters surpassing adjacent bone‑bone marrow pairs, and colon‑breast clusters involving extracellular‑matrix remodeling, lipid metabolism, and immune modulation.

## Context
In AI research, large language models are increasingly used to synthesize heterogeneous biomedical knowledge into actionable insights. This work demonstrates how an LLM can act as an agent that curates cross‑tissue protein data, generating hypotheses that single‑dataset analyses cannot capture due to limited scope and bias.

## Implications
These findings provide a global, comparable landscape of conserved protein co‑abundance that can guide mechanistic investigations and therapeutic target selection. Clinicians and drug developers may leverage the identified clusters to design tissue‑specific interventions, accelerating discovery across multiple organ systems.
