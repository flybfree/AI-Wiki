## Summary
This paper tests how large language models can generate product ideas for college students priced under $50. Using three idea pools—human‑created, GPT‑4 zero‑shot generated, and GPT‑4 few‑shot generated—the study measures purchase intent, novelty, and similarity. AI ideas outperform human ones on average purchase intent, especially with few‑shot prompting, though they are seen as less novel.

## Key Takeaways
- AI‑generated ideas achieve a higher average purchase intent than human ideas, with few‑shot prompting yielding the best results.
- The same AI ideas show lower perceived novelty and higher pairwise similarity to other ideas, suggesting a narrower solution space.
- When ranking only the top 10 % of ideas, AI concepts are seven times more likely to appear in that elite group than human ones.

## Context
The rapid rise of large language models has sparked interest in their creative potential across various domains. This study contributes by applying LLMs directly to product ideation, a critical step for innovation pipelines where speed and diversity matter. It highlights the tension between quantitative performance metrics and qualitative judgments like novelty.

## Implications
For product development teams, integrating AI‑generated ideas can boost the odds of high‑performing concepts entering market research. Practitioners should balance the efficiency gains with strategies to mitigate perceived lack of originality, such as prompting for diverse variations or combining AI outputs with human refinement.
