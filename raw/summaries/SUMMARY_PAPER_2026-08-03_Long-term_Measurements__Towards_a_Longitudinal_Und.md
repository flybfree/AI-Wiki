## Summary
The paper argues that language models pose long-term risks by shaping human cognition and behavior over time, a risk not evident in brief interactions. It proposes measuring these changes longitudinally to understand diachronic effects of AI use. These findings suggest that future AI deployments should prioritize long‑term impact assessment.

## Key Takeaways
- Longitudinal metrics are needed to detect subtle cognitive or affective shifts caused by repeated model engagement.
- Computational NLP must integrate social‑science measurement frameworks to capture emergent behavioral trends over time.
- Early detection through online modeling enables proactive alignment rather than post‑hoc remediation and reduces reliance on retroactive audits.

## Context
Current AI safety research focuses on short‑term outputs and immediate user feedback, overlooking the cumulative impact of repeated interactions. This work bridges that gap by applying longitudinal study methods from social sciences to AI interaction data.

## Implications
Practitioners can embed these measurement techniques into alignment frameworks to anticipate and mitigate long‑term harms. The approach supports a proactive safety culture where model behavior is continuously monitored for positive user outcomes.
