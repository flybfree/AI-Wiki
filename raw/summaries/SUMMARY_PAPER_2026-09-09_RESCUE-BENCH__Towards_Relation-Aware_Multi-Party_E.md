## Summary
The paper introduces RESCUE, a benchmark for relation-aware emotional support conversation systems, evaluating how LLMs handle evolving interpersonal dynamics. Experiments with ten large language models show they excel on local cues but fail on tasks requiring prediction of relational patterns and viewpoint shifts. The findings highlight current limitations in modeling complex relationships.

## Key Takeaways
- RESCUE provides 191 sample conversations with rich annotations, enabling evaluation of relational understanding beyond isolated emotional states.
- Current LLMs perform well when support is triggered by immediate cues but struggle with prediction tasks such as relation pattern and viewpoint forecasting.
- The benchmark reveals a gap between local cue recognition and holistic relational modeling in multi-party support.

## Context
Multi‑party conversational AI must account for evolving social dynamics, yet most systems treat emotions as static. This work pushes the field toward models that can infer and adapt to relational shifts, aligning with broader goals of personalized and context‑aware assistance.

## Implications
For practitioners, RESCUE offers a concrete evaluation framework to improve relational intelligence in chatbots and virtual companions. The industry will need to develop architectures that integrate long‑term memory and dynamic relationship modeling to deliver truly supportive multi‑user experiences.
