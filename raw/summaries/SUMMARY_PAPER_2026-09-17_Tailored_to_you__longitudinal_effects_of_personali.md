## Summary
This research investigates the long-term psychological and behavioral impacts of interacting with personalized language models compared to non-personalized versions. By analyzing longitudinal data from users over five days, the study identifies how different personalization methods—specifically memory-based versus survey-based approaches—influence user trust, self-disclosure, and feelings of regret regarding AI interactions.

## Key Takeaways
- The research indicates that many changes in human-AI interaction patterns over time are primarily driven by repeated exposure to the technology rather than the specific personalization techniques employed.
- Participants who interacted with memory-based models (conditioned on prior conversation history) demonstrated higher levels of self-disclosure and perceived the AI as being less "creepy," suggesting a smoother integration of personalized data into user experience.
- In contrast, participants using survey-based personalization reported higher levels of regret regarding their disclosure of personal information, highlighting that the method of data collection can significantly impact a user's psychological comfort.

## Context
As the AI industry moves toward more personalized and agentic systems, understanding the long-term effects on human behavior is critical for safety and ethics. This paper addresses a significant gap in current research by looking beyond immediate interaction metrics to examine how these systems influence self-perception and interpersonal dynamics over time.

## Implications
These findings suggest that AI developers must carefully consider the specific methods used to implement personalization, as different techniques can lead to vastly different user experiences regarding trust and regret. For practitioners, this means that prioritizing "seamless" personalization might come at a hidden psychological cost, necessitating a more nuanced approach to data collection and user transparency in the design of personalized AI systems.
