## Summary
The paper introduces MyMentorLLM, a multimodal voice‑text simulation for CBT training that creates 2,100 complete sessions linking patients with disorders, trainees and expert supervisors. It finds that simulated patient emotional profiles are mirrored by trainees, supervision quality varies across LLMs, and native speech‑to‑speech models produce outcomes closest to human performance.

## Key Takeaways
- The generated CBT sessions achieve high therapist fidelity because patients display disorder‑congruent emotional profiles that trainees replicate authentically.  
- Supervisor feedback improves diagnostic accuracy in 5 out of 7 LLM models, indicating that expert guidance can correct overestimated trainee competence.  
- Symptom identification accuracy rises with larger model sizes, suggesting scale influences performance.

## Context
This work demonstrates that large language models can simulate therapeutic dialogue at a level comparable to human clinicians, opening possibilities for scalable mental‑health training. It also highlights the need for careful calibration of AI supervisors to prevent biased or harmful feedback.

## Implications
Practitioners and developers should integrate such simulations into CBT curricula while monitoring patient fidelity and supervisory quality. The findings suggest that scaling AI‑driven therapy education is feasible but requires rigorous evaluation across model size, supervision style, and potential adverse effects.
