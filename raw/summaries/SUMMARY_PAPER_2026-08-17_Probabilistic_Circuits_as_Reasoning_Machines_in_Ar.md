## Summary  
This paper proposes probabilistic circuits as a framework for reasoning under uncertainty in artificial intelligence. It demonstrates that PCs enable exact polynomial‑time computation of various inference queries, addressing the NP‑hardness of general probabilistic models. The work also argues that probability should be treated as a core language for AI, linking it to logic and information theory.

## Key Takeaways  
- Probabilistic circuits provide exact computation for marginals, conditionals, most probable explanations, expectations, and related queries in polynomial time.  
- They solve the computational challenge that probabilistic inference is NP‑hard across many models by imposing structural constraints on the circuit.  
- The thesis synthesizes a decade of research, integrating foundational theory, algorithmic advances, deep learning integration, hybrid model designs, and symbolic machine learning paradigms.

## Context  
In AI, handling uncertainty remains a bottleneck that hampers scalable reasoning and decision making. PCs offer a structured alternative that aligns with logical foundations and information‑theoretic principles. This work bridges theoretical insights with practical implementations, offering a coherent pathway from abstract probability to real‑world inference tasks.

## Implications  
Practitioners can leverage PCs for reliable inference in hybrid models, improving the accuracy of decisions made under uncertainty. Industry may adopt PCs to enhance deep learning pipelines where exact probability estimates are essential for robust performance.
