## Summary
The paper investigates a random quadratic form process on a sphere driven by Brownian noise, showing that small forcing breaks symmetry and leads to full synchronization while preserving the original law of the process. It distinguishes between partial synchronization in the unforced case and long‑term anti‑polar clustering followed by merging clusters under forcing.

## Key Takeaways
- The random quadratic form without forcing exhibits partial synchronization due to its inherent symmetries.
- An arbitrarily small Brownian forcing breaks these symmetries, causing a transition from anti‑polar configuration to full synchronization over time.
- This multiscale behavior reveals how bias magnitude and initialization scale affect the dynamics of continuous‑time machine learning models.

## Context
The study connects classical stochastic processes with modern AI architectures such as neural ODEs and transformer encoders that rely on continuous‑time representations. Understanding how small perturbations alter synchronization mechanisms is crucial for designing robust deep learning systems.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that initializing bias parameters at low scales can prevent unwanted symmetry breaking in training dynamics. This insight may guide the design of more stable and efficient machine learning models.
