## Summary
This paper introduces the Marine Engine Fault Dataset, an open-access repository containing multi‑sensor time series from a turbocharged three‑cylinder marine diesel engine under both reference and fault conditions. The dataset records five distinct anomaly classes caused by physical interventions such as cooling‑water pump cavitation and turbine degradation, enabling systematic comparison of baseline performance with induced faults.

## Key Takeaways
- The dataset captures multi‑sensor time series including operating, thermal, pressure, flow and combustion variables for both reference and fault scenarios.  
- Five anomaly classes are implemented through controlled physical interventions that affect specific subsystems, producing interpretable response patterns linked to the fault type.  
- Technical validation confirms that reference measurements remain physically coherent across loads while faults produce progressively distinguishable behaviours based on severity.

## Context
The scarcity of openly available marine‑engine diagnostic datasets hampers research in predictive maintenance and anomaly detection. This work addresses that gap by providing a benchmark with documented operating conditions, subsystem interventions and system‑level measurements, which is essential for training and evaluating AI models in maritime machinery.

## Implications
For practitioners, the dataset offers a reliable source to develop fault diagnosis algorithms without proprietary data constraints. Its structured metadata supports reproducible research across condition monitoring, degradation modelling and other AI applications in marine engineering.
