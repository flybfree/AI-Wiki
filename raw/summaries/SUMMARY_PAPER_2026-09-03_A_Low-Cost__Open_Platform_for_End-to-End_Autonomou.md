## Summary
This paper introduces a low‑cost open experimental platform that lets researchers test end‑to‑end autonomous driving on miniature Ackermann vehicles. The system links physical experiments, a printed urban track, data collection tools, trajectory registration, and a Webots digital twin to evaluate command‑conditioned behavior cloning. Results show the learned policy can follow lanes with a mean cross‑track error of 6.1 cm in real closed‑loop tests and improves further when using a wider camera field of view.

## Key Takeaways
- The platform’s integrated hardware, track, and digital twin enable controlled experiments that bridge simulation and real‑world autonomous driving.  
- A neural policy trained on command‑conditioned data can achieve near‑human performance, with cross‑track errors close to 4.7 cm when the camera field is widened from 58 to 120 degrees.  
- Using synthetic data generated by the digital twin combined with real demonstrations yields a higher‑capacity policy that completes all four track routes in closed loop, whereas a compact baseline or a model trained only on real data fails.

## Context
The work addresses a longstanding challenge in autonomous driving: transferring knowledge from simulation to physical reality. By providing an open, reproducible testbed, it supports sim‑to‑real research where image quality and field of view critically influence performance. This highlights the importance of visual fidelity in training neural policies for real‑world deployment.

## Implications
The platform offers industry practitioners a practical way to validate autonomous driving algorithms without expensive hardware. Researchers can generate large synthetic datasets, test robustness under varying camera conditions, and iterate quickly, accelerating progress toward safer, more reliable autonomous systems.
