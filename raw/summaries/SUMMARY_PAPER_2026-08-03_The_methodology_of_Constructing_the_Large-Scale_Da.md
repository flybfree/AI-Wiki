## Summary
This paper presents a comprehensive methodology for constructing a large‑scale dataset of Russian social media posts that contain presuicidal or anti‑suicidal signals, aiming to improve automated detection and timely intervention. The authors report the creation of over 50 000 annotated texts, along with basic classification experiments showing promising performance across different annotation levels.

## Key Takeaways
- The dataset exceeds fifty thousand Russian social media posts manually labeled for presuicidal or anti‑suicidal content, providing a rich resource for model training.  
- Annotation quality is enhanced through a structured instruction process and post‑correction steps, reducing common labeling inconsistencies.  
- Early experimental results demonstrate that even with limited annotation depth, models can achieve reasonable detection accuracy on the task.

## Context
The rise of mental health awareness has highlighted the need for scalable AI tools to identify at‑risk individuals online. This work contributes to that effort by supplying a Russian‑language corpus that addresses language‑specific nuances absent in many existing datasets, thereby advancing cross‑cultural research in affective computing and suicide prevention.

## Implications
For practitioners, this dataset enables the development of reliable classifiers that can be integrated into social media monitoring systems for early intervention. The methodology also offers a replicable framework for building similar annotated corpora in other languages, supporting broader public health initiatives worldwide.
