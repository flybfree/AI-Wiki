## Summary
The paper introduces Combodied Agents as a human‑centric paradigm that integrates software and embodied actions to model personal state trajectories. It shows how such agents can detect missed medication, send reminders, or deliver the drug, but they lack insight into why the event occurred. The framework unifies perception, memory, prediction, and intervention within a closed loop.

## Key Takeaways
- Combodied Agents treat human states as primary objects, not just software state changes.
- They use purpose‑bound, uncertainty‑aware representations instead of full digital twins to preserve agency.
- Evaluation is scenario‑centered with metrics that protect user autonomy and consent.

## Context
Current AI agents often focus on completing external tasks or delivering physical objects without understanding the underlying human experience. This gap limits trustworthy assistance in health and daily life contexts.

## Implications
Practitioners must design systems that respect evolving agency, allowing reversible interventions and clear feedback. The paradigm encourages interdisciplinary collaboration between software, robotics, and human services for sustainable benefit.
