## Summary
This paper surveys co‑evolution in agentic systems, introducing a three‑stage taxonomy that shows how human constraints are gradually removed as agents and their environments adapt together. It highlights research on peer‑driven adaptation, task‑feedback loops, and meta‑level evolvable mechanisms, while also outlining evaluation and safety challenges for such open‑ended systems.

## Key Takeaways
- Agent‑Agent Co‑Evolution involves dynamic peers that act as adversaries, collaborators, or organizational units, each imposing adaptive pressure on the others to drive continuous improvement.  
- Agent‑Environment Co‑Evolution extends the loop to include tasks, feedback signals, and interaction spaces that evolve alongside agents, creating a feedback‑rich environment for self‑driven progress.  
- Meta Co‑Evolution explores the possibility of making the evolution mechanism itself evolvable, allowing higher‑order changes in how adaptation occurs.

## Context
The rapid rise of autonomous agents demands systems that can improve beyond human design constraints, yet most current approaches rely on static learning contexts.
