## Summary
The paper introduces CoJEPA, a unified training framework that merges Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) and contrastive learning within a single shared backbone. By applying JEPA to masked tokens for local sequence understanding and contrastive loss on the class token for global representation, CoJEPA eliminates the need for an EMA teacher while preserving stable training. Experiments show that CoJEPA matches or exceeds both individual methods on global and local music interpretation tasks, especially in tonal and harmonic contexts.

## Key Takeaways
- The combined objective removes reliance on a teacher‑student dynamic, providing stability without extra parameters.
- Local predictions generated by JEPA enrich sequence tokens beyond what contrastive learning alone can achieve.
- CoJEPA achieves state‑of‑the‑art performance across both global and local music representation tasks.

## Context
Current music AI research often trades off between global and local understanding, requiring separate models or large scale training. This work demonstrates that complementary inductive biases can replace massive model scaling, offering a more efficient alternative for rich audio representations.

## Implications
For practitioners, CoJEPA suggests that smarter loss design can yield better performance than simply increasing model size, encouraging research into objective‑centric training strategies. The approach could be adapted to other domains where global and local cues are both valuable.
