## Summary
The paper introduces AMIE Video, a Gemini‑based multi‑agent system that delivers expert‑level performance in real‑time video consultations. In an OSCE trial with 30 physicians, 15 actors and 100 scenarios, the AI matched or exceeded human clinicians on history‑taking, diagnosis, management and physical observation while patients preferred its interface.

## Key Takeaways
- AMIE Video achieved clinical performance comparable to primary care physicians across key assessment tasks.  
- Patient actors rated the video interface as more effective than text chat for communication and feeling understood.  
- The system excels in real‑time audio‑visual perception but still lacks fine anatomical precision and subtle affective nuances.

## Context
Medical AI has progressed from text‑only models to multimodal agents that integrate speech, vision and reasoning, yet few have demonstrated human‑level competence in live video consultations. This work provides a benchmark showing that such systems can be clinically useful within the sensory complexity of telehealth.

## Implications
The results suggest that expert‑grade AI could augment primary care by handling routine assessments, freeing clinicians for complex cases. As regulatory pathways for multimodal AI are evolving, this study supports early adoption and highlights remaining technical limits to address before full deployment.
