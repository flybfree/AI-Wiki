## Summary
This study investigates how university students perceive the development of their generative AI skills over time and how these perceived trajectories relate to changes in learning practices and academic outcomes. Using a mixed-methods approach combining a retrospective questionnaire (313 responses), interviews with 18 students, descriptive and comparative analyses, exploratory structural equation modeling, and thematic analysis, the authors found that GenAI skill development follows heterogeneous trajectories and is unevenly distributed across skill domains, with academic benefits concentrated primarily in assignment-based tasks rather than examinations.

## Key Takeaways
- Perceived GenAI skill-development trajectories are heterogeneous across students: 34.2% reported rapid early improvement followed by a plateau, 28.4% reported steady improvement, and 21.1% reported delayed acceleration. Development was also uneven across specific skills, with evaluating GenAI outputs perceived as the most challenging competency to improve, while assignments were identified as the most frequently selected driver of skill growth across subject areas.
- Qualitative interview data revealed evolving learning practices including increasingly specific prompting strategies, tailored verification of outputs, and selective delegation of tasks to GenAI tools. However, some students reported stable routines, dependence on AI, skipped reasoning processes, or attributed improvements partly to better GenAI tools rather than genuine skill acquisition, highlighting the complexity of distinguishing skill development from tool capability gains.
- Structural equation modeling indicated a positive indirect association between perceived skill growth and academic gains mediated through adaptation of assignment processes, but academic gains did not clearly differ across developmental trajectory groups after multiplicity correction. Academic benefits were reported more often for assignments than for examinations, suggesting that GenAI skill development may primarily enhance process-oriented academic tasks rather than knowledge-recall assessments.

## Context
As generative AI tools become embedded in university curricula and assessment structures, understanding how students actually develop competencies for using these tools—rather than simply adopting them—has become a critical gap in educational research. This paper addresses that gap by moving beyond binary adoption studies to examine longitudinal skill-development trajectories, domain-specific challenges, and the mediating role of learning-practice adaptation, contributing to the broader literature on AI literacy and pedagogical integration in higher education.

## Implications
For educators and institutional policymakers, these findings suggest that GenAI training programs should account for heterogeneous learning trajectories and prioritize competencies such as output evaluation and critical verification rather than assuming uniform skill acquisition. For the broader AI-in-education field, the distinction between assignment-based and examination-based academic gains highlights the need to redesign assessment strategies and to develop measurement tools that can separate genuine human skill development from improvements driven by evolving AI tool capabilities.
