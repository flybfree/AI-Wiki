## Summary
The paper presents a large-scale corpus of transcribed English-language religious radio broadcasts captured from live webstreams during July 2025, comprising over 700,000 recordings and 60 million diarized lines of speech. The dataset is organized into linked tables that link stream metadata, recording metadata, and transcript lines, enabling detailed analysis of content, topics, and speaker information.

## Key Takeaways
- The corpus includes more than two thousand AM and FM stations rebroadcast in a single month, providing a comprehensive snapshot of religious radio programming across the United States.  
- Each fifteen‑minute segment is automatically transcribed and speaker‑diarized, allowing researchers to track who speaks and what they say within each broadcast.  
- The data are labeled by programming format and topic using a large language model, facilitating automated classification for thematic studies.

## Context
This work addresses the gap in AI research where religious media remains underrepresented despite its cultural significance. By generating a massive annotated dataset, the study demonstrates how machine‑learning techniques can be applied to real‑world broadcast content that has previously lacked structured text resources.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, the labeled corpus offers a ready resource for building models that understand religious discourse, improving natural language processing tools in niche domains. For researchers, it opens avenues to explore how social and political issues are framed within religious media, informing both policy analysis and AI ethics discussions.
