## Summary
The paper presents a formally verified design of IEEE‑754 binary32 and bfloat16 arithmetic for the ARCH HDL, showing that all operators can be synthesized from a single bit‑vector IR. It proves equivalence between SystemVerilog code, SMT‑LIB models and Lean proof models using Yosys‑to‑SMT miter checks. The FMA datapath is reimplemented to pipeline while preserving correct rounding.

## Key Takeaways
- Multiplier‑free operators are proved exhaustively equivalent to the SMT‑LIB floating point theory over all 2^64 inputs, demonstrating a complete verification of comparisons, conversions and binary bfloat16 arithmetic.  
- SAT‑hard multiplier bearing operators such as FP32 mul and FMA are proved correctly rounded in Lean without solving a multiplier equivalence, using a value‑level round‑to‑nearest‑even specification over exact dyadic values.  
- The reimplemented FMA uses a bounded 98‑bit datapath that matches the exact‑wide reference on all 2^96 inputs, inheriting its proven correct rounding.

## Context
This work addresses the need for reliable arithmetic in hardware generated by language models, where floating‑point operations are essential for AI inference pipelines. By providing a mathematically sound verification framework, the research supports trustworthy deployment of model‑driven hardware designs.

## Implications
The findings enable developers to rely on provably correct implementations of floating‑point units in low‑level synthesis tools, reducing risk in safety‑critical applications. For industry, it streamlines integration of AI accelerators that require precise arithmetic without extensive manual verification.
