## Summary
The paper investigates whether large language model training corpora contain unfiltered extremist speech. Using multiple definitions and an expert‑verified pipeline on the Dolma dataset it finds that hundreds of thousands of documents may include hate speech and calls for violence.

## Key Takeaways
- The study identifies a lower bound of hundreds of thousands of extremist or hate‑speech entries in Dolma, indicating substantial exposure risk. - It combines automated text processing with human verification to ensure accurate detection across varied definitions of extremism. - The findings suggest that current pre‑training pipelines may inadvertently train models on content that promotes violence and discrimination.

## Context
Large language model safety remains a pressing concern as models learn from massive internet corpora. Understanding the nature and extent of harmful material in training data is essential for building trustworthy AI systems.

## Implications
Researchers and developers must prioritize rigorous data auditing to mitigate bias and prevent amplification of extremist narratives. Failure to address this could lead to models that inadvertently endorse or propagate dangerous ideologies.
