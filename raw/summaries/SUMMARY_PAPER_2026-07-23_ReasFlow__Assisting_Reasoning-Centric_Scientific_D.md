## Summary
This paper introduces ReasFlow, an autonomous multi‑agent system designed to support reasoning‑centric scientific discovery in applied mathematics. By integrating a verification loop and a self‑improving knowledge retrieval mechanism, the system can generate complete research papers with rigorous proofs and empirical content from minimal prompts. Evaluated against state‑of‑the‑art open‑access baselines, ReasFlow consistently achieves the highest scores on a curated LLM review rubric.

## Key Takeaways
- The internal verification loop audits logical coherence and corrects fundamental errors before human inspection, ensuring theoretical derivations are sound.  
- An automated knowledge retrieval system surfaces both declarative facts and procedural heuristics, reducing expert intervention in literature synthesis and algorithm design.  
- ReasFlow unifies multiple research stages—literature synthesis, theorem proving, experimentation, and manuscript preparation—into a single autonomous workflow.

## Context
Recent advances in large language models have enabled AI agents to perform empirical tasks at scale, yet theory‑driven discovery remains under‑explored due to limited reasoning capabilities. This work addresses that gap by building a system that mimics the rigorous, proof‑oriented mindset of a graduate student while collaborating with an expert principal investigator.

## Implications
For researchers in applied mathematics and related fields, ReasFlow offers a practical tool to accelerate theory development without sacrificing rigor. In industry, it could streamline problem‑solving workflows where deep mathematical insight is required for product innovation or optimization.
