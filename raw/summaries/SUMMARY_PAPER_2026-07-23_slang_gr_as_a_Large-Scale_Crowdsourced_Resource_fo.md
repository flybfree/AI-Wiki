## Summary
The paper introduces slang.gr, a large crowdsourced lexicon of Greek non‑standard language, and presents computational analysis showing that slang is dominated by person‑related and evaluative terms, exhibits high morphological creativity, and is shaped by highly skewed participation with short user lifespans and overlapping communities. It also introduces a community‑based confidence score for definitions that integrates user roles, interaction patterns, and moderation signals.

## Key Takeaways
- Slang in Greek is strongly centered on person‑related and evaluative language, indicating its social function.
- The lexicon shows high morphological creativity, reflecting linguistic innovation within informal speech.
- Participation is highly skewed: users have short lifespans and communities overlap, leading to uneven contribution patterns.

## Context
This work tackles the challenge of modeling informal language in AI research by providing a structured multi‑layer taxonomy that improves interpretability while preserving behavioral structure. It offers a concrete resource for sociolinguistic NLP tasks, helping researchers understand how non‑standard expressions are generated and evaluated.
