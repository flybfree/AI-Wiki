## Summary
The paper investigates whether LLM-based multi-agent deliberation can faithfully replicate population opinion patterns and interaction-driven consensus in Korean policy debates using census-grounded personas. It finds that while arguments are diverse and stance shifts occur, the agents' output does not reliably mirror demographic differences and often reverses them, yet sealed-monologue models achieve similar final positions as full debates.

## Key Takeaways
- Persona agents fail to reproduce population opinion patterns, producing responses more concentrated than human data and sometimes reversing demographic splits.  
- Deliberations generate reasoned arguments that move stance significantly but much of the shift happens without peer exchange, as sealed monologues converge similarly to full debates.  
- Anchoring population-informed starting positions suppresses updating, indicating interaction is crucial for opinion change.

## Context
This work addresses a key challenge in deploying AI for public deliberation, where realistic representation and dynamic consensus formation are essential. By using census-grounded personas, the study highlights gaps between simulated diversity and actual societal heterogeneity.

## Implications
Practitioners must separate argument surfacing from accurate population modeling to avoid misleading simulations. The findings suggest that while AI can surface balanced arguments, further validation is needed before trusting it for policy impact assessments.
