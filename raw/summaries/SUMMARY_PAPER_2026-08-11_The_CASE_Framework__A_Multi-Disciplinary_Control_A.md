## Summary
The paper proposes the CASE framework to govern enterprise agentic AI by treating governance as four distinct problems across control theory, adaptive systems, supervisory cybernetics, and engineering operations. It formalizes cross‑layer coupling conditions and identifies an emergence gap that causes most production failures. The authors validate their model with empirical studies showing multi‑layer failure trajectories.

## Key Takeaways
- Agentic AI governance is decomposed into four separate scientific domains: control theory for individual agents, adaptive systems theory for collectives, supervisory cybernetics for human‑agent teams, and engineering operations for fleets. 
- The framework reveals a zero‑touch deployment paradox where excellence in one layer can degrade others, exposing the emergence gap as a critical failure point. 
- Empirical data show that 82 % of production failures trace back to multi‑layer trajectories and no existing tool fully covers Layer 2 (emergence).

## Context
Enterprise AI agents are proliferating faster than governance mechanisms can keep pace, creating systemic risk. Traditional DevSecOps practices are insufficient for the stochastic and emergent behaviors of modern agentic systems.

## Implications
The CASE framework shifts governance from a single‑discipline checklist to a multi‑layered scientific architecture that aligns with legal requirements such as the EU AI Act. Practitioners must adopt this model to move beyond ceremonial oversight toward truly effective human‑in‑the‑loop control.
