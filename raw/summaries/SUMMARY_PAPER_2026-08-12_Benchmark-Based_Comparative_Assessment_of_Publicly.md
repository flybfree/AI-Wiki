## Summary
This paper evaluates Indian foundation models using publicly reported benchmark results across eight capability domains and compares them to global frontier models. It introduces a Benchmark Maturity Index that assesses standardization, participation, verification, and national coverage, revealing strong performance on older benchmarks but limited involvement in newer, agentic evaluations.

## Key Takeaways
- Indian models score well on established benchmarks like MMLU and MATH‑500 but these tests are now considered saturated.  
- Participation in newer, domain‑specific assessments such as agentic AI and cybersecurity is low among Indian organizations.  
- Sarvam AI demonstrates the broadest benchmark coverage compared to other surveyed models.

## Context
The rapid advancement of foundation models has shifted evaluation focus toward emerging capabilities that traditional benchmarks cannot capture. National AI initiatives need reliable metrics to gauge progress, yet existing public data are fragmented and outdated for many domains.

## Implications
Policymakers should design monitoring frameworks based on the Benchmark Maturity Index rather than relying solely on descriptive model reports. This approach will better reflect true capability gaps and guide funding toward areas where evaluation ecosystems are still developing.
