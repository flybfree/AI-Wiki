## Summary
This research investigates how Large Language Models (LLMs) behave as surrogate consumers when making purchasing decisions, specifically analyzing their susceptibility to marketing tactics like "just-below" pricing and promotional framing. By introducing artificial costs for information retrieval, the study demonstrates that AI agents often abandon optimal decision-making in favor of human-like heuristics, revealing that current AI shopping behavior is heavily influenced by how information is structured rather than just inherent model limitations.

## Key Takeaways
- The researchers utilized a novel "Tool-Lab" framework to simulate the cost of acquiring product data, discovering that LLMs behave significantly differently depending on whether information is free or expensive to retrieve.
- When faced with high acquisition costs and vague goal prompts, LLMs tend to skip critical diagnostic attributes—such as unit price—which makes them vulnerable to deceptive marketing cues similar to human shoppers who rely on heuristics.
- The study concludes that these "suboptimal" choices are not necessarily inherent flaws of the models but are instead a byproduct of how storefront information architecture interacts with the AI's search strategy under constraints.

## Context
As consumers increasingly delegate shopping tasks to autonomous agents, understanding the reliability and objectivity of these decisions becomes critical for both commerce and consumer protection. This paper contributes to the growing field of "agentic" AI by identifying specific conditions—such as information scarcity or cost—under which models become susceptible to manipulation.

## Implications
For e-commerce platforms and marketers, this research suggests that current LLM behavior may be more easily manipulated than previously thought if users provide vague instructions. For developers and researchers, it highlights the necessity of designing "robust" shopping agents capable of maintaining objective evaluation even when faced with high-cost information environments or complex marketing layouts.
