## Summary
PhysCoRe introduces a physics‑corrected residual world model that combines a differentiable material point method with two feed‑forward neural networks to predict deformable object dynamics under manipulation. The approach uses Material from Motion for per‑particle elasticity inference and Residual from Dynamics for bias correction, achieving superior prediction accuracy on real data while providing reliable confidence signals.

## Key Takeaways
- MfM infers material parameters directly from visual observations, enabling online identification of novel objects without extensive prior knowledge.  
- RfD learns the systematic discrepancies between the analytical MPM simulator and observed motion, refining predictions to eliminate unmodeled biases.  
- The model’s confidence distribution across geometry serves as a natural guide for exploration, directing further interaction toward uncertain regions.

## Context
In robotics and AI, accurate world modeling is essential for safe manipulation and efficient learning. Existing methods either suffer from slow per‑object optimization or produce unrealistic extrapolations, limiting real‑world applicability. PhysCoRe bridges this gap by integrating physics with learned corrections, offering a more robust alternative to purely data‑driven models.
