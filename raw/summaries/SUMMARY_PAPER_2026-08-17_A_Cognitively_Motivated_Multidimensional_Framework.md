## Summary
The paper introduces a cognitively motivated multidimensional framework to evaluate metaphor explanations by breaking quality into six theoretically grounded dimensions. In an analysis of 11,200 human ratings it demonstrates that explanation quality is genuinely multidimensional, that annotator disagreement follows systematic patterns rather than random noise, and that the six dimensions collapse into two independent axes of judgment.

## Key Takeaways
- Explanation quality is not a single scalar but consists of six distinct dimensions each reflecting a different aspect of how well an explanation captures the metaphorical relationship.  
- Human annotators disagree in systematic ways across these dimensions rather than randomly, indicating that their judgments reveal underlying cognitive structures.  
- The six dimensions reorganize into two independent axes of judgment, suggesting that most human evaluations can be captured by just two primary factors.

## Context
In AI research the evaluation of open‑ended generation tasks often relies on holistic scores that obscure how different aspects of performance interact. This paper’s focus on structured multidimensional metrics aligns with a growing interest in diagnostic tools that can pinpoint where models succeed or fail, offering a more nuanced view than single‑number ratings.

## Implications
For practitioners developing metaphor generators the study suggests that automatic evaluators should be judged not only by overall accuracy but also by their ability to preserve the structure of human judgments. By preserving these dimensions, systems can provide richer feedback and improve performance in ways that holistic scores cannot capture.
