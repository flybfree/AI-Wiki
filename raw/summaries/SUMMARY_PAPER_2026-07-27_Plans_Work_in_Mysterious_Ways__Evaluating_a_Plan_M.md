## Summary
This paper introduces a Plan Mode for spreadsheet programming and tests it against a non‑planning baseline using a within‑subjects study with 24 participants. The results show that while task outcomes are comparable, the Plan Mode reduces user refinement effort and improves perceptions of creativity support and human‑machine collaboration.

## Key Takeaways
- Users completing tasks with Plan Mode required fewer revisions than those using the non‑planning baseline, indicating a smoother workflow despite similar final results.  
- The tool was perceived as providing stronger creative assistance under the Plan Mode, suggesting it aligns better with users’ expectations for innovative problem solving.  
- Human‑machine collaboration felt more balanced in the Plan Mode, implying that the planning step helped distribute cognitive load between user and AI.

## Context
Spreadsheet programmers often work iteratively and prioritize speed over strict technical correctness, making upfront planning less natural than in other programming domains. This study addresses a gap by examining whether plan‑oriented agents can be adapted to such workflows without disrupting the user experience.

## Implications
The findings suggest that integrating Plan Modes into spreadsheet tools could enhance productivity and user satisfaction even when tasks are not highly technical. Practitioners may adopt these methods to streamline complex calculations while preserving a collaborative, creative approach to problem solving.
