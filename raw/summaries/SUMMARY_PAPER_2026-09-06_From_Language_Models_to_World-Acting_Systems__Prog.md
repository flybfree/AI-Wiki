## Summary
The paper reviews recent research on agentic AI, showing how large language models become consequential when integrated with external systems. It finds that action-interface expansion is the most advanced capability, while robust completion and independent verification remain limited. The authors propose a justified delegation heuristic rather than treating autonomy as an observed law.

## Key Takeaways
- Action‑interface expansion is documented more convincingly than robust completion, recovery, authorization, or independent verification.
- Model Context Protocol and Agent2Agent improve interoperability but do not establish trustworthy delegation; multi‑agent organization adds specialization alongside cost and correlated failure.
- Persistent simulations and world models support training and planning but do not themselves demonstrate agency; robotics and self‑driving laboratories show bounded feasibility rather than unattended open‑world reliability.

## Context
The rapid integration of language models into real‑world tasks is reshaping expectations of autonomy, yet the literature often conflates model competence with system trust. This paper clarifies that progress in one area does not guarantee progress in another, highlighting gaps between capability and safe execution across digital, social, virtual, and physical domains.

## Implications
For practitioners, the study urges a staged validation approach that couples model performance with harness reliability and environmental safety. It also calls for capability‑based permissions and durable state management to ensure accountability when agents act beyond human control.
