## Summary
This paper proposes a policy analysis framework that lets stakeholders evaluate AI governance proposals by surfacing trade‑offs and normative assumptions rather than delivering verdicts. The method combines expert qualitative input with computational text analysis to create transparent rubrics, visualizes attribute emphasis, and benchmarks large language models against a calibrated model.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework structures policy assessment around multiple attributes, enabling users to surface priorities without prescribing outcomes.  
- It uses a hybrid mixed‑methods approach that integrates expert insights with computational validation of rubric construction.  
- Domain‑trained rubric‑calibrated models serve as benchmarks for comparing general‑purpose LLMs on analytical consistency.

## Context
AI governance is rapidly evolving, yet debates often reduce complex trade‑offs to binary positions, obscuring underlying values and assumptions. This work addresses the need for a systematic tool that can make these nuances visible across jurisdictions and policy contexts.

## Implications
Policymakers, analysts, and researchers will gain a reusable, jurisdiction‑agnostic method to align their normative commitments with analytical priorities, fostering more transparent and balanced AI governance discussions. The framework also provides a benchmark for evaluating LLM performance in policy analysis tasks.
