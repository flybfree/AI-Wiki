## Summary
The paper aims to investigate how acoustic and linguistic features jointly influence perception of emotional valence and arousal in spontaneous Finnish speech. It uses a newly released affective corpus to test whether combining text- and audio-based features yields better regression performance than each alone. The results show that combined features improve valence prediction, while the complementary effect is modest for arousal.

## Key Takeaways
- Combining text‑based linguistic cues with acoustic surface characteristics boosts accuracy in modeling perceived emotional valence across Finnish speakers.
- For arousal perception, adding audio features does not substantially enhance regression models beyond using either modality alone.
- The study provides new empirical evidence that the interaction between text and audio contributes specifically to valence but not strongly to arousal in spontaneous Finnish speech.

## Context
Understanding affective perception through multimodal feature integration is central to affective computing research. This work extends prior findings from other languages by applying them to Finnish, a language with limited existing studies on both acoustic‑phonetic and text analysis of affect. The methodological approach aligns with efforts to create interpretable models that balance computational efficiency with perceptual realism.

## Implications
For AI practitioners developing emotion recognition systems, the paper suggests prioritizing multimodal fusion for tasks where valence is the primary target. It also highlights the need for domain‑specific evaluation datasets when applying cross‑linguistic results. The insights can guide more accurate affective modeling in Finnish‑language applications such as voice assistants or mental health monitoring tools.
