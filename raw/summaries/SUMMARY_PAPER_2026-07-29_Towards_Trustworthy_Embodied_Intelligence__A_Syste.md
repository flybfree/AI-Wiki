## Summary
The paper defines trustworthy embodied intelligence as sustained safe success, emphasizing that reliable task execution under variation is needed beyond mere completion. It proposes a four‑layer framework—model, system, evidence, deployment—and introduces graded trustworthiness levels based on bounded claims across capability, safety, assurance, governance, and evidence.

## Key Takeaways
- The model layer must generate action proposals with calibrated uncertainty and explicit safety preferences to ensure task competence.
- Integrated sensing, computation, control, hardware safeguards, fault containment, and fallback constitute the system layer that enables dependable authorized actions.
- Trustworthiness is evaluated through evidence layer mechanisms like evaluation, verification, validation, traceability, and structured assurance arguments.

## Context
Embodied AI systems face real‑world risks where failures can cause physical harm, prompting a need for systematic trust frameworks. This work bridges control theory, robotics, dependable computing, and autonomous driving to address these challenges.

## Implications
Researchers must prioritize layered safety and evidence generation, while industry adopts graded trustworthiness metrics to guide deployment decisions and standardize risk‑aware AI.
