## Summary
This paper investigates whether written narratives alone can trigger human temporal illusions and examines how large language models (LLMs) respond to those same narratives. The study finds that humans only prefer expected scenarios in two of five illusion types, whereas most LLMs align with literature‑predicted outcomes across four illusions.

## Key Takeaways
- Human readers show strong preference for expected scenarios only when the manipulation is directly visible in text, indicating reliance on external cues rather than internal simulation.  
- The majority of LLMs (14 models) consistently choose the scenario predicted by published research, suggesting they retrieve factual information instead of generating human‑like temporal biases.  
- Reasoning traces reveal that about 70% of model responses cite psychological studies, highlighting a pattern of retrieval rather than genuine understanding.

## Context
The paper contributes to AI research on how language models simulate human cognition and the limits of their ability to replicate perceptual phenomena. By comparing human perception with LLM behavior across a controlled set of temporal illusions, it highlights a gap between model outputs and actual psychological processes.

## Implications
For developers, this work suggests that LLMs may be overly aligned with external knowledge rather than generating nuanced, context‑driven responses. Practitioners should consider the distinction when deploying models for tasks requiring genuine human perception or subjective judgment.
