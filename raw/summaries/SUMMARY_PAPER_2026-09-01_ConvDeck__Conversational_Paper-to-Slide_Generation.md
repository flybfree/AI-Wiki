## Summary
This paper introduces ConvDeck, a multi‑agent system that enables conversational refinement of academic papers into slide decks. By splitting the generation process into stage‑specific loops, users can give feedback at each decision point, improving both narrative flow and visual quality. The authors report that this approach raises user satisfaction while maintaining coherence.

## Key Takeaways
- Stage‑specific conversation loops let users critique outline decisions early, preventing later bottlenecks in content allocation.
- Agents can act directly on edits or respond conversationally to clarify feedback, supporting iterative revision without a full deck completion.
- Evaluation shows higher user goal satisfaction and preserved narrative coherence, content quality, and visual presentation.

## Context
Current AI slide generation often relies on closed‑loop critique after the entire deck is built, limiting real‑time user involvement. ConvDeck’s distributed interaction model aligns with human feedback cycles, offering a more natural dialogue between author and system.

## Implications
Practitioners can adopt stage‑specific feedback to streamline presentation creation, reducing revision time and enhancing engagement. The method also demonstrates that conversational agents can handle nuanced editing tasks while preserving technical standards in academic communication.
