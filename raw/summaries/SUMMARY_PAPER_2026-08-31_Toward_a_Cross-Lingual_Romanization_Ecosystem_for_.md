## Summary
The paper introduces the Sinitic Romanization Ecosystem—a cross‑lingual design framework for Mandarin and Cantonese romanization—and demonstrates its effectiveness through open‑source tools and speech‑to‑romanization experiments. The framework produces aligned schemes (CantRomZJ1, MandRomZJ1) that cut error rates by 7.80% and 10.61% compared with the Pinyin+Jyutping baseline.

## Key Takeaways
- The ecosystem enforces a phonetic correspondence principle, mapping similar sounds to similar symbols across languages.
- It aligns historical‑phonological cognates, ensuring consistent romanization strings despite language differences.
- Each scheme follows a one‑phoneme‑one‑symbol rule and uses only basic Latin letters for simplicity.

## Context
Cross‑lingual romanization remains a bottleneck in low‑resource Sinitic speech technology, limiting transfer learning between dialects. This work offers a systematic solution that can be applied to multiple Sinitic varieties beyond the study pair.

## Implications
The framework improves model performance and reduces development cost for practitioners needing reliable romanization pipelines. It also fosters community collaboration through open‑source infrastructure, encouraging broader adoption in AI research and industry.
