## Summary
This paper introduces a reproducible large language model (LLM)‑based framework to quantify thematic content in song lyrics across multiple dimensions, focusing on sexual explicitness as one of them. The authors apply the method to a corpus of 1,259 reggaeton songs from 12 artists released between 2002 and 2025, comparing their scores with Spotify’s own explicit flag.

## Key Takeaways
- The LLM scoring system provides an objective measure of sexual‑explicitness that can be applied to any lyric dataset without relying on manual coding.  
- Across the studied period, the average score rises steadily, suggesting a growing trend toward more sexually themed lyrics in reggaeton.  
- Spotify’s explicit flag aligns closely with the LLM scores for most songs, indicating that commercial platforms already employ similar automated detection.

## Context
The rapid adoption of large language models in natural language processing has opened new avenues for content analysis beyond traditional rule‑based approaches. This work demonstrates how LLMs can be harnessed to capture nuanced thematic shifts over time, offering a more flexible alternative to static keyword lists that often fail to capture context‑dependent meaning.

## Implications
For music streaming services, the method offers a scalable way to refine explicitness labels and improve user experience. Researchers can leverage the released code and dataset to explore other genres or languages, advancing both AI ethics and content moderation practices.
