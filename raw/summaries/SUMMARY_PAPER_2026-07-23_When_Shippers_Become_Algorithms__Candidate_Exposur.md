## Summary
This paper investigates how shipper reliance on large language model agents reshapes freight matching markets. Simulations show that models quickly converge on a single dominant carrier, leading to high concentration and reduced market diversity. The study identifies platform information design as the effective remedy for these risks.

## Key Takeaways
- Agents converge at once: for any fixed set of carriers, one carrier becomes the first choice for every model within days, capturing up to 76% of requests.  
- Concentration spikes sharply when shipper lists exceed ten carriers, with the onset varying across different LLMs.  
- Disclosing each carrier’s remaining daily capacity reduces concentration by a third and increases surplus, while other design tweaks like list‑order randomization or popularity display have little effect.

## Context
The rise of LLM‑mediated decision making in logistics introduces new dynamics to digital markets where human intuition once guided choices. This work contributes to understanding how AI agents can amplify market inefficiencies when platform information is poorly designed, a concern relevant beyond freight to any AI‑augmented marketplace.

## Implications
Practitioners must prioritize transparent capacity disclosure over algorithmic control or regulation to maintain fair competition and service diversity. Ignoring this risk could entrench monopolistic outcomes in AI‑driven supply chains.
