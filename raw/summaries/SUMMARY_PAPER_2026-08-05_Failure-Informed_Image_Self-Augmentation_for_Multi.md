## Summary
The paper introduces Failure-informed Image Self-Augmentation (FISA) to boost multimodal large language models by creating image augmentations derived from the model’s own failure cases. These augmentations are verified through self‑examination to ensure they do not introduce noise. Experiments on visual question answering show consistent performance gains both in-distribution and out-of-distribution.

## Key Takeaways
- FISA generates visually challenging yet answer‑preserving images directly from the model's error examples, avoiding generic augmentations.
- The framework uses dual fidelity filtering to keep semantic meaning intact while improving difficulty.
- Synthetic samples generated by FISA outperform generic image augmentation baselines in data efficiency.

## Context
Current MLLM self‑improvement relies on large annotated datasets or text‑only augmentations. Image generation is underutilized because existing methods lack alignment with model weaknesses. This work bridges that gap by leveraging failure feedback to produce task‑relevant visual challenges.

## Implications
Practitioners can reduce reliance on costly annotation pipelines and improve model robustness without extra data. The approach offers a scalable, self‑supervised strategy for continual learning in multimodal systems. This approach can be extended to other modalities, such as audio or structured data, for broader continual learning.
