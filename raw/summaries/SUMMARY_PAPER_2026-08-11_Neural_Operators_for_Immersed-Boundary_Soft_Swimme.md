## Summary
This paper introduces neural‑operator surrogates that predict the hydrodynamic fields generated by planar and volumetric eel swimmers in immersed‑boundary simulations. The models are trained on regular‑grid data from adaptive fluid–structure codes and condition on geometry and Reynolds number, achieving low global relative L² errors across multiple trajectories.

## Key Takeaways
- The planar neural operator predicts velocity components, scalar vorticity, and pressure with a full‑domain global relative L² error of 3.51 % on high‑Reynolds‑number trajectories, showing that pressure prediction can be accurate enough for many design studies.  
- For volumetric swimmers the three‑model approach yields errors of 3.44 %, 5.58 %, and 19.2 % for velocity, vorticity, and pressure respectively, highlighting that pressure remains a weaker predictor than velocity and vorticity.  
- The surrogates are conditioned on swimmer geometry and Reynolds number, enabling rapid parametric studies without recomputing full simulations.

## Context
Neural operators provide differentiable, low‑cost function approximation for complex physics problems, reducing reliance on expensive CFD runs. This work extends the concept to moving‑boundary flows where both swimmer shape and surrounding flow evolve, demonstrating a bridge between high‑fidelity simulation and AI‑driven prediction.

## Implications
Engineers can use these surrogates to explore design variations, control strategies, and parameter sweeps in seconds rather than hours. The modest error levels suggest that neural operators could become standard tools for rapid prototyping and optimization of swimming robotics.
