## Summary
The paper introduces Poli‑Bias, a counterfactual framework that measures how large language models treat legally equivalent political conflict scenarios differently when country identities are swapped. Across 13 contemporary LLMs it finds that model responses vary systematically based on the countries involved and user affiliations, revealing hidden biases in framing, evaluation and legal reasoning.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework decomposes response disparities into five interpretable dimensions, allowing researchers to pinpoint where unequal treatment occurs such as description, evaluation or defense of actions under international law. 
- Country identities systematically affect how equivalent actions are described, evaluated, and defended in LLMs, indicating a bias that is not captured by single‑metric assessments. 
- The study demonstrates that these biases persist across diverse model families and sizes, highlighting the need for fine‑grained auditing tools beyond coarse fairness metrics.

## Context
Measuring political bias in AI systems has become increasingly important as models are used to inform public discourse and policy. Traditional fairness metrics often overlook context‑specific nuances such as legal reasoning or geopolitical framing, which can lead to misleading conclusions about model behavior.

## Implications
For practitioners developing or deploying LLMs, Poli‑Bias offers a systematic way to audit political evenhandedness and sycophancy before release. The methodology could inform regulatory standards that require transparent bias reporting in high‑stakes applications like conflict analysis tools.
