## Summary
This paper introduces FinCUABuildBench and its associated FinCUABuildAgent as a benchmark for evaluating whether agents can autonomously construct dynamic financial computer‑using agent (CUA) tasks. The results show that while existing construction methods perform poorly, the new agent achieves a qualification rate of 31.3%, demonstrating that autonomous task generation is feasible with meaningful evaluation value.

## Key Takeaways
- FinCUABuildBench provides 576 construction requests spanning 24 financial workflows and three types of runtime variation, offering extensive coverage for automated testing.  
- Under the same model backbone, existing agent‑based construction methods achieve only a qualification rate of 1.3–8.3%, highlighting their limited effectiveness.  
- FinCUABuildAgent reaches a qualification rate of 31.3%, showing that agents can autonomously build tasks that are both diverse and evaluable.

## Context
The field of AI research increasingly relies on benchmarks to assess agent capabilities, yet financial scenarios remain under‑evaluated due to manually crafted tasks that do not reflect real‑world variability. This work addresses the gap by creating a scalable framework for generating and assessing dynamic financial CUA tasks automatically.

## Implications
Autonomous task construction can broaden evaluation coverage in finance, encouraging both researchers and practitioners to adopt more realistic testing scenarios. The methodology may inspire new standards for agent performance measurement across complex domains.
