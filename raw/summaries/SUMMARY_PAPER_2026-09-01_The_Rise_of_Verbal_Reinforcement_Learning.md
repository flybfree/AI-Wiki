## Summary
This paper introduces Verbal Reinforcement Learning (VRL), a unified framework that categorizes how natural language feedback influences language agents. It identifies three distinct pillars — grounding, deliberative, and learning — explaining when verbal cues are used and what they modify in an agent’s lifecycle.

## Key Takeaways
- Language can serve as a grounding signal by defining tasks through goals, states, and reward structures.
- Natural language may act as deliberate feedback that guides reasoning during testing without altering model parameters.
- Verbal feedback can also function as a learning signal that updates the agent’s underlying model during training.

## Context
The rise of VRL reflects broader trends in AI where human‑like interaction is integrated into system design. By formalizing verbal reinforcement, researchers aim to align language models with real‑world user intent and improve interpretability across applications.

## Implications
For practitioners, this taxonomy offers a roadmap for incorporating natural language feedback into agent development pipelines. It highlights opportunities to create agents that are both more capable and better aligned with human preferences through systematic use of verbal reinforcement.
