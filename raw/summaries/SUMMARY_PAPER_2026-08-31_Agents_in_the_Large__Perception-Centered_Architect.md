## Summary
This paper introduces a Perception‑Centered Architecture (Pera) that organizes persistent language agents around continuous perception and control, enabling them to adapt to evolving user needs and environmental changes. The framework retrospectively categorizes recent work, analyses a case study, and offers guidance for building long‑lived adaptive AI systems.

## Key Takeaways
- Pera structures agents by continuously perceiving service‑relevant signals from task executions, internal context, and external environment changes to generate lifecycle tasks that drive ongoing operation.  
- The architecture treats persistent assistance as a series of evolving lifecycle tasks rather than isolated task solutions.  
- By mapping the shift from bounded task solving to long‑lived adaptive intelligence, Pera provides a clear architectural transition for future research.

## Context
The rapid advancement of cognitive language agents has focused on short‑term task resolution, yet real‑world applications demand sustained support across changing contexts. This paper addresses that gap by proposing an architecture that emphasizes perception and adaptation over static task execution.

## Implications
Pera offers practitioners a roadmap for designing AI assistants that remain useful over time without constant reconfiguration. The framework can be integrated into service platforms to reduce maintenance overhead and improve user experience in dynamic environments.
