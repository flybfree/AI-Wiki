## Summary
This paper investigates how students from Canadian and South Korean universities view the ethicality of using generative AI in coding assignments. It finds that Canadian respondents are more likely than Korean counterparts to deem such use unethical and policy‑violating, even though policies are identical.

## Key Takeaways
- The amount of AI‑generated code embedded in tasks strongly drives students’ moral judgments.
- Cross‑cultural differences persist despite uniform institutional rules, highlighting a gap between policy and perception.
- Hofstede’s dimensions such as power distance, individualism, and uncertainty avoidance help explain these divergent ethical reasoning patterns.

## Context
Generative AI is rapidly reshaping university computing curricula, prompting educators to balance innovation with academic integrity. Understanding cultural influences on student attitudes is essential for designing equitable assessment practices.

## Implications
Policymakers should craft AI‑use guidelines that respect local cultural contexts while preserving core principles of honesty. Researchers must continue cross‑cultural studies to inform globally applicable ethical frameworks.
