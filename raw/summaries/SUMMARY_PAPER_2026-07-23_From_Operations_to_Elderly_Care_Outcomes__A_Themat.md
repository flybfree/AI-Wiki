## Summary
This paper conducts a thematic review of thirty seminal studies that combine industrial engineering and operations research with elderly care. It categorizes the work into home healthcare operations, polypharmacy management, and clinical chronotherapy, highlighting a shift from static deterministic models to dynamic stochastic frameworks enhanced by artificial intelligence.

## Key Takeaways
- The literature shows a methodological evolution toward dynamic stochastic optimization integrated with AI rather than relying on simple linear programming. 
- Operational improvements such as staff routing are still largely process‑level and have not been linked to measurable clinical outcomes in the reviewed studies. 
- There is a lack of holistic models that connect hospital care transitions to community services, leaving a gap between theoretical efficiency and patient health results.

## Context
The rapid aging demographic creates unprecedented pressure on healthcare systems, making AI‑driven decision support essential for scalable solutions. This review situates these advances within the broader trend of applying machine learning and digital twins to complex service environments, emphasizing interdisciplinary integration.

## Implications
Practitioners must move beyond isolated process optimizations toward integrated, multi‑level care models that embed human factors and smart technologies. Embedding AI tools like large language models into systems analysis can bridge the operational‑outcome gap, fostering resilient healthcare delivery for aging populations.
