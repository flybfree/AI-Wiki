## Summary
The paper conducts a scoping review of AI audits that treat system integration as a core evaluation component, analyzing 58 studies from a corpus of 4,259 documents. It identifies three integration sites — inter‑component, system‑environment, and multi‑system — each addressing risk exploration, determination, coordination, and procedural regularity. The review concludes that current AI audits remain fragmented and struggle to capture cross‑layer failures.

## Key Takeaways
- The audit corpus is limited in measuring integration‑specific risks despite a growing number of studies.
- Gaps exist between existing AI evaluation practices and traditional safety‑critical audit expectations.
- Access to information and resources heavily influences the design and scope of system‑integration audits.

## Context
AI systems increasingly rely on multiple components and deployment environments, creating complex interaction points that are vulnerable to failure. Traditional component‑level audits cannot fully capture these cross‑layer risks. This paper highlights a missing link in AI safety research by focusing on integration as an evaluation pillar.

## Implications
Practitioners must adopt systematic approaches that evaluate compatibility, completeness, and oversight across system boundaries. Embedding integration audits into the AI lifecycle will improve reliability and regulatory compliance in high‑stakes applications.
