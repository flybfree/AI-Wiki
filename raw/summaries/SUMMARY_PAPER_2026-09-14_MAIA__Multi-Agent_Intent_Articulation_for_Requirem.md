## Summary
This paper addresses the articulation bottleneck in bespoke art commissions, where clients struggle to translate vague emotional concepts into precise visual requirements. The authors introduce MAIA, a multi-agent system that employs Socratic questioning and a strict verification protocol to help users articulate their intent before any creative generation occurs. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in cognitive support for users and enhanced visual completeness and executability of the resulting briefs as validated by professional concept artists.

## Key Takeaways
- MAIA operates under a "Verification over Invention" rule, using multi-agent Socratic inquiry to scaffold requirement discovery and transform abstract feelings into verified text-based visual briefs.
- A within-subjects study with 16 participants revealed statistically significant gains in cognitive support compared to a minimal baseline, with thematic analysis confirming the underlying mechanism of structured intent articulation.
- Blind evaluations by three professional concept artists confirmed that AI-generated briefs significantly improved both visual completeness and executability across sampled tasks, with MAIA demonstrating directionally larger improvements than the baseline condition.

## Context
The intersection of human creativity and generative AI has long struggled with prompt engineering and requirement specification, often leading to misaligned outputs or inefficient iteration cycles. This research shifts focus upstream from generation to discovery, addressing a critical gap in how non-expert users communicate artistic vision to both human artists and AI systems. By formalizing intent articulation as a distinct phase, the study contributes to broader efforts in human-AI collaboration and interactive creative workflows.

## Implications
For practitioners and developers, MAIA demonstrates that structured, multi-agent scaffolding can significantly reduce the cognitive load of requirement specification while improving downstream creative outcomes. The validation gate mechanism offers a practical framework for preventing unverified or misaligned content from entering production pipelines. Ultimately, this approach could standardize client-artist communication in digital art industries and enhance the reliability of AI-assisted creative tools by ensuring inputs are precisely defined before generation begins.
