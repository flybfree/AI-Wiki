## Summary
The paper reviews 336 GUI-agent papers from January 2018 to April 2026 and answers five research questions about the field’s maturity, architecture, evaluation, lifecycle concerns, and future opportunities. It finds rapid growth since 2024 with most work focused on mobile and web interfaces, modular perceive‑reason‑act loops, but underdeveloped recovery, human escalation, safety enforcement, auditability, and systematic testing.

## Key Takeaways
- The field has expanded sharply since 2024 with most work focused on mobile and web interfaces.  
- Architectures rely heavily on modular perceive‑reason‑act loops while mechanisms for recovery, human escalation, safety enforcement, and auditability remain immature.  
- Evaluation is increasingly interactive yet still centered on task success and lacks comparability across protocols.

## Context
GUI agents are moving from isolated prototypes to closed-loop systems that integrate perception, reasoning, execution feedback, recovery, and human oversight. This shift reflects the need for robust software engineering practices in AI interfaces.

## Implications
Without addressing lifecycle‑centered testing, observability, privacy controls, and governance, GUI-agent capabilities cannot translate into deployable, maintainable solutions. Practitioners must embed these concerns early to ensure safety, security, and long‑term reliability of interactive AI systems.
