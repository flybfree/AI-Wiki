## Summary
This paper presents an automated system that extracts quantitative data from 76,000 energy‑system studies published since 2010, compiling 3.2 million structured data points and 20 million metadata entries into a FAIR database. The approach enables researchers to audit assumptions, compare empirical observations with model predictions, and explore research trends across technologies, regions, and time periods.

## Key Takeaways
- the automated extraction yields a comprehensive dataset of 3.2 million quantitative values linked to 20 million metadata entries, covering diverse energy‑system models and technologies  
- the resulting FAIR database allows scholars to directly compare academic assumptions with observed empirical data, revealing systematic divergences across studies  
- the interactive dashboard facilitates flexible filtering, analysis, and download of the dataset for specific research questions

## Context
The rapid expansion of literature on energy systems creates a need for scalable, transparent methods to aggregate quantitative findings. Automated extraction leverages natural‑language processing and structured data pipelines to transform unstructured papers into machine‑readable information, supporting reproducibility and large‑scale meta‑analysis.

## Implications
For the AI community, this work demonstrates how large‑scale text mining can produce actionable datasets that enhance model validation and policy insight. Practitioners in energy modeling will gain a reusable resource that reduces duplication of effort and accelerates evidence‑based decision making.
