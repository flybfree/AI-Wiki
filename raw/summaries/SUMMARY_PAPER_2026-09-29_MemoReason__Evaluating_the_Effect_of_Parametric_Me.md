## Summary
This paper investigates whether Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrate genuine contextual reasoning or merely rely on facts memorized during training. The authors introduce MemoReason, a benchmark that pairs factual reasoning tasks with structurally identical versions containing fictitious entities to isolate the influence of parametric memory. Their evaluation reveals a significant performance drop when models encounter unfamiliar contexts, confirming a strong memorization bias, yet shows that direct recall of stored answers is not the primary failure mechanism.
