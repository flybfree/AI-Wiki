## Summary
This paper presents a system for ranking reasoning traces generated by large language models to verify numerical claims in English and Arabic. The authors compare an LLM‑based verifier with fine‑tuning and a lightweight TF‑IDF reward model, finding the former yields higher recall while handling conflicting classes better.

## Key Takeaways
- The LLM‑based approach improves Recall@5 compared with the TF‑IDF method, especially on non‑conflicting claims.  
- A reward model that aggregates scores by verdict group performs well on the Conflicting class despite lower overall recall.  
- Sub‑claim decomposition does not help; it adds noise rather than improving verification accuracy.

## Context
Automated numerical claim verification combines language understanding with quantitative reasoning, a key challenge for large‑scale fact checking systems. This work contributes to the growing effort of integrating LLMs into structured verification pipelines and highlights trade‑offs between model complexity and performance on multilingual data.

## Implications
The findings suggest that fine‑tuned LLM verifiers are more effective than simple feature‑based reward models, guiding developers toward richer reasoning rather than shallow scoring. Practitioners can leverage this insight to design robust systems for both English and Arabic claim verification, improving trust in automated fact checking services.
