## Summary
The paper investigates how people's preferences for emotional support shift when AI is used versus human interaction, focusing on the mismatch between choice and delivery. It finds that AI support is rated superior only among those who chose it, yet interacting with AI increases willingness to choose again. A 28‑day study shows daily conversations gradually redirect preference toward AI.

## Key Takeaways
- AI support is perceived as better than humans only when participants have voluntarily chosen the AI partner.
- Regardless of whether the interaction matches the participant's original choice, exposure to AI conversation raises future intent to use AI again.
- Over time, personalizing AI conversations leads to a progressive shift away from human emotional support.

## Context
The study addresses a growing gap between theoretical AI empathy ratings and real‑world user behavior in affective computing. It highlights that preference formation is not static but evolves with repeated interaction, influencing how users allocate their limited social bandwidth.

## Implications
For designers of empathetic AI, this suggests that personalization can be both a strength and a driver of dependency. Practitioners should consider the long‑term impact of shifting preferences on human connection and ethical deployment of emotional support systems.
