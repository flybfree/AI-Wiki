## Summary
This paper introduces SonicCaps, a large‑scale audio captioning dataset that pairs roughly 15 million captions with 700 k short audio clips generated by a multimodal model. The study demonstrates that the dataset’s high semantic diversity and fine‑grained descriptions lead to higher human ratings than existing datasets. Moreover, training CLAP models on SonicCaps using multi‑caption sampling yields stronger performance in audio retrieval and zero‑shot classification across both public and commercial benchmarks.

## Key Takeaways
- The dataset generates about 24 captions per audio clip, covering main descriptions, rephrased variants, and semantic tags, which directly addresses low diversity in prior captioning sets.  
- Human evaluation shows that these captions are perceived as more descriptive and precise, correlating with higher quality judgments compared to standard datasets.  
- Multi‑caption sampling during CLAP training consistently improves retrieval and classification results, indicating a clear benefit of diverse caption representation for audio models.

## Context
Audio‑language modeling has progressed rapidly thanks to large multimodal models that can generate captions from sound. However, most existing captioning corpora suffer from limited semantic variety and generic descriptions, which hampers downstream tasks like retrieval and classification. SonicCaps tackles this gap by creating a richly annotated dataset that better mirrors the ambiguity and nuance of human auditory perception.

## Implications
For researchers, SonicCaps offers a benchmark that encourages more diverse caption generation strategies and multi‑caption sampling techniques. Practitioners can leverage its high‑quality captions to fine‑tune retrieval models, achieving better zero‑shot performance on both public and commercial audio datasets. The release of the dataset and specialized CLAP models on Hugging Face further democratizes access, accelerating progress in audio understanding tasks.
