## Summary
The paper argues that the concern about AI misuse in medical training is actually a misclassification problem, where learners incorrectly select an inappropriate level of AI assistance. It introduces the SCAN framework to guide real‑time decision making and shows how skill acquisition can go wrong at the task level.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper reframes AI misuse as a failure to correctly classify whether an AI should Substitute, Complement, Aid, or be Non‑Negotiable in a given clinical subzone. - It identifies passive engagement with correctly classified AI tasks as a hidden route to mis‑skilling because learners stop seeking expert help. - The framework predicts that detection of misclassification can prevent skill failure through targeted curriculum interventions.

## Context
Artificial intelligence is increasingly embedded in medical education, raising questions about how it supports or hinders clinical reasoning development. This work contributes by grounding AI integration in cognitive science and Vygotsky’s zone of proximal development rather than assuming uniform adoption across learners.

## Implications
Educators can use SCAN to design curricula that monitor classification errors and intervene early, reducing the risk of skill erosion. The approach offers a measurable way to assess AI’s impact on learning trajectories, fostering safer and more effective medical training programs.
