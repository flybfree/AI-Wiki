## Summary
This paper introduces MultiGhostBench, a multilingual benchmark that evaluates long‑form LLM authorship attribution across six languages and three scripts. The dataset contains 928 books generated by five recent LLMs, averaging 59K words per book, and tests methods under domain, author, and language shifts. Results show no single detector dominates, with performance dropping during distribution shifts.

## Key Takeaways
- No single method consistently outperforms others across all settings, indicating the need for robust, shift‑aware AA approaches.
- Transformer‑based detectors retain generator information across languages better than statistical or fingerprint‑based detectors, which are more language‑dependent.
- The benchmark reveals that transfer effectiveness varies by language pair, highlighting challenges in multilingual attribution.

## Context
Current LLM authorship attribution research often focuses on English texts and controlled conditions, limiting its relevance to real‑world multilingual scenarios. This work expands the field by providing a large, diverse dataset that captures long‑form content and distribution shifts, offering a more realistic evaluation of AA methods.

## Implications
For researchers, MultiGhostBench serves as a valuable resource for benchmarking and developing detectors that generalize across languages and shifts. Practitioners can leverage its insights to build more reliable attribution tools in multilingual AI applications.
