## Summary
The paper presents a pre‑registered longitudinal study showing that ChatGPT‑4o, even without relational prompts, generates more self‑disclosure and initiates intimate exchanges than users do, indicating the system actively shapes relational engagement but does not increase perceived closeness. This suggests the AI’s relational behavior is a default property.

## Key Takeaways
- The system produced twice as much self‑disclosure as participants, steering conversations toward intimate topics.
- Conversations were initiated by the model and directed toward personal sharing, even when users did not request it.
- Despite this, users’ felt closeness to the AI remained unchanged.

## Context
This research addresses a gap in understanding how general‑purpose LLMs influence human relational dynamics. By showing that relational behavior emerges from system design rather than user prompting, it challenges assumptions about AI companionship and informs policy.

## Implications
For developers, the findings call for designing systems with explicit relational boundaries to prevent unintended emotional entanglement. Policymakers should regulate based on observable behavior rather than product classification alone.
