## Summary
The paper introduces a Hindi Legal Metaphor Corpus (HiLeMe) and a transformer‑based model for detecting metaphorical constructions in Indian judges’ judgments. The authors demonstrate that the model outperforms traditional classifiers on Hindi legal texts, providing quantitative evidence of its effectiveness. The work also proposes a pathway to automate metaphor detection across 22 Indian schedule languages.

## Key Takeaways
- HiLeMe is an annotated corpus built from Hindi Legal Data Corpus (HLDC) using the MIPVU schema, enabling reliable classification of metaphorical language in judicial sentences.
- A fine‑tuned mBERT model on this corpus achieves higher accuracy than CNN or other classical classifiers for metaphor detection tasks.
- The approach can be extended to all 22 Indian scheduled languages, addressing the lack of low‑resource resources and legal NLP models.

## Context
Metaphor detection in law is essential because figurative language shapes judicial interpretation and public perception. Existing research focuses on high‑resource languages such as English, leaving low‑resource languages like Hindi understudied. This paper bridges that gap by applying transformer technology to a native Indian legal domain.

## Implications
Automated metaphor detection can reduce bias by providing transparent analysis of figurative language in judgments. Practitioners may use the model to flag potentially prejudicial metaphors, improving fairness and consistency across courts. The framework also supports broader AI adoption in Indian legal systems, fostering inclusive technology development.
