## Summary
This study compares three AI interaction designs in a learning task about nuclear safety protocols. It finds that an unrestricted conversational bot yields the largest learning gains, while a Socratic‑guided model shows lower gains and adaptive EEG monitoring reveals higher cognitive engagement. The results suggest that unrestricted access may boost performance but not necessarily deeper understanding.

## Key Takeaways
- The unrestricted chatbot produced higher learning gains than both constrained modes (p < .03, d > 0.80), indicating it is more effective for factual recall.
- Adaptive EEG monitoring in the third condition revealed significantly higher cognitive engagement (p = .018), suggesting users were more mentally active when the system adjusted to their state.
- Cluster analysis shows unrestricted‑mode participants used a direct answer‑retrieval strategy, implying gains may stem from post‑test evaluation rather than genuine learning.

## Context
The paper addresses a growing debate about whether AI should provide unfiltered assistance or scaffolded guidance in education. By integrating brain‑sensing data, it highlights the need for methods that monitor learner engagement beyond simple performance metrics.

## Implications
For educators and developers, this research warns against assuming unrestricted AI always improves learning outcomes; instead, adaptive systems that respect cognitive load may foster deeper understanding. Industry practice should balance user convenience with evidence of genuine knowledge acquisition.
