## Summary
The paper proposes a multi-criteria framework for comparing sociotechnical interventions, using misinformation as a case study. Researchers surveyed 39 experts on 40 interventions across five criteria: political feasibility, effectiveness, user acceptance, cost, and implementation effort. The findings show that the most effective interventions are not always the most acceptable or feasible. The study highlights the need for systematic comparison tools.

## Key Takeaways
- Experts prioritize effectiveness over public acceptability and implementation feasibility.
- Highly effective interventions may be politically infeasible or socially unacceptable.
- Cost and effort constraints often limit adoption despite high efficacy.

## Context
Sociotechnical design in AI, such as content moderation and privacy interfaces, relies on balancing technical performance with societal impact. Existing evaluations focus narrowly on effectiveness, ignoring broader implementation realities. These findings echo broader challenges in AI governance where technical solutions must align with public values.

## Implications
Practitioners must adopt holistic decision frameworks that weigh trade‑offs among criteria to avoid suboptimal solutions. The framework can guide policy and product design beyond misinformation into other domains like recommendation systems. Integrating this framework could improve stakeholder alignment and resource allocation.
