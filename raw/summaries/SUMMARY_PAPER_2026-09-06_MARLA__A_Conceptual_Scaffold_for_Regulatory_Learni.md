## Summary
The paper introduces MARLA, a conceptual scaffold for regulatory learning under the EU AI Act, outlining a five-stage cycle of Map, Assess, Report, Learn, Adapt. It demonstrates how legal requirements can be turned into socio‑technical practices at local, national and European levels. The case studies show that each stage produces its own documentable form of learning.

## Key Takeaways
- MARLA structures regulatory learning as a cyclical process where mapping legal obligations to practice creates initial documentation.
- The framework highlights the need for separate but linked documents from technical and legal stakeholders at each implementation level.
- Piloted examples illustrate how learning can be transferred from national pilots to broader European governance.

## Context
The EU AI Act aims to embed safety, trustworthiness and market readiness into innovation ecosystems. Regulatory learning is essential because evidence must inform interpretation and adaptation as technologies evolve. This paper addresses the gap between those who generate evidence and those who use it within distinct professional domains.

## Implications
MARLA offers practitioners a shared vocabulary that can streamline communication across legal, technical and operational teams. By institutionalising the five‑stage cycle, regulators may improve consistency and responsiveness to emerging AI risks. The approach could become a reference model for other jurisdictions adopting similar frameworks.
