## Summary
This paper presents an agentic AI system designed to help scientists at European XFEL efficiently analyze large, complex datasets by integrating domain expertise with facility‑specific knowledge. The authors evaluated 16 AI tools through literature review, interviews, and a user study, producing two prototypes that address key challenges in scientific data analysis.

## Key Takeaways
- The system tackles the fragmentation of documentation, tools, and support channels that hinder scientists’ ability to retrieve relevant information quickly.
- It generates source code tailored to high‑performance computing environments, reducing manual coding effort and errors.
- Evaluation with expert users demonstrates that the agentic approach improves data analysis workflows by providing contextual assistance and actionable outputs.

## Context
The rapid growth of AI tools has created a landscape where many specialized applications exist, yet few are built for highly domain‑specific scientific tasks. This research highlights the need for maintainable, adaptable AI support that respects the unique constraints of facilities like European XFEL, offering a model for integrating AI into complex experimental workflows.

## Implications
For researchers and developers, this work suggests that targeted AI agents can bridge gaps between raw data and actionable insights without replacing human expertise. In industry, such solutions could streamline data processing pipelines across diverse scientific domains, enhancing productivity and accelerating discovery.
