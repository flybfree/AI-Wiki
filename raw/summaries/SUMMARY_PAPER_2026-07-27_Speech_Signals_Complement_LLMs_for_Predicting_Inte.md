## Summary
This paper investigates whether speech signals can improve the prediction of interpersonal attraction beyond what large language models (LLMs) achieve using only conversation transcripts in Japanese speed‑dating settings. The authors combine a transcript‑only LLM with a supervised speech predictor and find that their combined model yields higher pairwise ranking accuracy than the transcript‑only approach, though the benefit is conditional rather than universal.

## Key Takeaways
- Speech can complement transcript‑only LLM prediction, but this complementarity depends on specific conversation conditions rather than being universally effective.  
- The improvement in pairwise ranking accuracy over the transcript‑only model is significant across all evaluated conditions.  
- Per‑participant Pearson correlation gains vary by round and rating direction and are not statistically significant after correction.

## Context
The study addresses a key limitation of current AI models that rely solely on textual data, highlighting the potential value of multimodal inputs such as speech for social prediction tasks. By showing that speech can add predictive power even when transcripts already encode much information, it underscores the need to consider diverse signal modalities in human behavior modeling.

## Implications
For practitioners developing AI systems that assess interpersonal dynamics, integrating speech could enhance accuracy without over‑relying on text alone. This research suggests a more robust approach to predicting attraction by leveraging both linguistic and acoustic cues, potentially informing future applications in social interaction analytics.
