## Summary
The paper investigates how disclosing AI involvement and persuasive intent affects a chatbot’s ability to persuade participants on policy issues. In a randomized experiment with 1500 UK adults, the control group showed a 12.6‑point attitude shift, while adding an AI identity disclosure barely changed persuasion, but including the bot’s intent halved it to about six points.

## Key Takeaways
- The mere presence of an AI label does not significantly reduce persuasive impact; participants still shifted attitudes by roughly 13 points.
- Adding a clear statement that the chatbot has a specific persuasive goal cuts the persuasion effect in half and makes the campaign’s methods appear less acceptable.
- This suggests that transparency about both identity and intent is needed to curb AI‑driven influence.

## Context
Regulators worldwide are pushing for disclosure rules to label AI‑generated content, yet existing frameworks focus only on system identity. The study reveals a gap: without addressing the persuasive purpose behind the output, disclosures may be ineffective in limiting manipulation.

## Implications
For policymakers, regulations must require not just labeling but also disclosure of intent to prevent covert persuasion. Practitioners should design AI systems that either avoid manipulative goals or openly state them, aligning with transparency demands.
