## Summary
This paper investigates how real‑world performance characteristics of a 55 kW/79 kWh battery energy storage system (BESS) and a three‑unit 2.4 kW electrolyser affect participation in multiple Danish electricity ancillary service markets. Using experimental data from 2022 to 2025, the authors embed hardware limits such as ramp rates, efficiency curves, and setpoint tracking into a price‑driven optimization model that yields daily profits of 1,749.27 DKK for the BESS and 289.46 DKK for the electrolyser.

## Key Takeaways
- The optimized profit figures are derived from integrating measured hardware constraints with market prices, showing that ignoring real performance can underestimate revenue potential.  
- Both systems benefit from stacking services across markets, but their profitability diverges due to differing response times and energy storage capacities.  
- The study demonstrates that embedding experimental results into optimization models is essential for accurate multi‑year profit projections in flexible resource participation.

## Context
The integration of hardware performance data with market economics is a critical step toward reliable AI‑driven forecasts for renewable energy systems. By grounding model assumptions in empirical measurements, the approach improves the credibility of long‑term revenue estimates that rely on stochastic optimization techniques.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, this research provides a template for calibrating simulation tools using actual device behavior, reducing overestimation of profit potential. Practitioners can apply similar methods to other flexible resources, ensuring more realistic planning and investment decisions in the evolving energy market landscape.
