## Summary
The paper introduces Unified Policy Architecture (UPA), a governance framework designed to unify fragmented AI authorization, security, and compliance mechanisms for autonomous enterprise AI agents operating as an Enterprise Operating System. UPA integrates policy control beyond simple authorisation into runtime obligations, human approvals, compliance evidence, audit trails, and evaluation processes.

## Key Takeaways
- UPA provides a single declarative policy model that governs AI agents, tools, workflows, memory usage, enterprise resources, and agent‑to‑agent interactions, eliminating siloed policies.  
- The architecture extends governance to runtime obligations such as approvals and compliance checks, producing auditable evidence for each decision made by autonomous systems.  
- UPA supports extensible plugins and industry‑specific policy packs, enabling organizations to tailor governance without rewriting core logic.

## Context
Enterprise AI is moving toward fully autonomous operating environments where multiple agents collaborate on complex tasks. Traditional security models were built for static codebases, not dynamic agent workflows, leading to gaps in oversight and accountability. UPA addresses this gap by offering a unified policy engine that can be embedded into any enterprise AI OS.

## Implications
For practitioners, UPA simplifies compliance audits and reduces the risk of policy drift across autonomous systems. For industry leaders, it enables scalable governance that aligns with regulatory requirements while preserving operational agility in AI‑driven workflows.
