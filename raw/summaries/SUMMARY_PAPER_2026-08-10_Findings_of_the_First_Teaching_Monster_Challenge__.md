## Summary
The paper introduces the Teaching Monster Challenge, a benchmark designed to evaluate whether AI agents can adapt instructional content to specific learner personas, which is known as Pedagogical Content Knowledge. Our results show that while current systems generate accurate subject material, they perform poorly in tailoring presentation and adapting to the learner’s needs, and automatic judging reveals a systematic bias toward lower‑scoring outputs.

## Key Takeaways
- Current AI teaching agents excel at delivering factual content but struggle with pedagogical adaptation, presenting material in ways that do not align with the learner’s persona.  
- The LLM‑based judge separates a clear low‑performing tail but ranks the strongest systems poorly, producing near‑identical scores for top models.  
- Because top systems receive similar scores, their pairwise rankings do not reflect human preference, highlighting a limitation of automated evaluation.

## Context
AI agents can now solve complex problems and produce long‑form multimodal content, yet they lack the ability to teach effectively. This paper fills that gap by providing a concrete benchmark for Pedagogical Content Knowledge in AI education.

## Implications
For researchers, the benchmark offers a testbed to improve both teaching systems and automatic judges. For industry practitioners, it signals a need for more human‑centric evaluation methods before deploying AI tutors at scale.
