## Summary
This paper presents the evolutionarily recurrent decision model (ERDM), a computational framework that investigates how evolutionary mismatch, bounded rationality, and satisficing drive both adaptive and maladaptive behaviors. The study demonstrates that distinct strategies such as learned helplessness, avoidance, healthy relationships, and aggression emerge naturally from simulated agents across varied environments.

## Key Takeaways
- Adaptive and maladaptive strategies like learned helplessness and avoidance arise organically in the model without being programmed, reflecting bounded cognitive systems shaped by ancestral constraints.  
- The model’s simulations include threat, prey/goal‑pursuits, and alliances, showing that competition for rewards mirrors survival metrics and highlights ecological validity.  
- Validation under adverse childhood experiences confirms that these behaviors emerge naturally, supporting the theory of modern‑ancestral environmental mismatch.

## Context
ERDM contributes to AI research by integrating evolutionary dynamics with reinforcement learning, offering a mechanistic view of human behavior that bridges cognitive science and machine intelligence. ## Implications
For practitioners in psychology and AI, ERDM provides a testable framework for understanding psychopathology as emergent properties of bounded systems.
