## Summary
This review analyzes how artificial intelligence is transforming biological research workflows while assessing the associated biosecurity risks, concluding that threat levels depend critically on user expertise, intent, access to materials, and existing safeguards. The authors find that AI significantly enhances digital capabilities such as protein design and screening evasion, yet physical execution in wet laboratories remains constrained by tacit knowledge barriers. Consequently, the paper advocates for a defense-in-depth governance framework that aligns regulatory responsibilities with specific capability thresholds across the biological AI ecosystem to mitigate high-consequence risks while preserving beneficial uses.
