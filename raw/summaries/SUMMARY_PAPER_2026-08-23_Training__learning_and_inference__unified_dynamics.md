## Summary
The paper introduces a unified view of training, learning and inference as dynamics driven by atomic generation facts embedded in a Generation‑Fact Graph. Using nanoGPT experiments it shows that training produces finite‑amplitude responses that reorganize distributed support, enabling stable capability formation while inference remains a frozen projection of those learned states.

## Key Takeaways
- Training is framed as the evolution of a parameter‑optimizer system where each action creates a stateful functional response tied to target boundaries.  
- Learning manifests as persistent reorganization of functional support, observable when target‑specific states are compared with readout limits.  
- Inference is described as a static projection of training dynamics, allowing gating and rollback to reveal causal recruitment without additive effects.

## Context
This work builds on the idea that AI processes can be represented as composable facts rather than opaque black boxes. By linking training loops to scientific fact graphs, it offers a more interpretable framework for understanding model behavior in deep generative networks.

## Implications
For researchers, the unified dynamics provide tools to diagnose why models degrade or recover, enabling targeted interventions. For practitioners, the approach may simplify debugging and improve reproducibility across different architectures and datasets.
