## Summary
This paper introduces a demand‑driven framework that integrates vertiport siting, discrete‑event fleet simulation, and door‑to‑door travel‑time feasibility for on‑demand urban air mobility. The study shows that scaling the network from four stations to sixteen stations improves completion time and regularity but does not eliminate deadhead flights caused by spatial demand imbalance.

## Key Takeaways
- Larger fleets increase vehicle‑arrival regularity and reduce average travel time, yet they still suffer from deadhead relocations when demand is unevenly distributed across vertiports.  
- The discrete‑event simulation captures multi‑vehicle dispatch, battery swaps, and service regularity, revealing that operational constraints persist even with expanded capacity.  
- Travel‑time savings are most pronounced for longer or congested trips where residual non‑flight time remains after flight duration.

## Context
The integration of AI‑driven demand modeling with physical simulation is a frontier in transportation network design, offering a data‑centric approach to urban air mobility that balances user convenience with operational feasibility. This work exemplifies how machine learning can generate spatial trip clusters and how event‑based models validate those placements under realistic constraints.

## Implications
Practitioners can use this framework to prioritize vertiport locations where demand density justifies additional fleet size, avoiding unnecessary capital expenditure on stations that would only create deadhead flights. The methodology supports policy makers in assessing whether UAM truly reduces congestion for specific trip lengths and travel patterns.
