## Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models such as ChatGPT exhibit racial bias in sentiment responses by adapting an implicit association test. Using 126 prompts across four GPT versions, the authors found a small main effect of racial condition but no significant model differences or interaction effects; sensitivity analyses and Tukey tests revealed weak or non‑significant results.

## Key Takeaways
- The study detected a modest main effect of racial condition on sentiment scores (F=2.04, p=.042) yet the effect vanished in rank‑transformed analysis and pairwise comparisons.  
- Model identity did not influence outcomes, indicating that bias is not tied to specific architecture versions.  
- The only significant comparison was a post‑hoc European‑Indigenous Australian contrast, suggesting the findings are hypothesis‑generating rather than robust.

## Context
Understanding algorithmic bias is crucial as LLMs increasingly shape decisions in sensitive domains like healthcare and government services; psychological measurement techniques can provide interpretable benchmarks for such models. This work bridges behavioral science with AI evaluation by applying a well‑established implicit test to model outputs.

## Implications
For researchers, the study underscores the need for interdisciplinary designs that combine rigorous sentiment analysis with sensitivity checks to avoid overstated bias claims. Practitioners should treat reported effects as exploratory signals rather than definitive evidence of discrimination in LLM behavior.
