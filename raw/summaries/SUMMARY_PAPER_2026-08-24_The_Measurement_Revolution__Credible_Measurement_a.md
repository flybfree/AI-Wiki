## Summary
The paper argues that AI is reshaping economic measurement by turning unstructured data into structured variables cheaply, moving the bottleneck from generating measures to selecting among plausible ones. It outlines three stages—discovery, construct definition, observation—and stresses that credible inference requires explicit validation criteria rather than informal proxy claims.

## Key Takeaways
- The discovery stage relies on AI’s ability to extract features from text and images, but researchers must still define what those features represent as a measurable variable.
- Construct definition demands rigorous anchoring of the AI‑generated proxy to an explicit criterion, preventing reliance on vague “reasonable” assumptions.
- Observation is complicated by potential bias in AI predictions; without a random validation sample, inference remains fragile.

## Context
AI’s capacity to process massive unstructured data has made new measurement proxies possible at scale. Traditional econometrics often struggles with the cost and complexity of creating such variables, so this work highlights how the field must adapt its methodological standards.

## Implications
For researchers, the paper calls for a shift from ad‑hoc variable creation to systematic validation protocols. Practitioners will need to embed these criteria into their AI pipelines to ensure that AI‑driven measurements are trustworthy and reproducible.
