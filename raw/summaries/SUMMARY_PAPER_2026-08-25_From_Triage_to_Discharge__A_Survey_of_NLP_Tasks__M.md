## Summary
This paper surveys 46 recent studies that apply natural language processing to the emergency department workflow, covering triage classification, clinical summarisation, automatic diagnosis, report generation, and discharge documentation. It finds a clear shift toward pretrained transformers and large language models, while also highlighting ongoing challenges such as limited generalisability and noisy inputs.

## Key Takeaways
- The review shows that most ED NLP tasks now rely on pretrained language models rather than task‑specific architectures, improving performance but reducing interpretability.  
- Interactive clinical systems are gaining interest, suggesting a move toward real‑time assistance during patient care rather than post‑hoc analysis.  
- Evaluation practices are evolving toward clinically grounded metrics that respect the nuance of medical language and workflow constraints.

## Context
The rapid advancement of large language models has opened new avenues for AI in healthcare, yet most prior work focuses on hospital‑wide applications or isolated tasks. This survey bridges that gap by concentrating on the high‑stakes, time‑sensitive environment of emergency departments where decisions can have immediate clinical consequences.

## Implications
For practitioners, these findings suggest that integrating pretrained models into ED workflows could reduce documentation burden and support faster triage decisions. Researchers should prioritize generalisability testing and develop evaluation frameworks that align with real‑world clinical data quality to ensure safe deployment.
