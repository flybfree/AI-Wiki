## Summary
This paper introduces the SCOPE dataset that links stigma signals in survivor narratives to peer support responses on Reddit, using a multi‑dimensional taxonomy for both stigma and support. The analysis shows that stigma narratives emphasize internalized distress while non‑stigma narratives focus more on interpretation of experiences, with internalized stigma being the dominant category. Community replies remain stable across stigma types, with information and esteem support appearing most frequently.

## Key Takeaways
- Stigma narratives place greater emphasis on internalized distress, whereas no‑stigma narratives center on interpreting situations and experiences.  
- Internalized stigma is identified as the most prevalent stigma category within the dataset.  
- Community responses are broadly stable across different stigma types, with information support and esteem support being the most common forms of peer assistance.

## Context
The study addresses a gap in AI research where stigma and social support are examined separately rather than together, highlighting how computational models may miss nuanced interactions between these phenomena. By integrating linguistic, contextual, and emotional analyses, the work demonstrates that existing sentiment‑based approaches could overlook important dimensions of survivor discourse.

## Implications
These findings suggest that AI systems designed for content moderation or mental‑health support must account for stigma’s layered impact on narratives to avoid misclassifying supportive responses as harmful. Practitioners should incorporate multi‑dimensional taxonomies and stable response patterns into algorithmic design to foster safer, more empathetic online environments.
