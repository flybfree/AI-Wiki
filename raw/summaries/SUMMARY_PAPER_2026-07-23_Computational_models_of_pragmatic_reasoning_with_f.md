## Summary
This paper introduces SAGE, a neuro‑symbolic framework that merges the generative power of language models with symbolic reasoning to model pragmatic language use. Evaluated across three case studies, SAGE achieved high accuracy and often outperformed baselines, though component analyses reveal an asymmetry between LM proposers and evaluators.

## Key Takeaways
- LM proposers reliably generate alternative expressions that fit pragmatic modeling needs, while LM evaluators tend to produce intuitive judgments rather than formal theoretical measures.  
- The framework’s symbolic selector module implements cognitively motivated computational steps that bridge the gap between generation and evaluation.  
- Neuro‑symbolic models show promise for explaining human pragmatics but face limitations in component-level performance.

## Context
Integrating generative language models with cognitive theory addresses a longstanding challenge: replacing manual specification of alternative sets with data‑driven, flexible approaches. This work exemplifies the shift toward hybrid AI systems that can both create and assess linguistic alternatives automatically.

## Implications
For practitioners, SAGE offers a blueprint for building chatbots or assistants that generate contextually appropriate responses without hard‑coding every possible option. However, developers must recognize the asymmetry between generation quality and evaluation accuracy when deploying such models.
