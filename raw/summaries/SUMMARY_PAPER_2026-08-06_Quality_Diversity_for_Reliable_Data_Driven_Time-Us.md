## Summary
The paper introduces a Quality Diversity framework that quantifies uncertainty in data‑driven time‑use optimization for health recommendations. By integrating predictive uncertainty into the optimization process, it yields recommendations that balance expected benefits with confidence levels. The study demonstrates diverse high‑quality activity compositions that align with health outcomes under uncertainty.

## Key Takeaways
- The QD framework explicitly incorporates model uncertainty into objective functions derived from compositional data analysis of a large child cohort.
- Optimization is guided toward regions of lower uncertainty while preserving the diversity and quality of time‑use structures.
- The approach reveals explicit relationships between activity compositions and health indicators such as BMI, life satisfaction, and cognition.

## Context
This work addresses a gap in AI‑driven health optimization where models maximize expected outcomes without accounting for prediction confidence. By embedding uncertainty into decision making, the method aligns with broader trends toward robust and trustworthy algorithmic recommendations.

## Implications
Practitioners can rely on more reliable time‑use suggestions that reduce risk of unrealistic advice. The framework supports AI applications in behavioral health where safety and consistency are paramount.
