## Summary
This paper provides a comprehensive primer on evaluating large language models in healthcare, emphasizing the critical need for rigorous assessment to ensure patient safety and model benefit. The authors outline four essential evaluation domains—study design principles, statistical methods, capability evaluation, and clinical context evaluation—to address the unique challenges posed by LLMs, such as probabilistic outputs and sensitivity to prompt variations.

## Key Takeaways
- LLM evaluation presents distinct challenges compared to traditional machine learning due to probabilistic and open-ended outputs, as well as behavioral shifts influenced by prompt design and accumulated context, necessitating specialized assessment frameworks that account for non-determinism.
- The review details four core evaluation areas: study design principles, statistical methods, capability evaluation (covering multiple-choice, agentic, and multi-turn benchmarks alongside operational metrics like token usage), and clinical context evaluation (addressing free text accuracy through human review, LLM-as-a-judge approaches, and clinical trial methodologies).
- The authors stress the importance of aligning specific evaluation methods with the underlying research question while highlighting potential pitfalls and underlying concepts to guide researchers in designing rigorous and pragmatic healthcare LLM assessments that avoid common methodological errors.
