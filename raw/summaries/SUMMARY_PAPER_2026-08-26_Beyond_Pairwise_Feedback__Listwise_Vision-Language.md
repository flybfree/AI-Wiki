## Summary
This paper introduces a new framework that uses vision-language models to generate listwise rankings of image outcomes instead of the traditional pairwise comparisons used in Bradley‑Terry reward learning. The authors evaluate this Plackett‑Luce approach on Meta‑World manipulation tasks and find it matches or exceeds performance of K‑wise Bradley‑Terry, RL‑VLM‑F baselines, achieving an 86 % mean final success rate that rivals the oracle baseline.

## Key Takeaways
- The work replaces pairwise feedback with listwise rankings using the Plackett‑Luce model, allowing VLMs to exploit their ability to rank multiple candidates.  
- Across all environments at least one ranking size K in {3,4,5} consistently performs as well or better than other methods in mean success rate.  
- The best PL configuration reaches an 86 % mean final success rate and matches the oracle baseline on Drawer Open.

## Context
Vision‑language models provide rich semantic supervision that can be leveraged for reinforcement learning from human feedback, yet most existing approaches limit them to binary comparisons. This limitation wastes the information contained in higher‑order rankings generated by VLMs. The Plackett‑Luce formulation is a natural extension that captures ordinal preferences and adapts to various ranking depths.

## Implications
Listwise supervision can make reward learning more flexible and efficient, enabling RL systems to use richer feedback signals without redesigning the preference model. Practitioners may adopt this approach to improve performance on complex manipulation tasks where multiple outcomes are relevant.
