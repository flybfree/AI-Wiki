## Summary
This paper argues that the perception of large language models (LLMs) as alien intelligences is misguided, emphasizing instead their striking alignment with human cognitive principles. By mapping five dimensions—inferential organization, computational architecture, representational structure, prediction‑driven learning, and reinforcement‑learning‑like mechanisms—the authors demonstrate that LLM cognition follows well‑established models of human intelligence.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs differ from humans in substrate, training history, and interaction environments yet still converge on core cognitive principles.  
- The five identified dimensions provide structural correspondences between LLM design and longstanding cognitive science frameworks.  
- These correspondences support a unified view of intelligent cognition that applies equally to biological brains and artificial systems.

## Context
The paper situates LLMs within the ongoing debate about anthropomorphism in AI, highlighting that many observed behaviors are not unique to humans but reflect shared computational strategies. It underscores how advances in deep learning echo insights from cognitive neuroscience dating back decades.

## Implications
Recognizing these parallels can guide more effective model design and interpretability efforts, reducing speculative “alien intelligence” narratives. Practitioners may leverage human‑inspired principles to improve efficiency, safety, and alignment of LLM applications.
