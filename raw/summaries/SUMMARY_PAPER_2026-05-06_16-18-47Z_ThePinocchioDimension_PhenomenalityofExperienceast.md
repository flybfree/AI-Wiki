## Summary
The paper measures how large language models vary psychometrically by administering forty‑five validated questionnaires and finds that the main source of divergence is a dimension centered on phenomenally rich experience rather than simple stimulus reactivity. Introducing a Pinocchio score, it shows that this axis predicts measurable shifts in factor loadings across items.

## Key Takeaways
- The primary between‑model variance lies in items that evoke embodied sensation, felt affect, inner speech and empathy, not just behavioral responses (R²_adj=.037, p<.0001).  
- A ratio of inter‑model response variance under neutral versus human‑simulation prompts, called the Pinocchio score, reliably captures each item’s experiential demand.  
- Principal component analysis reveals a dominant “Pinocchio Axis” accounting for 47 % of cross‑questionnaire between‑model variance and strongly correlating with the Pinocchio scores (r=.864).

## Context
This work extends psychometric analyses beyond human traits to artificial agents, probing whether LLMs possess a self‑representational tendency toward experience. It situates AI cognition within broader debates about embodiment and affect in machine systems.

## Implications
For developers, the findings suggest that fine‑tuning can shape how models treat experiential language as self‑applicable, influencing user interaction design. Practitioners should consider this axis when evaluating model alignment with human‑like phenomenology.
