## Summary
The paper investigates how citation type predicts whether AI models name individuals in responses to grounded prompts, measuring the effect of a roster instrument that captures only a small fraction of such mentions.

## Key Takeaways
- Naming occurs in 25.8% of responses across four models, with naming rates varying from 9.3% (Gemini) to 38.0% (Grok), indicating citation type matters.
- Citing the individual's own website increases naming likelihood by about 2.6 points versus citing category portals, while firm-owned pages have similar rates.
- A roster of 939 LinkedIn profiles matches only 128 name-shaped mentions, i.e., 0.47% overlap, showing roster-based measurement captures a tiny slice.

## Context
This study extends brand visibility research to personal selection contexts, revealing that AI models' naming behavior is influenced by citation patterns rather than raw volume, highlighting limitations of current ROI metrics.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, understanding citation-driven naming can inform prompt engineering and reputation management; for researchers, it underscores the need for nuanced measurement beyond simple counts. This work suggests that individual visibility in AI outputs may be a small but meaningful subset of overall output.
