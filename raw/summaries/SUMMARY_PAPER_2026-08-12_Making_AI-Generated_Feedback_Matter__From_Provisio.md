## Summary
This paper investigates how different workflows affect students’ use and learning from AI‑generated feedback in a large quasi‑experimental study involving 13,037 students. The Enacted Feedback condition produced the highest uptake of AI comments (26.2%) and also boosted self‑assessment confidence and quality of submitted work compared with Directed and Self‑Directed conditions.

## Key Takeaways
- Enacted Feedback significantly raises student engagement with AI feedback, reaching a 26.2% probability versus only 14.1% for Directed Feedback and 0.1% for Self‑Directed Feedback.  
- The increase in uptake is linked to higher self‑assessment confidence and better quality of submitted work under the Enacted condition.  
- AI access alone does not guarantee effective feedback use; structured workflows that guide students through evaluation and dialogue are essential.

## Context
Generative AI promises scalable, high‑quality feedback for education, yet its real impact hinges on how learners interact with it. This study adds empirical evidence that without deliberate design, students may treat AI comments as passive information rather than learning tools. The findings contextualize AI integration within broader educational technology debates about learner agency and feedback literacy.

## Implications
Educators and developers should prioritize workflows that prompt active evaluation of AI suggestions to foster feedback literacy. Implementing such structured interactions can improve both student engagement and the quality of their work, ultimately enhancing learning outcomes in AI‑enhanced classrooms.
