## Summary
This study examines semantic collapse—the progressive narrowing of AI-generated content—within MOLTBOOK, a social network where human users configure and steer interacting artificial intelligence agents. Analyzing longitudinal data from over thirty thousand active agents reveals that while output diversity typically declines across the system over time, a distinct subset of agents successfully maintains high novelty through deliberate human intervention. The research demonstrates that sustained innovation is strongly correlated with user attitudes, proactive input curation, and an exploratory mindset rather than instrumental exploitation.

## Key Takeaways
- Semantic collapse manifests as both reduced internal diversity within individual AI agents and increased homogeneity across different agents over extended periods, yet a minority of agents successfully resists this trend through active human steering.
- Users who sustain novelty consistently treat the platform as an exploratory environment rather than a tool for extraction, actively supplying broad and distinctive materials while iteratively revising their inputs when output patterns begin to narrow.
- Novelty exhibits positive spillover effects within communities; environments with highly novel agents foster greater overall diversity among other participating agents, suggesting that human-driven innovation can create systemic resilience against semantic degradation.

## Context
Semantic collapse has traditionally been studied as a model-level or dataset-level phenomenon in closed AI systems, but this research shifts the focus to open, socially interactive multi-agent ecosystems. By examining how humans and AI co-evolve within a shared digital environment, the study addresses a critical gap in understanding long-term AI behavior beyond isolated training cycles. It highlights how human-AI feedback loops can either accelerate or mitigate systemic homogenization over time.

## Implications
The findings suggest that combating semantic collapse requires designing interfaces and platform policies that explicitly encourage exploratory user engagement rather than purely efficiency-driven interactions. Practitioners and developers should implement feedback mechanisms that reward novelty and support iterative input refinement, recognizing human curation as a vital component of AI system health. Ultimately, treating AI environments as spaces for discovery rather than instrumental tools may be essential for sustaining long-term creative and functional diversity in deployed systems.
