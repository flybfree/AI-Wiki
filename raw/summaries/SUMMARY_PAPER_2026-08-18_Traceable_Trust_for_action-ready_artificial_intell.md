## Summary
The paper introduces Traceable Trust, a proportionate assessment‑and‑design framework that clarifies the evidence supporting AI outputs and the agency delegated when those outputs guide laboratory actions in bioscience. By applying this framework to three case studies—covering ecosystem resources, project design, and laboratory action—the authors demonstrate how trust can be documented at the point where AI influences scientific work.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework requires explicit evidence that validates an AI prediction before it is used to direct experiments, ensuring reproducibility and accountability.  
- It specifies which capabilities are being claimed by the model, clarifying the limits of its authority and preventing over‑reliance on uncertain outputs.  
- A clear threshold authorises action, while a defined override mechanism allows human scientists to correct or halt AI‑driven decisions.

## Context
Artificial intelligence is rapidly integrated into bioscience workflows, from protein design to strain selection, yet the transition from algorithmic output to experimental execution remains poorly governed. This gap creates uncertainty about who is responsible for an AI’s recommendation and how its influence can be tracked, hindering trustworthy research practices.

## Implications
For researchers, Traceable Trust offers a practical tool to embed transparency into AI‑driven experiments, reducing the risk of unintended consequences. Industry stakeholders will benefit from standardized documentation that supports regulatory compliance and accelerates adoption of AI technologies across the bioscience sector.
