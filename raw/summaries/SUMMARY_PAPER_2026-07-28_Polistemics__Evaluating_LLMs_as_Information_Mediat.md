## Summary
This paper proposes Polistemics, a benchmark to evaluate large language models as mediators of political information in elections, emphasizing epistemic responsibility over mere reproduction. Experiments on the 2025 German and Dutch elections reveal that while LLMs perform well under clear evidence, they fail when information is absent, vague, or contradictory, flattening political language intensity.

## Key Takeaways
- High aggregate scores mask systematic failures of models to handle missing, ambiguous, or conflicting political data.  
- Reliable mediation depends on the clarity and consistency of input evidence, not just model capability.  
- Model outputs are influenced by party priors shaped by labels and output language, causing biased intensity reduction.

## Context
The rapid adoption of LLMs in political communication has raised concerns about their role as information intermediaries rather than passive text generators. This work addresses a gap where prior benchmarks focus on factual accuracy without considering the epistemic agency of citizens or how models interact with imperfect real‑world data.

## Implications
Policymakers and developers must design evaluation frameworks that capture both performance metrics and ethical considerations like epistemic modesty. Ignoring these dimensions could lead to systems that appear competent yet fail to support informed democratic participation.
