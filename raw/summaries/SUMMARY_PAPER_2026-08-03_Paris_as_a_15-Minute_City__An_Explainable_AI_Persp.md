## Summary
The paper investigates how the availability of local services within a short walk or bike ride influences mobility patterns in Paris using mobility trajectories and socioeconomic data. It finds that higher point‑of‑interest density reduces private car use for short trips but this effect is weaker outside central areas. Explainable AI models highlight trip purpose, home‑work distance, service availability, vehicle ownership, public‑transport subscription and sociodemographic factors as key predictors.

## Key Takeaways
- High POI density correlates with lower car use for short trips, especially in the inner agglomeration.
- Car ownership and licence availability increase predicted car use when services are scarce.
- Public‑transport subscription is linked to reduced car dependence under low service conditions.

## Context
This work advances explainable AI applications in urban mobility research by linking statistical models to policy‑relevant variables. It shows how machine‑learning interpretability can complement traditional accessibility metrics, providing transparent insights into why certain mobility choices occur.

## Implications
Policymakers and planners can use these findings to design service networks that maximize active travel and minimize car dependence. Practitioners should consider local heterogeneity when applying 15‑minute city concepts, ensuring interventions are tailored to specific demographic and spatial contexts.
