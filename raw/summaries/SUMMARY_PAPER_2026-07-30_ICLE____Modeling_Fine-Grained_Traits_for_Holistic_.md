## Summary
This paper introduces ICLE++, a new annotated corpus of persuasive student essays that includes both holistic scores and trait‑specific scores. The authors argue that existing AES models, which rely only on the ASAP dataset, may lack generalizability, so ICLE++ provides a more comprehensive benchmark for testing generalization and evaluating emerging multi‑trait scoring approaches.

## Key Takeaways
- ICLE++ expands the annotation set beyond holistic scores to include detailed trait‑specific evaluations, enabling finer‑grained analysis of essay performance.  
- The corpus is designed to test whether models trained on ASAP can generalize to other evaluation settings such as multi‑trait and cross‑prompt scoring.  
- By combining holistic and trait scores, ICLE++ offers a richer signal for researchers to improve model robustness across diverse assessment contexts.

## Context
Automated essay scoring (AES) has seen rapid advances in deep learning models that predict overall quality from raw text. However, most research focuses on the ASAP corpus, which may not reflect real‑world diversity of student writing styles or trait emphasis. This work addresses a gap by providing an annotated dataset that captures both holistic and granular trait information.

## Implications
ICLE++ will serve as a valuable resource for AI researchers aiming to develop fairer, more nuanced AES systems. Practitioners in education technology can leverage the trait‑specific scores to fine‑tune models for personalized feedback, ultimately improving student learning outcomes through targeted assessment.
