## Summary
This paper surveys how commonsense reasoning is being woven into computer vision, moving beyond object detection to holistic scene understanding. It reviews methods that use knowledge graphs, scene graphs, neuro‑symbolic models, and commonsense‑augmented transformers, while highlighting gaps such as dataset bias and integration challenges.

## Key Takeaways
- Knowledge graph based approaches enable visual systems to query relational facts, improving spatial reasoning about object interactions.  
- Neuro‑symbolic hybrids combine neural perception with symbolic knowledge representation, offering a path toward interpretable vision models.  
- Current datasets often lack diverse commonsense instances, leading to biased or incomplete scene understanding.

## Context
The field of computer vision is shifting from isolated pixel analysis to contextual scene comprehension, driven by the need for AI that can interact meaningfully with humans and environments. This paper situates these advances within ongoing debates about integrating symbolic knowledge into deep learning pipelines.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, commonsense‑aware vision can lead to more reliable autonomous systems in logistics, healthcare, and robotics. Practitioners should prioritize data diversity and hybrid architectures to mitigate bias while building models that truly understand everyday scenes.
