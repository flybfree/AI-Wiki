## Summary
This paper introduces ScienceArena, an olympiad‑style benchmark that aggregates problems from thirteen public science competitions across physics, chemistry and biology to test the reasoning abilities of large language models. The study demonstrates that several recent LLMs achieve scores comparable to medalists on these exams, while highlighting persistent weaknesses in chemistry and long‑horizon consistency.

## Key Takeaways
- Medalist notes indicate that failures often arise from visual grounding, structure fidelity, and global problem control rather than missing terminology.
- Two strong LLM judges remain within one point of expert total scores when calibrated against medalist ground truth on archived answers.
- Top models obtain medal‑equivalent rubric scores on several public international exams, confirming the benchmark’s effectiveness for evaluating scientific reasoning.

## Context
ScienceArena addresses a growing challenge in AI research: distinguishing genuine scientific understanding from superficial pattern matching. By using process‑credit rubrics and expert‑audited data, it provides a more realistic metric than simple answer‑matching benchmarks. This work aligns with efforts to create domain‑specific evaluations that reflect the complexity of real‑world problem solving.

## Implications
For researchers, ScienceArena offers a scalable way to compare LLMs on scientific tasks without costly human grading. For industry practitioners, it suggests that models must improve visual and structural reasoning to be competitive in high‑stakes scientific applications.
