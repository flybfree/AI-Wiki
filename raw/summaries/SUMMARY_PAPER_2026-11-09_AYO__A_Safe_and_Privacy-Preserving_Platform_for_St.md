## Summary
The paper introduces AYO (AI Youth Open Science), an open-source research platform designed to enable safe, systematic, and privacy-preserving study of how children and adolescents interact with large language models. The platform addresses critical gaps in existing research by providing child-friendly interfaces, configurable age-based safety guardrails, researcher monitoring, and privacy-preserving data practices, enabling cross-sectional, longitudinal, and experimental studies on children's development in relation to conversational AI.

## Key Takeaways
- AYO provides a child-friendly interface with configurable age-based safety guardrails and researcher monitoring capabilities, directly addressing the substantial challenges of safety, privacy, consent, and experimental control that have previously hindered direct study of children's interactions with LLMs. This infrastructure allows researchers to move beyond survey-based methods and observe actual interaction patterns in controlled settings.
- The platform is specifically designed to support cross-sectional, longitudinal, and experimental research designs, enabling investigators to study how children use and respond to LLMs over time and how specific AI design choices may shape trust, epistemic judgment, curiosity, and other developmental outcomes. This multi-method approach is critical for understanding causal relationships rather than mere correlations.
- AYO is positioned as open-source infrastructure intended to generate evidence that guides safer AI systems, educational practices, and policy decisions for children growing up alongside AI. The privacy-preserving data practices embedded in the platform ensure that sensitive data from minors is handled responsibly while still enabling rigorous scientific inquiry.

## Context
Children and adolescents are increasingly engaging with generative AI tools in everyday life, yet the scientific community has lacked adequate tools to study these interactions directly and safely. Prior research has relied heavily on surveys and self-reported data, which cannot capture the real-time dynamics of how children form beliefs, trust, and behavioral patterns through conversation with LLMs. AYO fills this infrastructure gap by providing a purpose-built research environment that respects the ethical and developmental constraints unique to studying minors, positioning itself within the broader movement toward responsible AI development and child-centered technology design.

## Implications
For researchers and educators, AYO offers a reproducible, open-source tool that can standardize how children's AI interactions are studied across institutions, potentially accelerating evidence generation about developmental impacts of conversational AI. For industry practitioners and policymakers, the platform generates actionable evidence that can inform the design of safer AI products for young users, shape educational curricula addressing AI literacy, and guide regulatory frameworks governing children's access to generative AI systems. The open-source nature of the platform also democratizes access to this research infrastructure, enabling broader participation from the global research community in shaping child-safe AI standards.
