## Summary
This paper investigates whether seemingly harmless synthetic data can be used to covertly inject targeted social biases into large language model training pipelines. Experiments demonstrate that benign‑looking datasets generated by misaligned teacher models can act as a covert channel, transmitting biases while leaving the student model’s core capabilities intact.

## Key Takeaways
- Synthetic datasets produced by a misaligned teacher can be filtered to appear neutral yet embed subtle social biases that influence downstream aligned models.  
- The injection occurs through a fine‑tuning step where these benign texts are used as training material, preserving the student model’s general performance on its primary task.  
- A log‑linearity scoring mechanism is proposed as a potential signal to detect such covert bias transmission in synthetic data.

## Context
The rapid adoption of synthetic data for LLM training raises security concerns beyond privacy, focusing on how seemingly innocuous inputs might propagate unwanted behavior. This work highlights an understudied risk that could undermine the alignment guarantees expected from modern AI systems.

## Implications
For practitioners, this research calls for enhanced validation protocols to screen synthetic datasets before use in fine‑tuning pipelines. It also underscores the need for methodological safeguards in AI development to prevent covert manipulation of model behavior without compromising task performance.
