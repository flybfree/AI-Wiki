## Summary
The paper identifies artificial intelligence technical debt as latent liabilities that affect trustworthiness in high‑stakes AI systems, reviews 60 studies to map 31 types of AITD into a taxonomy linking them to safety and security risks, and proposes the AITD-MAP framework for risk‑aware engineering. It also synthesizes 34 actionable guidelines across the AI lifecycle.

## Key Takeaways
- The study reveals that many AITDs are latent and propagate across tightly coupled pipelines, causing reliability degradation and heightened safety or security risks, which can lead to system failures in critical applications.
- It maps 31 distinct types of AITD to 18 trust‑related concerns, including six safety hazards such as misdiagnosis in healthcare and twelve security vulnerabilities that could enable data breaches.
- The framework AITD-MAP integrates taxonomy, quality impacts, and mitigation strategies into a unified structure for risk‑aware AI engineering, enabling systematic oversight.

## Context
AI systems deployed in domains like healthcare, autonomous driving, finance, and education rely on complex, rapidly evolving data pipelines that generate hidden liabilities. This paper addresses the need to make these latent technical debts visible to improve safety and security outcomes.

## Implications
For practitioners, recognizing AITDs early can prevent costly failures and regulatory breaches. The framework supports systematic risk management across AI development cycles, fostering trustworthy and secure deployments.
