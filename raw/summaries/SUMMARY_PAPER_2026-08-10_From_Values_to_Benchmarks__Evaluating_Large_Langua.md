## Summary
The paper introduces Grip on LLMs, a framework for evaluating Dutch governmental language models. It finds that models trade off environmental cost and financial expense for quality and that factuality and honesty are separate traits. The authors release an overview tool for stakeholders.

## Key Takeaways
- No single model excels across all evaluation dimensions, requiring trade‑offs between quality, energy use, and cost.
- Higher factual accuracy does not guarantee higher honesty; models can be correct yet unaware of uncertainty.
- Bias remains largely independent of both environmental impact and financial expense.

## Context
The rapid adoption of large language models in public administration demands evaluation methods that respect ethical values and non‑English linguistic needs. This work shows that existing benchmarks often ignore sustainability and cost, limiting responsible deployment.

## Implications
Governments must balance performance with environmental and financial constraints when selecting LLMs. The released overview helps policymakers understand trade‑offs without technical expertise, fostering transparent decision‑making.
