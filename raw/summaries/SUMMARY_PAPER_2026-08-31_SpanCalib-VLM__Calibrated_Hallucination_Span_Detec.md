## Summary
The paper introduces SpanCalib-VLM, a hybrid system that pairs a multimodal sequence tagger with a generative vision‑language model to detect hallucinated text spans in LVLMs. By fusing the tagger’s calibrated probabilities with the generator’s candidate spans through a Union‑Calibrated Fusion strategy, the ensemble achieves strong calibration and detection performance on the SHROOM‑Visions English split.

## Key Takeaways
- The hybrid approach combines XLM‑RoBERTa‑Large fused with SigLIP via cross‑attention to produce a discriminative tagger that provides calibrated confidence scores.  
- Candidate spans generated by Qwen3.5‑4B‑SHROOM‑SFT are re‑scored using the tagger’s probabilities, improving overall IoU and calibration correlation.  
- The ensemble reaches a Pearson calibration correlation of 0.41, an overall IoU of 0.39, clean‑response IoU of 0.91, and detection accuracy of 70.7%.

## Context
Large vision‑language models often generate hallucinated text that is hard to locate with reliable confidence. Traditional methods either sacrifice speed for calibration or recall, creating a trade‑off that limits practical deployment.

## Implications
This work demonstrates that calibrated hallucination detection can be achieved without sacrificing inference efficiency, offering a scalable solution for real‑world applications such as content verification and automated fact‑checking in multimodal AI systems.
