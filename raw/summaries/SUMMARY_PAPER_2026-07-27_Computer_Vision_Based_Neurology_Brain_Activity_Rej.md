## Summary
This paper presents a computer vision based architecture that automates the labeling of independent components in EEG data, significantly speeding up preprocessing for cognitive development studies. The system integrates with popular tools such as ICLabel and EEGLab, achieving an accuracy of 89.45% while reducing processing time by 7200 fold compared to manual analysis.

## Key Takeaways
- The proposed computer vision pipeline replaces the labor‑intensive manual selection of independent components, delivering a consistent classification outcome with high precision.
- Accuracy reaches 89.45%, which is sufficient for near real‑time applications in medical research and clinical decision support.
- The tool’s compatibility with existing EEG software interfaces ensures seamless adoption across diverse research environments.

## Context
Automated EEG source separation remains a bottleneck because each component must be inspected by an expert, limiting the scale of large‑scale studies. Advances in AI and computer vision offer promising solutions that could democratize access to high‑quality data analysis.

## Implications
Researchers can now process thousands of EEG recordings without prohibitive time costs, accelerating discovery of cognitive changes linked to disorders. Practitioners benefit from faster turnaround times for diagnostic evaluations and more reliable source localization, enhancing both clinical practice and scientific insight.
