## Summary
The paper introduces SwarmWorld, a simulation where homogeneous language-model agents self‑organize into technological societies without predefined roles or communication protocols. It demonstrates that decentralized agents can build functional artifacts and outperform isolated search baselines through collaborative construction and persistent artifact networks.

## Key Takeaways
- Agents evolve distinct behaviors such as exploration, construction, maintenance, and coordination, reflecting a shift from individual to collective function as the simulated world matures.
- Technological portfolios become broader and more resilient than strong best‑of‑N isolated‑search baselines because collaboration enables reuse through physical observation rather than direct communication.
- Explicit cultural mechanisms enhance organization but functional benefits depend on both outcome quality and timescale, showing that stigmergy alone can support capable societies.

## Context
This work addresses a longstanding challenge in multi‑agent AI: how decentralized systems can generate complex, persistent technologies without centralized control. It highlights the potential of language models to simulate emergent social structures akin to biological ecosystems, offering insights into collective learning and resource management.

## Implications
For practitioners, SwarmWorld suggests that designing agents around shared physical environments may yield more robust solutions than purely communicative approaches. In industry, this could inform the development of autonomous systems where local interaction drives innovation without explicit instruction.
