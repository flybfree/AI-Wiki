## Summary
This paper examines how generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) reshapes academic writing and publishing, focusing on the inclusion of World Englishes. Through a dialogue among five sociolinguists, it argues that while GenAI can democratise scholarly production, it also risks marginalising minority language varieties and flattening nuance if not ethically designed.

## Key Takeaways
- The article highlights how GenAI tools may reinforce existing hierarchies by privileging dominant English norms over minoritised varieties.  
- It stresses the need for equity‑informed policies that embed critical AI literacy into scholarly communities to prevent linguistic injustice.  
- Inclusive co‑design of GenAI systems is presented as a crucial strategy to preserve nuance and agency in academic discourse.

## Context
The rapid integration of generative models into research workflows has sparked debate about their impact on language diversity, prompting scholars to reassess the ethical dimensions of AI use in publishing. This study situates these concerns within broader sociolinguistic discussions on World Englishes and institutional responsibility.

## Implications
For researchers and publishers, the findings suggest that without deliberate safeguards, GenAI could exacerbate linguistic inequities in scholarly communication. Implementing inclusive design practices is essential to ensure that AI supports rather than undermines linguistic diversity in academia.
