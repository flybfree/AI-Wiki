## Summary
This paper introduces EP-Mem, an Elastic Privacy Memory architecture designed to mitigate privacy risks for Large Language Model agents acting as delegates in human-agent-human communication by enforcing context-dependent social information disclosure boundaries based on long-term relational dynamics. The system utilizes a token-level memory stratified by user-configurable policies and a pluggable privacy engine that aligns controls across summary, detail, and boundary granularities throughout the agent's lifecycle. Experimental results demonstrate significant improvements in privacy classification accuracy and disclosure-permission judgment while reducing privacy leakage by 75.6% compared to baseline approaches.
