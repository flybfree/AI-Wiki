## Summary
This paper introduces Science Edge Evaluation (SEE), a multimodal benchmark designed to test large language models’ ability to perform complex scientific reasoning in chemistry, biology, and materials science. The evaluation of 19 multimodal large language models shows that even the top model achieves only 48.7% accuracy, highlighting a significant gap between current AI capabilities and real‑world experimental knowledge.

## Key Takeaways
- The best-performing model reaches 48.7% accuracy on SEE questions, indicating limited reliability in scientific inference.
- General‑purpose models outperform science‑specialized ones on average, suggesting that domain‑specific training does not guarantee better performance.
- Tool use raises the best accuracy to 52.7%, yet more information does not always translate into reliable reasoning.

## Context
The rapid integration of large language models into scientific research has sparked interest in their potential to augment human expertise. However, existing benchmarks often lack rigorous validation against peer‑reviewed literature and experimental practice, leading to overstated performance claims. This paper fills that gap by providing a concrete, expert‑curated evaluation.

## Implications
For researchers, the findings warn against assuming AI can replace careful scientific judgment without robust evidence grounding. Industry stakeholders should prioritize developing models that respect experimental boundaries rather than merely expanding knowledge pools. Practitioners must focus on building systems capable of deriving novel insights from verified data.
