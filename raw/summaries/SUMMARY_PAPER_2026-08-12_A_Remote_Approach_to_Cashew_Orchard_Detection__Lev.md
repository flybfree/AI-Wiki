## Summary
This paper presents a remote mapping approach for cashew orchards in Guinea‑Bissau using satellite imagery and active learning. The method combines Sentinel‑2 data with margin‑based active learning to select the most informative training points, producing a nationwide map with 94 % balanced accuracy without on‑site processing.

## Key Takeaways
- The study creates two open datasets and a 10 m resolution cashew map for the country using only off‑site satellite imagery.  
- Margin‑based active learning reduces the number of required training points while maximizing discriminative power, leading to high balanced accuracy.  
- The approach scales to nationwide coverage, offering a cost‑effective alternative to field surveys.

## Context
Remote sensing combined with active learning is an emerging AI technique for environmental monitoring where limited ground data are used to improve model performance. This work demonstrates how such methods can generate detailed spatial maps without expensive onsite validation.

## Implications
The generated map supports deforestation tracking, biodiversity protection, and sustainable cashew management in Guinea‑Bissau. Practitioners can leverage the dataset for policy decisions, corporate sustainability reporting, and further AI research in agroforestry monitoring.
