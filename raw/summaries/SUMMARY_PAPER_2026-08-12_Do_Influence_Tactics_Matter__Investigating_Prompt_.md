## Summary
This paper investigates whether psychologically inspired influence tactics embedded in prompt phrasing affect the performance of large language models when generating code. By operationalizing eight influence tactics into structured prompt templates and testing them on five open-weight LLMs across two benchmark suites, the authors find that urgency‑focused framings can lower functional correctness and security while other tactics may have mixed effects.

## Key Takeaways
- Urgency‑emphasized prompts reduce code functionality and introduce security vulnerabilities.  
- The study demonstrates that not all influence tactics are equally detrimental; some may improve maintainability without compromising safety.  
- Prompt framing is a critical lever for human‑AI interaction, with linguistic cues directly shaping model output quality.

## Context
Current AI research often focuses on technical improvements to LLMs, yet the subtle ways language shapes behavior remain understudied in real‑world tasks like software generation. This work bridges that gap by linking established persuasion psychology to measurable code outcomes, highlighting a missed opportunity for more nuanced prompt design.

## Implications
Engineers and developers should avoid urgent or coercive phrasing when prompting LLMs to generate reliable code. Designing transparent prompts that respect psychological cues can lead to safer, higher‑quality software systems and foster trust in AI assistance.
