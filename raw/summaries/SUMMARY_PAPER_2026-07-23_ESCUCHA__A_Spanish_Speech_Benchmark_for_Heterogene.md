## Summary
The paper introduces ESCUCHA, a benchmark for Spanish speech understanding under diverse acoustic conditions, aiming to evaluate large audio language models across reasoning abilities. It contains 1,000 human‑curated question‑audio pairs totaling 162.9 hours of real‑world recordings, covering durations from seconds to over 80 minutes and featuring multiple accents and non‑normative speech.

## Key Takeaways
- ESCUCHA provides a large, heterogeneous dataset of 1,000 question‑audio pairs spanning 162.9 hours, enabling evaluation across varied acoustic conditions.
- The benchmark includes both perceptual and reasoning categories, with nine perceptual tasks and ten reasoning tasks, highlighting the need for models to understand language beyond simple acoustics.
- It captures linguistic diversity through multiple Spanish accents and non‑normative speech, ensuring fairness in model performance measurement.

## Context
The rapid growth of large audio language models has created a demand for benchmarks that reflect real‑world listening challenges. Existing datasets often lack realistic acoustic variation and diverse speaker characteristics, limiting the validity of reported performance.

## Implications
This benchmark will guide researchers to develop models robust to real‑world speech conditions, benefiting industry practitioners who must deploy reliable voice assistants in multicultural environments.
