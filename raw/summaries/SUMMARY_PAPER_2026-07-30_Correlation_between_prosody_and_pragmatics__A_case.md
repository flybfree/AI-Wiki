## Summary
This paper investigates the multifunctional use of the Persian discourse marker hālā in spontaneous conversation, combining pragmatic analysis with prosodic cues. It finds that while temporal readings occur, they are frequently mixed with other functions, and that duration and intensity serve as primary acoustic signals distinguishing these roles.

## Key Takeaways
- Textual uses of hālā are significantly shorter than its temporal uses, suggesting a prosodic cue linking length to topic‑related signaling.  
- Interactive functions correlate with higher intensity, indicating that the marker’s pitch or loudness is heightened when managing turns or engaging listeners.  
- Modal functions such as epistemic stance and emotional expression tend toward lower intensity, reflecting a subtle prosodic contrast that signals attitude rather than action.
