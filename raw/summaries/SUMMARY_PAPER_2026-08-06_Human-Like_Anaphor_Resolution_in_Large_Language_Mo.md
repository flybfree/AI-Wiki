## Summary
This paper investigates how five open‑weight Large Language Models resolve anaphoric references, comparing their behavior to human cognition. The authors measure processing difficulty using the linking hypothesis and accuracy on comprehension questions, finding that some models align with human sensitivity to discourse prominence and distance while showing weaker or absent sensitivity to semantic interference.

## Key Takeaways
- Some LLMs exhibit human‑like sensitivity to discourse prominence and distance‑based factors in anaphor resolution.  
- These models perform poorly when semantic interference is present, indicating a lack of alignment with that cognitive factor.  
- The results demonstrate selective cognitive alignment across the tested models rather than uniform human mimicry.

## Context
Understanding anaphor resolution in LLMs bridges cognitive science and natural language processing, revealing how computational systems approximate human mental processes. This work contributes to debates on the limits of current AI in modeling discourse coherence and contextual understanding.

## Implications
The findings suggest that improving LLM performance may require targeted training on discourse structure rather than solely on large token corpora. Practitioners should consider these cognitive constraints when designing applications requiring nuanced anaphor handling.
