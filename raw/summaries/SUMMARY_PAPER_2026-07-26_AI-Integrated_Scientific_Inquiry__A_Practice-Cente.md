## Summary
This paper proposes a practice‑centered vision for integrating AI into science education, treating AI tools as authentic scientific instruments aligned with the Next Generation Science Standards. The authors demonstrate how computer vision, clustering, and generative modeling can support observing, analyzing, and modeling in inquiry while prompting critical reflection on the tools themselves. Their work outlines a pathway toward agency‑based AI that operates across the full investigative cycle.

## Key Takeaways
- Students should use simplified versions of genuine scientific instruments such as computer vision for observation, clustering for analysis, and generative models for modeling to maintain core functions while reducing complexity.
- Each AI instrument must include a distinct reflection point encouraging students to evaluate how the tool works and where it can mislead, fostering discipline‑based AI literacy.
- The authors advocate building foundational knowledge of scientific inquiry before introducing agentic AI that spans multiple practices.

## Context
The rapid integration of artificial intelligence into research practices creates an urgent need for educational frameworks that reflect real scientific workflows rather than isolated lessons. This paper contributes to that discourse by grounding AI instruction in the investigative core of science, ensuring relevance and depth for learners.

## Implications
Educators can adopt these instrument‑based approaches to make AI visible as a collaborative partner in inquiry, enhancing both scientific understanding and critical thinking. Industry stakeholders may leverage such models to develop curricula that prepare students for future AI‑augmented scientific roles.
