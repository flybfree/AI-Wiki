## Summary
The paper introduces AdaLens, an interactive system designed to monitor and steer long-running agentic data analysis workflows. It addresses the gap between conventional interfaces that lack observability and steerability for evolving analyses. Evaluation through case studies and a user study demonstrates how AdaLens improves analyst control over autonomous reasoning.

## Key Takeaways
- AdaLens uses a storyline‑based representation to unify analytical plans, execution progress, findings, and data‑column involvement, providing a coherent view of the agent’s journey.
- The system enables steerability by grounding interventions in specific analytical elements, allowing users to redirect low‑value paths or deepen promising ones without disrupting parallel branches.
- Evaluation shows that AdaLens reduces analyst cognitive load and increases confidence in long‑running analyses compared with existing turn‑by‑turn interfaces.

## Context
Long‑running agentic data analysis is becoming a core capability of large language models, enabling multi‑step reasoning across vast datasets. Traditional interactive tools were built for discrete steps, making them ill‑suited to the parallel and evolving structures typical in such workflows. This paper tackles that limitation by proposing an integrated oversight mechanism.

## Implications
AdaLens will influence how researchers and practitioners design autonomous data pipelines, encouraging more transparent and controllable AI systems. By offering real‑time steering, it can improve reproducibility and trustworthiness of large‑scale analyses, benefiting both academic studies and industry applications.
