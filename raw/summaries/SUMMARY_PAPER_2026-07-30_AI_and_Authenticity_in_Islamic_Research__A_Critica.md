## Summary
This paper evaluates six leading generative AI systems on fifty open‑ended Islamic questions covering Qur’an, Hadith, Fiqh, ethics and pastoral advice. The mixed‑method analysis shows that while the models can assist with introductory learning, they frequently generate hallucinations, lack verifiable citations, and do not consistently respect Madhhab differences or handle uncertainty appropriately.

## Key Takeaways
- AI responses often produce false or unverified religious references, indicating a high rate of hallucination in Islamic knowledge generation.  
- The systems struggle to acknowledge jurisprudential disagreement and Madhhab diversity, providing uniform answers that ignore scholarly variance.  
- Current models are deemed insufficiently reliable for authoritative rulings or scholarly research without human verification.

## Context
Generative AI is rapidly integrated into religious education and guidance worldwide, yet few studies have empirically examined its trustworthiness in high‑stakes Islamic domains. This work fills a gap by providing concrete evidence on reliability across multiple Islamic knowledge areas.

## Implications
Researchers should treat AI as a supplementary tool rather than a primary source for Islamic scholarship. Practitioners must verify outputs against authenticated texts and qualified experts to ensure doctrinal correctness.
