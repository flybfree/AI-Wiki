## Summary
This study investigates the generalization capabilities of persona effects in language models, examining whether learned behavioral relationships predict responses to novel questions and transfer across different models and prompts. The research reveals that while persona effects are often predictable within specific contexts, they lack portability; target refitting consistently outperforms borrowing gains from other model pairs or prompt variations. Furthermore, the analysis highlights that apparent transfer benefits may stem largely from calibration rather than genuine predictive value added by source relationships.

## Key Takeaways
- Persona effects exhibit a fundamental trade-off between predictability and portability: models can accurately capture persona behaviors within a fixed setup, yet these learned relationships fail to transfer effectively to new questions or different model checkpoints without significant performance degradation compared to target-specific refitting strategies.
