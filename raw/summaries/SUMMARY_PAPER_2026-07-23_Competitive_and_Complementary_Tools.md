## Summary
The paper models the human‑tool‑task interaction as a single dynamical system where competence and reliance co‑evolve, revealing a bistable outcome: tool availability either preserves competence or drives it into low dependence. Above a critical threshold the competent state collapses irreversibly, leaving users fully dependent on the tool.

## Key Takeaways
- Above a critical tool availability the competent state is destroyed and competence collapses toward a low dependent floor as the user outsources completely.
- Lowering availability does not reverse collapse until a far lower threshold, so history of practice rather than the current tool fixes the state.
- The collapse threshold depends jointly on the competence a user brings to a task and on the tool's transparency, the fraction of its working a user can reconstruct.

## Context
This work situates AI tools within a broader dynamical systems perspective, challenging the view that AI merely augments human ability but instead reshapes cognitive architecture. It highlights how large language models can become autonomous agents, transferring agency irreversibly to the tool.

## Implications
For practitioners, it suggests designing transparent, decomposable tools to preserve competence. Education must resist tool dependence by fostering deep understanding and historical practice. The paper reframes AI deployment as a choice between competence and dependency.
