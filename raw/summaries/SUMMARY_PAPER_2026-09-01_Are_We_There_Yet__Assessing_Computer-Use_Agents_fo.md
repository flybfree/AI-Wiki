## Summary
The paper investigates the performance of computer-use agents for blind screen‑reader users while interacting with desktop applications. It reports that GPT‑5 achieved a 52.5% success rate in a three‑week diary study involving eight participants. The authors also note recurring grounding and planning failures.

## Key Takeaways
- GPT‑5 reached the highest success rate at 52.5% across 1,258 commands but still fell short of human performance.
- Grounding failures dominate trace analysis, indicating agents often cannot map UI elements to actions reliably.
- Beyond automation, users require assistance with constraint tracking and termination decisions.

## Context
Computer‑use agents aim to combine natural language reasoning with visual grounding to perform GUI tasks autonomously. This study brings the first empirical evaluation of such agents in a real‑world setting for blind users, highlighting gaps between theory and practice.

## Implications
The findings suggest that current CUA systems need stronger multimodal grounding mechanisms before they can be trusted for assistive technology deployment. Practitioners should incorporate iterative user feedback to refine planning and constraint handling.
