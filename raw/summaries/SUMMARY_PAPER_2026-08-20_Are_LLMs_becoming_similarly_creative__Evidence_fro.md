## Summary
The paper analyses LLM creative outputs over three years using Infinity-Chat100 and Alternate Uses Task, finding a significant decrease in output diversity suggesting convergence across models. The analysis uses sentence‑embedding similarity to quantify diversity, revealing that newer models produce fewer distinct responses than earlier ones.

## Key Takeaways
- statistically significant decrease in model output diversity over time  
- suggests LLMs may be converging in creative substance across models  
- could diminish human agency in human‑AI co‑creative work

## Context
This study addresses the gap between verifiable benchmarks and open-ended creativity, highlighting that current progress is measured by diversity loss rather than quality improvement. It underscores a shift from purely quantitative performance metrics to qualitative concerns about originality and uniqueness in AI-generated content.

## Implications
If trends continue, AI‑generated content may become homogenized, reducing opportunities for unique human‑AI collaboration; researchers must consider ethical implications of LLM homogenization in creative industries. This could affect design processes, marketing, and education where originality is valued, prompting a reevaluation of how to preserve diversity in AI outputs.
