## Summary
This paper introduces WDL‑OPD, a mixture‑constrained co‑training method that stabilizes on‑policy distillation by training two policies simultaneously: an anchor policy generates rollouts and an auxiliary policy evaluates visited states. By matching the geometric mixture of their token distributions to a frozen teacher via reverse KL, both policies receive gradients. Experiments show that WDL‑OPD outperforms single‑policy OPD configurations across multiple scale settings.

## Key Takeaways
- Freezing the auxiliary policy recovers an anchor‑plus‑contrast proxy target closely related to OPD² and W2S‑OPD, demonstrating that the auxiliary can be treated as a static delta.  
- Joint training of both policies introduces branch‑level degrees of freedom that a static delta cannot express, enabling more expressive updates.  
- In Qwen3 experiments at 1.7B and 4B scales, WDL‑OPD achieves the highest student checkpoint performance in four scale‑domain settings.

## Context
On‑policy distillation aims to align a student model with a teacher using trajectories generated by the student itself, which mitigates state mismatch but often suffers from instability due to feedback loops. Recent advances have explored co‑training or hybrid approaches, yet few provide rigorous comparisons across diverse curricula and initializations.

## Implications
WDL‑OPD offers a more stable and expressive alternative for training large language models, potentially reducing the need for complex curriculum design. Practitioners can leverage this method to improve model performance without sacrificing training efficiency, especially in resource‑constrained settings where single‑policy OPD may degrade.
