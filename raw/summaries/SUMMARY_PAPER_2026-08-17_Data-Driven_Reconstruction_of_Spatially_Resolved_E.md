## Summary
This paper presents a deep learning approach to reconstruct spatially resolved electron and ion energy distributions from measured macroscopic plasma observables, demonstrating that such data contain enough information to infer key kinetic properties of low‑temperature plasmas. Using paired datasets generated by 2D‑3V PIC‑MCC simulations, three neural network architectures—U‑Net, fully connected neural ODE (FNO), and MeshGraphNet—are trained to map observables to energy density functions. The FNO achieves the best reconstruction accuracy, recovering both bulk plasma and sheath characteristics with high fidelity.

## Key Takeaways
- The study shows that macroscopic plasma diagnostics can be inverted to obtain detailed EEDFs/IEDFs without assuming a specific distribution shape such as Maxwellian.  
- Among the evaluated models, the fully connected neural ODE (FNO) outperforms traditional convolutional networks and graph‑based methods in reconstructing both density and temperature profiles.  
- Physics‑based validation confirms that the reconstructed EDFs correctly predict plasma reaction rate coefficients, proving the inverse mapping’s reliability.

## Context
This work bridges AI research with plasma physics by applying deep learning to an inverse problem that is otherwise intractable for conventional methods. It highlights how neural networks can serve as surrogate models for complex kinetic simulations, reducing computational cost while preserving essential physical insights.
