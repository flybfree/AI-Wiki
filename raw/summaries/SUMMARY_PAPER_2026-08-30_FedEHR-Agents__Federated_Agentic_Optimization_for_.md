## Summary
The paper introduces FedEHR‑Agents, an experience‑centric federated learning framework that lets autonomous clinical EHR agents share and refine their modeling experiences across hospitals without moving patient data. Experiments on multi‑hospital benchmarks show the approach consistently beats local and federated baselines in prediction tasks and works well at various federation scales and LLM backbones.

## Key Takeaways
- FedEHR‑Agents treats clinical modeling experience as a federated resource, aggregating preprocessing steps, model development, historical memory, task evaluation, and TextGrad prompt refinement across hospitals.  
- The framework aggregates this experience with evidence‑guided methods to create global meta‑prompts that improve local agents without exposing raw patient data.  
- Results demonstrate superior performance over both non‑federated and federated baselines across diverse prediction tasks, showing robustness to scale changes and different LLM models.

## Context
Federated learning has been applied mainly to model parameters, leaving the richer experiential knowledge generated by autonomous agents underutilized. This work bridges that gap by treating experience as a shareable asset, aligning with trends toward privacy‑preserving AI collaboration in healthcare.

## Implications
Hospitals can now collaborate on clinical modeling without sharing sensitive patient records, accelerating innovation while maintaining compliance. Practitioners should consider federated experience aggregation when deploying autonomous EHR agents to maximize model performance and adaptability across institutions.
