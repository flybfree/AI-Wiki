## Summary
This paper introduces a multi‑agent embodied conversational system designed to generate English dialogue at CEFR‑aligned proficiency levels for learners experiencing foreign language anxiety. A pilot study with Japanese university students compared the simplified output of this system to that of an unsimplified agent, finding 87.4 % of sentences matched the learner’s self‑assessed level versus 54.1 % for the unsimplified version.

## Key Takeaways
- The multi‑agent loop with a level classifier produces dialogue that aligns closely with the learner’s perceived CEFR level, significantly improving relevance compared to random LLM output.
- Simplified conversational content reduces linguistic demand, which may alleviate anxiety by preventing overwhelming complexity in early learning stages.
- Although the small sample did not reach statistical significance for FLA reduction, the usability findings highlight a promising design approach for adaptive language agents.

## Context
The rise of large language models has opened new avenues for personalized language tutoring, yet many systems lack mechanisms to match linguistic output with learner proficiency. This work addresses that gap by embedding CEFR‑based level assessment within an embodied agent framework, illustrating how AI can adapt not only content but also interaction style to support affective learning.

## Implications
For educators and developers, the findings suggest that integrating proficiency‑aware generation into conversational agents can enhance learner engagement and reduce anxiety. As AI tutoring tools become more common in language education, such adaptive designs will be crucial for scalable, effective second‑language instruction.
