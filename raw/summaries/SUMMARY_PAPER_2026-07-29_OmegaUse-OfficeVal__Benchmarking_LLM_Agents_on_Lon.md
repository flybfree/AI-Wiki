## Summary
OmegaUse-OfficeVal introduces a benchmark that evaluates large language model agents on long‑horizon office‑suite tasks, providing task‑level economic grounding by pairing each task with human labor time and a price proxy. The study finds that all evaluated LLMs are substantially cheaper and faster than human workers, yet their deliverable quality remains below the human baseline.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark consists of 100 tasks requiring an average of 2.32 hours of human labor to complete.
- LLM inference costs are lower than human labor costs for all evaluated models.
- Despite cost advantages, LLMs have not yet matched human‑level output quality.

## Context
The rapid advancement of language models has prompted a need for rigorous evaluation methods that consider both performance and economic impact. Traditional benchmarks often ignore real‑world labor costs, making it difficult to assess whether AI agents can replace human workers in office environments profitably. OmegaUse-OfficeVal addresses this gap by integrating measurable economic signals into the evaluation framework.

## Implications
For researchers, the benchmark offers a standardized way to compare LLM capabilities against tangible business metrics such as cost and time savings. For industry stakeholders, it highlights that while LLMs can reduce expenses, they still fall short of delivering the quality expected from human agents. Practitioners should use this data to set realistic expectations and plan phased integration strategies for office‑suite automation.
