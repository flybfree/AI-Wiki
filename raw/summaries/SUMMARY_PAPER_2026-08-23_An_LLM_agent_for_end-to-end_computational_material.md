## Summary
The paper introduces MAESTRO, an LLM agent that automates the full computational materials discovery pipeline for metal‑organic frameworks. By linking literature to crystal structures and curating a computation‑ready database, it screens candidates progressively by cost and identifies high‑performance separation materials from unrelated studies.

## Key Takeaways
- The system integrates diverse algorithms into a single LLM agent that handles data extraction, structure retrieval, and multi‑stage screening without manual intervention. - It demonstrates that promising MOF candidates for wet flue gas separation come from completely different research papers, highlighting the value of cross‑study synthesis. - Progressive computational cost scaling allows efficient identification of high‑performance materials while avoiding exhaustive searches.

## Context
This work advances AI’s role in materials science by replacing fragmented toolchains with a unified language model that can orchestrate heterogeneous stages of discovery. It illustrates how large language models can serve as intelligent pipelines, reducing reliance on specialized software and enabling rapid iteration across application domains.

## Implications
For researchers, MAESTRO offers a scalable framework to explore vast material spaces without exhaustive computational budgets. Industry stakeholders can leverage the agent’s ability to surface high‑value candidates from disparate literature, accelerating development timelines for sustainable separation technologies.
