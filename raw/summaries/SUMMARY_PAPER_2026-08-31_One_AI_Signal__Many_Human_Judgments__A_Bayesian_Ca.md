## Summary
This paper introduces a Bayesian cascade model that treats AI credibility indicators as shared public signals, examining how they interact with individual user judgments in the spread of online information. The analysis reveals that while AI can improve crowd accuracy, over‑reliance on weak or single AI signals may preserve incorrect predictions by blocking corrective private impressions.

## Key Takeaways
- AI changes what public history means: crowd agreement may stem from accumulated independent human evidence or repeated reliance on the same AI prediction.  
- Stronger reliance on AI can both preserve correct predictions and lock in incorrect ones, creating a preservation‑correction trade‑off.  
- Individual users vary widely, discounting the AI completely to relying on it enough to cascade, affecting overall crowd performance.

## Context
The study addresses a growing need for rigorous analysis of how algorithmic signals influence human decision‑making in information ecosystems. By extending classic Bayesian cascade theory with an AI indicator as a public signal, the work bridges machine‑learning research and social science, offering a framework for understanding real‑world misinformation dynamics.

## Implications
For platform designers, this insight suggests that diversifying AI signals across users can mitigate over‑reliance on weak indicators. Practitioners should therefore consider how to balance AI assistance with human judgment to maintain an informative public discourse.
