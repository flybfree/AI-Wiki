## Summary
The paper examines how agentic AI, which enables systems to act on behalf of users, compares its outcomes with four established domains such as ad blockers, recommender engines, robo‑advisors and spam filters. It finds that technical choices about what agents can do are often shaped by political decisions that depoliticize governance.

## Key Takeaways
- The improvement in spam inbox quality occurred because the system was technically configured to filter messages, but the collective ability of users to challenge those configurations disappeared.
- When proprietary infrastructure and closed standards absorbed contestation, user‑aligned agency became less durable and displacement increased.
- Agentic AI’s governance is being consolidated around the Model Context Protocol and the Agentic AI Foundation before user preferences can influence its actions.

## Context
This work situates agentic AI within a broader history of digital intermediaries where agency was mediated by technical standards. The study highlights that similar patterns of depoliticization appear in contemporary AI development, suggesting a recurring tension between efficiency and democratic oversight.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings warn that without open governance structures users may lose influence over algorithmic decisions. Industry should prioritize transparent protocols to preserve contestation capacity alongside technical performance.
