## Summary
This paper introduces a new framework called constitutive AI unaccountability to describe situations where AI accountability is conceptually impossible due to the way actors, systems, and institutions are structured. The authors demonstrate that nine categories of unaccountability arise in practice, including an inverted anthropomorphism configuration, and they validate the framework with a diagnostic set of 20 questions applied to the OpenClaw open‑source agentic AI system.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper argues that some AI accountability gaps are not merely technical problems but are rooted in the very configuration of actors, systems, and institutions, making them unachievable regardless of standards or reforms.  
- It identifies nine constitutive categories of AI unaccountability organized into structural, technological, and normative clusters, showing how they reinforce each other through eight directed interdependencies.  
- The framework is operationalized as a 20‑question diagnostic tool that revealed seventeen of the twenty conditions in OpenClaw, highlighting an example where the AI agent is the only identifiable actor.

## Context
The rapid deployment of autonomous, agentic AI systems creates complex sociotechnical environments where traditional accountability models break down. This work situates these challenges within a broader research agenda that seeks to move beyond incremental fixes toward understanding fundamental limits of responsibility in AI ecosystems.

## Implications
For researchers and practitioners, the constitutive framework offers a diagnostic lens for spotting accountability voids before they become crises. It also calls for policy and design interventions that address systemic configurations rather than only technical transparency measures.
