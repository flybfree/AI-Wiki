## Summary
The paper introduces FLARE, a systematic and uncertainty‑aware framework that evaluates the financial and operational implications of AI adoption in healthcare. It combines fuzzy logic, time‑driven activity‑based costing, and ROI analysis to estimate costs across development, operation, and service delivery under uncertain conditions. The case study on AI‑assisted large vessel occlusion detection shows a break‑even threshold of about 3,992 patients per year.

## Key Takeaways
- FLARE quantifies conventional pathway cost, AI‑related development and recurring costs, and AI‑enabled savings within a unified activity‑based model.
- The analysis identifies a break‑even point around 3,992 patients annually, with positive first‑year ROI at typical stroke volumes of roughly 5,000 patients per year.
- Economic benefit depends on patient volume, verification time, infrastructure choices, and workflow design, not just algorithmic performance.

## Context
Current AI evaluations in healthcare focus heavily on model accuracy while neglecting the broader economic impact. This gap hinders decision‑makers from assessing whether an AI tool is truly viable for routine clinical use. FLARE addresses this by embedding financial and operational considerations into a transparent evaluation process.

## Implications
Clinicians, administrators, and policymakers can now use FLARE to make informed choices about AI deployment based on real‑world resource constraints. By making uncertainty and trade‑offs explicit, the framework supports early‑stage adoption decisions that align with both clinical outcomes and fiscal sustainability.
