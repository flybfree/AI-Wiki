## Summary
The paper introduces SpatialWorld, a benchmark that tests multimodal large language models' ability to solve interactive spatial tasks in real-world scenarios. It evaluates 15 advanced agents across eight simulation backends and reports average task success rates ranging from 14% to 17%, showing limited performance despite advanced capabilities.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark demonstrates that even state-of-the-art models like GPT‑5 struggle with interactive spatial reasoning, achieving only about 17.4% success on tasks requiring active exploration and long‑horizon planning.
- Task success correlates poorly with execution efficiency, indicating that agents often fail to plan effectively despite completing many steps.
- Performance varies significantly across domains, revealing a lack of generalizable spatial understanding beyond the specific simulation environment.

## Context
Current multimodal models excel at static visual question answering but rarely face real‑world interaction where partial observation and active decision making are required. This gap limits their practical deployment in robotics, home automation, and collaborative AI systems.

## Implications
For researchers, SpatialWorld provides a standardized test to prioritize improvements in exploration algorithms and long‑term planning strategies. For industry, it signals that reliable spatial agents remain an open challenge before widespread adoption of multimodal assistants in physical environments.
