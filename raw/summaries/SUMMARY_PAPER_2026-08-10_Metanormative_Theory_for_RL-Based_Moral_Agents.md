## Summary
This paper bridges metanormative theory and reinforcement learning to create criteria for classifying an RL agent’s behavior as moral. It proposes a framework that interprets ethical norms through the lens of normative justification, enabling systematic evaluation of value‑aligned agents. The authors demonstrate how this theoretical lens can be applied directly to RL architectures.

## Key Takeaways
- Metanormative theory provides a principled way to assess whether an agent’s actions are justified by higher‑order moral norms, not merely by reward signals.
- The paper links these normative justifications to specific RL components such as reward shaping and exploration strategies, offering concrete criteria for moral classification.
- By integrating metanormative concepts with RL design, the authors suggest a method for comparing different value‑alignment approaches on ethical merit.

## Context
