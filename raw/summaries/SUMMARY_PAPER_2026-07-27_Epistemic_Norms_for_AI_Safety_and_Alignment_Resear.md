## Summary
This paper argues that mainstream AI research focuses on capability growth while safety work must address rare catastrophic failures under uncertain conditions. It identifies two independent axes — capability profile and risk profile — where current epistemic practices fall short, and proposes the Epistemic Code for AI Safety and Alignment (ECAISA) to improve documentation, verification, and trust.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper distinguishes between a capability profile that shows no hazardous behaviours and a risk profile that bounds worst‑case outcomes under fat‑tailed uncertainty.  
- It highlights the near‑absence of institutionalised independent verification as a critical gap in alignment research.  
- ECAISA provides an eight‑principle framework, scoring rubric, disclosure ladder, tiered applicability, adjudication procedure and anti‑gaming mechanisms to constrain how safety claims are documented.

## Context
AI safety is increasingly recognised as complementary rather than derivative of capability development, yet most funding and publication incentives still reward high average performance. The paper’s bibliometric baseline shows that verification processes remain fragmented, limiting the reliability of safety assessments in a field where rare failures can be catastrophic.

## Implications
For researchers, ECAISA offers a concrete governance model to make safety claims auditable without certification, encouraging more rigorous documentation. For industry and policymakers, it sets a standard for responsible AI development that balances transparency with confidentiality concerns.
