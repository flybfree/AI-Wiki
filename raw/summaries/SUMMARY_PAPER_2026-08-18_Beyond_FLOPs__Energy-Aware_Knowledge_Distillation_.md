## Summary
This paper investigates whether the traditional measure of Floating Point Operations (FLOPs) reliably predicts energy consumption for large language models used in software engineering tasks such as clone detection and vulnerability prediction. The authors conduct experiments with Morph, a many‑objective optimization distillation method, and compare FLOP‑based estimates to those generated by direct energy surrogates that model CPU and GPU power usage.

## Key Takeaways
- FLOPs does not consistently correlate with actual energy use; surrogate models provide more accurate predictions. 
- Energy‑aware distillation using surrogates can cut inference energy consumption up to 90% while reducing memory footprint by about 86%. 
- The accuracy loss from these efficiency gains is modest, indicating a good trade‑off between performance and sustainability.

## Context
Large language models dominate many software engineering applications but their heavy compute demands strain both hardware resources and the environment. Traditional cost metrics like FLOPs often overlook real power draw, leading to suboptimal model selection for edge devices.

## Implications
For practitioners, adopting energy surrogates in distillation pipelines can produce deployable LLMs that fit consumer hardware without sacrificing much accuracy. This approach supports greener AI practices and broader adoption of SE tools beyond data‑center scale.
