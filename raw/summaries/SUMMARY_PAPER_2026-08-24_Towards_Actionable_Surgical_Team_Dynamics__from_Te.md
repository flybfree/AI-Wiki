## Summary
The paper introduces an extended multimodal dataset for surgical team interaction analysis, building on existing operating room recordings to provide speaker diarization, transcripts, and multi-level annotations that capture performance, interaction processes, and individual traits. It also adds counterfactual annotations describing plausible alternative outcomes when interactions fail, enabling computational modeling of teamwork dynamics.

## Key Takeaways
- The dataset integrates multimodal data—audio, video, text—into a single analysis-ready format with speaker diarization and structured transcripts.
- Counterfactual annotations are introduced to model how observed interaction failures could have led to different team outcomes, supporting studies of breakdowns and variability.
- The annotations follow standardized protocols for performance evaluation and non‑technical skills, allowing systematic comparison across teams.

## Context
In high‑stakes AI research, capturing the nuanced coordination between human agents is essential yet challenging due to fragmented data sources. This work bridges that gap by delivering a unified resource that can be leveraged for both empirical analysis and model training in collaborative environments.

## Implications
The dataset will enable researchers to evaluate how individual actions and interaction patterns jointly influence safety outcomes, informing the design of AI‑assisted tools that augment rather than replace human teamwork. Practitioners can use these insights to refine protocols and improve real‑world surgical coordination.
