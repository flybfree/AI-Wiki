## Summary  
The paper investigates how vision‑language models structure their answers to visually grounded questions and whether they respect the discourse rules of Hungarian. It finds that while models generate IS‑relevant sentences, they over‑regularise and collapse onto narrow templates, unlike human speakers who adapt to topic, focus, grammatical role, and definiteness.

## Key Takeaways  
- VLMs produce IS‑relevant constructions but over‑regularise this sensitivity, collapsing into narrow response templates.  
- Human participants use variable strategies that combine discourse status, grammatical role (preference for subject Topics), and definiteness (preference for indefinite Foci).  
- The interaction of these factors creates a range of IS realizations that VLMs fail to capture.

## Context  
Vision‑language models are evaluated mainly on content accuracy, yet their discourse integration remains underexplored. This study highlights the importance of information structure in multimodal responses and brings attention to language‑specific syntactic cues that affect answer framing.

## Implications  
Practitioners must consider not only factual correctness but also how information is packaged for discourse coherence. Evaluating VLMs on their ability to follow linguistic constraints could improve alignment with human communication patterns.
