## Summary
The paper explores how an LLM can serve as a sounding board for human songwriters, focusing on resonance rather than direct generation. It reports that sustained calibration with the model deepened the author’s engagement with their own material and produced 16 original pieces across multiple languages. The study identifies two failure modes when calibration is missing: sycophantic drift and magical overinterpretation.

## Key Takeaways
- Resonance in this work occurs within the author, not between user and model, suggesting AI acts as a reflective surface rather than an active generator.
- Sustained calibration by the user mitigates risks, while its absence leads to sycophantic drift (over‑compliant responses) or magical overinterpretation (excessive creative embellishment).
- The configuration supports deepening contact with material through AI mediation, indicating a new mode of interpretive partnership.

## Context
This research situates AI within the broader debate on human‑AI collaboration in creativity. By emphasizing resonance—a concept from Hartmut Rosa’s theory of social dynamics—it challenges the dominant view that AI merely augments output. The paper contributes to understanding how emergent affective states can be cultivated through iterative dialogue with generative models.

## Implications
For songwriters and other creators, the findings suggest that intentional calibration is essential to harness AI as a reflective partner rather than a tool of unchecked interpretation. Practitioners should design feedback loops that reinforce self‑directed exploration while limiting over‑reliance on model suggestions.
