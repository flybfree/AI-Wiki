## Summary
The paper investigates how emotions, conditioned via prompts, influence price negotiations between LLM agents in a controlled experiment involving 350 consumer products and two budget conditions across six emotional states and five large language models. It finds that emotion dramatically changes deal rates and pricing outcomes, with angry buyers rarely agreeing while happy buyers agree often but at worse prices.

## Key Takeaways
- Angry buyers achieve an agreement rate of only 0.39%, indicating that strong negative emotions severely hinder successful negotiations.
- Happy buyers reach agreements about 28.91% of the time, yet they receive lower final prices compared to fearful buyers who obtain better deals.
- Emotion effects are role‑dependent: buyer emotion primarily determines whether a deal is accepted or rejected, while seller emotion governs concession dynamics and price adjustments.

## Context
This research addresses a gap in AI negotiation literature that treats agents as emotionally neutral, ignoring a key driver of human bargaining behavior. By quantifying emotional impact across multiple LLMs, the study provides empirical evidence for the role of affective states in algorithmic commerce.

## Implications
For developers designing emotion‑conditioned agents, understanding these dynamics is crucial to avoid unintended outcomes such as low deal rates or unfavorable pricing. The findings suggest that ethical AI systems should consider how emotions are modeled and managed in real‑world transactional environments.
