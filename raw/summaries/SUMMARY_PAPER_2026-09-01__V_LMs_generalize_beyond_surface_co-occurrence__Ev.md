## Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models can learn abstract grammatical number concepts beyond surface-level co-occurrence patterns. By using cross‑modal examples that combine visual and textual cues, the authors show that VLMs generalize across both modalities and treat linguistic versus extra‑linguistic information similarly internally.

## Key Takeaways
- The model’s representations for numbers are not limited to lexical frequency effects; it can infer number from visual stimuli alone.  
- Cross‑modal exposure conditions produce comparable internal dynamics, indicating shared learning mechanisms for abstract abstraction.  
- This evidence supports the claim that statistical learners like VLMs generalize beyond surface co‑occurrence and exhibit genuine rule‑like behavior.

## Context
Understanding how language models capture syntactic concepts is crucial for building systems that are robust to distributional biases. Prior work has relied on text alone, which may mask deeper abstraction. This study bridges that gap by leveraging multimodal data, offering a clearer view of model learning processes.

## Implications
For developers, the findings suggest that multimodal training can yield more reliable and interpretable models for tasks involving number agreement. Practitioners should consider cross‑modal evidence when evaluating model generalization beyond surface statistics.
