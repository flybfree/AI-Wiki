## Summary
The paper challenges the notion that all large language model outputs belong to a single “AI language” and instead shows that each model exhibits its own linguistic profile, similar to human idiolects. It compares two corpora—one from 2024 using six models and another from 2026 with the same prompts—and finds both a generational shift in style across cohorts and persistent individual differences among models.

## Key Takeaways
- Each model retains a unique linguistic profile, evidenced by contraction frequencies ranging over 1,200 to over 30,000 per million words within the same cohort.  
- Computational descriptors and stylometric principal component analysis reveal a generational shift between the 2024 and 2026 cohorts of model outputs.  
- Treating LLM output as idiolectal provides a useful framework for studying variation, change, detection, forensic linguistics, and usage‑based language models.

## Context
The rapid evolution of large language models has led researchers to treat their outputs as a homogeneous product, obscuring the subtle stylistic fingerprints that distinguish individual systems. This paper highlights how such idiolectal variation can influence model comparison, bias assessment, and downstream applications.

## Implications
Recognizing idiolectal differences encourages more nuanced evaluation of LLM performance across time and models. Practitioners can leverage these signatures for detection tasks, forensic analysis, and developing usage‑based language strategies that respect individual model quirks.
