## Summary
This paper proposes a quantitative, multi-dimensional framework for evaluating the creativity of AI-generated music, building on Margaret Boden's theoretical model of creativity defined through value, novelty, and surprise. Rather than collapsing musical creativity into a single score, the authors introduce a domain-specific ensemble of complementary metrics that capture distinct dimensions of musical artifacts, enabling researchers to assess AI-generated compositions with greater objectivity and rigor.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework operationalizes Margaret Boden's three pillars of creativity—value, novelty, and surprise—into concrete, domain-specific quantitative metrics tailored to the musical domain, moving beyond vague qualitative assessments toward measurable evaluation criteria that can be applied consistently across AI-generated musical outputs.
- The authors explicitly reject the reduction of musical creativity to a single unifying measure, instead proposing an ensemble of complementary metrics that acknowledge the inherent complexity of musical artifacts and the difficulty of defining meaningful multi-objective compositional criteria, thereby preserving the multidimensional nature of what constitutes creative music.
- The framework is designed with a dual purpose: it provides researchers and developers with principled quantitative tools for empirically evaluating creative outputs to inform model development and reduce unnecessary computational costs, while simultaneously laying the groundwork for future generative models to optimize directly for creativity by adopting these metrics as explicit learning objectives during training.

## Context
As generative AI systems have advanced dramatically in producing music, poetry, visual art, and other creative outputs, the field has lacked rigorous, standardized methods for evaluating whether these outputs genuinely exhibit creativity or merely mimic stylistic patterns. This gap is particularly acute in music, where creativity involves complex interactions of harmony, rhythm, structure, emotional resonance, and cultural context. By grounding evaluation in Boden's well-established creativity theory and translating it into quantitative musical metrics, this paper addresses a critical methodological void that has hindered progress in both AI music generation research and broader computational creativity studies.

## Implications
For AI researchers and developers, this framework offers a principled methodology that can guide model architecture choices, training strategies, and evaluation pipelines, potentially reducing wasted computational resources spent on models that produce technically proficient but creatively shallow outputs. For the music industry and creative AI practitioners, the framework opens a path toward generative models that are explicitly optimized for creativity as a measurable objective, which could shift AI music tools from novelty-driven novelty engines toward systems that genuinely produce valuable, surprising, and novel compositions worthy of human creative engagement.
