## Summary
The paper argues that while neural theorem provers have made impressive progress, they only hint at a larger opportunity for AI to transform mathematics and deepen collaboration with mathematicians. It proposes an optimistic vision where AI augments rather than replaces human insight in mathematical research.

## Key Takeaways
- Neural theorem provers demonstrate capability but their successes mask the broader potential of AI to reshape how mathematics is performed and understood.
- The essay advocates for a proactive integration of AI tools into mathematical practice, encouraging mathematicians to view AI as a supportive partner rather than an endpoint.
- This optimistic outlook calls for research that explores new applications beyond automated proof checking, such as hypothesis generation and exploratory reasoning.

## Context
In the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence, advances in machine learning have sparked debates about their role in scientific domains. Mathematics remains a discipline where rigorous logic is paramount, making its integration with AI both challenging and valuable.

## Implications
For mathematicians, embracing AI could unlock new avenues for discovery and deepen understanding of complex structures. For the broader field, this shift may inspire interdisciplinary projects that blend logical rigor with computational creativity, advancing both theory and practice in mathematics.
