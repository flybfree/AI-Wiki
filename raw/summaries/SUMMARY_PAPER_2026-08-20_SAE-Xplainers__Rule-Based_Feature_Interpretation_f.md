## Summary
This paper introduces SAE‑Xplainers, a method that combines geographic input modulation with rule‑based interpretation to make deep learning models of extreme Earth events more interpretable. The approach improves reconstruction quality and enables human‑readable rules that align with scientific knowledge while revealing feature absorption.

## Key Takeaways
- Geographic location‑based modulation enhances SAE performance by tailoring inputs to local environmental patterns, leading to better reconstruction and higher feature utilization.  
- The ensemble of rule‑based SAE‑Xplainers translates high‑dimensional features into explicit rules that are consistent with established climate science.  
- Feature absorption is identified as a mechanism where certain predictors dominate the model’s decision process, providing insight into model behavior.

## Context
The integration of large weather and climate datasets into deep learning models has accelerated research on extreme events but often suffers from black‑box predictions that hinder operational use. SAE‑Xplainers address this gap by preserving scientific interpretability while leveraging modern neural architectures.

## Implications
These rules can be directly incorporated into early warning systems, offering stakeholders clear explanations of model decisions and supporting trust in AI‑driven climate services. Practitioners may adopt the method to improve transparency without sacrificing predictive power.
