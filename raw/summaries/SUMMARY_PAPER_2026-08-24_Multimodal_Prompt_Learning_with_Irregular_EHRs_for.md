## Summary
This paper introduces a multimodal prompt‑learning framework designed to improve critical care prediction when electronic health records (EHRs) contain missing or irregular modalities such as physiological time series and clinical notes. The method integrates four distinct prompts—generative, missing‑signal, missing‑type, and temporal—and demonstrates superior performance across two missingness scenarios compared with existing approaches.

## Key Takeaways
- Generative prompts create surrogate latent representations for modalities that are absent, allowing the model to infer their meaning from available data.  
- Missing‑signal prompts explicitly differentiate between observed clinical signals and those generated by the framework, preventing overfitting to synthetic information.  
- Temporal prompts condition aggregation on specific time‑series configurations, capturing modality‑specific dependencies while respecting missingness patterns.

## Context
In AI for healthcare, multimodal EHRs combine structured physiological streams with unstructured clinical notes to support decision making. However, real‑world data often suffer from partial or complete modality loss, degrading model reliability and leading to unsafe predictions in intensive care settings.

## Implications
The framework provides a practical solution that can be deployed in ICU monitoring systems where sensor failures or documentation gaps are common. By ensuring robust predictions even with incomplete records, it reduces the risk of missed interventions and enhances patient safety across clinical workflows.
