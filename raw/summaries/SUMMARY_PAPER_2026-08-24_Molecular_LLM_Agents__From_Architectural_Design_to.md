## Summary
The paper proposes a dual‑perspective framework for molecular LLM agents that bridges architectural design and scientific autonomy. It introduces an architectural view covering representation, perception, toolboxes, and learning, and a ladder of four autonomy levels from assistive to agenda‑driven agents. The combined model helps compare existing systems, spot gaps, and guide future development.

## Key Takeaways
- Molecular LLM agents require chemically faithful perception across strings, graphs, 3D structures, spectra, simulations, and wet‑lab data.
- A four‑level autonomy ladder—L1 to L4—offers a scalable taxonomy from fixed workflows to scientific agenda setting.
- The framework enables systematic comparison of current agents and identification of missing capabilities or deployment risks.

## Context
Molecular science demands integration of symbolic, graph, and experimental data that most general AI systems cannot handle. This work addresses the need for domain‑specific perception and autonomy in a field where errors can have real‑world consequences. By proposing a unified design perspective, it moves beyond isolated experiments toward robust agent pipelines.

## Implications
The framework can streamline research on molecular discovery by providing clear benchmarks and risk assessments. Practitioners may adopt the ladder to incrementally build agents that evolve from simple assistants to fully autonomous scientists. This could accelerate drug discovery, materials development, and other chemistry‑driven industries.
