## Summary
The paper audits a maximum‑variation purposive corpus of 40 empirical records published between 18 July 2025 and 17 July 2026, examining how the age of the newest named generation influences the credibility of AI‑generated evidence. It also treats its own two‑day production as a reflexive case of frontier‑model‑assisted research creation, showing that rapid AI assistance can be inspected without treating model output as an independent validation.

## Key Takeaways
- The median age of the newest named generation is 281 days, with ages ranging from 11 to 939 days, indicating variable model maturity across the corpus.
- Thirty‑five records involve a superseded family, seven have precise dated identifiers, three show refreshed models, and one adds a late sensitivity test, highlighting frequent model turnover and reporting challenges.
- The paper distinguishes model age from claim currency and outlines six reporting practices to make AI‑assisted research inspectable.

## Context
This work addresses the growing reliance on generative‑AI tools in empirical research, where rapid model updates can obscure provenance and reproducibility. By auditing a curated set of records, it reveals how model age influences evidential weight and underscores the need for transparent reporting practices that acknowledge the tool’s role as an assistance rather than a source.

## Implications
Researchers must adopt systematic ways to track model versions and claim currency to avoid misleading conclusions in AI‑assisted studies.
