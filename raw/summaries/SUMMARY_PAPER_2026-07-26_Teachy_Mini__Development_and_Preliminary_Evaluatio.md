## Summary
This paper presents Teachy Mini, a knowledge‑based generative social robot built on the Reachy Mini platform that implements key design requirements for responsible tutoring in higher education. A preliminary study with 24 participants compared Teachy Mini to a control robot and found it was perceived as more responsibly aligned with teaching behavior while showing consistent use of personalization, grounded explanations, Socratic questioning, affective support, and learner‑anchored feedback. Although acceptance, motivation, and learning gains were not significantly different, exploratory analyses hinted at potential benefits when learner preferences are considered.

## Key Takeaways
- Teachy Mini integrates knowledge‑based design principles into a generative social robot to promote responsible tutoring behavior in higher education.
- The robot’s consistent application of personalization, slide‑grounded explanations, Socratic questioning, affective support, and feedback was observed across the study session.
- Preliminary results indicate no major differences in acceptance, motivation, or learning effectiveness between Teachy Mini and a control robot.

## Context
Generative social robots powered by large language models are being explored as tools for personalized education, yet their deployment raises concerns about misinformation and lack of transparency. This work contributes to the field by operationalizing knowledge‑based design requirements into a tangible system, providing empirical evidence on how such design can shape responsible robot behavior.

## Implications
For educators integrating AI tutors, Teachy Mini demonstrates that embedding clear informational prerequisites can guide robots toward more trustworthy interactions. Practitioners should consider these design principles when developing or evaluating generative social robots to ensure they support learning without unintended negative consequences.
