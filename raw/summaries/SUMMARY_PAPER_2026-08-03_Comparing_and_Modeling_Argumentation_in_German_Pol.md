## Summary
The paper aims to compare argumentation patterns in German political communication across three arenas while keeping the COVID‑19 topic constant. It presents a 17 k‑sentence annotated corpus and a pilot study on automatic detection of argumentative passages. The analysis shows that justification by expert knowledge appears more often in press conferences than in committee meetings, contrary to expectations.

## Key Takeaways
- The corpus reveals that justification by domain‑specific expertise is more frequent in press conferences than in committee meetings despite the same topic.
- Automatic boundary detection proves difficult because argument boundaries are not clearly marked in the text.
- Models of identifying argumentative passages exhibit confirmation bias, leading to predictions that align with existing expectations.

## Context
This work contributes to AI research on multimodal argumentation analysis by focusing on political discourse. It highlights the challenge of extracting structured argument components from unstructured speech transcripts, a problem relevant for natural language processing and dialogue systems. The findings illustrate how domain‑specific communication styles affect computational models.

## Implications
For researchers, the study underscores the need for better annotation strategies to capture nuanced justification in political texts. For industry practitioners, it suggests that automatic argument detection tools should account for bias and boundary ambiguity when applied to real‑world speech data.
