## Summary
This study evaluates the performance of GPT‑5.4 in inductive content analysis of open‑ended survey responses from a European PhD student sample, comparing it with five human coders using a standardized coding scheme. The results show that agreement between humans and the LLM is moderate, with an Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) of 0.61 for coding and 0.54 for theme generation, values near the internal consistency of both human and LLM outputs.

## Key Takeaways
- The ARI scores indicate a reasonable level of agreement between human coders and GPT‑5.4, suggesting the model can approximate human judgment in this specific task.  
- Within‑entity consistency is low for certain variables and correlates with lower between‑entity agreement, highlighting how data characteristics affect reliability.  
- The LLM’s internal ARI (0.76) exceeds that of humans (0.68), implying the model may be more consistent than human coders in this context.

## Context
The rapid adoption of large language models for qualitative research has prompted calls for empirical evidence on their analytical capabilities, especially in inductive coding where nuanced interpretation is required. This paper contributes to that discourse by providing a concrete benchmark using a real‑world survey dataset and a well‑established prompting protocol.

## Implications
Practitioners can leverage GPT‑5.4 as a scalable tool for preliminary coding, reducing manual workload while maintaining acceptable reliability. However, the observed variability across variables underscores the need for human oversight to address data‑driven inconsistencies in inductive analysis.
