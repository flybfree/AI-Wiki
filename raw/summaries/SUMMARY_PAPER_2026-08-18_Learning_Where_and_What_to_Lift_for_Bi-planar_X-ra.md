## Summary
The paper proposes LiftXR, an interleaved geometry‑guided framework that recovers CT volumes from bi‑planar X‑ray projections by first generating a 3D anatomical layout and then refining it with perceptual feedback. The interleaved design ensures that layout feedback directly influences intensity calibration. Experiments on two public datasets show LiftXR surpasses recent X‑ray‑to‑CT methods and yields higher quality reconstructions for downstream segmentation.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework separates spatial recovery from intensity estimation, allowing the layout to guide region‑specific calibration during reconstruction.
- A 3D anatomical parser uses both boundary and intensity cues to improve the initial layout generated by X‑ray projections.
- LiftXR consistently outperforms state‑of‑the‑art methods on benchmark datasets, producing CT volumes that support better external segmentation.

## Context
Reconstructing medical images from limited views remains a critical challenge in AI‑driven radiology. Traditional approaches struggle with depth ambiguity and blurred intensity distributions, limiting clinical utility. These limitations hinder real‑time applications and increase reliance on noisy reconstructions, which can propagate errors to downstream tasks.

## Implications
This work opens new avenues for integrating anatomical priors into reconstruction pipelines, potentially reducing false positives in automated diagnosis. Practitioners can leverage LiftXR to generate more accurate CT volumes from sparse X‑ray data, improving diagnostic workflows without additional hardware. The approach also reduces computational overhead by avoiding explicit depth maps, making it suitable for edge devices.
