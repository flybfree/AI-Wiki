## Summary
This paper introduces HERMES, a multi‑agent framework designed to extract structured geoscience data from ultra‑long scientific texts and visual elements. Applied to the 55‑volume Treatise on Invertebrate Paleontology, HERMES generated a database of 32 277 fossil taxa and 451 878 attributes with high accuracy.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework achieved average F1 scores near 0.90 for entities and 0.91 for attributes, indicating reliable extraction across diverse fossil groups.  
- Extraction efficiency improved sixfold compared to a fully manual baseline, demonstrating substantial productivity gains.  
- The system transferred performance between geoscience domains such as palaeomagnetism and geochemistry without additional training.

## Context
The accumulation of authoritative scientific literature in unstructured formats limits its utility for modern data‑driven research. Existing extraction methods struggle with the scale and complexity of geological monographs, creating a bottleneck for knowledge reuse.

## Implications
HERMES offers a sustainable infrastructure that can transform historical geoscience texts into FAIR‑oriented datasets, enabling large‑scale integration across institutions and applications. Practitioners can leverage this pipeline to accelerate discovery and reduce manual curation costs in the field.
