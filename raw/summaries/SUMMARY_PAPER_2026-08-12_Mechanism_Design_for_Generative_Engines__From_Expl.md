## Summary
This paper addresses the strategic tension between content creators who seek model citations and platforms that must maintain answer quality, proposing a mechanism design framework to resolve it. Through repeated Stackelberg simulations the authors show that citation competition can lead to deteriorating document quality, and they introduce VCR—a verifiable‑content reward system—that aligns creator incentives with trustworthy attribution.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper demonstrates that GEO attacks produce rewrites that sacrifice factual accuracy while still attracting citations.  
- A local best‑response analysis reveals that citation competition can converge to an inert outcome where no player gains a net benefit.  
- VCR improves the Net defense‑utility score by 12.1 percentage points over the strongest baseline, achieving a win‑win outcome under empirical equivalence.

## Context
Generative engines now allocate attention and value through citations, turning this resource into a competitive arena that threatens both platform trustworthiness and content integrity. The study situates this issue within AI‑driven web ecosystems where model outputs influence user behavior and downstream services.

## Implications
For researchers, the findings highlight the need for incentive structures beyond simple penalties to preserve answer quality in generative systems. Practitioners can adopt VCR to foster sustainable collaboration between creators and platforms, reducing citation wars and enhancing overall system performance.
