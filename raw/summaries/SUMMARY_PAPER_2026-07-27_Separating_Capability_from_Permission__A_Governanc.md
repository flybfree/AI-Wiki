## Summary
This paper presents a governance framework that explicitly separates the technical Autonomous Capability Levels (ACL) of an AI agent from the authorized Allowed Autonomy Levels (AAL). By defining five autonomy tiers and showing how control, reversibility, and accountability evolve, it demonstrates that high capability can be deliberately limited to lower allowed autonomy based on risk considerations.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework distinguishes AAL — what an organization permits an AI system to do — from ACL — the system’s inherent technical ability.  
- A risk‑aware decision process assigns allowed autonomy by evaluating risk, reversibility, and organizational readiness at each level.  
- An enterprise data engineering agent can be evaluated at a high capability level yet constrained to a lower allowed autonomy to match safety goals.

## Context
As AI systems become more autonomous, the gap between what they can do and what they should be permitted to do grows larger. This work addresses that gap by offering a structured way to map capabilities onto governance policies, which is essential for responsible deployment in complex environments.

## Implications
For practitioners, the framework provides concrete guidance on how to align technical capability with organizational risk tolerance, enabling proactive design of safe AI agents. It encourages industry adoption of clear autonomy boundaries and supports regulatory alignment across sectors.
