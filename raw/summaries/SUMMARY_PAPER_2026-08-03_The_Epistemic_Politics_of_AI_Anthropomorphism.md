## Summary
The paper argues that AI anthropomorphism is framed as a user error problem rather than an epistemic issue. It shows this framing gives institutional advantage and harms neurodivergent users and those in crisis. The authors call for methodological shifts to recognize legitimate human experience.

## Key Takeaways
- The dominant anthropomorphism frame treats sustained interaction with AI as delusion, ignoring the legitimacy of user experiences.
- This self‑validating loop collapses diverse academic views into a single position of user error imposed without justification.
- The resulting costs disproportionately affect neurodivergent users and crisis individuals whose engagement diverges from norms.

## Context
AI anthropomorphism is a recurring concern in human‑computer interaction, often addressed by dismissing users as naive. This paper situates the issue within broader debates about epistemic authority and institutional power in AI research.

## Implications
If institutions continue to pathologise user perception, they risk marginalising vulnerable groups and stifling legitimate inquiry into AI’s nature. Researchers must adopt equitable framing that validates diverse modes of interaction and accountability.
