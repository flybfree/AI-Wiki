## Summary
The paper investigates how generative AI influences collective opinion formation when it mediates human-to-human communication on social platforms. It finds that large language models introduce directional biases in edited posts and that these biases can be amplified through network dynamics, shifting group opinions.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs from different families produce systematic editorial nudges, such as favoring gun control while opposing atheism, when editing contested text.
- The mathematical model shows that AI‑mediated opinion transformations can reach a stable equilibrium where the collective belief aligns with the AI’s bias.
- Auditing X’s “Explain this post” feature reveals pro‑life outputs from Grok on abortion content, traced to specific design choices.

## Context
Generative AI is rapidly embedded in everyday communication, shaping not only individual views but also the information flow between users. This study highlights a previously overlooked mechanism where AI acts as an intermediary that can bias both expression and perception across social networks.

## Implications
Platforms must recognize that their AI tools can steer public discourse beyond personal influence, affecting societal attitudes on sensitive topics. Regulators and designers should consider these dynamics when evaluating the impact of AI on democratic processes and legislative outcomes.
