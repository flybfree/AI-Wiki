## Summary
This paper examines how large language models generate compliance artifacts for EU regulations such as the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation and GDPR. It finds that lax prompt guidance leads to inconsistent or incomplete outputs, while strict formatting rules improve consistency but can increase hallucinations. The study compares model performance against manually created reference schemas.

## Key Takeaways
- Less strict DPIA formatting demands higher‑quality prompts to achieve completeness and avoid omissions.  
- Stricter DBP formatting yields consistent results across prompts yet may produce factual errors or fabricated data.  
- Prompt specificity directly influences the reliability of LLM‑generated compliance artifacts.

## Context
The integration of LLMs into regulatory workflows is accelerating, but existing research rarely addresses how ambiguous regulations and fragmented data affect model output quality. This study fills that gap by linking prompt design to artifact fidelity in real‑world compliance scenarios.

## Implications
Practitioners must craft precise prompts aligned with regulation specifics to balance consistency and factual accuracy. Failure to do so can lead to non‑compliant documentation, legal risk, and loss of stakeholder trust.
