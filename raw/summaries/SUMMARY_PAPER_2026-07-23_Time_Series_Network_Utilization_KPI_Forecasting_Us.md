## Summary
The paper aims to forecast network bandwidth utilization using a range of machine learning models and evaluates them on a benchmark dataset. It identifies that advanced deep learning architectures such as bidirectional LSTMs achieve the lowest error metrics while balancing computational cost.

## Key Takeaways
- Seasonal decomposition and Prophet provide baseline accuracy but are outperformed by tree‑based models like Random Forest and XGBoost.
- Deep learning models, especially bidirectional Convolutional LSTMs, deliver the best performance with low MAPE and NRMSE.
- The study highlights a trade‑off between model complexity and computational efficiency, guiding practitioners to choose models that match their infrastructure constraints.

## Context
In AI research, forecasting problems are increasingly tackled by hybrid architectures that combine traditional statistical methods with neural networks. This work contributes to the discourse on scalable network resource planning.

## Implications
For network engineers, the findings suggest that investing in GPU‑enabled deep learning pipelines can yield measurable savings in over‑provisioning costs. Business owners gain a data‑driven basis for capacity budgeting, improving both financial and service quality outcomes.
