## Summary
The paper investigates how to maximize performance of a transformer language model given fixed compute budget, showing that current models are undertrained relative to optimal size and token count. It discovers that scaling both parameters proportionally yields best results under limited resources.

## Key Takeaways
- Larger models trained with more tokens achieve higher perplexity when compute is held constant.
- The relationship between model capacity and training data scales linearly, indicating efficient use of compute.
- Prior approaches often ignore this optimal tradeoff, leading to suboptimal performance.

## Context
This work aligns with the trend toward compute-efficient AI development, emphasizing that scaling laws govern model behavior. It contributes to understanding how resource allocation impacts large language model capabilities.

## Implications
Practitioners can allocate training budgets more effectively by following the identified scaling law, reducing waste and improving cost per token. The findings guide future research on efficient model design and deployment.
