## Summary
The paper aims to examine how culturally loaded expressions affect LLM‑based machine translation using the Chinese‑Japanese Dream of the Red Chamber dataset. It identifies three challenges: performance gaps, human evaluation disagreement, and metric unreliability. These findings suggest that current LLM MT systems need cultural adaptation.

## Key Takeaways
- Frontier LLMs show noticeable performance drops when translating culturally embedded content from Dream of the Red Chamber, especially idiomatic references and historical allusions.
- Human evaluators with different cultural backgrounds produce substantial disagreements in translation judgments.
- Common automatic metrics cannot reliably assess quality for this culturally specific task.

## Context
Machine translation research often assumes linguistic equivalence across cultures, yet real‑world texts embed idioms and historical references that LLMs may misinterpret. This study highlights a gap between technical capability and cultural nuance in AI systems.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, ignoring cultural context can lead to inaccurate translations affecting brand perception. Researchers must develop evaluation frameworks that respect cultural dimensions beyond surface language.
