## Summary
The paper reviews 22 fairness metrics used to assess bias in hiring AI and finds they often quantify severity but ignore uncertainty. It then applies risk‑science principles to a resume‑screening case study and proposes an AI Risk Report Card for communicating risks to stakeholders.

## Key Takeaways
- Most fairness evaluation methods focus on measuring the magnitude of bias rather than the confidence or uncertainty around those measurements.
- The literature lacks systematic ways to characterize both the likelihood that bias occurs and how severe it might be, which risk science seeks to address.
- The proposed AI Risk Report Card offers a framework for reporting risk assessments in a way that stakeholders can act upon.

## Context
AI systems increasingly influence employment decisions, making fairness evaluation critical. Current scholarly practices often treat fairness as a deterministic outcome without addressing uncertainty, limiting their usefulness beyond the lab.

## Implications
Integrating risk science into AI evaluation will improve transparency and decision‑making for policymakers and employers. A standardized reporting tool can help align technical assessments with real‑world risk management goals.
