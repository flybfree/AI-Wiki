## Summary
This paper introduces GeoBenchLLM, a benchmark designed to evaluate large language models on geo-related tasks by combining twelve diverse public datasets. The study demonstrates that reasoning ability and model size strongly influence performance across spatial and temporal understanding. Results are shared publicly at the provided GitHub repository.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark integrates twelve distinct geodata sources, ensuring a broad assessment of LLM capabilities beyond narrow tasks.  
- Reasoning performance is more predictive than sheer model size when measuring geo-spatial comprehension.  
- The public availability of GeoBenchLLM enables reproducible research and community-driven improvement.

## Context
Current AI evaluation often focuses on homogeneous datasets that limit insight into real‑world applicability. This work addresses that gap by showcasing the variability in LLM behavior across geographic domains, highlighting a need for more nuanced benchmarks. Such context matters as geospatial applications become increasingly embedded in everyday services.

## Implications
Practitioners can use GeoBenchLLM to benchmark their models before deploying them in mapping or navigation tools. Industry stakeholders will benefit from transparent performance metrics that guide investment in reasoning‑focused model upgrades rather than merely scaling up size.
