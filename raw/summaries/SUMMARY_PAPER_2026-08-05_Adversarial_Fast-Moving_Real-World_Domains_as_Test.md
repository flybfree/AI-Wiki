## Summary
The paper proposes using fast‑moving real‑world domains as objective testbeds for AI scientists, comparing model outputs against expert solutions in Formula 1 and Magic: The Gathering. It finds that while models generate many ideas, only a small fraction match the actual innovations or decklines, highlighting a gap in filtering and prioritization.

## Key Takeaways
- In F1 the best model matched ten of forty real innovations out of one hundred sixty‑six ideas generated across runs.
- In MTG Gemini 3 Flash recovered five of seven new‑set cards from the third‑place PT deck and its selected cards were the most widely adopted by experts, with a Spearman rank correlation of 0.74 (p=0.0003).
- The results indicate that AI scientists excel at idea generation but struggle with coherent novelty, filtering, and prioritization.

## Context
This work addresses a long‑standing challenge in evaluating scientific creativity: existing benchmarks often use synthetic tasks or retrospective targets that can be biased by prior exposure. By grounding evaluation in live expert outputs, the study offers a more realistic measure of AI’s ability to contribute novel solutions.

## Implications
For researchers, the findings suggest designing benchmarks that require not just generation but also selection and alignment with real‑world performance. For industry, it underscores the need for AI systems that can curate ideas efficiently rather than merely produce them.
