## Summary
This paper develops a general agent-based modelling framework aligned with the Capability Approach to create credible policy simulations grounded in empirical data and domain-expert knowledge. It applies the framework to financial inclusion in Egypt, modelling heterogeneous individuals and firms whose behaviour depends on financial states, barriers, opportunities, and preferences. By separating feasibility parameters from preference parameters, the study shows how observed inclusion gaps can be interpreted as differences between what agents can access and what they choose to pursue.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper argues that credible policy simulations require more than plausible dynamics; they must demonstrate that assumptions, parameters, and outputs are empirically grounded and accurate enough for policy use. This is operationalised through a two-stage fitting process: initialisation builds representative synthetic populations, while calibration estimates behavioural parameters from real-world data.
- The model distinguishes opportunities from preferences by fixing feasibility parameters that determine agents’ opportunities and calibrating preference parameters across population groups. This separation allows researchers to analyse institutional and social barriers separately from agents’ motivations, priorities, and financial decision-making.
- The calibration stage produces transparent, group-specific hypotheses about the drivers of financial-inclusion gaps. These gaps can then be analysed as mismatches between agents’ feasible opportunities and realised outcomes, giving policymakers a clearer basis for targeting interventions.

## Context
Agent-based models are increasingly used in AI-assisted policy analysis because they can represent heterogeneous agents, feedback loops, and emergent social outcomes. However, their usefulness depends on credibility, especially when models inform public policy in complex domains such as finance, development, and inclusion. This paper contributes to that challenge by combining agent-based simulation, empirical calibration, and a normative framework that links capabilities, opportunities, and realised outcomes.

## Implications
For researchers and practitioners, the framework offers a practical way to make agent-based policy simulations more transparent, testable, and stakeholder-relevant. It can help policymakers identify whether inclusion gaps are mainly caused by lack of access, institutional barriers, or differing preferences, which is essential for designing effective financial inclusion interventions. In industry and government settings, such methods can improve trust in simulation-based decision support by connecting model outputs to observable data and domain expertise.
