## Summary
This study investigates why existing design recommendations from adjacent domains struggle to apply directly to AI companion systems, which prioritize emotional connection and long-term relational interactions over instrumental tasks. Through a multi-method approach encompassing literature review, practitioner co-analysis, heuristic evaluation, and expert assessment, the researchers identified nine key design principle areas that reveal significant tensions when adapting prior guidelines. The findings demonstrate that ethical considerations and user experience design are deeply intertwined in AI companion contexts, necessitating highly context-sensitive approaches rather than straightforward transfer of existing frameworks.

## Key Takeaways
- AI companions differ fundamentally from traditional AI systems by focusing on emotional connection and long-term relational engagement, making their design uniquely complex and consequential for human well-being.
- Existing design recommendations from adjacent domains contain inherent tensions when applied to AI companions, as evidenced through a rigorous four-phase multi-method investigation that synthesized nine distinct principle areas of conflict.
- Ethical guidelines and user experience principles cannot be treated in isolation within AI companion design; instead, they require nuanced, context-aware integration to address the relational and emotional dimensions of human-AI interaction.

## Context
As large language models become increasingly integrated into everyday life, AI companions have emerged as a distinct category of conversational agents focused on fostering emotional bonds rather than executing task-oriented functions. This shift raises important questions about how design frameworks developed for transactional or utility-focused AI systems translate to contexts where trust, empathy, and long-term psychological impact are paramount. Understanding these translation gaps is critical for advancing responsible human-AI interaction research.

## Implications
Practitioners and researchers must move beyond directly copying established design guidelines when developing AI companions, recognizing that ethical and UX considerations require tailored, context-sensitive strategies. The identified tensions provide a structured analytic framework for evaluating and refining companion-specific design practices, ultimately supporting the creation of more responsible and emotionally intelligent systems. Industry stakeholders should prioritize iterative, multi-method evaluation processes to navigate the complex relational dynamics inherent in AI companion development.
