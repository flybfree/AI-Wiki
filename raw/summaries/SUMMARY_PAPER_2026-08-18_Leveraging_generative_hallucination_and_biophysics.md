## Summary
This paper introduces MCTH, a Monte Carlo Tree Hallucination framework that jointly designs biomolecular sequences and structures while treating folding and inverse‑folding models as fixed black‑box predictors. By allocating an inference budget across design trajectories and using confidence, uncertainty, and cross‑expert consensus, the method outperforms simpler sampling strategies in protein‑RNA, DNA, ligand, and protein interactions. The approach works without fine‑tuning or backpropagation through component models.

## Key Takeaways
- MCTH casts sequence‑structure co‑design as an uncertainty‑aware planning problem over hallucinated states generated by pretrained folding and inverse‑folding models, allowing a fixed inference budget to be allocated adaptively across competing design paths.  
- The framework leverages Monte Carlo Tree Search to balance model confidence, uncertainty, and consensus/disagreement among multiple predictors, resulting in improved sample efficiency compared with static sampling or cycling strategies.  
- Evaluations on held‑out AlphaFold3 and Chai‑1 models show that the adaptive search yields transferable performance gains beyond what a search‑time oracle could provide.

## Context
The integration of generative hallucination with biophysical constraints offers a unified way to tackle understudied non‑protein modalities such as DNA/RNA complexes, which lack abundant high‑quality structural data. This work exemplifies how black‑box AI components can be combined into a single planning layer without requiring joint training.

## Implications
For biotech and synthetic biology, MCTH enables rapid generation of functional biomolecular designs that respect chemical and geometric constraints, accelerating drug discovery pipelines. Practitioners can adopt the method as a plug‑and‑play tool within existing AI workflows, reducing reliance on costly experimental validation.
