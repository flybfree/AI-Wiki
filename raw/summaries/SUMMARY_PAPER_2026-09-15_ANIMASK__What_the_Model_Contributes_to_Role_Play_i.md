## Summary
This paper introduces ANIMASK, a simulation framework designed to isolate and measure how assigned character personas interact with the inherent defaults of large language models during role-playing scenarios. By freezing narrative scripts into persistent story worlds and replaying them from key decision points, researchers can directly compare authorial intent against unmasked model behavior. The study demonstrates that while simulated narratives consistently drift toward flatter, more cautious storytelling, the designated personas remain strictly intact, proving that character identity is preserved by the prompt while the underlying model dictates behavioral boundaries.

## Key Takeaways
- ANIMASK freezes books and scripts into interactive story worlds where characters operate on their own motivations, allowing researchers to replay narratives from specific freeze points and isolate persona-driven decisions from base model tendencies.
- Across an extensive evaluation spanning 40 stories, six different actor models, and 3,846 decision points, the simulated replays consistently converge toward flatter, cooler narratives with open tensions, yet in-story interviews confirm that each assigned persona remains fully present and obeyed throughout.
- In three out of four tested choices, the model’s default response already falls within what the persona would
