## Summary
This paper investigates two methods for evaluating student teams after tabletop exercises in computing education, finding that clustering provides fast, low‑cost feedback while large language models can generate rubric‑based scores. The study shows clustering is valid and reliable with minimal computational load, whereas GPT‑5.2 aligns better with instructor ratings than GPT‑4o.

## Key Takeaways
- Clustering groups teams that behave similarly, allowing instructors to give rapid, targeted feedback with low computational requirements.
- Large language models can produce rubric‑aligned assessments, but performance varies: GPT‑4o often disagrees with human scores while GPT‑5.2 shows significantly lower error rates.
- The research integrates both approaches into an open‑source TTX platform called INJECT to support scalable and practical assessment.

## Context
Assessing teamwork in tabletop exercises remains challenging because the tasks are open‑ended and time‑sensitive, yet existing tools often lack automated feedback. This work demonstrates how AI can complement human judgment by offering structured evaluation that scales across many learners.

## Implications
For educators, these methods enable more timely assessment without overburdening instructors. For industry practitioners, the approach offers a way to evaluate collaborative problem solving in realistic scenarios, potentially improving training outcomes and identifying skill gaps early.
