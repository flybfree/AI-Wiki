## Summary
The paper evaluates blockchain-based integrity mechanisms in Greece’s Fiscal Electronic Mechanisms and central tax system eSEND, comparing hardware devices with software invoicing platforms. It finds that hardware implementations using hash‑chain structures provide stronger tamper resistance than the electronic invoicing provider services and myDATA platform which lack such guarantees.

## Key Takeaways
- The hardware fiscal devices employ double or triple hash‑chain cryptographic architectures to guarantee transaction immutability, offering robust integrity against alteration. - The transmission protocol between these devices and the central database incorporates encryption, sequential validation, and blockchain verification to reinforce security. - In contrast, electronic invoicing services and myDATA rely solely on software without blockchain integrity mechanisms, creating a weaker protection posture.

## Context
This study contributes to the broader AI research on secure digital tax ecosystems by applying cryptographic principles to real‑world governmental systems. It highlights how decentralized ledger technology can be leveraged to enhance data reliability in public administration, aligning with trends toward transparent and tamper‑proof government services.

## Implications
For policymakers and IT practitioners, the findings suggest a shift from purely software‑based invoicing to hardware‑anchored blockchain solutions for critical fiscal transactions. Adopting such architectures could reduce fraud risk and improve trust in digital tax processes across similar jurisdictions.
