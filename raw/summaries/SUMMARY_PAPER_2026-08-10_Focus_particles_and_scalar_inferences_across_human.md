## Summary
This paper investigates how humans and large language models interpret focus particles such as “even” and “only,” which are used to structure scalar alternatives in formal semantic theories. By comparing judgments from participants and LLMs on a set of 100 sentences, the authors find that while both groups produce similar outputs, the underlying mechanisms differ.

## Key Takeaways
- Humans rely on explicit mental representations of scalar scales, leading to consistent but context‑sensitive interpretations of “even” and “only.”  
- LLMs generate comparable responses through statistical pattern matching rather than a dedicated scalar representation system.  
- The convergence of outputs suggests that scalar reasoning can be approximated without a unified theoretical framework.

## Context
Understanding how different agents model scalar concepts is crucial for building AI systems that mimic human nuance and for evaluating the robustness of semantic representations in natural language processing. This work contributes to debates about representational alignment between biological cognition and artificial models.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings highlight the need to design evaluation protocols that distinguish between algorithmic similarity and genuine scalar reasoning. In industry, recognizing these differences can guide the development of more human‑like conversational agents while avoiding overreliance on superficial output matching.
