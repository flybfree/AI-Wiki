## Summary
This paper introduces VortexChat, an agentic framework that autonomously designs integrated photonic devices from natural language specifications using a large language model decision agent. The system iteratively combines topology generation, gradient refinement, and full-wave simulation to meet all performance thresholds on the Vortex100 Benchmark without human intervention. Experiments demonstrate a fabricated terahertz vortex beam multiplexer that matches simulation predictions in efficiency, mode purity, and crosstalk.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework uses an LLM as a decision agent to decompose design objectives and orchestrate computational tools, enabling fully automated inverse photonic design.
- VortexChat achieves strict adherence to absolute performance metrics on the benchmark, showing that autonomous systems can replace expert supervision in device optimization.
- Fabricated devices produced by VortexChat exhibit high‑efficiency operation, high mode purity, and low inter‑channel crosstalk, confirming physical fidelity.

## Context
The integration of large language models into engineering workflows is a growing trend aimed at reducing reliance on manual simulation and expert intuition. This work exemplifies how AI agents can act as decision makers within closed‑loop pipelines that combine symbolic reasoning with numerical optimization.

## Implications
Autonomous photonic design could accelerate product development cycles, lower R&D costs, and enable rapid prototyping of complex integrated circuits. Practitioners may adopt VortexChat’s modular approach to embed LLM agents into existing simulation environments for scalable innovation.
