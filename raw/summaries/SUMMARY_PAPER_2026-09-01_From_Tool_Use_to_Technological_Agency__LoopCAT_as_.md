## Summary
This paper introduces LoopCAT, an open‑source, local‑first computer‑assisted translation environment built with OpenAI Codex and GPT‑5.5/5.6 models. The authors present a pedagogical framework that links workflow competence, evaluative judgment, and technological agency, but they report no new student learning outcomes as evidence.

## Key Takeaways
- LoopCAT integrates local project storage, translation memories, terminology files, quality assurance checks, document exchange, and optional connections to AI services, creating a closed‑loop system for teaching.  
- The framework organizes learning around four participation modes: operating the workflow, evaluating outputs, inspecting and configuring mechanisms, and making or defending bounded interventions.  
- The study separates implemented capabilities from proposed educational benefits and notes that no new student‑learning outcomes were observed.

## Context
The paper addresses a growing need in AI education to give students hands‑on experience with generative tools while fostering critical assessment of their decisions. By embedding AI assistance within a locally hosted CAT environment, it illustrates how local infrastructure can support transparent, inspectable learning activities that mirror real‑world translation workflows.

## Implications
LoopCAT offers practitioners a model for designing AI‑augmented training that emphasizes agency and evaluation rather than merely demonstrating tool use. While the immediate educational impact remains unproven, the approach could influence curriculum design, industry training programs, and research on locally hosted generative AI systems.
