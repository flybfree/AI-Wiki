## Summary
This paper surveys the emerging field of collaborative learning from Euclidean to graph-structured data, aiming to consolidate research on how agents can train and infer locally while sharing information across networks. It organizes findings along three dimensions—effectiveness, efficiency, and privacy preservation—for traditional grid‑based data and introduces a taxonomy for graph scenarios, highlighting open challenges and promising directions.

## Key Takeaways
- The conventional machine learning pipeline is constrained by scalability and privacy issues, prompting the need for collaborative approaches that keep training local.  
- Recent collaborative methods such as federated and decentralized learning focus on Euclidean data with regular grid structures, which limits their ability to model complex relational patterns.  
- Graph‑structured data enables message‑passing mechanisms that are well aligned with collaborative environments, yet the opportunities and challenges of applying these techniques remain largely underexplored.

## Context
The shift from static Euclidean datasets to networked graph representations reflects a broader trend in AI toward modeling real‑world relational structures. As applications grow more distributed, the ability to learn efficiently while preserving privacy becomes increasingly critical for practical deployment.

## Implications
For researchers, this survey provides a roadmap that bridges theory and practice, guiding work on scalable, privacy‑preserving collaborative learning. Practitioners can leverage graph‑based message passing to design systems that adapt to dynamic networks, unlocking new functionalities in fields such as social recommendation and multi‑agent coordination.
