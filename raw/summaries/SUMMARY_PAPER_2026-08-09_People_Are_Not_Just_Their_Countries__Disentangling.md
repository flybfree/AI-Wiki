## Summary
This paper investigates how large language models align with human values across European populations, moving beyond country‑based analyses to include socio‑demographic factors such as education, income, occupation and religion. Using the European Social Survey, it finds that both individual demographics and the respondent’s country of residence significantly influence value alignment, and that these variables complement each other in explaining patterns.

## Key Takeaways
- Socio‑demographic variables like education, income, occupation and religion produce substantial variation in LLM value alignment beyond nationality alone.  
- Country of residence explains a large share of the variance, comparable to the full set of demographic factors considered together.  
- The explanatory power of country versus demographics shifts depending on which subset of survey questions is examined.

## Context
The study addresses a growing concern that AI systems may reflect or amplify societal biases by assuming uniform value alignment across national borders. By integrating large‑scale European data, it contributes to the broader effort to make LLM assessments more inclusive and representative.

## Implications
Practitioners must design value‑alignment evaluations that consider both demographic diversity and geographic context to avoid misrepresenting model behavior. This research underscores the need for regionally aware AI governance frameworks.
