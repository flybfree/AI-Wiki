## Summary
The paper reviews recent work on robustness and explainability in digital health AI, organizing methods into a framework that addresses technical and ethical challenges. It highlights advances such as data‑scarcity robust models and gradient‑based explanations tailored to high‑stakes domains like intensive care and neonatal health.

## Key Takeaways
- Robustness techniques are being developed for limited data sets and distributional shifts, which is crucial because healthcare data often have small or biased samples. 
- Explainability methods range from feature attribution to counterfactual explanations, enabling clinicians to trust model decisions. 
- The review stresses that both robustness and explainability must be integrated throughout the AI lifecycle, not just at deployment.

## Context
Trustworthy AI is a growing concern as machine learning models are deployed in high‑risk healthcare settings where errors can have severe consequences. This paper contributes by synthesizing technical advances into an accessible framework, offering a reference for researchers and clinicians navigating complex trust dimensions.
