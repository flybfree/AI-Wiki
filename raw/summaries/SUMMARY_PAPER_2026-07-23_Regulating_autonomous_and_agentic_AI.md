## Summary  
The paper examines how current regulations struggle with autonomous and agentic AI and proposes a forward‑looking framework that moves from reactive oversight to active, adaptive governance. It evaluates four existing regimes—UK content platforms, data protection, financial services, and the EU AI Act’s cross‑sectoral regime—and identifies gaps in their assumptions about knowledge control.

## Key Takeaways  
- Regulatory assumptions about regulatee knowledge and control no longer hold true because much of that lies elsewhere in the AI supply chain which thus needs to be brought within the scope of regulation.  
- Retrospective supervisory oversight becomes ineffective as a risk management tool due to AI autonomy generating new systemic risks.  
- The paper proposes potential solutions that will transform regulation from a reactive process to an active one, enabling regulators to adapt to challenges of AI autonomy.

## Context  
Autonomous and agentic AI systems operate with decision‑making capabilities that extend beyond the immediate operator, creating governance complexities not addressed by traditional regulatory models. This shift demands new legal concepts that can trace responsibility across distributed components of the AI supply chain.

## Implications  
For regulators, the implications are a move toward proactive risk assessment and continuous monitoring rather than periodic audits. For industry practitioners, it means designing systems with built‑in compliance pathways to align with evolving, active regulatory frameworks.
