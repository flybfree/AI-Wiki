## Summary
The paper introduces ImageEval 2026, a shared task for culturally grounded Arabic multimodal evaluation that includes two tasks: AynVQA (spoken visual question answering and hallucination detection in English and Modern Standard Arabic) and CRAI‑Bench (evaluating cultural accuracy of text‑to‑image generation). It reports results from 14 participating teams, emphasizing the challenges faced by systems handling Arabic speech and image‑text reasoning.

## Key Takeaways
- AynVQA requires models to answer visual questions in both English and MSA while detecting hallucinations; performance varies significantly across the two language tracks.  
- CRAI‑Bench measures how well generated images respect cultural norms, revealing large discrepancies between model outputs and culturally appropriate content.  
- Teams employed a wide range of approaches—zero‑shot prompting, fine‑tuning vision‑language models, speech‑recognition pipelines, ensembling, and score calibration—to address the task.

## Context
This work addresses the need for evaluation frameworks that respect linguistic diversity and cultural context in multimodal AI. It pushes beyond English‑centric benchmarks toward Arabic language tasks, reflecting growing interest in non‑English AI applications and the importance of culturally aware models.

## Implications
For practitioners, it underscores the importance of culturally aware models to avoid misrepresentation of Arab cultures. For researchers, it sets a benchmark for evaluating Arabic speech and image‑text reasoning, encouraging more inclusive datasets and evaluation protocols that support broader adoption of multilingual AI systems.
