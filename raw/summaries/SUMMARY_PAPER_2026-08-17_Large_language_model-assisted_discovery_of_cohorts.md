## Summary
The paper presents a question-driven framework that automatically generates PubMed queries from research vocabularies and uses a large language model to extract explicit cohort names from retrieved literature. Applied to youth aggression genetics, the system identified 44 eligible cohorts among 5,254 records, outperforming manual catalogues which missed 17 cohorts.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework automatically generates PubMed queries and extracts cohort names using an LLM, reducing reliance on prior knowledge.
- Manual screening retained 44 eligible cohorts while automated extraction achieved agreement with human annotators within expected range.
- Existing cohort catalogues captured only 27 of the 44 eligible cohorts, highlighting gaps in curated resources.

## Context
This work addresses a longstanding challenge in multi-study analyses where identifying relevant participant cohorts is labor‑intensive and often limited by static catalogue entries. By integrating AI‑driven literature mining, it expands the pool of discoverable cohorts beyond manually curated lists.
