## Summary
This paper proposes the Authority Resolution Framework (ARF), a five‑domain ontology that captures how authority is assigned across organisational roles, informal influence, business concepts, codified processes, machine‑readable permissions and real‑world context. The framework introduces an Authority Relation primitive that links actor, action, object, bounded context, justification chain and a calibration measure called the DNA‑Coefficient to quantify gaps between formal rules and actual practice.

## Key Takeaways
- ARF defines a cross‑domain primitive called the Authority Relation (AR) that binds actor, action, object, bounded context, justification chain and a calibration coefficient known as the DNA‑Coefficient. 
- The framework provides machine‑interpretable authority provenance and scope using JSON‑LD representations and knowledge‑graph query patterns for resolution. 
- ARF treats authority resolution as a knowledge‑representation and reasoning problem at the intersection of ontology engineering, semantic AI, agentic AI and AI governance.

## Context
The rapid rise of autonomous AI agents demands mechanisms that go beyond technical capability to assess whether actions are authorised in their specific contexts. Traditional permission models fail to capture nuanced organisational influence and real‑world variability, creating gaps between documented rules and actual decision making.

## Implications
ARF equips developers with a scalable ontology for auditing AI decisions, improving transparency and accountability across enterprise systems. By integrating authority resolution into AI pipelines, practitioners can reduce risk of unauthorized actions and align automated behaviour with corporate governance standards.
