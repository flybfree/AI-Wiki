## Summary
The paper proposes a Wolfram Language implementation of Reified Input/Output Logic to formalize AI governance. It demonstrates that GPT-4 can translate legal statements into the formalism but often fails with hallucinated functions and missing temporal scope. A case study shows an embodied guard dog obeys computational contracts, producing auditable justifications.

## Key Takeaways
- The implementation of Reified Input/Output Logic provides a framework for stating obligations, permissions, and norms that can be checked against AI behavior without needing to understand its reasoning.
- GPT-4 translation failures reveal hallucinated functions, omitted temporal scope, and code that runs but encodes the wrong norm, highlighting gaps in current legal formalization.
- The guard dog case study shows how computational contracts can directly generate symbolic justifications for an agent’s actions, enabling transparent governance.

## Context
AI systems increasingly operate with opaque reasoning making traditional oversight difficult. Formal methods like Reified Input/Output Logic aim to create checkable rules that align AI actions with intended legal or ethical constraints. This work bridges formal logic and practical AI deployment by embedding these rules into code.

## Implications
Formalizing law as executable code could enable real-time compliance monitoring across industries. Practitioners may adopt Wolfram Language tools to embed governance directly into models, reducing liability risks and improving transparency in automated decision making.
