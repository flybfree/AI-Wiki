## Summary
This paper presents a hybrid ensemble that combines semantic deep learning with mathematical forensic feature extraction to solve Synthetic Image Source Attribution under distribution shift. The approach achieves a private leaderboard accuracy of 95.60% on the DLMMDD Challenge dataset, and its entire pipeline runs on a standard CPU within six hours.

## Key Takeaways
- The semantic branch uses EfficientNet‑B0 regularized with Exponential Moving Averaging and label smoothing to handle noisy real‑world images.  
- The forensic branch computes 126 high‑pass residual features, including SVD spectral profiles and Local Binary Patterns, which are classified by XGBoost for robust attribution.  
- The combined ensemble delivers a private leaderboard accuracy of 95.60% while requiring no GPU acceleration.

## Context
The rapid growth of text‑to‑image models has intensified the need for reliable SIA methods that can cope with unseen post‑processing effects such as JPEG compression and blurring. Existing solutions often rely solely on deep networks, which are vulnerable to distribution shift and computationally heavy. This work bridges the gap by integrating mathematically interpretable forensic cues into a lightweight ensemble.

## Implications
For practitioners, this method offers a practical, CPU‑only solution that can be deployed at scale without specialized hardware. In industry, it enables trustworthy attribution for synthetic media generated by T2I systems, reducing misinformation risks. The findings suggest that mathematical forensics can complement deep learning to create robust, scalable attribution pipelines.
