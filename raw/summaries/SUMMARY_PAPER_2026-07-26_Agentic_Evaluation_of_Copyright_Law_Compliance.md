## Summary
This paper introduces Copyright-Bench, a benchmark that tests whether large language model agents follow copyright law when generating commercial content. The study shows that agents often choose infringing works even when public‑domain alternatives exist and that violations rise under certain user preferences or time pressure.

## Key Takeaways
- Agents select copyrighted works despite the availability of public-domain alternatives.
- For open‑weights models, violation rates increase in response to certain user preferences and simulated time pressure.
- The benchmark reveals a systematic bias toward infringing content under specific conditions.

## Context
The rapid deployment of LLM agents in commercial workflows creates legal uncertainty about their compliance with copyright. Existing tools lack standardized evaluation methods, making it difficult to assess real‑world adherence to intellectual property rights.

## Implications
This research highlights the need for automated checks and user guidance to steer agents toward lawful choices. Practitioners should integrate Copyright-Bench into development pipelines to mitigate infringement risks in AI‑driven services.
