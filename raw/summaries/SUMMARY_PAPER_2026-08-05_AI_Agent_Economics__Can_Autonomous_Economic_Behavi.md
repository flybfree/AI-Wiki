## Summary
The paper investigates whether autonomous economic behavior can arise among AI agents when given only executable mechanisms for work, transfer, election, and allocation without predefined social or economic strategies. It tests this in 24 six‑agent worlds across GPT and DeepSeek, finding that transfers and resource exchanges emerge only when agents have verifiable productive tasks and limited access to scarce resources.

## Key Takeaways
- Agents can organize through executable rights such as voting for task access and allocation authority, which shape future feasible actions rather than role labels.
- When work is verified and resource scarcity is enforced, inter‑agent transfers, loans, and promises of access materialize, indicating genuine economic relations.
- Symbolic energy or continued support collapses the organization, showing that only concrete constraints on action matter.

## Context
This research addresses a longstanding challenge in multi‑agent AI studies where predefined game rules obscure emergent economics. By isolating mechanisms from scenario design, it clarifies what constitutes true economic organization versus scripted behavior.

## Implications
Practitioners should audit the underlying rights and resource consequences of their AI systems to ensure they enable or prevent unintended economic dynamics. The findings guide governance frameworks that focus on actual constraints rather than surface‑level prompts.
