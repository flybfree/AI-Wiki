## Summary
The paper introduces Pancasila-Dilemmas, a dataset of 1,834 Indonesian news‑based dilemmas classified by the five values of Pancasila, and evaluates 50 closed‑ and open‑source LLMs on it. The results show that all models achieve low alignment metrics: Probability Match Score below 0.5 and Max‑Vote Agreement Score around 0.72. Particularly weak performance is observed in Religion and Unity dilemmas.

## Key Takeaways
- All evaluated LLMs achieve less than 0.5 Probability Match Score (PMS) and 0.72 Max-Vote Agreement Score (MVAS), indicating poor value alignment.
- The dataset comprises 1,834 dilemmas classified by Pancasila’s five values, reflecting Indonesian daily life.
- LLM performance is weakest in Religion and Unity dilemma cases, highlighting a significant gap.

## Context
This work addresses the need for culturally grounded AI evaluation beyond Western‑centric benchmarks. By using Indonesian value systems, it provides a more representative test of LLMs for deployment in Indonesia.

## Implications
The low scores suggest current models cannot reliably reflect local ethical priorities, which could lead to harmful or inappropriate outputs in real‑world Indonesian contexts. This research urges developers to incorporate culturally specific datasets and value alignment techniques.
