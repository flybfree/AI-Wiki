## Summary
This study investigates how effectively human readers can distinguish AI-generated Japanese haiku from human-written poems using a diverse range of contemporary large language models. Through a survey administered to university students in Tokyo, the research reveals that while some models remain moderately detectable, state-of-the-art systems like GPT-5 and Gemini 2.5 perform at chance levels, making their poetic outputs nearly indistinguishable from human authorship. Crucially, the findings demonstrate a strong dissociation between aesthetic appreciation and actual authorship detection, as participants frequently misattributed AI poems to humans based on perceived fluency and poetic quality rather than accurate classification.
