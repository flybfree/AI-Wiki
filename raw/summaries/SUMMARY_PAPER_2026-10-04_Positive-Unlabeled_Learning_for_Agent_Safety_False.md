## Summary
This paper addresses the critical problem of false alarms generated by safety monitors for language-model agents interacting with external tools and environments. The authors frame false-alarm auditing as a positive-unlabeled (PU) ranking problem, where verified-safe non-alarmed trajectories serve as the only labeled positives while alarms remain unlabeled. Their proposed two-stage framework—combining Trust-aware PU Supervision, Reliability-gated Rank Distillation, and Consensus-guided Structural Refinement—achieves a macro AUPRC of 0.6444, outperforming eight PU baselines by 5.27–16.98 absolute percentage points and recovering 33.3% more false alarms than the strongest baseline at a 5% review budget.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper identifies a fundamental challenge called "monitor-induced selection": observed safe references are those accepted by the monitor, while the hidden safe alarms of interest are precisely those the monitor incorrectly flags, making the available positive examples poorly representative of the positives that need to be recovered. This selection bias is the core obstacle that distinguishes this PU problem from standard PU learning settings.
- The proposed framework operates in two stages: Trust-aware PU Supervision adapts safe references toward the alarm domain and shields plausible false alarms from excessive negative pressure, while Reliability-gated Rank Distillation consolidates consistent ordering preferences from multiple PU reference models into a single student model. A subsequent Consensus-guided Structural Refinement step further improves ranking using hierarchical safe-reference support, alarm relations, and predicted reference consensus.
- The method requires no alarm safety labels for fitting and leaves the underlying safety monitor unchanged, meaning it functions as a post-hoc auditing layer. Across mainstream safety monitors, it achieves a macro AUPRC of 0.6444, surpassing eight evaluated PU baselines by 5.27–16.98 absolute percentage points, and recovers 33.3% more false alarms than PULDA at a 5% review budget.

## Context
As language-model agents increasingly interact with external tools, APIs, and environments, safety monitors have become essential guardrails to prevent harmful or unintended actions. However, conservative monitoring strategies inevitably produce a high volume of false alarms, which consume extensive human review resources and erode trust in alert systems. This paper sits at the intersection of AI safety monitoring, PU learning, and ranking distillation, addressing a practical bottleneck in deploying agent safety systems at scale. By reformulating false-alarm auditing as a PU ranking problem, the authors connect a practical deployment challenge to a well-studied machine learning paradigm, enabling principled solutions without requiring new labeled data.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying agent safety systems, this framework offers a label-efficient auditing mechanism that can be layered on top of existing monitors without retraining or modifying them, significantly reducing the human review burden. The 33.3% improvement in false-alarm recovery at a 5% review budget translates directly into cost savings and faster iteration cycles for teams operating safety-critical agent pipelines. More broadly, the work demonstrates that PU learning techniques can be adapted to handle domain-specific selection biases in safety-critical applications, potentially informing future designs of monitoring and auditing infrastructure for autonomous agents across industries.
