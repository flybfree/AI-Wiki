## Summary
This paper conducts a systematic literature review of Explainable AI (XAI) research from 2024 onward, focusing on how it addresses the EU Right to Explanation under GDPR Art. 15(1)(h), the AI Act’s Art. 86, and related legal instruments. The authors analyzed 2643 records, selecting 57 full texts; only 19 papers integrate both legal and technical viewpoints, revealing a persistent interdisciplinary gap that hampers practical compliance.

## Key Takeaways
- Most studies misidentify the GDPR legal basis for explanations, overlooking the specific rights granted to individuals.  
- Few engage with the CJEU’s Dun & Bradstreet judgment, likely because it was published after the AI Act’s final version in July 2024.  
- The distinction between explanation form (addressed to the addressee) and content (governed by legal purpose) is frequently conflated.

## Context
The EU Right to Explanation aims to ensure transparency when automated systems affect personal decisions such as loans, hiring, or healthcare. As AI becomes more embedded in critical services, regulators seek technical solutions that satisfy legal obligations without compromising model performance. This review highlights how current research often treats legal requirements and algorithmic explainability as separate concerns rather than a unified design challenge.

## Implications
For practitioners, the identified gaps suggest a need for interdisciplinary frameworks that align XAI techniques with both GDPR’s addressee‑focused form and Art. 86’s purpose‑driven content. Without such synthesis, the Right to Explanation may remain a formal legal duty without a viable technical pathway, limiting its real‑world impact on AI deployment and user trust.
