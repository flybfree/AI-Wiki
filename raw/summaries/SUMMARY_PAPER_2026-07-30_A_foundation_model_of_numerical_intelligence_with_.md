## Summary
The paper introduces UNICON, a foundation model that demonstrates numerical intelligence across disciplines. It learns from graph-based examples and applies inferred relations to new queries without retraining. The model matches specialist performance on unseen domains and improves when combined with language agents.

## Key Takeaways
- UNICON infers predictive relations from graph contexts and transfers them to novel queries, achieving expert-level accuracy without domain-specific training.
- The same model performs well across scientific and social systems that were not present in the training corpus, showing cross-disciplinary generalization.
- Training on diverse corpora enhances performance on unseen disciplines, highlighting the importance of data diversity for foundation models.

## Context
This work addresses a gap in AI where language-centric models cannot handle numerical reasoning. By treating graphs as contextual input, UNICON provides a unified framework that can be applied to any domain requiring quantitative inference. The approach aligns with broader trends toward modular, transferable AI components.

## Implications
Practitioners can deploy UNICON as a reusable building block for applications ranging from physics simulations to social network analysis. Its ability to generalize across unseen fields may reduce the need for costly retraining pipelines and accelerate innovation in interdisciplinary research.
