## Summary
This paper compares vision-language models (VLMs) with large language models (LLMs) in a text‑only setting to see if multimodal training improves human alignment during natural reading. The study finds that VLMs do not provide a uniform advantage, while LLMs remain the baseline for aligning with human brain activity.

## Key Takeaways
- Multimodal pretraining does not create a global benefit in human alignment across all sentences.  
- Language‑internal representations are the primary factor driving model‑human similarity during reading.  
- VLM advantage may appear selectively when sentences contain strong visual semantic content, as shown by both fMRI and eye‑tracking data.

## Context
The work builds on growing interest in multimodal AI systems that aim to mimic human perception. By isolating visual learning from text processing, the study clarifies how training modalities influence model behavior without confounding factors.

## Implications
For researchers, this suggests focusing on language‑centric objectives may be more effective than adding vision data for alignment. Practitioners should consider selective multimodal integration rather than assuming universal gains in human‑like performance.
