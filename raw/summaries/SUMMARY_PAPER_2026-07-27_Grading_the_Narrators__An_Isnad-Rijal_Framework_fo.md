## Summary
This paper proposes an Isnad‑Rijal framework that attaches graded reliability to claim‑level transmission chains in multi‑agent knowledge systems, inspired by classical Islamic hadith grading. The authors map hadith concepts to AI pipelines, implement a relational schema for claim chains and narrator grades, and evaluate the system on 20 000 physics textbook claims, reporting partial successes and identified limitations.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework introduces graded per‑domain transmitter reliability that is attached to each claim in a transmission chain.  
- It combines weakest‑link quarantine with independent‑chain corroboration for content criticism.  
- Evaluation shows the grade‑recovery loop missed the highest‑fault narrator, indicating incomplete handling of extreme cases.

## Context
AI knowledge systems increasingly rely on autonomous transformations rather than direct retrieval, yet provenance and reliability assessment remain fragmented. Classical hadith science solved a similar problem with systematic grading and chain evaluation, offering a proven model for trustworthy information flow in AI pipelines.

## Implications
The Isnad‑Rijal framework provides practitioners with an operational method to evaluate claim provenance at the source level, reducing misinformation risk across scientific and educational domains. By integrating graded narrator registers into multi‑agent workflows, organizations can make more transparent and accountable knowledge sharing decisions.
