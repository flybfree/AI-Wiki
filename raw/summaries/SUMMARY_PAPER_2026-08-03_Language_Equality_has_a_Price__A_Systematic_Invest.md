## Summary
The paper evaluates nine open-weight and commercial large language models as language agents in a multi-turn dialogue game across thirty languages, including the twenty-four EU languages plus six others. It finds that none of the open‑weight models perform well on the official EU languages, while all commercial systems outperform them, with the best Chinese scores still below those of US commercial models.

## Key Takeaways
- In every official EU language both commercial LLMs score higher than any open‑weight model, and the two weakest average below 40 points across the EU‑24. - Commercial systems maintain an advantage even in languages that have four orders of magnitude less public web text, indicating linguistic parity can be achieved beyond simple crawl data. - The median non‑English language costs 31% more to run than English and scores 10% lower.

## Context
This study addresses a gap in AI fairness research by measuring performance across diverse languages rather than relying on English‑centric benchmarks, highlighting the need for inclusive evaluation frameworks. It underscores that current model capabilities are not uniformly distributed globally despite advances in multilingual training.

## Implications
For developers and policymakers, the findings suggest that commercial models must invest in language‑specific resources to close gaps, while open‑weight projects should prioritize targeted improvements rather than assuming parity from public data alone. The results call for new evaluation standards that consider both cost and performance across low‑resource languages.
