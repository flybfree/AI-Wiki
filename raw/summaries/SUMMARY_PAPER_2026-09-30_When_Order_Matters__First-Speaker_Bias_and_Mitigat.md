## Summary
This paper investigates sequential multi-agent debate (MAD) and identifies a significant first-speaker bias where the initial agent disproportionately influences the final outcome, potentially negating the reasoning advantages of stronger models placed later in the sequence. To address this imbalance, the authors explore personality prompting based on the Big Five model, specifically examining agreeableness and extraversion as behavioral interventions. The study reveals that assigning low agreeableness to a strong agent positioned last can effectively restore its influence and enhance final accuracy, demonstrating that debate efficacy depends critically on both speaking order and induced interaction behaviors.
