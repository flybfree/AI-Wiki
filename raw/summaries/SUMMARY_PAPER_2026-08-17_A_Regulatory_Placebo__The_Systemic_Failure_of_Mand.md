## Summary
The paper argues that mandatory labeling of generative AI is a reactive, symbolic measure that masks deeper legal needs. It shows that current regulations create implementation problems and may stall technological progress, revealing regulators’ cognitive limits in understanding modern tech logic.

## Key Takeaways
- The study identifies three theoretical strands—value dilution theory, information authenticity theory, and proactive regulation theory—as reflections of regulators’ limited grasp of AI’s logical evolution.  
- Mandatory labeling functions as a regulatory placebo, satisfying symbolic compliance while obscuring genuine legal demands for the technological era.  
- This formalistic compliance risks hindering AI’s evolutionary trajectory by imposing unnecessary hurdles rather than fostering responsible innovation.

## Context
The rapid advancement of generative artificial intelligence has prompted governments worldwide to consider mandatory labeling schemes as a precautionary measure. While well‑intentioned, such policies often stem from public anxiety and institutional inertia rather than evidence‑based assessment, highlighting a gap between perceived risk and actual technological complexity.

## Implications
For policymakers, the findings suggest moving beyond identity‑label governance toward content‑focused regulation that addresses real risks without stifling innovation. Practitioners should prioritize clear, technology‑aligned standards to ensure AI benefits are realized safely and effectively.
