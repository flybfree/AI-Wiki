## Summary
The paper investigates how humans and classification models behave when entraining to code-switched speech in Mandarin-English, Hindi-English, and Spanish-English dialogues. It finds that lexical entrainment generalizes across language pairs but acoustic-prosodic and CSW style aspects vary with context. Classical and Transformer classifiers capture some patterns but prioritize features not most salient to humans.

## Key Takeaways
- Lexical entrainment is robust across the three language pairs, indicating a shared linguistic mechanism.
- Human attention shifts between lexical and prosodic cues depending on conversational context, unlike models that favor non‑lexical signals.
- Classification models consistently overlook CSW style features, highlighting a gap in capturing stylistic entrainment.

## Context
This work extends entrainment research from written and monolingual spoken settings to multilingual code-switched dialogue. It contributes to understanding how language mixing influences auditory processing and model training pipelines.

## Implications
For AI developers, the findings suggest that current models may produce unnatural speech by ignoring context‑specific cues. Addressing this gap could improve naturalness of conversational agents in real‑world multilingual settings.
