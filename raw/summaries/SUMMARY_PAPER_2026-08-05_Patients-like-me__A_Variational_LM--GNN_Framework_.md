## Summary
The paper introduces Patients-like-me (PLM), a unified language model‑graph neural network framework that improves clinical prediction by combining local patient semantics with global cohort structure. Experiments on MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV show PLM outperforms state‑of‑the‑art methods while adding only modest computational overhead.

## Key Takeaways
- PLM uses a variational expectation‑maximization algorithm to alternate LM and GNN updates under a supervised objective, enabling efficient training.  
- The framework consistently improves performance across both encoder‑only and decoder‑only language model backbones on clinical prediction tasks.  
- Reference‑patient explanations are generated by retrieving influential similar patients, with edge‑masking confirming that top references have the greatest impact.

## Context
Current AI for healthcare relies heavily on either large language models or graph neural networks, each addressing only part of the problem and lacking interpretability. Integrating these approaches in a single pipeline addresses this gap but requires careful training strategies to maintain efficiency. This work demonstrates that such integration is feasible with modest overhead.

## Implications
PLM offers clinicians transparent explanations for predictions by highlighting relevant similar patients, supporting trustworthy decision‑making. The method’s scalability and low computational cost make it suitable for real‑world deployment in electronic health record systems.
