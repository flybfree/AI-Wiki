## Summary
The paper introduces a mathematical framework that evaluates whether an enterprise AI system can maintain lawful, explainable, adaptable, and accountable decision outcomes over time despite personnel turnover, model replacement, regulatory shifts, and changing incentives. It proposes a Legacy Score derived from a penalized geometric mean of six core factors, alongside models for confidence and risk separation, authority‑aware retrieval, decision memory, change velocity mapping, and a federated knowledge graph.

## Key Takeaways
- The Legacy Score quantifies the durability of AI governance by combining knowledge retention, governance strength, human oversight, adaptability, feedback learning, and jurisdictional fidelity into a single penalized geometric mean metric.  
- Decision Confidence and Decision Risk models split evidentiary confidence from consequence severity to guide routing decisions without compromising risk awareness.  
- The framework includes eight AI Decision Integrity Rules that enforce accountability across the decision lifecycle, supported by a reproducible stress‑test protocol.

## Context
Enterprise AI systems now operate in environments where human expertise is fluid and regulations evolve rapidly. Existing governance models lack a unified quantitative language to assess long‑term integrity, leaving practitioners to rely on qualitative checklists rather than measurable indicators.

## Implications
This framework equips organizations with a testable metric for evaluating AI legacy, enabling proactive maintenance of decision quality as teams change or laws shift. Practitioners can use the Authority‑aware retrieval and calibrated abstention mechanisms to align AI outputs with legal hierarchies while preserving human oversight.
