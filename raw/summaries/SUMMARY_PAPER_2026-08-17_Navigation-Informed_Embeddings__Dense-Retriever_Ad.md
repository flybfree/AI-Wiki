## Summary
This paper introduces Navigation-Informed Embeddings (NIE), a method that adapts a dense retriever using traces generated by agentic search workflows. By treating the stopping document as a soft positive and incorporating path documents with ordinal constraints, NIE improves support Recall@20 from 72.2 to 78.0 on an independent benchmark. The stop‑oriented version reaches 76.9 overall, while the path variant lifts long‑path performance to 55.4 versus 46.7 for the unadapted encoder.

## Key Takeaways
- NIE adapts a dense retriever without new relevance labels by leveraging query, retrieval, and stopping traces from agentic workflows.
- The stop‑document is used as a soft positive, boosting overall recall to 78.0 compared with the baseline of 72.2.
- Incorporating preceding path documents with geometric decay yields higher long‑path performance (55.4) than the unadapted encoder (46.7).

## Context
Agentic retrieval workflows generate rich trace information that is often discarded, yet this data can inform model adaptation. Recent work shows that such traces can improve retrieval without costly label updates, aligning with trends toward efficient, zero‑label fine‑tuning in large language models.

## Implications
For practitioners, NIE offers a lightweight way to keep dense retrievers relevant as query distributions shift, reducing the need for manual labeling or synthetic data. This approach supports scalable adaptation in production systems where trace logs are already available, fostering more responsive and cost‑effective retrieval pipelines.
