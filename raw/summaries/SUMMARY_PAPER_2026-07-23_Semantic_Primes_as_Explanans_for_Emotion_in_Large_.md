## Summary
This paper investigates whether the semantic primes of the Natural Semantic Metalanguage (NSM) can serve as a primitive explanatory mechanism for emotions in large language models. Experiments across four instruction‑tuned LLMs reveal that NSM primes are recoverable internal variables and that they influence model emotion more strongly than conventional appraisal directions, while also being treated interchangeably with corresponding emotional states.

## Key Takeaways
- NSM primes are identifiable as internal elements within the LLMs examined.
- When a prime‑based direction is used instead of an appraisal‑based one, the model’s generated emotion changes about threefold and is more selective in its impact.
- The model does not distinguish between the prime explication and the associated emotion, treating them as equivalent.

## Context
Understanding how emotions emerge from neural representations remains a central challenge for AI researchers. Traditional explanations often rely on higher‑level appraisal concepts that may obscure underlying computational processes. This work shifts focus to more fundamental linguistic primitives, offering a new lens for probing model internals without relying on external affective labels.

## Implications
Identifying primitive variables like NSM primes could lead to more transparent and interpretable models, reducing reliance on opaque emotional outputs. Practitioners may leverage this insight to design better prompt engineering strategies that align with the underlying computational mechanisms of LLMs.
