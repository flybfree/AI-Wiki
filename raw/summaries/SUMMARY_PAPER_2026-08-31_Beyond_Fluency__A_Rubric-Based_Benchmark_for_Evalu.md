## Summary
This paper introduces a rubric‑based benchmark that measures Saudi dialect and cultural competence in large language models, moving beyond standard MSA fluency tests. The evaluation of four state‑of‑the‑art systems shows they cluster within a narrow macro‑average band (42.7‑53.1 %) and all record at least one negative‑scoring prompt.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark separates model‑agnostic criteria derived from ground truth from model‑specific scoring, enabling systematic comparison across diverse systems.  
- All four models fall within a tight performance range, with none surpassing 55 % accuracy, indicating that Saudi dialect competence remains largely unsolved.  
- Ambiguous framing dominates error types (37.3 % of failures), whereas outright hallucination accounts for only 11.2 %, highlighting the need to assess pragmatic and register nuances.

## Context
Current AI benchmarks overwhelmingly prioritize Modern Standard Arabic fluency, overlooking the everyday dialectal and cultural richness that underpins real‑world usage in Saudi Arabia. This gap leaves models inadequately prepared for authentic interactions with native speakers.

## Implications
For researchers, the rubric provides a reproducible framework to evaluate cultural competence beyond raw token generation. For industry practitioners, it signals that deploying Arabic language systems must account for dialectal and pragmatic nuances to avoid user dissatisfaction and miscommunication.
