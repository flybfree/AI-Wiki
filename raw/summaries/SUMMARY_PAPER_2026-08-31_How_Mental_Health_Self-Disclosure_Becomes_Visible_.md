## Summary
This study investigates how mental health self‑disclosure becomes visible in online communities by analyzing Reddit posts from users with eight different conditions. The authors find that language surrounding disclosure is most noticeable in the month before diagnosis for six of the conditions, earlier for PTSD and later for borderline personality disorder, and it persists after the announcement rather than fading away.

## Key Takeaways
- Language‑visible burden peaks just prior to disclosure for six conditions, indicating a heightened emotional or communicative load at that moment.  
- The theme “Seeking Clinical Explanations” shows the largest early‑to‑late difference before disclosure in five conditions, yet only nine of three hundred language–engagement correlations survive after correcting for user‑specific diagnosis disclosures.  
- Reddit engagement rarely tracks what users write; most correlation signals disappear when the diagnostic anchor is removed.

## Context
The paper contributes to AI research on social media analytics by demonstrating that community interaction metrics do not reliably reflect individual mental health needs, highlighting a gap between observable language and actual clinical relevance. This work underscores the importance of contextualizing algorithmic monitoring with user‑specific data rather than relying solely on aggregate engagement signals.

## Implications
For platform designers, this research suggests that current engagement models may mislead efforts to support users in need of mental health resources. Practitioners should consider diagnostic anchors when interpreting social media activity and develop tools that respect the uneven visibility of disclosure processes.
