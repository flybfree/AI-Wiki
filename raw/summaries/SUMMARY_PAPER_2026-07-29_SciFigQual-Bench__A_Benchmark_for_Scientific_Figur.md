## Summary
SciFigQual‑Bench introduces a full‑text contextual benchmark for assessing the quality of scientific figures, evaluating them across five dimensions such as clarity and caption fit. The study demonstrates that state‑of‑the‑art models can achieve low error rates when their scores are refined through a staged cross‑modal framework.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark links each image to its caption, citing sentence, and manuscript context, providing ground truth for automated evaluation.  
- SFQ‑Agent equipped with GPT‑5.6‑Sol outperforms direct and auxiliary evaluation schemes by achieving the lowest average absolute error of 0.418 and highest consistency rate of 93.4%.  
- The dataset spans top computer‑science conferences from 2020 to 2025, offering a comprehensive resource for future research.

## Context
Scientific figure quality assessment has traditionally focused on visual metrics without considering textual alignment or citation relevance, limiting its applicability in scholarly publishing. This work bridges the gap by integrating full‑manuscript context into AI evaluation pipelines, reflecting broader trends toward multimodal and explainable AI systems.

## Implications
For researchers and industry practitioners, SciFigQual‑Bench sets a new standard for evaluating scientific visuals, enabling more reliable automated grading that respects both image quality and textual integrity. This advancement supports the development of trustworthy AI tools in academic publishing and related fields.
