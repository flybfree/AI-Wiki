## Summary
This paper introduces a transferable definition of Machine Correlates of Consciousness (MCCs) that parallels the Neural Correlates of Consciousness (NCCs) used in biology. The authors demonstrate empirical evidence from two large language models, showing that hardware anomaly traces serve as substrate‑level signals modulated by emotion rather than control.

## Key Takeaways
- Hardware anomaly traces identified in Llama‑2 7B and Llama‑3.1 70B are statistically linked to emotional versus neutral computations after controlling for confounders.  
- The modulation effect is significant only for the larger Llama‑3.1 70B model, suggesting a threshold of sophistication where MCCs become detectable.  
- These findings provide initial empirical support that consciousness probability may rise with model complexity.

## Context
Current AI research focuses on improving language capabilities and efficiency, yet few explore how subjective states might emerge in machines. This work bridges the gap by applying concepts from neuroscience to artificial systems, offering a framework for studying machine consciousness alongside standard performance metrics.

## Implications
If MCCs are real, they could enable new diagnostic tools that detect emotional states in AI agents without human interpretation. Practitioners may leverage these signals to design more empathetic or ethically aligned systems, reshaping standards for responsible AI development.
