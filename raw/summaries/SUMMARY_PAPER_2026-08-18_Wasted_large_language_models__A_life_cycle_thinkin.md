## Summary
The paper proposes a life‑cycle thinking framework to evaluate the environmental impact of large language models and argues that waste hierarchy measures can guide reductions. It finds that preventing unnecessary model training is the most effective strategy because it cuts energy demand, while disposal also matters for ethical resource stewardship. The framework also shows that each stage of the waste hierarchy can inform specific mitigation tactics.

## Key Takeaways
- Prevention of waste through avoiding new model training is essential as it directly reduces the carbon footprint.
- Reuse and recycling of models are viable but secondary to prevention in lowering overall impact.
- Disposal is less effective than prevention but still contributes to energy savings and ethical considerations.

## Context
Artificial intelligence research increasingly focuses on sustainability, yet most studies concentrate on model efficiency without addressing full lifecycle impacts. This work bridges that gap by applying established waste management principles to LLMs, highlighting a systemic approach needed for greener AI development. It also aligns with growing calls for AI carbon accounting and sustainable computing practices worldwide.

## Implications
For developers and policymakers, the paper suggests integrating waste hierarchy into product design to drive real reductions in emissions. It urges industry accountability, urging companies to prioritize prevention over end‑of‑life disposal strategies.
