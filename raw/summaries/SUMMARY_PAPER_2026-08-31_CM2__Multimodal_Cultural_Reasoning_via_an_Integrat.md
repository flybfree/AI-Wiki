## Summary
This paper introduces CM2, a multi-agent framework that enables multimodal cultural reasoning by integrating perception, retrieval-augmented generation, networked reasoning, gated fusion, and reward-driven feedback. Experiments on the CM2D benchmark show consistent improvements over chain-of-thought and standard reasoning methods across various MLLM backbones.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework’s multi-agent structure mirrors human cultural interpretation pathways, allowing cross‑modal arbitration.
- Ablation studies demonstrate that each module—perception, retrieval, gated fusion, and feedback—contributes uniquely to performance gains.
- Conflict resolution within the agents is validated by analyses showing genuine multimodal trade‑offs.

## Context
Multimodal Large Language Models excel in narrow STEM tasks but struggle with holistic cultural understanding. This work addresses a gap by modeling interdisciplinary reasoning as an integrated pipeline rather than isolated steps.

## Implications
CM2 could be deployed to generate culturally aware responses in education, media, and customer service where context blends images, text, and symbols. Practitioners may adopt its modular design for scalable, explainable multimodal AI systems.
