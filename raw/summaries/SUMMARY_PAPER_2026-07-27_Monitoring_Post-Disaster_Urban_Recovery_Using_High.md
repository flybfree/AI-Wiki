## Summary
The paper presents an unsupervised framework that uses multi‑temporal SAR time series and deep‑learning anomaly detection to monitor urban recovery after the 2023 Türkiye‑Syria earthquake. By applying the method to four severely affected cities, it reveals distinct reconstruction patterns such as persistent anomalies in damaged zones, temporary container settlements, and new residential districts.

## Key Takeaways
- Persistent temporal anomalies identified by COSMO‑SkyMed SAR series correspond to ongoing reconstruction activities in physically altered areas.
- The framework generates spatially explicit recovery maps that highlight both structural changes captured by SAR and temporary settlement patterns.
- Nighttime‑light indicators from SDGSAT‑1 complement SAR data, showing later stages of electrical restoration and socioeconomic activity.

## Context
This work advances AI‑driven disaster monitoring by demonstrating how unsupervised deep learning can extract meaningful signals from remote sensing time series without requiring labeled recovery datasets. It contributes to the growing body of research on spatio‑temporal anomaly detection for urban resilience.

## Implications
Practitioners can deploy this approach as a scalable tool for early detection of reconstruction progress, reducing reliance on costly ground surveys. The integration of SAR and nighttime light data offers a complementary monitoring strategy that can be adapted to other disaster scenarios worldwide.
