## Summary
The paper investigates whether LLM-generated error highlights and correction suggestions improve professional translator productivity, translation quality, and user experience compared to standard post‑editing and QE‑derived highlights. The study found no gains in productivity or quality over regular post‑editing, but APE highlights were preferred over QE highlights and suggestion prompts enhanced overall satisfaction.

## Key Takeaways
- The study shows that professional translators did not become faster or produce higher‑quality translations when using LLM‑derived error highlights, indicating limited impact on core metrics.  
- APE‑generated highlights received better reception than those derived from QE, suggesting users find them more relevant to the translation task.  
- Adding correction suggestions improved the user experience, even though they did not boost productivity or quality.

## Context
As machine translation quality rises, post‑editing becomes less labor‑intensive but still requires human oversight. Researchers are exploring AI‑assisted tools that can surface errors and provide corrective guidance to reduce workload without compromising output standards.

## Implications
These findings suggest that while LLM‑based error highlights may not replace traditional QE, they could be valuable as supplemental aids for improving user satisfaction in post‑editing workflows. Practitioners might integrate such suggestions selectively to enhance the editing experience rather than rely on them for productivity gains.
