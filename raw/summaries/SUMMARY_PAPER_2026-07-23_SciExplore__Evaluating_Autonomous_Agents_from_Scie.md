## Summary  
The paper introduces SciExplore, a benchmark designed to evaluate autonomous agents in scientific research workflows. It demonstrates that current LLMs and agents struggle with complex tasks such as structured knowledge synthesis, revealing significant performance gaps.

## Key Takeaways  
- The benchmark comprises 103 expert‑curated tasks across ten scientific disciplines, covering database navigation, ambiguous literature retrieval, missing reference completion, and cross‑source structured knowledge synthesis.  
- Performance degrades sharply as task complexity increases, with extremely low accuracy on the most challenging synthesis tasks.  
- Existing benchmarks focus on general‑domain retrieval or static question answering, omitting higher‑level reasoning needed for realistic scientific workflows.

## Context  
Current AI evaluation often neglects multi‑step, domain‑specific reasoning required in scientific contexts. This work fills that gap by providing a comprehensive suite of tasks that simulate the full information‑seeking and synthesis process.

## Implications  
These findings warn that autonomous agents cannot yet reliably handle nuanced integration of heterogeneous scientific sources. It urges developers to prioritize grounding and synthesis capabilities for future research tools.
