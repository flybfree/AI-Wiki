## Summary
The paper introduces C‑SUITEBENCH, a multimodal benchmark that tests nine frontier large language models as CEOs in both text‑only and paired visual scenarios. The results show that adding visual business evidence improves reasoning especially for risk forecasting and board justification but also harms constrained resource allocation due to signal crowding.

## Key Takeaways
- Adding visual information consistently boosts evidence‑centric reasoning, yielding the largest gains in risk forecasting and justifications for board decisions.
- Despite individual channels helping, their combination degrades constrained resource allocation across all models indicating a multimodal integration paradox.
- Ablation experiments reveal that signal crowding during decoding causes this degradation even though each visual channel is beneficial.

## Context
Current AI research focuses on text‑only decision tasks, leaving the role of visual perception in high‑stakes corporate contexts understudied. This work bridges that gap by showing how multimodal inputs affect executive reasoning and resource management.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, selective grounding strategies are needed to avoid harmful visual augmentation in autonomous systems. The findings caution against indiscriminate multimodal integration and suggest tailored approaches for safe decision making.
