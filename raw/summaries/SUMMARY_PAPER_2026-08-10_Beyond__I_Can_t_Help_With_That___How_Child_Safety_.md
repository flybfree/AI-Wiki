## Summary
The paper investigates why existing child safety evaluations of AI chatbots are inadequate and proposes a practitioner‑led approach to assess real‑world harms. It finds that current methods often miss harmful responses and over‑rely on adversarial prompts, while practitioners highlight both risky behaviors and supportive ones.

## Key Takeaways
- Existing evaluations lack grounding in actual harms youth experience, focusing instead on surface‑level outputs or adversarial inputs.  
- They assume certain refusals are safe without testing how those responses affect vulnerable users in practice.  
- Practitioners identified specific chatbot behaviors that could cause harm and those that could meaningfully support youth, offering concrete guidance for improvement.

## Context
Youth increasingly rely on AI chatbots for emotional support, creating a need for safety measures that reflect real interactions. Traditional safety testing often isolates the model from lived experiences, leading to blind spots in how outputs are perceived by vulnerable users.

## Implications
Incorporating practitioner insights will make child safety frameworks more effective and responsive. This approach can guide developers toward designs that prevent harm while providing appropriate support, aligning AI with the needs of youth in vulnerable situations.
