## Summary
This paper introduces a systematic behavioral taxonomy that bridges the gap between ODD specifications and demonstrable ADS actions, offering 21 competencies across Highway, Urban, and Hub domains. The framework links ODD layer parameters to safety, compliance, comfort, and efficiency properties, enabling scenario families for SOTIF testing. Validation is demonstrated through rule‑enforced trajectory optimization.

## Key Takeaways
- The taxonomy organizes behavioral competencies into longitudinal and lateral axes within three operational domains, providing a standards‑grounded mapping from ODD to actionable behavior.  
- Each competency is evaluated against four properties—safety, compliance, comfort, and efficiency—to ensure comprehensive SOTIF coverage.  
- The Hub domain emerges as underspecified and isolated, highlighting a research gap that requires dedicated investigation.

## Context
Autonomous driving safety relies on translating high‑level ODD rules into concrete behaviors, yet existing tools lack a unified taxonomy linking specifications to measurable actions. This work fills that void by grounding the mapping in established AV standards such as AVSC00008202111 and SAE J3237.

## Implications
Practitioners can now generate test scenarios systematically, improving SOTIF evidence generation for regulators. The identified Hub gap signals a strategic area where further research could enhance urban edge‑case handling in ADS systems.
