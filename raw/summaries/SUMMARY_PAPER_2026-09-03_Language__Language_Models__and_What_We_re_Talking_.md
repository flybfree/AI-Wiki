## Summary
The paper argues that language models are not neutral technical artefacts but reflect the linguistic worlds encoded in their training data, especially when using translated or synthetic Italian data. It questions whether these models represent true Italian language or merely a product of curation and testing on unnatural inputs. The author suggests that the intended purpose—technical tool versus linguistic study—shapes what language we expect the model to produce.

## Key Takeaways
- Language models trained on translated or synthetic data may not embody authentic Italian but rather reflect the specific linguistic world imposed by those sources, raising doubts about their representativeness as true language models.
- The distinction between technical products and tools for studying language matters because it determines whether we aim to create models that mimic a language or investigate its structure, leading to diverse outcomes.
- Testing on unnatural data reveals how fragile these models are; they may fail in ways that expose the limits of their training context rather than genuine linguistic inability.

## Context
This work fits within ongoing debates about the role of natural language processing in AI research, where many systems prioritize performance over linguistic authenticity. By focusing on Italian as a case study, the authors highlight how model behavior can be shaped by data provenance and curation choices, prompting a reevaluation of what constitutes a “language model” beyond raw accuracy metrics.

## Implications
For practitioners, this suggests that designing models should consider both functional goals and the ethical implications of representing language. Industry may need to separate research prototypes from production tools to avoid misrepresenting linguistic capabilities. Researchers must also be aware that testing on synthetic inputs can mask deeper issues about data quality and model fidelity.
