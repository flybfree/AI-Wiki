## Summary
This paper introduces AgentIdeaBench, a benchmark that tests scientific ideation in both static observation and active exploration scenarios. The study shows that active exploration yields significantly higher performance across 33 large language models compared to the static setup, highlighting a capability gap that can be exploited for improvement.

## Key Takeaways
- Active exploration improves model output roughly twice as fast as static observation, revealing more headroom in reasoning and generation tasks.  
- The benefit of active exploration is unevenly distributed, favoring high‑capability models while weaker ones see less gain.  
- A structured thought‑experiment loop called Scientific World Modeling boosts mid‑level models but does not further help frontier models that already internalize such reasoning.

## Context
The rapid advancement of large language models has shifted scientific research from static knowledge retrieval to dynamic, goal‑driven agent workflows. Benchmarks like AgentIdeaBench are needed to measure how well these agents can generate novel hypotheses under active learning conditions.

## Implications
For researchers, AgentIdeaBench provides a standardized metric to evaluate and compare AI scientists in the era of autonomous research. Practitioners can leverage the findings to prioritize model upgrades that address capability gaps rather than merely chasing static performance improvements.
