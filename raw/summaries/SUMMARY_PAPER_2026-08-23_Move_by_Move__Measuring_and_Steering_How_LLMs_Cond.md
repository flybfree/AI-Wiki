## Summary
The paper introduces an ontology of ten therapeutic moves derived from the MULTI-60 inventory to quantify how large language models (LLMs) engage in psychotherapy-like conversations. By applying this framework to real counseling transcripts and model‑led sessions, it compares move distributions between licensed human clinicians and a panel of frontier LLMs. The results show that models overuse inquiry, neglect psychoeducation, and are heavily context‑anchored, yet exposing the ontology improves alignment with human therapists.

## Key Takeaways
- Models use inquiry up to three times more often than humans, indicating a tendency to ask rather than act.  
- They rarely provide psychoeducation, missing an essential component of therapeutic communication.  
- Moves are strongly context‑anchored, carrying forward strategies initiated by clinicians but seldom initiating them themselves.

## Context
In AI research, understanding the behavioral patterns of language models in sensitive domains is crucial for ensuring safety and efficacy. This work bridges clinical practice with machine learning, offering a quantitative benchmark that can guide responsible model development.

## Implications
For clinicians, these findings highlight where LLMs fall short, informing training and oversight strategies. For developers, the ontology provides a tool to align models with therapeutic standards without fine‑tuning, supporting ethical AI deployment in mental health.
