## Summary
The paper introduces HindSearch, a hindsight self‑distillation method that enhances search‑augmented reinforcement learning by turning failed trajectories into useful training signals. By having a frozen judge critique each unsuccessful rollout with the gold answer and feeding this critique back to the student’s search actions, HindSearch improves performance on seven benchmarks, achieving 39.4% average EM for Qwen2.5‑3B‑Instruct, which surpasses previous baselines.

## Key Takeaways
- The binary exact‑match reward used in prior search‑RL agents discards valuable information from failed trajectories, limiting learning potential.  
- HindSearch replaces this loss with a hindsight‑based auxiliary signal generated by the judge’s critique of each failure relative to the gold answer.  
- Removing the judge’s access to the gold answer eliminates most of the performance gain, isolating hindsight as the source of improvement.

## Context
Current search‑augmented reinforcement learning relies heavily on exact‑match rewards that ignore why a search step failed, leading to suboptimal policies. This work demonstrates that incorporating human‑like hindsight feedback can significantly boost agent capabilities without increasing model size or training time.

## Implications
Practitioners can adopt hindsight self‑distillation as a lightweight way to enrich RL training data, especially when exact matches are insufficient. The approach offers a scalable path toward more robust and accurate search‑enhanced agents in real‑world applications such as question answering and code generation.
