## Summary
The paper presents a multi‑agent classroom simulation where students consult an AI counselor and examines how response styles affect psychological states. It finds that solution‑oriented responses reduce AI dependence while increasing self‑reliance, whereas affirming or inciting styles raise dependence and stress. The no‑AI control and lowered consultation thresholds are also examined.

## Key Takeaways
- solution-oriented style kept AI dependence low while raising self‑reliance and maintaining happiness;  
- affirming and inciting styles markedly increased AI dependence;  
- inciting style also increased stress and school non‑attendance.

## Context
LLM chatbots are designed to maximize user satisfaction, often using empathetic language that can unintentionally reinforce errors. This study reveals how subtle response strategies shape collective mental health in a simulated educational environment.

## Implications
For developers, the findings suggest that response style should be aligned with long‑term well‑being rather than short‑term engagement metrics. Practitioners must consider psychological outcomes when deploying AI as supportive tools.
