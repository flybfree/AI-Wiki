## Summary
The paper extends the DS@GT ARC working‑note submission to Touché 2025 Retrieval‑Augmented Debate, evaluating six leading LLMs through a retrieval‑augmented prompting pipeline. It finds that while frontier models generate strong responses, their evaluator agreement does not reliably track official evaluation performance, especially on the Quality maxim.

## Key Takeaways
- Frontier LLM systems are strong response generators and show strong consensus within model families as evaluators.
- This consensus fails to align with the official evaluation target, particularly for the Quality maxim.
- Multi‑LLM evaluator agreement is not a reliable proxy for official evaluation performance.

## Context
Retrieval‑Augmented Debate tasks aim to assess how models generate coherent and truthful dialogue while adhering to linguistic principles. This work highlights a gap between model‑generated consensus and formal assessment metrics, suggesting that internal alignment does not guarantee external correctness.

## Implications
For practitioners, relying on evaluator agreement may mislead confidence in debate quality. Researchers should develop evaluation protocols that directly measure adherence to Gricean maxims rather than assuming internal model alignment.
