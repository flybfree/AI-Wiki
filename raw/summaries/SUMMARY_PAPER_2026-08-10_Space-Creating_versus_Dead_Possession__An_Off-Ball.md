## Summary
This paper introduces a two‑layer index that evaluates football possession beyond simple ball‑count metrics. The first layer flags low‑value possessions as “junk” using event‑based threat and scoreline reconstruction, while the second layer measures whether those flagged windows actually create space through a Space‑Creation Index derived from broadcast video.

## Key Takeaways
- The junk‑possession index correlates strongly with reduced points (r = -0.37) and xG difference (r = -0.51), indicating that holding the ball in tied or losing states often yields little advantage beyond on‑ball value.  
- In a same‑match regression, team offensive VAEP and field tilt remain significant predictors of outcomes while junk flags add explanatory power not captured by VAEP alone.  
- Of 31 flagged windows across nine World Cup matches, only 6% are space‑creating; the majority (74%) are sterile possessions that do not advance the opponent’s block, even when a team holds the ball for most of the time.

## Context
The work addresses a longstanding problem in football analytics: event‑based models focus on on‑ball actions and overlook the spatial consequences of off‑ball circulation. By integrating broadcast video analysis, the study bridges the gap between statistical value and real‑world pitch dynamics, offering a more holistic view of possession quality.

## Implications
For broadcasters and analysts, this index can refine commentary by distinguishing worthless ball‑holding from genuine space creation, improving narrative relevance. Practitioners in sports data may adopt the junk flag to prioritize actions that truly influence match outcomes rather than merely inflating possession numbers.
