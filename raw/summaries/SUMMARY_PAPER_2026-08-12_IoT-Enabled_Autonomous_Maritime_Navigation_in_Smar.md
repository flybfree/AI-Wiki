## Summary
The paper proposes a curriculum‑guided reinforcement learning framework that trains a shared recurrent policy for IoT‑enabled autonomous maritime navigation in smart ports. The approach centralizes training offline while all decisions are made on the device, improving reliability and scalability under partial observability and dense traffic. Simulations show better collision avoidance and stability than baseline methods.

## Key Takeaways
- Centralized offline training enables a shared recurrent policy that generalizes to unseen high‑density port scenarios.
- The curriculum adapts difficulty gradually, enhancing temporal reasoning for edge devices with limited compute.
- Full onboard execution ensures compliance with IoT edge intelligence paradigms and reduces latency.

## Context
Autonomous navigation in maritime environments faces challenges from partial observability and congestion, where centralized control is impractical. This work aligns with trends toward decentralized AI that respects resource constraints while maintaining safety. It contributes to the broader field of reinforcement learning for real‑world robotics by demonstrating a scalable policy sharing mechanism.

## Implications
Practitioners can deploy IoT navigation units in smart ports without needing cloud infrastructure, lowering costs and improving resilience. The framework supports regulatory compliance with autonomous systems and paves the way for future integration of mixed human‑machine decision loops.
