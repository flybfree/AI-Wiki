## Summary
The paper introduces PHOENIX, a system that equips CubeSats with an onboard fine‑tuned small language model to detect faults during the 85‑minute ground‑unavailable window and issue recovery commands within the 5‑10 minute contact period. The AI agents on Earth read structured health reports generated by a diffusion‑model trained synthetic fault data, enabling predictive self‑healing. Preliminary benchmark results show improved detection rates compared to existing methods.

## Key Takeaways
- PHOENIX deploys a compact fine‑tuned small language model that runs continuously on the satellite’s Aethero NxN-ECM hardware, allowing real‑time fault reasoning without ground contact.
- The system uses a memory of past repairs so inference can be reused, reducing computational load and extending satellite lifetime.
- Ground AI agents process a concise health report generated by a diffusion model trained on synthetic fault data, enabling validated commands within the limited contact window.

## Context
CubeSat missions face high failure rates because faults during orbital downtime go unnoticed. Traditional ground‑based monitoring cannot act quickly enough, limiting mission success. This work addresses that gap with an autonomous AI loop that operates entirely within the satellite’s own resources and a short Earth contact.

## Implications
The approach reduces reliance on frequent ground contacts, lowering launch costs and increasing operational windows for data collection. It also demonstrates how synthetic data can augment scarce fault examples in machine learning, offering a scalable model for future small‑sat constellations.
