## Summary
The paper proposes a framework for ethical LLM‑assisted research that clarifies when scientific reasoning should remain under human control. It argues that the key to preserving epistemic legitimacy is not how much work the model does, but whether adequate verification and accountable human ownership are in place. The authors introduce an “epistemic audit” as a structured record of delegation, verification, provenance, and responsibility.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework distinguishes five constructs—origin O(g), verification V(g), responsibility R(g), human ownership M(g), and outcome E(g)—to separate the source of a claim from its checking process.  
- Ethical legitimacy depends on adequate verification (V(g)) and accountable human ownership (M(g)), not on the degree of machine involvement itself.  
- An epistemic audit is created to make AI‑assisted reasoning transparent, reviewable, and traceable.

## Context
Large language models are now routine tools in scientific tasks such as literature synthesis, hypothesis generation, coding, and formal reasoning. Their integration raises concerns about who remains responsible for the validity of results and how to ensure that delegated work does not erode epistemic accountability. This paper offers a normative lens to address those concerns.

## Implications
Researchers can adopt the epistemic audit to embed verification steps into their workflows, thereby preserving scientific credibility. Industry practitioners may use the framework to design AI tools that are transparent and ethically accountable, fostering trust among users and stakeholders.
