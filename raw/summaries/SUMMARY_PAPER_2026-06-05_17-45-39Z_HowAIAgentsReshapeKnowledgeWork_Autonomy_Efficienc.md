## Summary
The paper investigates how autonomous AI agents like Computer replace conversational assistants such as Search in knowledge work. It finds that agents automate task decomposition, speed up completion, and broaden the scope of tasks users can perform.

## Key Takeaways
- Autonomous agents generate 26 minutes of work per session versus only 33 seconds for Search, automating decomposition and execution that users would otherwise do manually.
- Per‑query dissatisfaction drops by 55% on Computer compared with Search, indicating higher execution quality from autonomous handling.
- Completion time falls from 269 to 36 minutes (87% reduction) and estimated cost drops by 94%, while agents also query across occupational boundaries and composite tasks.

## Context
Autonomous AI agents represent a shift from reactive assistants to proactive executors that can orchestrate multi‑step workflows. This study provides empirical evidence of the efficiency gains when such agents replace human‑mediated search processes in knowledge work.

## Implications
For practitioners, integrating autonomous agents can dramatically cut task time and cost while improving output quality. The expanded scope suggests new roles for AI to handle complex, cross‑functional tasks that were previously limited by manual orchestration.
