## Summary
This paper evaluates large language models’ ability to perform literature reviews in physics, astrophysics, and cosmology by comparing expert-selected references with those generated by three mid‑2025 LLMs (ChatGPT-4o, Gemini) and a 2026 model ChatGPT Pro 5.5. The study finds only about 6% overlap between human and AI selections, indicating that current models cannot independently reproduce expert search skills, though they can assist humans.

## Key Takeaways
- Human‑selected literature overlaps with AI‑generated references at less than 6%, showing limited independent competence of mid‑2025 LLMs in reproducing expert search.  
- Fabricated references account for 3% of AI outputs, while the majority (64%) are real papers containing metadata mismatches such as incorrect titles, authors, years, journals, DOIs or links.  
- The 2026 ChatGPT Pro 5.5 model eliminates both fabrication and metadata errors in a single‑project test, suggesting rapid improvement potential.

## Context
The rapid advancement of large language models has prompted researchers to explore their utility beyond general conversation, especially in knowledge‑intensive fields like physics where precise literature tracking is critical. This study provides empirical evidence that while LLMs can generate plausible citations, they remain prone to hallucinations and factual errors, highlighting the need for human oversight.

## Implications
For scientists, AI tools must be integrated as collaborative assistants rather than autonomous sources of information, requiring verification protocols to catch metadata mismatches. Industry stakeholders should invest in model monitoring pipelines to ensure reliability before deploying LLMs in research workflows.
