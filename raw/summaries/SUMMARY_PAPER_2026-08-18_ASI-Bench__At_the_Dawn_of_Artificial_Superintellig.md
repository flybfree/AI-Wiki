## Summary  
The paper introduces ASI-Bench, a benchmark designed to test AI’s ability to explore novel scientific problems and execute research autonomously by progressively reducing human guidance. Across 18 state‑of‑the‑art agent‑model configurations the average performance drops from 50.91 with full guidance to 26.62 when agents must select methods themselves, highlighting a sharp decline in capability. The benchmark is open for community contribution.

## Key Takeaways  
- ASI-Bench evaluates both innovative exploration and autonomous scientific execution across 60 tasks in 11 domains, moving beyond traditional answer‑based benchmarks.  
- Performance drops sharply when AI must determine methods independently, showing current systems rely heavily on human guidance.  
- The benchmark is publicly available at https://asibench.apexin.ai/submit for researchers to add new tasks and challenge AI limits.

## Context  
Artificial superintelligence research hinges on demonstrating that machines can generate original knowledge without constant human direction. Existing benchmarks focus on retrieval or task completion, which do not capture the full scope of scientific autonomy. ASI-Bench addresses this gap by integrating expert‑crafted projects with progressive withdrawal of guidance.

## Implications  
For AI developers, ASI-Bench provides a clear metric to assess progress toward truly autonomous research agents. For the broader field, it signals that current models are still limited and that significant engineering breakthroughs may be required before achieving superintelligent capabilities. Practitioners can use the benchmark to prioritize research on method selection and self‑directed execution.
