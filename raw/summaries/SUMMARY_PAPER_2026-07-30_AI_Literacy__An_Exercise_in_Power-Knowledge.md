## Summary
The paper argues that current AI literacy frameworks focus on technical use and responsible consumption, which limits genuine epistemic agency. It proposes a reconceptualized approach based on power‑knowledge theory, critical consciousness pedagogy, and digital scholarship to empower individuals as agents who can critique and govern AI.

## Key Takeaways
- The existing consumer‑oriented view of AI literacy reinforces structural inequities by privileging those with access to tools.
- A three‑part framework—contextual use, critical interrogation, participatory governance—shifts focus from passive consumption to active epistemic participation.
- Unequal access to generative AI reproduces historical epistemic injustices and must be addressed within any literacy model.

## Context
Generative artificial intelligence is rapidly reshaping information production across media, education, and policy. Scholars increasingly call for literacy frameworks that go beyond skill‑based training to address power dynamics in knowledge creation.

## Implications
Practitioners should redesign curricula and tools to embed critical interrogation of AI outputs and inclusive governance practices. This shift could reduce algorithmic bias and foster more equitable participation in the AI ecosystem.
