## Summary
The paper introduces agentic programs as a new kind of scientific software that merges deterministic algorithms with bounded language model judgments to perform tasks in materials science. The authors demonstrate this concept through DeMARS, an agentic program that builds atomistic models from disordered crystal structures measured experimentally.

## Key Takeaways
- Agentic programs combine deterministic computational steps with limited LLM-based reasoning, allowing scientific judgment within a defined scope.
- The system performs task-specific verification at each step, ensuring correctness while delegating complex interpretation to the language model.
- DeMARS undergoes episodic maturation, improving performance over time as it accumulates feedback and experience.

## Context
This work responds to the growing integration of large language models into scientific workflows where human‑like reasoning is needed for ambiguous data. In computational materials science, traditional pipelines rely on fixed algorithms that cannot adapt to irregular experimental measurements without manual intervention.

## Implications
Agentic programs could automate routine model construction tasks, reducing reliance on expert labor and accelerating discovery cycles. For industry and researchers, such systems promise scalable, reproducible workflows that handle variability in experimental data while maintaining scientific rigor.
