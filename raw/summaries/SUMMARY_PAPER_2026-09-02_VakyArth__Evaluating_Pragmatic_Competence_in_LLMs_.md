## Summary
This paper introduces VakyArth, a benchmark for pragmatic competence in Indic languages such as Hindi, Punjabi, Tamil, and Malayalam. The study finds that multilingual LLMs consistently fail to capture culturally rooted pragmatic meanings across five phenomena: deixis, speech acts, implicature, social pragmatics, and coherence.

## Key Takeaways
- MCQ accuracy exceeds NLI accuracy in all model‑language combinations, indicating a systematic gap in handling explicit versus implicit meaning.  
- Translation performance does not reliably track pragmatic understanding, suggesting that automatic translation metrics can misclassify fluent yet pragmatically unfaithful outputs.  
- Indo‑Aryan languages show a translation advantage over Dravidian languages, highlighting language‑specific cultural conventions.

## Context
Pragmatic reasoning is essential for natural human communication but remains underexplored in low‑resource Indic languages where linguistic diversity and cultural specificity are high. This work bridges that gap by providing the first large‑scale evaluation of pragmatic abilities across multiple Indian languages.

## Implications
For AI developers, VakyArth underscores the need to incorporate language‑specific pragmatic knowledge into model training pipelines. Practitioners must move beyond generic translation metrics toward assessments that respect cultural nuances in meaning construction.
