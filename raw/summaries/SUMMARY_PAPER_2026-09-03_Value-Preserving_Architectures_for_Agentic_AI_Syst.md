## Summary
The paper proposes value-preserving architectural patterns for agentic AI systems to align complex multi‑agent setups with human values such as privacy fairness and safety. It identifies three specific designs: a federated topology for privacy, a distributed architecture for pluralism, and a guard‑agent system for detecting unfairness. The study links these patterns to real‑world use cases.

## Key Takeaways
- A privacy‑aware architecture uses a federated topology that keeps data local, reducing exposure of personal information.
- A distributed architecture encourages diversity among agents, supporting pluralistic perspectives in decision making.
- A guard‑agent component continuously monitors interactions to flag and mitigate unfair outcomes.

## Context
Agentic AI and large language model based multi‑agent systems are rapidly deployed across domains where human values must be safeguarded. Traditional software engineering emphasizes functionality over ethical design, creating a gap that this work addresses by focusing on architectural choices that embed responsibility.

## Implications
These patterns provide concrete guidance for developers building trustworthy MAS, helping them anticipate value risks and integrate safeguards early in the system lifecycle. The framework can become a reference standard for responsible AI architecture.
