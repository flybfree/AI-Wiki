## Summary
The paper introduces Evo-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate large language models' ability to autonomously improve their operating harness across Search, Office, and General agent domains. Experiments show top frontier models achieve up to 16.6 point gains, approaching human‑engineered baselines.

## Key Takeaways
- Top models reach massive absolute gains of about 16.6 points on all three suites.
- Autonomous evolution outperforms artificial harness in General tasks and excels in Search but underperforms in Office due to workflow specificity.
- The synthesized harnesses serve as transferable reasoning structures that consistently boost diverse policy models.

## Context
LLMs have accelerated autonomous agent research, yet most evaluations only measure static task performance. Harness evolution—agents’ capacity to self‑optimize their operational framework—remains underexplored and hard to benchmark objectively.

## Implications
Evo-Bench provides a standardized metric for harness evolution, guiding researchers toward agents that can iteratively refine their reasoning structures. Practitioners can leverage these findings to design more adaptable, self‑improving systems in real‑world applications.
