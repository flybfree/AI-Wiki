## Summary
This research note demonstrates how trademark data can serve as a complementary source to patents for tracking artificial intelligence innovation. By analyzing AI‑related trademarks, the authors reveal that companies use these technologies to launch new goods and services, offering insights into both the scope and mechanisms of exploitation. Their empirical study of Italian firms shows that trademark records provide timely, globally accessible information across all economic sectors.

## Key Takeaways
- AI trademarks capture dimensions of innovation that patents miss, such as brand‑related applications and market positioning.  
- The data source is timely and available worldwide, covering every industry rather than being limited to specific patent filings.  
- Empirical evidence from Italian firms demonstrates how trademark adoption patterns reflect the diffusion and commercialization of AI technologies.

## Context
Artificial intelligence is rapidly evolving, prompting scholars and policymakers to seek robust metrics for innovation measurement. Traditional patent databases are incomplete because they focus on technical inventions rather than market‑driven uses. Trademarks fill this gap by documenting how firms brand and sell AI‑enabled products and services.

## Implications
These findings suggest that trademark data can be integrated into broader AI innovation monitoring frameworks, enhancing policy relevance. Practitioners in technology management may leverage trademark records to forecast competitive moves and strategic positioning of AI ventures.
