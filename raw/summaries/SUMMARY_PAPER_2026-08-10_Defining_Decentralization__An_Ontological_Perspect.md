## Summary
The paper addresses the lack of a universal definition of decentralization in computer science and proposes a formal ontology that treats it as a relational property specific to communication systems. It introduces two new metrics—Void Tolerance and Imperviousness—to evaluate systems such as federated learning and blockchain, demonstrating consistent assessments where prior definitions fail.

## Key Takeaways
- Decentralization is defined as a system‑level property rather than merely the spread of components across nodes.
- The ontology distinguishes decentralization from distribution, preventing conflation with trust or specific architectures.
- Void Tolerance measures how much unused communication capacity remains, while Imperviousness quantifies resistance to external interference.

## Context
Decentralized AI and machine learning are rapidly expanding, yet existing research often mixes up related concepts, leading to ambiguous analyses. This work fills a semantic gap that hampers reproducibility and cross‑system comparison in the field.

## Implications
Practitioners can now apply objective metrics to any communication architecture, enabling fair benchmarking across heterogeneous platforms. The ontology provides a common language for future research on decentralized systems, strengthening both theoretical rigor and practical deployment decisions.
