## Summary
This paper introduces SymptomAI, a set of conversational AI agents that interview users about symptoms and generate differential diagnoses within the Fitbit app. In a randomized study with 13,917 participants, the AI’s diagnoses were significantly more accurate than those from independent clinicians (OR = 2.47, p < 0.001). The results also show that agents that conduct dedicated symptom interviews outperform baseline user‑guided conversations.

## Key Takeaways
- SymptomAI achieved higher diagnostic accuracy than human clinicians in a blinded comparison across 517 validated cases.
- Agents that perform a dedicated symptom interview before offering a diagnosis improve performance relative to default user‑guided discussions (p < 0.001).
- The findings generalize beyond Fitbit users, as an auxiliary analysis of 1,509 conversations from a general US population panel confirmed similar results.

## Context
The study addresses the gap between high‑performing diagnostic language models on curated medical vignettes and their real‑world applicability for everyday symptom reporting. By deploying AI agents in a consumer health app, researchers provide empirical evidence that conversational systems can rival clinical assessments in routine settings.

## Implications
These results suggest that dedicated symptom interviewing strategies are essential for reliable AI diagnostics, guiding developers to prioritize structured data collection over casual chat. The study also highlights the potential of wearable‑linked AI to uncover strong physiological correlations with acute conditions, offering new avenues for preventive health interventions.
