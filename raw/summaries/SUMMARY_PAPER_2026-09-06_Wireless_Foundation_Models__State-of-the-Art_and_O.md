## Summary
This paper surveys wireless foundation models (WFMs) by organizing them into five physical‑layer task families and examining how they are pretrained, adapted, and evaluated. The analysis reveals that while WFMs show growing evidence of reusable representations, this evidence is inconsistent across tasks, datasets, architectures, and evaluation settings.

## Key Takeaways
- Reusable wireless representations exist but their extent varies widely depending on the specific dataset, modality, architecture choice, pretraining objective, adaptation protocol, and distribution shift.  
- Evaluation protocols are fragmented and lack standardization, making it difficult to compare transfer performance across studies or task families.  
- Open challenges include limited data availability for some tasks, absence of unified benchmarks, and insufficient out‑of‑distribution adaptation capabilities.

## Context
The rapid expansion of foundation models in AI has driven a need for reusable representations that can be applied across diverse applications. Wireless systems generate massive heterogeneous data streams, yet current research often treats each physical‑layer task as isolated, hindering progress toward truly generalizable models.

## Implications
For researchers and industry practitioners, the findings emphasize the importance of designing robust pretraining objectives and adaptable architectures to enable flexible deployment in real‑world RF environments. Addressing these gaps will be crucial for advancing practical wireless AI solutions that can scale across tasks and environments.
