## Summary
This paper introduces constitutional governance in metric spaces, a unified polynomial‑time framework that integrates social choice aggregation, deliberation, amendment, and consensus. The authors prove the generalised median satisfies key properties such as no misreport weak dominance and bounded compromise gaps, demonstrating it works across seven canonical settings.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework assigns each amendable component of a constitution a metric space, an aggregation rule, and a supermajority threshold, enabling end‑to‑end polynomial‑time decision making.  
- The generalised median is shown to be the optimal rule that scores proposals against the status quo while preserving sincere voting dominance and limiting compromise gaps.  
- Amendments are handled by the same rule with possibly higher thresholds, allowing self‑governance without iterative isolation of stages.

## Context
The work addresses a longstanding challenge in algorithmic social choice: integrating multiple governance processes into a single efficient computation. By leveraging metric spaces and AI mediation, it offers a theoretical benchmark for real‑time consensus in digital communities.

## Implications
For practitioners designing online platforms or organizational decision systems, this framework provides a scalable method to balance participation, fairness, and adaptability. It also sets a new standard for research on polynomial‑time social choice algorithms in complex, multi‑stage settings.
