## Summary
This paper reviews hybrid quantum neural networks, which blend classical neural‑network components with quantum information processing units to create a practical framework for near‑term quantum technologies. It highlights that while recent benchmarks have not yet shown large‑scale advantages, theoretical work identifies tasks where quantum models provide provable benefits and hybrid designs achieve promising results using compact quantum parts and few trainable parameters.

## Key Takeaways
- Hybrid quantum neural networks combine classical neural‑network modules with quantum circuits to leverage both classical learning strengths and quantum parallelism.  
- Theoretical analyses show that certain machine‑learning tasks have provable advantages for quantum models, suggesting potential for scalable advantage if hardware constraints are met.  
- Practical implementations using small quantum components and reduced trainable parameters have demonstrated promising performance on selected problems.

## Context
Hybrid quantum neural networks sit at the intersection of deep learning and emerging quantum computing, aiming to bridge the gap between theoretical promise and real‑world deployment. Their development is crucial for advancing AI research that can exploit quantum resources without requiring full quantum computers.

## Implications
For researchers, this review offers a clear roadmap to prioritize hybrid architectures over fully classical or purely quantum models. For industry, it signals early opportunities to integrate quantum components into existing neural‑network pipelines, potentially accelerating innovation in data‑heavy applications.
