## Summary
The paper proposes an ontology-guided multi-agent framework for extracting evaluation objects from Chinese library and information science review texts; it combines candidate discovery, ontology-constrained classification, and domain-specific processing to achieve high performance metrics compared with rule-based and zero-shot baselines.  

## Key Takeaways
- Multi-agent workflow improves recall and stability.  
- Ontology-based boundary constraints enhance fine-grained classification and reduce category confusion.  
- The framework attains Precision 90.33%, Recall 84.55%, Entity-level F1 87.34%.  

## Context
This study addresses the challenge of extracting abstract, context-dependent evaluation objects in scholarly reviews, where existing methods focus on research articles and struggle with ambiguous types and overlapping categories.  

## Implications
The framework supports structured utilization of evaluative texts for evidence-based research and STI mining, offering methodological support to scholars seeking reliable data extraction. It also provides a scalable approach that can be adapted to other domains requiring entity-level evaluation object identification.
