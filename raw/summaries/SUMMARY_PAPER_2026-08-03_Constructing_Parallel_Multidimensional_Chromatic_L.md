## Summary
This paper introduces two multidimensional chromatic lexicons for Russian and English to enable corpus‑assisted analysis of colour terms in literary texts. The study compares the use of confirmed chromatic lexis, visual descriptors, hue, saturation, and temperature across poems by Andrei Bely and Emily Dickinson, showing that confirmed chromatic terms appear 3.4 times more often in Bely’s work than in Dickinson’s.

## Key Takeaways
- The lexicons contain 224 Russian and 141 English entries, each classified by hue, saturation, and temperature to capture the full semantic range of colour references.  
- A pilot analysis revealed a significant disparity: confirmed chromatic terms are far more frequent in Bely’s corpus than in Dickinson’s, highlighting language‑specific lexical patterns.  
- The study demonstrates that coding decisions about borderline cases (Ambiguous_visual) do not alter the primary quantitative findings, confirming the robustness of the multidimensional approach.

## Context
In natural language processing and literary analysis, tools for extracting and interpreting colour terminology are scarce, limiting cross‑lingual comparative studies. This work contributes a reusable methodology that bridges AI research with linguistic corpus analysis, offering a concrete example of how multimodal lexicon construction can support semantic mining in historical texts.

## Implications
The transparent procedure described here can be adopted by researchers developing multilingual NLP pipelines to enrich semantic representations with domain‑specific concepts like colour. Practitioners in literary AI and cultural heritage digitization will benefit from the ability to systematically quantify visual language across languages, fostering more nuanced interpretive models.
