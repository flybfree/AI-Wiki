## Summary
This study evaluates how well AI-generated multimodal stories align with the lived practices, language, values, and visual expectations of five African communities. Quantitative annotations and qualitative focus groups reveal that cultural alignment depends on contextual fit rather than mere markers, leading to a taxonomy of alignment categories and misalignment mechanisms.

## Key Takeaways
- Cultural alignment is not determined by recognizable markers alone but by how those markers integrate social, linguistic, procedural, and visual contexts within each community.  
- The evaluation produced a taxonomy comprising five broader cultural marker categories and eight recurring mechanisms that cause misalignment between AI stories and local expectations.  
- Automated LLM judges show variable reliability across communities, with no single judge providing consistent scores, highlighting the need for community-calibrated validation.

## Context
The paper addresses a growing concern in AI ethics: the risk of culturally insensitive outputs when models generate content without grounding in local realities. By combining mixed methods and diverse cultural representatives, it contributes to research on inclusive AI design that respects regional diversity.

## Implications
For practitioners, this work calls for evaluation pipelines that incorporate community feedback to calibrate automated judges before deployment. It underscores the importance of human review where automated scores deviate from culturally grounded judgments, ensuring AI storytelling remains respectful and effective across diverse African contexts.
