## Summary
This study examines how large language models embody personality-driven emotional labor strategies in everyday social interactions. The results show that models tend toward deep acting when their associated personas score high on Conscientiousness and Emotional Stability, indicating a systematic preference for internalizing emotions over surface displays.

## Key Takeaways
- Models align more towards deep acting than genuine expression under persona conditions.
- Conscientiousness consistently predicts the preference for deep acting strategies across evaluations.
- The influence of personality on model outputs varies both between models and emotional contexts, as confirmed by entropy analysis.

## Context
Understanding how AI systems mirror human personality traits is crucial for designing socially aware agents. This research bridges psychometric theory with generative modeling, offering a framework to assess the authenticity of AI personas in real‑world scenarios.

## Implications
These findings suggest that developers must calibrate LLM personas to align with intended emotional labor profiles to avoid unintended affective mismatches. Practitioners can leverage this insight to improve user trust and reduce misinterpretation of AI behavior.
