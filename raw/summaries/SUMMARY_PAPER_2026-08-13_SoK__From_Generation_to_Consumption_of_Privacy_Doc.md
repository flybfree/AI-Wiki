## Summary
This paper presents a systematic software engineering service‑oriented knowledge (SoK) that maps the lifecycle of privacy documents from definition to consumption across 290 papers published between 2010 and 2025. The analysis reveals fragmented approaches to generation, analysis, compliance checking, and usability evaluation, highlighting fifteen research trends and twenty‑one open opportunities.

## Key Takeaways
- Privacy documents are often defined ad‑hoc and lack a consistent scope definition across studies, leading to overlapping or contradictory interpretations of what constitutes a privacy policy versus a short notice.  
- The majority of work focuses on manual extraction techniques that ignore the structured nature of modern data sources, resulting in low accuracy when extracting consent terms from UI elements.  
- Existing evaluation frameworks prioritize usability for end‑users but neglect developer‑centric metrics such as integration effort and maintainability.

## Context
The rapid rise of AI‑driven platforms has intensified the need for transparent data practices, yet most research treats privacy documents as static artifacts rather than dynamic components of software systems. This gap limits the ability to align document generation with automated compliance checks and continuous improvement cycles.

## Implications
For researchers, the SoK offers a shared baseline to prioritize AI‑centric policy analysis tools that can ingest large volumes of consent data. Practitioners should adopt lifecycle‑oriented design patterns to reduce manual effort and ensure both user trust and system integrity.
