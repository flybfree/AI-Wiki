## Summary
The paper investigates whether the ACL Responsible NLP Checklist is merely a box‑ticking exercise by analyzing 73,922 checklist responses from EMNLP 2025 Main and Findings tracks. It finds that many authors treat ethics as an afterthought, produce brief or empty justifications, and sometimes create logical contradictions between parent and child checklists.

## Key Takeaways
- Authors often isolate ethics questions from the bulk of their paper, treating them as superficial add‑ons rather than integrated considerations.
- A large proportion—44.9 %—of justifications are poor or bad‑faith, consisting of brief or empty statements that do not substantiate claims.
- Logical contradictions appear in 6 % of checklists, and 53 % of authors dismiss potential risks or social impacts that should be addressed.

## Context
The Responsible NLP Checklist was introduced to promote transparency and ethical practice in machine‑learning research. This study reveals a gap between the checklist’s intent and its implementation across major conferences, highlighting concerns about superficial compliance.

## Implications
If checklists are perceived as mere box‑ticking, they may undermine genuine responsible AI development. Practitioners should adopt stricter evaluation criteria such as minimum word counts and deeper scrutiny of potential risks to ensure meaningful progress.
