## Summary
This paper presents findings from interviews with five AI safety benchmark developers across academia and industry, revealing a significant disconnect between how safety benchmarks are perceived as useful tools for evaluating generative AI risks and their actual practical utility in real-world safety evaluations of deployed systems. The authors identify two core challenges driving this gap—difficulty achieving inter-rater reliability on safety constructs and insufficient clarity around addressing threats to external validity—and argue that benchmarks must be actively integrated with deployment contexts rather than merely grounded in them.

## Key Takeaways
- The study uncovers a fundamental disconnect between the intended usefulness of safety benchmarks as cited in academic literature and their practical usefulness when applied to safety evaluations of deployed generative AI systems, suggesting that benchmarks as currently designed and maintained do not adequately serve real-world evaluation needs.
- Two specific challenges contribute to this disconnect: first, the difficulty of achieving inter-rater reliability on safety constructs, meaning that different evaluators may disagree on what constitutes a safety failure or risk, undermining benchmark consistency; second, a persistent lack of clarity regarding how to address threats to external validity, meaning benchmarks may not generalize to the actual conditions under which AI systems operate in production environments.
- The authors propose concrete pathways for more transparent communication between academic and industry AI safety stakeholders, arguing that benchmarks must not only be grounded in deployment contexts but actively integrated with them to bridge the gap between theoretical rigor and practical reality.

## Context
As generative AI systems are increasingly deployed across high-stakes domains such as healthcare, finance, and public services, safety benchmarks have become the dominant mechanism for assessing model risks. However, a growing body of evidence indicates that these benchmarks often fail to capture the nuanced, context-dependent safety concerns that arise in real-world deployment. This paper addresses a critical gap in the AI safety research ecosystem by centering the perspectives of the very people who design and maintain these benchmarks, revealing how institutional and methodological pressures create misalignment between academic evaluation standards and industry safety practices.

## Implications
For practitioners and industry leaders, this research signals that relying on standardized safety benchmarks alone may provide a false sense of security about deployed AI systems, and that evaluation frameworks must be co-developed with deployment teams to ensure external validity. For the AI safety research community, the findings call for structural changes in how benchmarks are designed, maintained, and communicated across academic-industry boundaries, emphasizing transparency and shared understanding as prerequisites for benchmarks that genuinely reflect real-world risk. Policymakers and standards bodies should take note that current benchmarking practices may not adequately protect public safety without deeper integration with operational deployment contexts.
