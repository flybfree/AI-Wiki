## Summary  
This paper investigates the impact of generative artificial intelligence on Norwegian newsrooms during the 2025 parliamentary election campaign, revealing a paradoxical “GenAI Catch‑22” where heavy reliance on AI undermines the capacity to monitor it. The study, based on ten months of interviews with managers and journalists, shows that optimistic visions for audience‑facing AI services collapsed while internal use persisted, exposing vulnerabilities in newsroom resilience.

## Key Takeaways  
- Newsrooms’ internal use of GenAI creates an internal threat rather than focusing solely on external disinformation.  
- Managers’ sociotechnical imaginaries lead to unrealistically optimistic expectations that cause planned audience‑facing AI services to fail and revert to mundane applications.  
- The Catch‑22 arises because monitoring AI relies on human expertise, yet extensive AI use erodes that expertise, hindering effective oversight.

## Context  
The rapid integration of generative AI into media production has sparked debate about its role in preserving journalistic integrity and democratic discourse. This research adds a concrete case study to the growing literature, illustrating how institutional optimism can clash with practical constraints within newsroom operations.

## Implications  
Practitioners must balance innovation with safeguards, recognizing that over‑reliance on AI can degrade human expertise essential for system monitoring. The findings urge newsrooms to adopt balanced strategies that preserve both technological benefits and editorial oversight.
