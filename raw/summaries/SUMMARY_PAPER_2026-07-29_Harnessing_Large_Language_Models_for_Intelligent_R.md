## Summary
This paper proposes a task‑oriented large language model (LLM) framework for dynamic resource scheduling in the Internet of Everything. By integrating task semantics, network conditions, and constraints into a multidimensional decision model, the approach enables more efficient allocation of computational resources. Simulation results show faster convergence, lower latency, and reduced energy consumption compared with conventional methods.

## Key Takeaways
- The LLM‑driven mechanism constructs a multidimensional scheduling model that aligns task requirements with real‑time network states, improving resource utilization efficiency.
- A prompt generation method is introduced to create a deep semantic link between tasks and network conditions, facilitating accurate feasibility verification.
- Real‑time external evaluation provides feedback on scheduling strategies, enhancing robustness and adaptability while boosting convergence speed and energy savings.

## Context
The rapid expansion of IoE devices creates complex, heterogeneous workloads that traditional rule‑based schedulers struggle to manage. Large language models, with their advanced reasoning abilities, offer a promising alternative for handling such nuanced tasks in resource‑constrained environments.

## Implications
This work demonstrates how LLMs can transform network orchestration, offering practitioners a scalable solution for improving IoE performance and sustainability. As AI integration deepens across critical infrastructure, the methodology may become a standard reference for intelligent resource management.
