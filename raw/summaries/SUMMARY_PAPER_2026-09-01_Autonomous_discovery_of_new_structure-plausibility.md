## Summary
The paper introduces autonomous agents that generate and test two million candidate structure‑plausibility laws, identifying eight Plausibility Rules for Inorganic Structures (PRIS). These rules encode five mechanisms of structural stability and show that experimental structures satisfy PRIS at 82–99 % while Pauling’s rules succeed only 6.5 %. The strictest set detects 87.9 % of damaged crystal structures, far outperforming distance cutoffs.

## Key Takeaways
- The agents produce and actively refute two million candidate laws, leaving eight robust PRIS that encode five physical mechanisms.
- PRIS-derived synthesis score screens 83.7 % of hard‑to‑synthesize structures while retaining 80.7 % of experimentally verified ones.
- Inverse‑design runs reduce the DFT validation queue by up to 67.3 % and keep 99.2 % of candidates whose bulk moduli meet design targets.

## Context
This work advances AI‑driven materials discovery by replacing heuristic screening with chemically interpretable laws, aligning computational effort with physical plausibility. It demonstrates how active learning agents can uncover hidden constraints in crystal formation that traditional methods ignore.

## Implications
Practitioners can rely on PRIS to prioritize high‑probability synthesis candidates, cutting experimental cycles and accelerating material development. The framework also explains rare low‑symmetry structures in GNoME, offering deeper insight into AI bias and error propagation.
