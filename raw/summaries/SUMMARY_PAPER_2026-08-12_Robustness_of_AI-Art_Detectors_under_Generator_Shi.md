## Summary
This paper investigates how current AI‑art detectors perform when applied to images generated by a newer diffusion model, Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium (SD3.5m). The study shows that detectors trained on older U‑Net‑based latent diffusion models degrade significantly under generator shift, misclassifying many SD3.5m images as human‑created while keeping false positives low. Among the evaluated detectors, the CLIP ViT‑L/14 model achieves the best performance.

## Key Takeaways
- Detectors trained on U‑Net‑based latent diffusion artwork suffer a sharp drop in accuracy when applied to SD3.5m images, indicating limited robustness to architectural changes.  
- The CLIP ViT‑L/14 classifier outperforms other models, suggesting that transformer‑based encoders may generalize better across generator families.  
- Grad‑CAM analysis reveals weaker and more diffuse activation patterns on false negatives, highlighting where the detectors fail to focus attention.

## Context
The rapid evolution of text‑to‑image generative models creates a growing gap between detection tools and the underlying architectures they were trained on. As new diffusion systems produce increasingly realistic art, existing classifiers risk becoming obsolete, undermining efforts to protect intellectual property and combat misinformation. This research underscores that AI‑art detectors must be designed with flexibility in mind.

## Implications
For developers, the findings call for detector frameworks that can adapt or be retrained quickly when new generator models appear. Practitioners should consider layered defenses where multiple detection methods complement each other to maintain reliability across diverse generative systems.
