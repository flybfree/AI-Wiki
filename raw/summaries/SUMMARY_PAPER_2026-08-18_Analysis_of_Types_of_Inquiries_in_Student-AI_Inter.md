## Summary
This paper investigates how CS2 students formulate questions to generative AI during two programming tasks, using the Graesser et al. taxonomy of 18 inquiry types. The study finds that only a few question categories dominate student interactions and that these categories shift significantly as problems evolve.

## Key Takeaways
- A small subset of question types accounts for most of the inquiries, indicating predictable patterns in how students seek help from AI.
- The diversity of questions decreases over time within each task, suggesting that initial uncertainty gives way to more targeted queries.
- Automated classification using a few‑shot learning approach reliably maps student dialogues onto the taxonomy, revealing the underlying structure of inquiry.

## Context
Understanding the nature and evolution of student‑AI interactions is crucial for designing effective educational technologies. As AI tools become integral to problem solving, researchers need insights into how learners adapt their questioning strategies across different tasks and difficulty levels.

## Implications
These findings suggest that educators can anticipate common question types and plan interventions to address gaps in inquiry depth. For developers, the taxonomy provides a framework to evaluate AI support quality and tailor responses to the specific stages of student problem solving.
