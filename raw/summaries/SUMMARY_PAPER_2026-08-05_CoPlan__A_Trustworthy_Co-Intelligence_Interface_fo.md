## Summary  
The paper introduces CoPlan, a co‑intelligent and contestable interface that enables human care planners to interact with specialized AI agents in generating and reviewing care plan arguments. The system demonstrates that combining co‑intelligence with contestability preserves human agency while maintaining clinical accountability.  

## Key Takeaways  
- A multi‑agent workflow lets each agent produce candidate interventions and supporting or challenging arguments, which are then reviewed by humans before the final plan is generated.  
- Human care planners can accept, reject, modify, or add arguments at any stage, ensuring that recommendations remain open to inspection and revision.  
- The approach was validated in an aging‑in‑place scenario, integrating adaptive team recruitment, role‑based argument review, final care‑plan generation, and practical follow‑up scheduling agents.  

## Context  
Current AI systems for care planning often output fixed recommendations that limit stakeholders’ ability to inspect or challenge the plan, potentially undermining trust and clinical judgment. This work addresses that gap by proposing a design where AI contributions are contestable and collaborative.  

## Implications  
The contestable interface model offers a template for trustworthy human‑AI systems across healthcare, encouraging industry adoption of designs that preserve accountability while adapting to real‑world constraints. Practitioners can leverage this framework to build care plans that evolve with input from both clinicians and patients.
