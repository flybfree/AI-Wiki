## Summary
This paper evaluates deep graph generative models against traditional network science configurations by measuring topological similarity and the effectiveness of node immunization strategies for epidemic mitigation. The study finds that two deep generative models produce synthetic networks whose structures closely mirror real-world social contact patterns, enabling reliable identification of effective intervention nodes.

## Key Takeaways
- Two deep graph generative models generate synthetic networks with high topological fidelity to actual social networks.
- These models allow precise detection of node immunization targets that effectively suppress epidemic or misinformation spread.
- The results demonstrate a strong advantage over configuration models in capturing complex network structures from data-driven learning.

## Context
Network science focuses on understanding the structural properties of real-world graphs, while deep generative models leverage neural networks to learn these distributions directly. This work bridges the gap between theoretical network analysis and practical AI applications by providing synthetic data that respects privacy constraints yet retains realistic topology.

## Implications
The findings suggest that deep graph generators can serve as valuable tools for epidemic planning and misinformation control without exposing sensitive contact data. Practitioners in public health, cybersecurity, and social media research can rely on these models to test interventions efficiently and safely.
