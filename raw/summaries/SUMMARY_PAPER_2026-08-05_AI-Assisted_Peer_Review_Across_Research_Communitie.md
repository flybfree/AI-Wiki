## Summary
This paper surveys reviewer‑facing AI policies across 111 top AI/NLP conferences and medical journals to reveal regulatory gaps, then evaluates AI‑generated peer reviews from ICLR 2026 and Nature Communications using a mixed dataset. It finds that while LLMs produce detailed and fluent reviews, they suffer systematic flaws such as overly positive recommendations, generic criticism, and uneven evidence grounding.

## Key Takeaways
- The survey uncovers substantial regulation differences between AI/NLP conferences and medical journals, highlighting inconsistent oversight of AI‑assisted review tools.
- Current LLMs generate detailed and fluent reviews but exhibit weaknesses like overly positive recommendations, generic criticism, and uneven evidence grounding that can mislead evaluators.
- Aggregate quality scores alone overestimate review quality; a multi‑dimensional evaluation approach is necessary.

## Context
AI‑assisted peer review aims to streamline scientific publishing by offloading parts of the review process to language models. However, without systematic insight into how venues regulate such tools or how capable they truly are, adoption risks misalignment with scholarly standards and potential bias amplification.

## Implications
For researchers, adopting AI reviews requires a rigorous evaluation framework that considers multiple dimensions beyond simple scores. For publishers and policy makers, clear guidelines on permissible AI use can mitigate ethical concerns and ensure equitable access to high‑quality review assistance.
