## Summary
The paper proposes a computational argumentation framework to locate zones of possible agreement (ZOPA) between conflicting parties by quantifying their subjective reasoning about peace agreements. By merging bipolar argument models and applying them to the Palestinian‑Israeli conflict, the authors demonstrate that negotiators can pinpoint mutually acceptable clauses grounded in citizens' narratives.

## Key Takeaways
- The ZOPA is defined not only by agreement terms but also by how each side reasons about those terms, allowing negotiators to see where subjective reasoning overlaps.
- Merging two bipolar argumentation frameworks creates a unified model that can be applied to real survey data and large language‑model retrieved arguments.
- Preliminary experiments show the framework can identify feasible ZOPAs in the Palestinian‑Israeli context, highlighting its potential for policy analysis.

## Context
This work advances AI research on conflict resolution by integrating natural language reasoning with formal argumentation theory. It demonstrates how machine‑learned models of public discourse can be used to generate structured representations of polarized opinions, a step toward more transparent and explainable negotiation tools.

## Implications
For peace‑building practitioners, the framework offers an objective way to visualize where citizens’ reasoning converges, supporting evidence‑based dialogue. In industry, it could inform AI systems that mediate disputes by surfacing common ground, enhancing trust and efficiency in conflict resolution processes.
