## Summary
This paper analyses and evaluates interoperability among defence and national security ontologies by documenting over sixty publicly available ontologies and introducing a new track for the Ontology Alignment Evaluation Initiative. The work defines eight matching tasks, consensus alignments derived from state-of-the-art alignment systems, and manually curated silver-standard mappings that combine consensus with unique suggestions.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper documents more than 60 defence and national security ontologies to provide a comprehensive baseline for interoperability studies.
- It introduces a new OAEI track consisting of eight matching tasks, consensus alignments aggregated from multiple alignment systems, and silver-standard mappings that merge consensus with unique suggestions.
- Manual validation creates the silver-standard by combining consensus outputs with mappings suggested by only one system.

## Context
The growing reliance on ontologies in defence and national security highlights a critical need for seamless data exchange across heterogeneous knowledge graphs. This work addresses the challenge of aligning diverse, domain‑specific ontologies, which is essential for integrated decision support systems.

## Implications
For practitioners, this framework offers a standardized evaluation methodology that can guide ontology development and integration projects. The results provide actionable insights into alignment performance, supporting more robust interoperability solutions in security applications.
