## Summary
The paper introduces Economic World Models (EWMs) as generative agents that simulate economies by modeling heterogeneous agents, beliefs, actions, and market institutions, producing dynamics from within. It proposes a six-level capability ladder for building such systems and finds most existing work stays at lower levels while higher‑level self‑evolving agents and aligned twins are rare.

## Key Takeaways
- EWM systems are organized into a six‑tier ladder ranging from fixed rule‑based agents to adaptive LLM‑driven, self‑evolving agents and sim‑to‑real economic twins. 
- Current research is concentrated on the lower tiers with simple agent simulations, while higher tiers involving endogenous institutions and persistent empirical alignment are scarce. 
- The authors release a curated paper list and resources to accelerate development of high‑fidelity sandbox environments for decision‑makers and AI training.

## Context
Economic simulation is crucial for testing policy scenarios and evaluating AI agents in complex market settings. This work bridges generative modeling with economic theory, offering a systematic blueprint that could reshape how we design realistic macroeconomic sandboxes.

## Implications
For policymakers, these models provide more trustworthy tools to anticipate systemic risks. For industry and practitioners, the roadmap enables scalable creation of AI‑driven agents that learn from simulated economies while maintaining alignment with real data.
