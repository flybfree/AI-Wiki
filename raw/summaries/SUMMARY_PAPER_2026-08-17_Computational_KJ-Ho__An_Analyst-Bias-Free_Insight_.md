## Summary
This paper introduces Computational KJ-Ho, a framework that aims to extract insights from qualitative data without human analyst bias by using a domain‑specialized LLM. The approach is organized into three layers: structuring the data, extracting insights, and generating strategies. Preliminary studies in Japanese marketing show that continued pre‑training on a marketing corpus is essential for performance.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework integrates the KJ method, Grounded Theory, and Peircean abduction to create an epistemology where structure emerges from the data itself rather than analyst assumptions.
- A three‑layer architecture leverages domain‑specialized embeddings derived from continued pre‑training on a marketing‑research corpus to enable cross‑interview analysis and insight extraction.
- Two novel evaluation metrics, InsightExtraction-F1 and MarketingQA, are proposed to measure the quality of extracted insights in a Japanese context.

## Context
The integration of qualitative research methods with large language models raises questions about bias and cultural relevance. This work contributes by demonstrating how continued pre‑training can produce embeddings that respect non‑Western epistemologies, offering a model for AI systems that handle diverse data sources without imposing Western biases.

## Implications
For researchers, the framework provides a scalable alternative to manual analysis that reduces inter‑analyst variability. For industry practitioners, it enables faster generation of actionable insights while maintaining cultural sensitivity, potentially reshaping how marketing teams leverage qualitative data in global markets.
