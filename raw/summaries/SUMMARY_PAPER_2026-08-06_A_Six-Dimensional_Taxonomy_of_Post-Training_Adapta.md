## Summary  
The paper aims to unify the scattered post‑training adaptation literature by presenting a six‑dimensional taxonomy that organizes techniques along mechanism, goal, data requirement, persistence, structural scope, and model type. It clarifies overlapping terms such as fine‑tuning versus retrieval augmentation and demonstrates how adaptation strategies evolve from traditional machine learning through deep learning, foundation models, large language models, and multimodal large language models.

## Key Takeaways  
- The taxonomy distinguishes commonly conflated terms like fine‑tuning, retrieval augmentation, and prompting by mapping them onto distinct dimensions of mechanism and goal.  
- It shows how adaptation strategies evolve from traditional machine learning through deep learning, foundation models, large language models, and multimodal large language models, highlighting a progression in technical capability.  
- The framework maps relationships among techniques including inheritance, supersession, hybridization, and layered deployment stacks, providing a vocabulary for tracking model changes.

## Context  
Post‑training adaptation is essential as AI systems become more complex and deployed in high‑stakes environments where governance demands explainability and control. This taxonomy helps researchers and practitioners navigate the growing body of methods without being overwhelmed by fragmented terminology.

## Implications
