## Summary
The paper challenges the common view that generative AI should eliminate friction in design ideation by focusing solely on speed and output quality. It argues that this perspective overlooks the essential role of reflection‑in‑action, where designers accept, reject, and rework ideas to develop rationales for communication. The authors propose viewing AI as a “friction agent” that supports reflective processes rather than merely smoothing workflows.

## Key Takeaways
- Generative AI tools are often framed as friction reducers, but the paper contends they should instead act as agents that create cognitive friction necessary for reflection‑in‑action.  
- Reflection involves both the selection of ideas and the construction of rationales that enable designers to think with and share those ideas effectively.  
- In group ideation, AI’s role is to facilitate the expression and explanation of ideas so that others can extend, reject, or combine them further.

## Context
Design ideation relies on iterative cycles where mental models are built through deliberate acceptance and rejection of proposals. Generative AI has been marketed as a productivity booster, yet its impact on the underlying cognitive processes remains underexplored. This paper situates AI within the broader design workflow to highlight how tools that impose smoothness may inadvertently hinder reflective depth.

## Implications
For designers, this reframing suggests integrating AI in ways that encourage deliberate thinking rather than automatic acceptance of outputs. Practitioners should consider AI as a scaffold for generating rationales, not a replacement for them. Industry adoption must balance efficiency with the preservation of reflective practices to maintain creative quality and collaborative learning.
