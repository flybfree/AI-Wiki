## Summary
This paper introduces StartupBench, an end-to-end benchmark that uses market‑validated AI startup workflows to evaluate general‑purpose agents. The study finds that even the strongest models complete only about 30 % of tasks despite notable partial progress.

## Key Takeaways
- Complex instruction following is identified as a major source of failure, causing many agents to misinterpret or ignore detailed user instructions.
- Domain‑specific expertise limits performance, as agents struggle with specialized knowledge that is not part of their general training.
- Market‑validated workflows remain beyond the reliable capabilities of current agents, indicating that real‑world tasks are still largely out of reach.

## Context
Current AI research often relies on researcher‑selected benchmarks that do not reflect actual user demands. This paper shifts focus to tasks that have proven demand in commercial products, highlighting a gap between reported progress and practical utility.

## Implications
The findings matter for the field by showing that advancing general‑purpose agents must address complex instruction following and domain expertise. Industry practitioners should design workflows with these constraints in mind to avoid overestimating agent reliability.
