## Summary
The paper investigates how UI/UX designers form design intent when interacting with generative AI, focusing on the lexical oscillations between vague vibe language and precise operational code. It shows that design intent is not a one‑way transmission but emerges through back‑and‑forth exchanges.

## Key Takeaways
- Designers oscillate between high‑level vibe language (L1) and detailed operational code (L3), showing that intent is not a one‑way translation but a back‑and‑forth process.
- Mismatches with AI outputs trigger designers to reinterpret their original meaning, shifting the interaction from instruction to consultation.
- One non‑oscillating trajectory serves as a negative case that may indicate conceptual misalignment rather than tool failure.

## Context
Generative AI tools are increasingly used as design assistants, yet most research treats prompts as static inputs. This study adds reflexive insight into the cognitive work of designers navigating ambiguous outputs and highlights the importance of situated interaction.

## Implications
For practitioners, recognizing oscillation can improve prompt engineering and collaborative workflows. For researchers, it underscores the need to model AI‑human interaction as dynamic rather than linear.
