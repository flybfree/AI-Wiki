## Summary
This paper investigates why large language models (LLMs) sometimes agree with human judgments of creativity and other times do not, across three studies that compare model outputs to human evaluations. The authors find that LLMs often align with human standards on novelty but diverge when contextual factors are considered, revealing distinct evaluation criteria within each model.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs generally follow a narrower set of human creativity standards and show strong convergence in the novelty dimension while showing clear divergence in the contextual dimension.  
- Individual LLMs have unique, model‑specific standards that affect which ideas they label creative, with broader standards better matching human distinctions.  
- Contextual information significantly shifts human ratings but has little impact on LLM outputs, indicating a lack of sensitivity to social or market cues.

## Context
The study highlights a persistent gap between automated and human creativity assessment, underscoring the need for transparent evaluation criteria in AI systems that mimic human judgment. By exposing how model‑specific standards shape outcomes, it contributes to broader discussions about alignment, fairness, and reliability in generative AI.

## Implications
Choosing an LLM as a creativity evaluator is not neutral; different models will rank ideas differently based on their internal standards. Practitioners must select or fine‑tune models that match the evaluation context they require, ensuring that automated judgments reflect the intended criteria rather than hidden biases.
