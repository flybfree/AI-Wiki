## Summary
This paper challenges the common belief that humans stay in the loop only because current AI is insufficient, arguing instead that human participation persists for three reasons: technical complementarity, normative developmental value, and emergence through co‑construction. The authors show that even highly capable AI systems cannot fully replace human involvement when these dimensions are at play.

## Key Takeaways
- Technical or complementarity grounds arise when humans bring capabilities or perspectives unavailable to AI, meaning the system’s performance depends on human expertise beyond its algorithmic limits.
- Normative or developmental grounds exist because participation itself is valuable for preserving human agency and fostering learning, not merely as a fallback when AI fails.
- Emergence grounds occur when the target activity is not pre‑specified but arises through interaction; in such cases human contribution is constitutive of the outcome rather than a temporary aid.

## Context
The rapid advancement of generative and autonomous AI has sparked debates on how much work can be delegated to machines. Traditional automation models assume that once an AI reaches a certain capability, humans will step back. This paper reframes the discussion by emphasizing that participation is not always a symptom of limitation but a structural feature of many tasks.

## Implications
For designers and ethicists, this view suggests that future AI systems must be evaluated not only on their ability to replace human labor but also on how they support or enable emergent activities. Recognizing persistent human involvement can guide more humane, effective, and responsible automation strategies across industry and research.
