## Summary
The paper introduces INS‑ActBench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating professional actuarial capability of large language models. It gathers 12,050 Q&A pairs from public exams and sample questions released by 16 actuarial associations, covering knowledge, long-context case reasoning, and spreadsheet/R-code practice tasks.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark separates domain knowledge from long‑context reasoning and tool use, highlighting that these abilities are distinct evaluation dimensions.  
- Frontier LLMs perform strongly on the INS‑Act‑Know subset but remain much weaker in case reasoning, tool‑based workflows, and jurisdiction‑sensitive practice tasks.  
- The results reveal a clear capability boundary that limits realistic professional workflows requiring auditable, context‑grounded decisions.

## Context
This work addresses the gap in evaluating LLMs across integrated actuarial tasks, moving beyond isolated benchmarks to holistic assessment. It provides a reproducible framework for measuring professional competence within a complex domain.

## Implications
Practitioners can use INS‑ActBench to benchmark model performance and identify weak areas. The industry may adopt these metrics to guide development of reliable actuarial AI tools for real‑world applications.
