## Summary
The paper investigates whether artificial intelligence systems can truly be creative by applying Margaret Boden’s framework of novelty, surprise, and value across combinatorial, exploratory, and transformational processes. It concludes that while AI excels at combinatorial creativity, it lacks the capacity for genuine exploratory or transformational creativity due to missing mechanisms for serendipity and a subject position to welcome chance events.

## Key Takeaways
- AI systems are structurally incapable of creative novelty beyond combinatorial recombination because they cannot generate surprise rooted in unexpected connections.  
- The absence of serendipity or accidental insight limits exploratory creativity, as these processes rely on human‑like unpredictability.  
- Transformational creativity is fundamentally absent from current AI, which cannot redefine existing domains through radical new perspectives.

## Context
This work matters because it challenges the prevailing optimism that AI can replace human creators and highlights a gap between algorithmic performance metrics and authentic creative experience. It situates AI within broader debates about machine agency and the philosophical limits of computational thought.

## Implications
For researchers, the paper underscores the need to design collaborative tools that augment rather than supplant human creativity. For industry, it suggests focusing on hybrid workflows where AI handles combinatorial tasks while humans provide exploratory insight and transformational vision.
