## Summary
This paper reviews the fragmented progress toward World Action Models (WAMs) and proposes a co‑evolution roadmap for embodied physical intelligence, emphasizing an “embodied brain” that translates multimodal context into state‑transition or capability requests. It identifies three coupled gaps—model roles and representations, objectives and standardization, system composition—and argues that WAMs can serve as predictive prototypes while a physical harness provides tools, verification, and trace logging.

## Key Takeaways
- The abstract highlights that current action models use incompatible action spaces and prediction targets, creating fragmented progress.  
- It stresses the need for shared contracts to align heterogeneous models, data, tasks, and embodiments into a modular stack.  
- The paper outlines closed‑loop post‑training as a method to convert verified interactions into reusable experience.

## Context
Artificial general intelligence demands agents that can reason and act in physical environments, yet existing approaches remain siloed due to mismatched conventions and limited interfaces. This work bridges those silos by advocating a unified framework for embodied cognition.

## Implications
For researchers, the roadmap offers a clear path toward more adaptable and self‑improving agents. For industry, it suggests that building shared standards could accelerate deployment of physical AI systems across diverse applications.
