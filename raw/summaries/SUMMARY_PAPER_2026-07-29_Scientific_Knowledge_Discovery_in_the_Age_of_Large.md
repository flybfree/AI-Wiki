## Summary
The paper surveys 34 peer‑reviewed studies that apply generative LLMs to literature retrieval and eligibility screening, identified via a Boolean search of the OpenAIRE Graph. It characterizes the models used, access methods, prompting techniques, ground‑truth sources, and evaluation metrics. Findings show diverse approaches but limited systematic comparison.

## Key Takeaways
- Generative LLMs enable flexible literature retrieval beyond static queries, reducing manual effort.
- Model access varies from open‑source to proprietary APIs, influencing reproducibility.
- Evaluation relies on precision/recall against manually curated ground truth, highlighting need for standardized metrics.

## Context
The surge of scholarly output strains traditional search tools, prompting research into AI‑driven alternatives. This survey highlights how LLMs can automate content discovery and eligibility checks, aligning with broader efforts to integrate AI in academic workflows.

## Implications
For researchers, the study suggests adopting LLM pipelines for efficient literature screening. For industry, it underscores potential cost savings but also ethical concerns about model bias and data privacy.
