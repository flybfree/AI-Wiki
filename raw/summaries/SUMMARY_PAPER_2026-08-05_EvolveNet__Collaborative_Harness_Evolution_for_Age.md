## Summary
The paper introduces EvolveNet, a method for evolving harnesses collaboratively without updating model weights. It shows that by letting each agent evolve its own version of the harness and then composing adaptations into a shared one, performance improves across multiple tasks. The approach avoids centralizing experience streams and enables parallel evolutionary searches.

## Key Takeaways
- EvolveNet shifts experience extraction to the data layer, allowing agents to evolve their own harnesses locally.
- It aggregates only the resulting program adaptations rather than raw workloads, which cannot be directly averaged or centralized.
- Independent adaptations from different agents are composed into a new shared harness, yielding larger gains especially in heterogeneous workloads.

## Context
Current AI research focuses on optimizing model weights through centralized training loops. This limits scalability and ignores the diversity of real‑world usage patterns generated by users and environments. EvolveNet addresses this gap by decentralizing evolution to harnesses that can be adapted per deployment while still contributing to a global improvement.

## Implications
The method enables continuous self‑improvement for autonomous agents without retraining large language models, reducing compute costs and latency. Practitioners can adopt it to maintain robust agent behavior across varied tasks and environments, fostering resilient AI systems in industry and research.
