## Summary
The paper introduces STeMP, a standardized protocol for reporting and guiding spatio‑temporal machine‑learning models in environmental research. It aims to improve model transparency, quality assessment, and practical applicability by providing clear guidance on critical decisions and parameters throughout the modelling workflow.

## Key Takeaways
- The protocol is structured into three sections—Overview, Model, and Prediction—to capture metadata, predictors, evaluation methods, software tools, and other workflow elements.  
- An R‑package with a web application automatically fills the protocol from modelling objects and flags common pitfalls, supporting both manual entry and semi‑automated generation.  
- The GitHub repository encourages community contributions and feedback, fostering iterative improvement of the standard.

## Context
Transparency in AI‑driven environmental mapping is crucial yet hindered by a lack of unified reporting standards. Without such protocols, model performance estimates are opaque, limiting trust among researchers and practitioners who rely on these models for decision‑making.

## Implications
STeMP enables reproducible research, facilitates cross‑study comparisons, and strengthens confidence in AI outputs used for policy and operational purposes. By standardizing how spatio‑temporal models are documented and evaluated, the protocol advances scientific rigor and promotes broader adoption of machine‑learning tools in environmental science.
