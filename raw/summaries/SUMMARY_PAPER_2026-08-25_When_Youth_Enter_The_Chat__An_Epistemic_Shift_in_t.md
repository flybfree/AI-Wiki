## Summary
The paper argues that current LLM‑based measures of student talk are insufficient because they ignore youth perspectives and misalign with students’ lived experiences. In a case study of multilingual 8th‑grade math conversations, participants contested both the LLM classifications and the coding schemes used to measure their talk, revealing epistemic exclusion.

## Key Takeaways
- Students’ interpretations of their math talk differ from LLM outputs, indicating measurement misalignment.
- Adult expert annotations and F1 scores do not capture youth agency or linguistic nuance.
- Centering youth as epistemic authorities through ethnographic methods reveals hidden biases in automated measures.

## Context
The rapid adoption of large language models for educational assessment raises questions about whose knowledge counts. This paper contributes to AI ethics by advocating co‑design with marginalized learners, challenging the assumption that algorithmic validation is neutral.

## Implications
Educators and developers must involve students in designing evaluation tools to ensure fairness. Ignoring youth epistemic authority risks reinforcing inequities in digital learning analytics.
