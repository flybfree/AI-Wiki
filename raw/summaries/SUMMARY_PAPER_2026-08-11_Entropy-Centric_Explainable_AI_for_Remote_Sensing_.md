## Summary
This paper introduces an entropy‑centric explainable AI framework for remote sensing image segmentation that enhances model transparency by focusing on feature uncertainty. The method outperforms existing XAI approaches, demonstrating higher relevance in highlighted regions and improved trustworthiness for critical applications.

## Key Takeaways
- Entropy is used to quantify the randomness of pixel predictions, providing a clear measure of confidence loss during segmentation.  
- A novel evaluation metric evaluates how well each explained region aligns with true semantic boundaries, improving assessment accuracy.  
- The entropy‑centric approach achieves superior performance compared with recent XAI adaptations for semantic tasks.

## Context
Explainable AI seeks to make deep neural network decisions interpretable, especially in high‑stakes domains like remote sensing where model opacity can hinder adoption. While many XAI techniques excel at classification, segmentation remains challenging due to spatial complexity and the need for region‑level insights. This work addresses that gap by integrating entropy as a central explanatory signal.

## Implications
Practitioners can leverage this framework to build more trustworthy remote sensing models without sacrificing accuracy. The entropy‑based XAI method offers a scalable solution for regulatory compliance, data sharing, and collaborative research in environmental monitoring.
