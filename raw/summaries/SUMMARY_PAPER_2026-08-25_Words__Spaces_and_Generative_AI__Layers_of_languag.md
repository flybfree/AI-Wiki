## Summary
The paper argues that language functions as a design material in architecture, especially when generative AI is used, linking linguistic theories to contemporary practice. It identifies three intertwined forms of language—discourse, programming languages, annotations—and proposes a research agenda for integrating them with generative AI models.

## Key Takeaways
- Discourse in architectural texts includes professional terminology and shapes meaning through natural language.
- Programming languages serve as the computational substrate that drives generative AI outputs.
- Annotations act as data labels that guide model interpretation and design decisions, influencing how AI translates concepts into visual forms.

## Context
In an era where text-to-X models automate design, understanding linguistic layers is crucial for effective integration. This paper situates these layers within broader AI research on generative metaphors, emphasizing the need to study language affordances across natural and artificial languages.

## Implications
Architects can leverage discourse to articulate intent, use programming concepts to fine-tune AI tools, and apply annotations for precise control. Recognizing language affordances helps align AI outputs with conceptual goals, fostering more meaningful and effective design processes.
