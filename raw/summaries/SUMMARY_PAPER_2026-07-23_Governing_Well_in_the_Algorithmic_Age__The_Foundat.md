## Summary
The paper argues that digital statecraft is needed because the governance of data, algorithms and infrastructure lacks conceptual foundations. It introduces ten principles derived from technical coherence and legitimate authority, showing how their absence leads to structural failures. The authors suggest that governing well in the algorithmic age may require rethinking statehood itself.

## Key Takeaways
- Public interest must be prioritized as the first condition; without it governance collapses into self‑interest or opacity.
- Human‑machine complementarity is essential; systems should augment rather than replace human judgment to avoid loss of agency.
- Governability by design requires that algorithmic tools are built with transparency and adaptability, not just deployed as black boxes.

## Context
The rapid integration of AI and data platforms into public services creates governance gaps where traditional state mechanisms cannot respond. This paper situates those gaps within a broader discourse on digital transformation and institutional resilience in the age of algorithms.

## Implications
Policymakers must embed these principles into legal frameworks to ensure accountability and traceability. Practitioners should adopt hybrid institutions that balance technical coherence with civic participation, fostering more legitimate algorithmic governance.
