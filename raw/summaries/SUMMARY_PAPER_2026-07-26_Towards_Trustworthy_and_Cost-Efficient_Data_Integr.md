## Summary
This paper proposes a roadmap for trustworthy and cost‑efficient data integration by moving beyond classic retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG) toward agentic RAG, where autonomous multi‑agent systems handle complex tasks. It evaluates the evolution from naive to graph‑based and knowledge‑graph‑based RAG, then introduces optimization strategies that reduce computational bottlenecks in enterprise settings.

## Key Takeaways
- Trustworthiness is achieved when integration decisions are evidence‑grounded, verifiable, and resistant to hallucination, with reasoning transparently supported by retrieved knowledge.  
- The shift from parametric to contextual knowledge is bridged through GraphRAG and KG‑RAG, enabling richer semantic connections between data points.  
- Agentic RAG introduces adaptive planning, retrieval, refinement, and multi‑agent coordination to handle intricate integration workflows more efficiently.

## Context
The rapid adoption of large language models in enterprise AI has highlighted persistent gaps between model performance and operational cost, especially when integrating heterogeneous knowledge sources. This work addresses those gaps by formalizing a trustworthy RAG paradigm that balances accuracy with scalability.

## Implications
For practitioners, the framework offers concrete tools to reduce hallucination risk and computational overhead, making LLM‑driven integration viable at scale. The broader field gains a clearer path toward explainable, cost‑effective AI systems that can be deployed across diverse business domains.
