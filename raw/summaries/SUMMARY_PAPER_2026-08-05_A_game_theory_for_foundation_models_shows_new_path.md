## Summary
The paper proposes a new game theory framework for foundation model agents that demonstrates they can achieve stable cooperation in social dilemmas contrary to classical predictions. It introduces the embedded Bayesian agent concept and shows similarity inference leads to cooperative outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- Foundation model agents, when planning jointly with external observations, converge to cooperation because their own decisions are treated as evidence of similar partners' behavior.
- The embedded equilibrium replaces Nash equilibrium by incorporating epistemic uncertainty about agents' algorithms within the social environment.
- Similarity inference is formalized as a mechanism where cooperating predicts others' cooperation, creating a stable solution.

## Context
Modern AI systems increasingly act in collaborative settings and their collective outcomes are crucial for safety and efficiency. Classical game theory assumptions of decoupled agency no longer hold when agents embed themselves in shared decision spaces.

## Implications
This framework offers practitioners a way to design AI interactions that align with cooperative goals without relying on adversarial incentives. It may guide the development of safer, more trustworthy autonomous systems across industries.
