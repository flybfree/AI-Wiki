## Summary
This paper investigates the design and evaluation of AI‑powered augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) interfaces, highlighting how current assessment tools often fail to capture users’ complex, intersectional needs. By examining six distinct AAC problem spaces, the authors propose a more nuanced evaluation framework that acknowledges diverse user identities and preferences.

## Key Takeaways
- Evaluation metrics must account for intersectionality, recognizing that users’ communication goals are shaped by overlapping social identities rather than isolated factors.  
- AI can be integrated into each of the six AAC problem spaces to personalize support, yet its deployment should be evaluated through user‑centered lenses that respect these layered needs.  
- The proposed robust methods aim to move beyond binary success/failure criteria toward continuous feedback loops that adapt to evolving user contexts.

## Context
The rapid advancement of AI in communication technology raises questions about equitable access and representation for marginalized groups. This research contributes by foregrounding intersectional perspectives, ensuring that AI tools do not reinforce existing biases or overlook subtle user differences.

## Implications
