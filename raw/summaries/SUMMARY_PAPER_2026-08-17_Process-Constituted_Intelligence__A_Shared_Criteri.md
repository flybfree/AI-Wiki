## Summary
The paper argues that intelligence is defined by process, not just output, and critiques current generative AI for lacking the underlying cognitive processes. It proposes a framework of strong equivalence across seven process features to compare human and machine cognition, aiming to make this comparison testable.

## Key Takeaways
- Intelligence is constituted by iterative activity through which output emerges, emphasizing that GenAI reproduces traces rather than actual reasoning.
- Strong equivalence requires matching seven process features between humans and machines, not just superficial similarity of outputs.
- Design principles are needed for GenAI to instantiate more process while preserving human judgment and creativity.

## Context
This work situates AI research within cognitive science debates about weak versus strong equivalence, highlighting a risk that outsourcing generative processes may erode essential capacities. It contributes to the broader discourse on aligning machine capabilities with human mental processes.

## Implications
Practitioners must adopt process‑focused audits and design guidelines to ensure AI tools enhance rather than replace human cognitive development. The framework offers a measurable standard for evaluating whether AI augments or diminishes human intelligence, guiding ethical deployment.
