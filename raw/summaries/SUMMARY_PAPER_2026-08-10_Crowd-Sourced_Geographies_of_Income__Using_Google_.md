## Summary
The paper investigates using crowd‑sourced Google Maps points of interest as a low‑cost proxy to estimate household income at the sub‑municipal level in Sao Paulo. The authors train regression models on POI counts and achieve an R² of 0.65, showing that such data can complement traditional censuses.

## Key Takeaways
- The best model combines non‑negative matrix factorization with gradient boosting to explain income variation through POI categories.
- PCA and NMF decompositions reveal that commercial POIs dominate the signal, indicating specific economic activities drive household wealth.
- Spatial validation avoids leakage, ensuring the R² estimate is reliable for policy planning.

## Context
This work bridges geospatial data science with socioeconomic research, showing how high‑frequency digital footprints can fill gaps in official statistics.
