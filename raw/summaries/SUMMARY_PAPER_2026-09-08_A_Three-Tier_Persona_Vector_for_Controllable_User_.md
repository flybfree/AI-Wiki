## Summary  
The paper introduces a three‑tier persona vector to generate diverse user inputs for evaluating LLM agents, comprising six categorical demographics, twelve continuous behavioral traits sampled with Gaussian noise around curated profile base vectors, and five continuous emotional states that shift in response to scenario context. It also adds a four‑level query‑complexity overlay controlling utterance phrasing from direct to deliberately vague. The model is evaluated inside a synthetic data pipeline across 64,698 multi‑turn conversations spanning eight named profiles and three production corpora.

## Key Takeaways  
- A 15.8 percentage‑point spread in agent goal‑achievement across personas confirms that trait vectors produce measurably different user behavior.  
- The same persona behaves differently across scenarios due to scenario‑reactive emotional state shifts, validating the design’s ability to model context‑dependent emotions.  
- Domain‑specific projects show a 15–20 percentage‑point gap between tier‑aware and pressure‑test personas, demonstrating faithful reproduction of real‑world difficulty distributions.

## Context  
This work addresses the limitation of flat role descriptions in LLM evaluation by providing an orthogonal, multi‑dimensional persona framework that captures demographic, behavioral, emotional, and linguistic complexity. It enables more realistic synthetic data generation for agentic testing, moving beyond near‑identical conversations generated by simple role prompts.

## Implications  
Practitioners can use this model to calibrate agents against diverse user profiles, reducing bias in performance metrics. The auditability of trait correlations simplifies implementation without requiring complex covariance matrices, making the approach scalable across industries and research settings.
