## Summary
The paper proposes Clustered Edge Intelligence (CEI) as an intelligence‑centric framework that treats derived intelligence as a first‑class entity. It introduces a three‑layer architecture and discusses technologies for inventorying, representing, communicating, discovering, observing, automating lifecycle, clustering, marketplaces, interoperability, and standardization.

## Key Takeaways
- CEI defines intelligence as an independently manageable entity that can be described, discovered, observed, shared, reused, and clustered across heterogeneous edge devices.  
- The three‑layer architecture separates intelligence inventories, semantic knowledge representation, and communication mechanisms to enable these operations.  
- Research dimensions include inventory management, discoverability, lifecycle automation, clustering mechanisms, marketplaces, interoperability, and standardization.

## Context
Current AI research often treats edge computing as a resource‑management problem or focuses on deploying lightweight models, overlooking the need for a unified view of intelligence generated at the network edge. This gap limits scalable reuse of insights across distributed applications.

## Implications
CEI could enable organizations to harvest, share, and repurpose edge intelligence without re‑training models, reducing latency and compute costs. Practitioners should explore inventory tools and clustering strategies to turn raw edge data into reusable knowledge assets.
