## Summary
The paper evaluates a GPT‑5.5 model for grading handwritten physics solutions from a large-scale Olympiad and university assessment, comparing AI scores to official human marks. It reports high total‑score correlations (0.91–0.97) and that the AI correctly identified the same five‑student team selected by humans in the final Olympiad selection.

## Key Takeaways
- The AI achieved strong agreement with official grades across three assessments, indicating reliable overall scoring.
- A second grading round using revised page‑by‑page instructions reduced disagreement rates, especially on questions where initial scores diverged significantly.
- Partial‑credit assessment remains challenging for experimental work, limiting the model’s precision in nuanced grading.

## Context
This study addresses a critical gap in multimodal AI: the need for human‑level accuracy when evaluating creative or partial‑credit solutions. By applying large language models to handwritten physics assessments, researchers demonstrate that such systems can serve as supplementary readers without compromising fairness if guided by detailed rubrics.

## Implications
For educators and exam administrators, this research suggests that AI grading can be integrated into selection processes as an audit tool, enhancing efficiency while maintaining human oversight. The findings also highlight the importance of refining instruction sets for AI to handle complex grading tasks accurately.
