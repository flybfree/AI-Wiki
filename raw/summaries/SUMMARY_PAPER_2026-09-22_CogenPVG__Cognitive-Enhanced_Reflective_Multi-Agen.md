## Summary
The paper introduces CogenPVG, a novel multi-agent framework designed to automate the creation of highly persuasive videos by mimicking human production workflows. By integrating psychological theories like the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM) into a four-stage pipeline—argument reasoning, storyboard planning, asset creation, and post-editing—the system achieves superior persuasion performance compared to existing methods.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework decomposes the complex task of persuasive video generation into four distinct sequential stages: argument reasoning, storyboard planning, asset creation, and post-editing. This modular approach mimics how human producers structure content from initial logic to final production, allowing for more granular control over the output quality.
- CogenPVG employs a "generator and critic" agent pair at every stage, utilizing a reflective refinement mechanism. This ensures that each step of the video production process—from the underlying logic to the visual assets—is iteratively improved and critiqued to maximize persuasive impact.
- The framework is grounded in established psychological theories, specifically the Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM). It uses critical thinking theory to guide the "central route" (logical reasoning) and focuses on heuristic-based multimodal assets for the "peripheral route," providing a more psychologically sound approach to persuasion than previous methods.
- CogenPVG is distinguished by its ability to handle general persuasive topics rather than being confined to specific commercial use cases, demonstrating that the framework can be applied across a wide variety of human communication goals and contexts.
