## Summary
The paper explores a post‑AGI economy where corporations own AI and robot agents that both produce and consume resources, showing that demand can be satisfied without humans. It finds three outcomes: a closed inter‑corporate growth model, removal of demographic bottlenecks, and complete decoupling of human welfare from GDP. The analysis concludes that ownership policy determines the future.

## Key Takeaways
- A closed inter‑corporate economy with zero human consumption is not degenerate; it behaves like von Neumann’s expanding economy where growth is positive and maximal because all output is reinvested.
- Once agents are manufactured, growth constraints shift from limited human reproduction to fabrication throughput and energy capture, allowing up to two orders of magnitude higher growth rates.
- Human welfare decouples completely from GDP, leaving only the human ownership share εt as a relevant variable; any positive consumption causes it to decay exponentially at the interest‑growth rate.

## Context
This work addresses the longstanding concern that automation will leave no one to buy goods, reframing the debate in terms of corporate control over autonomous agents. It contributes to AI economics by modeling a self‑sustaining machine economy and its implications for policy design.

## Implications
For policymakers, the paper suggests focusing on ownership structures rather than employment programs. For industry leaders, it highlights the need to manage AI agent networks to avoid collapse of human share in wealth.
