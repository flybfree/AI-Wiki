## Summary
This paper introduces SynthEx, an agentic framework that uses large language models to design synthetic routes for highly complex natural products beyond the scope of conventional retrosynthetic tools. The system generates competing strategies, integrates routine and key steps into a coherent plan, and iteratively critiques its own designs. In blinded expert evaluations, the proposed routes were judged comparable to published human syntheses, marking the first algorithmic route prediction that elicits genuine engagement from chemists.

## Key Takeaways
- SynthEx proposes competing strategies and assembles a sequence of routine and key steps into a cohesive route, demonstrating convergence superior to existing catalogue‑based tools.  
- The chemistry favored by SynthEx spans reaction space unavailable to traditional algorithms, highlighting its ability to handle densely functionalized polycyclic architectures.  
- Expert chemists judged the key steps comparable to those in published human syntheses and treated them as genuine synthesis plans, indicating that algorithmic route prediction can elicit authentic engagement.

## Context
The rapid development of large language models has enabled AI systems to generate text with unprecedented fluency, yet their application to chemical synthesis planning remains limited. This work bridges the gap by applying such models to a domain where synthetic creativity is essential, showing that AI can produce plans that rival expert intuition on challenging targets.

## Implications
For the field, SynthEx suggests that AI‑driven retrosynthesis could become a collaborative partner for chemists, accelerating discovery of novel routes and reducing reliance on exhaustive literature searches. In industry, the open database SynthAtlas offers a shared resource for complex molecules lacking documented syntheses, potentially lowering development costs and time to market.
