## Summary
This paper presents a practitioner‑driven study of Large Language Model (LLM) integration in Security Operations Centers, revealing that while LLMs are seen as valuable for automating routine tasks, they are not yet ready to handle high‑impact activities such as incident analysis. The authors identified 15 use cases across six functional categories and found that SOC readiness, technical depth, context awareness, and organizational knowledge are the main barriers rather than model limitations.

## Key Takeaways
- Practitioners rate automated report generation as feasible but consider high‑impact tasks like incident analysis still infeasible due to insufficient technical depth in LLMs.  
- The primary obstacles identified are not the models themselves but the readiness of SOCs and human factors that could lead to over‑reliance on LLM outputs.  
- Despite concerns, analysts express strong willingness to adopt LLMs because competitive pressure leaves few alternative solutions.

## Context
The rapid advancement of Large Language Models has sparked interest in applying them to cybersecurity, yet most research focuses on theoretical or isolated use cases. This study bridges that gap by grounding findings in real‑world SOC workflows through semi‑structured interviews and interactive scenarios, offering empirical insight into how practitioners perceive LLM suitability.

## Implications
For security professionals, the paper underscores the need for human‑centered design that ensures LLMs augment rather than replace analyst judgment. Organizations must invest in training, governance, and integration processes to build SOC readiness before deploying LLMs at scale.
