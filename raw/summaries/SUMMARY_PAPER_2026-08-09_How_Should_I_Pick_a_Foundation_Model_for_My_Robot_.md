## Summary
The paper tackles the challenge of choosing a foundation model for social robots by highlighting that public leaderboards lack relevance to real‑time embodied interaction. It introduces a five‑dimensional evaluation framework and a three‑tiered funnel to make selection more efficient and community‑oriented.

## Key Takeaways
- The five dimensions—conversational competence, user safety, embodied character, target scene effectiveness, and audience appropriateness—provide a structured way to assess models beyond generic benchmarks.  
- A three‑tiered evaluation funnel reduces cost by using general metrics first, then simulated interactions, and finally expensive robot‑specific tests.  
- Mapping these dimensions across all tiers creates a clear roadmap for researchers and developers seeking reliable model choices.

## Context
Foundation models dominate many AI applications but their performance in embodied social contexts remains underexplored. This work bridges the gap by proposing an evaluation paradigm that aligns with real‑world robot deployment constraints, encouraging reproducibility and shared standards across the community.

## Implications
Practitioners can rely on a transparent framework to select models that meet safety and usability goals without exhaustive testing. The collaborative approach fosters open research, accelerating progress in socially intelligent robots for industry and academia alike.
