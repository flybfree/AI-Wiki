## Summary
The paper investigates whether integrating multimodal listening behaviors such as verbal backchanneling and head nodding into an AI clone enhances its perceived authenticity. In a within-subjects study with 35 participants, adding these behaviors significantly improved the avatar’s attentiveness, sense of talking with the real person, and co‑presence.

## Key Takeaways
- Adding real-time predicted verbal backchannels and head nods to an AI clone improves perceived attentiveness because listeners interpret them as genuine engagement.  
- The study shows that the sense of talking with the real person increases when the avatar’s listening behaviors are synchronized with its speech output.  
- Co‑presence, the feeling of being together in a shared space, rises noticeably when the AI clone exhibits interactive listening.

## Context
Current AI clones excel at reproducing voice and text but often lack embodied social cues that convey presence. This research highlights a gap between technical fidelity and human perception of authenticity, underscoring the need for multimodal integration to meet user expectations.

## Implications
For developers, extending AI clone fidelity beyond vocal mimicry requires designing responsive listening modules. Practitioners should prioritize these behaviors in prototype testing to gauge impact on user trust and engagement. The findings suggest a shift toward holistic interaction design that includes both speech and non‑verbal cues.
