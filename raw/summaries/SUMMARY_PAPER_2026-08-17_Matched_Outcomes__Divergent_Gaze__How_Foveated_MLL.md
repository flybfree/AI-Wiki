## Summary
The paper investigates how multimodal large language models perform visual search tasks under foveated input, comparing their behavior to human eye movements on a benchmark dataset. It finds that models excel at detecting targets and reaching them quickly but do not replicate the temporal structure of human gaze scanning.

## Key Takeaways
- Models match or exceed humans in deciding whether a target is present and often locate it on the first saccade, indicating superior decision-making under matched retinal input.
- The overall scanpath generated by models is low‑entropy and self‑consistent, but it lacks the temporal dynamics that characterize human sequential scanning.
- No degradation regime observed when the model’s output does not recover human‑like search behavior, suggesting a fundamental architectural difference rather than limited visual acuity.

## Context
This work highlights a gap between static alignment scores and dynamic process metrics in AI vision research. While current MLLMs can answer spatial questions accurately, their inability to mimic the temporal flow of human eye movements limits their suitability for tasks requiring precise timing or sequential reasoning.

## Implications
For researchers, this signals that evaluating only outcome‑based metrics is insufficient when studying human‑like visual search. Practitioners should incorporate process‑level assessments into model validation pipelines to better align AI behavior with biological constraints.
