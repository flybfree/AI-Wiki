## Summary
This paper maps the AI governance provisions of twenty middle‑power jurisdictions, showing how they address systemic risk, evaluation, prohibitions with monitoring, and incident reporting for general‑purpose AI models. It finds that while many states adopt a similar set of rules, only a minority make them legally binding or explicitly define GPAI.

## Key Takeaways
- Sixteen jurisdictions engage at least three of the four governance areas, yet only about one in five provisions are codified into law.  
- Three‑quarters of binding instruments lack any definition of GPAI, leaving the model layer unaddressed.  
- Institutional capacity is inherited from older regimes, focusing on application rather than model creation.

## Context
Middle‑power economies lack frontier AI developers but face global risks from models built elsewhere, prompting them to draft their own rules. This study provides a granular view of how these emerging regulations fit into the broader AI accountability framework.

## Implications
Policymakers can use this dataset to identify alignment opportunities and where new coordination mechanisms are needed. Practitioners should focus on strengthening evaluation duties and defining GPAI to close gaps in current regimes.
