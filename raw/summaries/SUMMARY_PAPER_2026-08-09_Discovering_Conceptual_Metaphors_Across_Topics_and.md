## Summary
This paper introduces an unsupervised method for extracting linguistic metaphors from large corpora and clustering them into conceptual metaphor groups using structured algorithms. The approach reveals that left‑leaning podcasts often frame media stories as weapons, whereas right‑leaning sources treat the economy as a system undergoing vertical changes. These findings illustrate how metaphorical framing shapes audience perception.

## Key Takeaways
- The study shows that left‑leaning podcasts conceptualize media narratives as weapons, suggesting a focus on conflict and resistance.
- Right‑leaning podcasts describe economic conditions in terms of vertical changes, implying stability and hierarchy over time.
- The unsupervised clustering method uncovers these metaphorical patterns without manual annotation.

## Context
In natural language processing and AI research, identifying implicit conceptual frameworks is crucial for understanding bias and framing in media. This work contributes to the broader effort of using computational linguistics to decode ideological cues that influence public discourse.

## Implications
For content creators and analysts, recognizing these metaphors can inform more balanced editorial strategies and reduce polarization. Practitioners can leverage such insights to design communication that aligns with desired rhetorical goals while minimizing misinterpretation.
