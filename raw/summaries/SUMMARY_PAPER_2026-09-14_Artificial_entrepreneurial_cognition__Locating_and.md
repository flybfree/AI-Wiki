## Summary
This paper introduces "artificial entrepreneurial cognition" to investigate how entrepreneurship-relevant mental constructs are organized within large language models. By applying representation engineering, the authors successfully isolate and causally steer an internal neural direction representing opportunity recognition in Llama 3.1 8B-Instruct. Directly manipulating this latent feature functions as an "opportunity recognition dial," systematically shifting the model's entrepreneurial judgments and establishing a concrete representational form for distinguishing opportunity recognition from evaluation.

## Key Takeaways
- The authors constructed 636 matched scenario pairs to recover a specific neural direction corresponding to opportunity recognition, moving beyond traditional output-based inference to directly engineer internal model representations.
- Causal steering of this latent direction operates as an "opportunity recognition dial," where shifting it up or down directly alters the model's entrepreneurial judgments, providing empirical grounding for the theoretically contested distinction between opportunity recognition and evaluation.
- The identified representation remains recoverable, consequential, and geometrically distinct from opportunity evaluation and exploitation across four additional LLMs of varying scales and families, though steering it does influence neighboring entrepreneurial stages.

## Context
As large language models increasingly participate in complex cognitive workflows, understanding their internal architectures becomes critical for both AI safety and interdisciplinary research. This work bridges mechanistic interpretability with entrepreneurship theory by treating latent model representations as legitimate scientific objects rather than opaque black boxes. The findings contribute to the emerging field of representation engineering by demonstrating how domain-specific constructs can be precisely located, validated, and manipulated within modern neural networks.

## Implications
For AI developers, this methodology provides a replicable framework for auditing and steering internal model behaviors related to nuanced cognitive tasks beyond simple factual recall. Entrepreneurship researchers gain a novel computational tool to empirically test theoretical distinctions about opportunity recognition versus evaluation using artificial systems. Ultimately, the research demonstrates how domain theory can guide the causal manipulation of AI internals, paving the way for more transparent, interpretable, and controllable enterprise-focused AI applications.
