## Summary
This paper investigates whether users' perceived agency of a virtual human (VH) in virtual reality updates when control transitions between a human operator and a computer program during an ongoing interaction. Through a within-subject VR study with 24 participants across four control-transition conditions, the authors found that perceived agency remained anchored to the controller disclosed at the start of the interaction, showing no evidence of updating after transitions occurred. In contrast, social evaluations such as likeability and co-presence were more responsive to the actual controller's behavior over the course of the interaction.

## Key Takeaways
- Perceived agency is strongly anchored to first impressions: participants who were initially told a VH was computer-controlled continued to perceive it as computer-controlled even after a human operator took over control mid-interaction, and vice versa. This suggests that users do not dynamically re-evaluate agency attribution when the underlying controller changes, indicating a persistent cognitive anchoring effect in human-agent interaction.
- Social evaluations diverge from agency judgments: while perceived agency remained fixed, participants rated likeability and co-presence more highly in conditions where human control occurred later and covered a greater proportion of the interaction. This dissociation implies that users can respond to behavioral cues over time for social judgments even when their agency attribution remains locked to initial information.
- The experimental design used a structured interview task where participants acted as interviewers asking fixed questions to VHs, with control transitions occurring without participants' awareness. This methodology reveals that users are largely unable to detect or update their understanding of who is controlling a virtual agent during an ongoing interaction, raising concerns about transparency in hybrid human-AI systems.

## Context
As virtual humans increasingly serve as intermediaries in customer service, healthcare, education, and social platforms, hybrid systems that blend human and AI control are becoming practical realities. This paper addresses a critical gap in understanding how users cognitively process agency attribution when the underlying controller shifts mid-interaction, a scenario that existing research on static AI or static human agents has not adequately covered. The findings contribute to the broader literature on human-computer interaction, theory of mind in virtual environments, and the psychology of trust in AI-mediated communication.

## Implications
For practitioners designing hybrid virtual-human systems, these results suggest that transparency about control transitions must be established at the outset rather than assumed to be inferable during interaction, since users will not spontaneously update their agency perceptions. Industry applications such as customer-service chatbots that escalate to human agents, telehealth avatars, or social VR companions should carefully consider how initial framing shapes long-term user trust and perceived authenticity. The dissociation between agency perception and social evaluation also implies that behavioral quality can improve user experience even when agency attribution remains misaligned, offering design levers for improving engagement in AI-controlled systems.
