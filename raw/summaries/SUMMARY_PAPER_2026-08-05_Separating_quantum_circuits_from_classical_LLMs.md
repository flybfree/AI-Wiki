## Summary
The paper proves that low-depth quantum circuits (QNC^0) and shallow classical diffusion language models cannot be equivalent. It shows a distribution that QNC^0 can sample but DLMs cannot approximate within constant distance, even with limited chain-of-thought features.

## Key Takeaways
- The distributional separation: there exists a probability distribution generated by constant-depth quantum circuits of bounded fan-in gates that no constant-round diffusion language model can reproduce within constant approximation error, despite allowing sublinear chain-of-thought and output-token revision.
- The functional separation: the function computed by O(log log n)-depth QNC^0 circuits followed by an AND gate cannot be evaluated efficiently on a constant-depth transformer; any such decoder would require exponential width scaling, i.e., n^{Ω(1)}.
- Together these results establish unconditional quantum advantage over classical LLMs under realistic resource constraints.

## Context
Modern language models rely on diffusion processes and deep transformers that are believed to approximate any function efficiently. Quantum circuits of logarithmic depth offer alternative computation models with potential speedups, yet their relationship to classical model architectures remains unclear.

## Implications
These findings suggest that quantum advantage may not be limited to large-scale quantum hardware but could emerge in algorithmic complexity differences between quantum and classical deep models. Practitioners should consider both distributional and functional capabilities when evaluating quantum versus classical AI performance.
