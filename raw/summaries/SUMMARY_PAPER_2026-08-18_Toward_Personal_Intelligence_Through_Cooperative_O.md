## Summary
The paper proposes a cooperative observation framework for personal AI systems that balances usefulness, trust, and future access. A preliminary single‑subject study over six months shows the loop can sustain user engagement when behavior is useful and transparent.

## Key Takeaways
- The system builds a partial model of the user’s changing life through observation, but its quality depends on how much information it selects and compresses for each task.
- Useful behavior encourages users to maintain or expand the observation channel, while failures prompt correction, narrowing, revoking, or abandoning it.
- Observation is governed by user consent and control, forming a feedback loop that ties usefulness, trust, and future access together.

## Context
Personal AI must model users’ goals, constraints, and commitments to act effectively, yet the quality of this model is limited by what the system can observe. Broader observation alone does not guarantee better assistance because the system must still compress information for specific tasks.

## Implications
Designing personal AI that adapts to user feedback can improve trust and long‑term utility. Practitioners should prioritize inspectable, cooperative observation channels to sustain engagement and reduce abandonment.
