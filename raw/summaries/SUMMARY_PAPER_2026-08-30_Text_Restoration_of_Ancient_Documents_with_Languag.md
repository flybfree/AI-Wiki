## Summary
The paper investigates using language models to restore missing text from damaged ancient manuscripts caused by lacunae. It tests various model architectures and decoding strategies under different restoration scenarios.

## Key Takeaways
- The study shows that fully automated restoration is not feasible, requiring human assistance.  
- Performance depends heavily on which part of the document is missing and whether its length is known.  
- Results highlight a need for lacuna‑aware decoding to align with tokenization boundaries.

## Context
This work contributes to the growing interest in applying large language models to cultural heritage preservation. By quantifying model behavior across diverse manuscript conditions, it bridges AI research with paleographic practice.

## Implications
Paleographers can use these tools as decision‑support aids rather than replacements. The findings suggest that lacuna length awareness and context‑specific models are crucial for effective restoration workflows.
