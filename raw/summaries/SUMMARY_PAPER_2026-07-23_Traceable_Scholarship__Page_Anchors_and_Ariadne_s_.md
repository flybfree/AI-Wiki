## Summary
The paper introduces Traceable Scholarship as a normative condition for AI‑assisted humanistic research, arguing that generative fluency alone is insufficient and that traceability to source material must be maintained. It proposes page anchors and dual page numbers as mechanisms linking generated text to original editions, enabling retrieval correction and evidence grading.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper defines page anchors as dual page numbers that tether AI‑generated explanations to specific printed or digital pages, preventing the illusion of self‑contained knowledge.
- It introduces a citation‑first generation protocol where the model must first locate source material before producing text, enforced by a NO_EVIDENCE flag that blocks output without traceable evidence.
- A four‑level compliance framework and a Scope Contract are introduced to ensure human verification, making traceability a condition rather than a software feature.

## Context
Generative AI has transformed scholarly production, but the lack of provenance risks misinformation. This work situates traceability within three knowledge revolutions—print, digital, and generative AI—to address systemic gaps in evidence tracking across eras.

## Implications
For researchers, Traceable Scholarship ensures that AI‑augmented humanistic inquiry remains public and refutable. For publishers and platforms, the framework supports robust citation systems that can be integrated into AI agents, fostering trustworthy knowledge exchange.
