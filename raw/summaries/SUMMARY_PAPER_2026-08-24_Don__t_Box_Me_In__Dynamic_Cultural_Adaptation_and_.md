## Summary
The paper introduces DyCAC, a training‑free framework that models social interaction in multicultural settings by treating cultural preferences as dynamic mixtures rather than static identities. It achieves fluid alignment through continuous cognitive tracking and dialogue signals. Experiments show the model outperforms baselines on interactive benchmarks, highlighting its practical advantage for real‑time adaptation.

## Key Takeaways
- DyCAC treats culture as a time‑varying mixture of population profiles instead of a fixed demographic label.
- The framework uses dialogue‑style signals to calibrate these cultural references during ongoing conversation.
- A Theory‑of‑Mind memory module tracks interlocutor cognitive states, enabling turn‑level behavioral adaptation.

## Context
Social understanding in AI is moving beyond static demographic cues toward models that capture evolving preferences. This work aligns with the trend of integrating real‑time interaction data into language systems to improve relevance across diverse user groups. ## Implications
Practitioners can deploy DyCAC without retraining, offering a lightweight solution for adaptive conversational agents in multicultural environments.
