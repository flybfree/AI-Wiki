## Summary
This paper investigates how large language models reproduce standard language ideologies that privilege Inner Circle norms while marginalising Global South Englishes. It demonstrates that AI systems, their training data, design choices and public discourse all reflect these biases. The analysis uses the controversy over the word “delve” to illustrate policing of non‑dominant English.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper shows AI systems reproduce dominant language ideologies across multiple stages such as training data, design protocols, evaluation benchmarks, user feedback and public commentary.
- It uses the controversy over the word delve to illustrate how speakers of Global North English police norms of Global South users.
- The analysis identifies a standardisation paradox where AI may homogenize English while also exposing non‑dominant Englishes through diverse corpora, highlighting ongoing debates about legitimacy and ownership.

## Context
This research bridges sociolinguistics and artificial intelligence by showing that algorithmic outputs are not neutral but encode power relations. It contributes to the growing body of work on how technology mediates language legitimacy in World Englishes.

## Implications
For practitioners, inclusive design is essential to prevent marginalisation of non‑dominant Englishes and to mitigate real‑world harms caused by treating some varieties as less legitimate. The findings urge a shift toward models that recognise linguistic plurality.
