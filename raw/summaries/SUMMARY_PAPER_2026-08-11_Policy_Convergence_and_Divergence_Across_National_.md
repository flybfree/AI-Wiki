## Summary
The paper compares national and regional AI strategies to reveal how their policy design elements align or differ over time. It finds strong horizontal convergence on economic competitiveness, research support, and ethical AI use while vertical alignment varies by region with the AU showing highest convergence. The analysis uses a coding of 74 national and 3 regional strategies across goals, approaches, and principles.

## Key Takeaways
- Horizontal convergence is evident as most countries prioritize economic competitiveness, increased funding for research, and ethical AI frameworks, indicating shared strategic priorities despite geographic differences.
- Vertical divergence persists in human rights goals, participatory governance methods, and human‑centric values, showing that regional strategies do not uniformly adopt the same moral or inclusive approaches.
- The AU demonstrates the strongest vertical convergence across its member states, while the EU aligns on regulatory and economic matters but diverges on human‑centric principles, reflecting mixed regional alignment.

## Context
AI policy development has become a global priority as nations race to embed artificial intelligence into their economies and societies. This study adds a systematic analysis of how strategies evolve by examining both national and subnational frameworks through a common set of design elements.

## Implications
Policymakers can use these findings to anticipate which design choices are likely to spread across borders, helping them align AI strategies with broader regional goals while addressing persistent ethical gaps. The evidence also suggests that focusing on economic and research support may yield faster convergence than moral or participatory measures.
