## Summary  
This paper introduces CAIA, an AI‑assisted support system for text‑based online counselling, and reports a field evaluation that involved 34 professional counsellors conducting authentic sessions with trained student counsellees. The study generated 36 therapy threads and 1,257 AI outputs, providing empirical insight into how the tool is used in real practice.

## Key Takeaways  
- Substantial adoption was observed across all participants, indicating the tool is usable within real therapeutic workflows.  
- Professional autonomy emerged as a decisive factor for sustained acceptance; counsellors felt empowered to decide when and how to use AI suggestions.  
- Information accuracy proved critical; any errors in generated content led to reduced trust and lower usage rates.

## Context  
Mental‑health services face growing demand yet limited resources, making asynchronous email counselling a low‑threshold option that often lacks personal touch. Integrating large language models into such contexts raises questions about how AI can augment rather than replace human interaction responsibly.

## Implications  
For practitioners, CAIA demonstrates that tools must respect the clinician’s role and provide reliable interpretations to maintain trust. For developers, prioritizing interpretive functionalities and data fidelity is essential for responsible deployment in therapeutic settings.
