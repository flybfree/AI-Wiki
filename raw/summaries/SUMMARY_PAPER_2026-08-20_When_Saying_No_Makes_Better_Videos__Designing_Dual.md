## Summary
This paper introduces a two‑layer refusal system for AI video creation that aims to keep content pedagogically sound while preserving aesthetic appeal. The first layer lets educators revise scripts using multimedia learning theory, and the second uses automated metrics to detect incoherence or mismatched narrative visuals. Together they raise the quality of instructional videos.

## Key Takeaways
- Educators can iteratively reshape AI‑generated scripts based on principles of multimedia learning, ensuring that explanations align with how learners construct knowledge.
- Automated metrics evaluate instructional coherence and narrative‑visual synchronization, flagging violations before final output is accepted.
- The combined system demonstrates independent improvements in the same instructional dimensions across both human and metric evaluations.

## Context
AI tools are increasingly used to generate educational videos, but they often prioritize visual polish over learning effectiveness. This work addresses a gap by proposing structured resistance mechanisms that balance creative freedom with pedagogical rigor.

## Implications
Educators can adopt these dual gatekeeping steps to produce more reliable AI‑assisted content without sacrificing engagement. The approach offers a practical framework for integrating generative AI into curricula while maintaining instructional integrity.
