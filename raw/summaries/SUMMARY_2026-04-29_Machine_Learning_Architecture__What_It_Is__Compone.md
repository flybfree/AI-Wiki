## Summary
Machine learning architecture describes the structure of a system that processes data, trains models, and delivers predictions. It covers stages from ingestion to deployment and includes components like storage, versioning, evaluation, monitoring, and training. The article explains how each part influences model performance and scalability.

## Key Takeaways
- Data ingestion quality is critical because poor or unprocessed data leads to inaccurate models and wasted GPU resources.
- Storage solutions must be scalable, secure, and performant to support large datasets and real‑time inference without bottlenecks.
- Continuous monitoring of model predictions helps detect drift early, preventing performance loss in production.

## Context
Modern AI projects rely on integrated pipelines that combine data collection, storage, training, and deployment. The need for robust architecture stems from the complexity of handling massive volumes of data and the requirement for reliable, auditable models. Understanding these components is essential for teams aiming to move from prototypes to scalable services.

## Implications
A well‑designed ML architecture reduces debugging time, lowers operational costs, and improves business outcomes such as revenue growth through accurate predictions. For example, healthcare providers can lower readmission rates by deploying a reliable model that continuously monitors its accuracy. Practitioners must treat the pipeline as a product to ensure long‑term sustainability
