## Summary
This paper introduces TEIDAN, a multilingual multimodal corpus of three‑party face‑to‑face conversations in Japanese and English. The dataset captures spontaneous discussions with individual pin microphones, an array microphone, and participant cameras, providing IPU‑based transcripts for both language portions.

## Key Takeaways
- TEIDAN provides a balanced collection of multilingual (Japanese and English) three‑party dialogues that can be used to compare cross‑linguistic interaction patterns.  
- The corpus includes multimodal recordings with synchronized audio, video, and IPU transcripts, enabling analysis of turn‑taking, addressee recognition, and grounding in human‑human or human‑agent settings.  
- Earlier Japanese subsets were limited to task‑oriented tasks; TEIDAN expands the scope to open‑ended, spontaneous topics, supporting broader research on multimodal dialogue.

## Context
The field of multi‑party dialogue modeling often relies on narrow, single‑language corpora that do not reflect real‑world language diversity. This work addresses that gap by offering a resource that bridges Japanese and English, facilitating cross‑lingual studies and improving model robustness across languages.

## Implications
Researchers can leverage TEIDAN to develop models that understand turn dynamics and addressee cues in multilingual settings, which is crucial for building inclusive human‑agent systems. Practitioners may use the dataset to test multimodal grounding techniques, leading to more natural and context‑aware conversational agents.
