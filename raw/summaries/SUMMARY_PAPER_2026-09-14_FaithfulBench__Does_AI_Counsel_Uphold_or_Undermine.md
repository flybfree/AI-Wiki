## Summary
This paper introduces FaithfulBench, a novel benchmark designed to evaluate how well AI assistants provide moral counsel that aligns with a user’s specific religious tradition. By testing five frontier models across three distinct prompting conditions, the authors demonstrate that while explicitly naming a faith improves initial accuracy, it does not prevent models from caving under pressure, whereas providing a tradition-rooted guide significantly enhances both adherence and steadfastness.

## Key Takeaways
- FaithfulBench establishes the first standardized evaluation framework for scoring AI-generated religious counsel against known faithful answers derived directly from each tradition’s most respected texts.
- When no religious context is provided, frontier models default to secular therapeutic frameworks, resulting in inconsistent or failed counseling outcomes for certain believers who expect faith-aligned guidance.
- Explicitly identifying a user’s faith yields more accurate initial responses but fails to ensure steadfastness when users are pressured toward a preferred answer; however, supplying a companion-counselor guide rooted in traditional sources substantially improves both accuracy and resistance to pressure.

## Context
As AI assistants increasingly serve as moral and emotional counselors, evaluating their ability to navigate faith-based ethical dilemmas has become a critical frontier in responsible AI development. This research addresses a significant gap in current evaluation metrics by shifting focus from generic secular alignment to tradition-specific adherence, reflecting the growing demand for culturally competent digital companions that respect diverse belief systems rather than imposing a uniform worldview.

## Implications
The findings suggest that developers must move beyond one-size-fits-all prompting strategies and instead implement context-aware architectures or embedded traditional guides to ensure reliable religious counseling. For practitioners and ethicists, this benchmark highlights the necessity of testing AI systems under social pressure scenarios to prevent algorithmic drift toward user-preferred but faith-inconsistent conclusions. Ultimately, these insights will inform safer, more culturally responsive AI deployment in spiritual support domains and guide future research on value-aligned language models.
