## Summary
This paper introduces a human‑grounded framework to quantify how large language models (LLMs) distribute their content across the breadth of human writing on open‑ended topics. Experiments show that LLMs generate plausible but narrow outputs that cluster near the center of the human response space, highlighting a persistent “distributional gap” between model and human generation.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework measures distributional breadth by comparing the empirical distribution of human writing to that of LLM output using two metrics: LLM Coverage (LLM‑Cov) and In‑Boundary Rate (IBR).  
- Across ideation and narrative tasks, LLMs consistently produce content that is statistically plausible yet confined to a central region of the human response space.  
- This gap represents a “cultural reach” limitation where models miss the diversity and peripheral ideas present in human writing.

## Context
The study situates itself within ongoing debates about model reliability and creativity, emphasizing that current LLMs excel at surface‑level plausibility but lack true generative breadth. By grounding measurement in real human corpora, the work offers a practical tool for evaluating not just accuracy but also the richness of generated narratives.

## Implications
Researchers can now systematically assess whether their models capture the full spectrum of human expression, informing design choices that prioritize diversity over mere correctness. For industry practitioners, this framework provides actionable insights to improve model outputs and better align AI‑generated content with user expectations.
