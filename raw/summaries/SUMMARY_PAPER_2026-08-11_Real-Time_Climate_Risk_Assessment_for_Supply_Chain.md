## Summary
The paper introduces a data‑driven nowcasting framework that translates short‑term climate forecasts into actionable risk signals for Colombian agricultural supply chains. By integrating historical meteorological observations with supply chain modeling the authors demonstrate that precipitation nowcasts can be used to generate early warning indicators that guide inventory, sourcing and transport decisions.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework relies on short‑term precipitation nowcasts rather than satellite imagery or computer vision to produce reliable risk indicators.
- Risk mapping is performed using threshold‑based categorization which translates forecast probabilities into clear stakeholder‑oriented signals.
- A prototype built from official statistics and reanalysis data shows the feasibility of the approach without requiring advanced imaging technologies.

## Context
This work advances AI applications in climate risk assessment by focusing on nowcasting that uses only meteorological time series, highlighting how machine learning can operate within existing observational datasets. It underscores the importance of integrating temporal climate forecasts with logistical planning to improve resilience in regions prone to extreme weather.

## Implications
For agricultural producers and supply chain managers this model offers a practical early warning system that reduces losses from unpredictable rainfall events. The approach demonstrates how AI‑enhanced risk mapping can be deployed locally, supporting more resilient operations without costly satellite infrastructure.
