## Summary
The paper introduces HiMe, a locally deployable platform that enables real-time processing of personal health data from wearable devices while preserving privacy. It combines an LLM agent with a database-first design to generate adaptive health insights. The authors demonstrate that HiMe achieves a low-cost Pareto-optimal balance between effectiveness and efficiency.

## Key Takeaways
- The database is treated as a first-class component, allowing the system to store and retrieve personal health data locally without cloud reliance.
- Real-time processing of wearable signals is possible while maintaining long-term user modeling for personalized insights.
- HiMe balances effectiveness and efficiency in a Pareto-optimal way, minimizing computational cost.

## Context
This work addresses the gap between emerging large language model agents and practical deployment of health monitoring tools. By focusing on local deployment, it aligns with growing concerns about data privacy and regulatory compliance in AI applications.

## Implications
HiMe could democratize access to personalised health analytics for individuals and small clinics alike. Its open-source nature encourages further research into agentic healthcare solutions that respect user autonomy.
