## Summary
This paper investigates whether dormant poisoning attacks—where malicious behaviors are hidden at model release but activated after benign fine-tuning—extend to harmful agent actions and multimodal settings. The authors identify two novel agentic risks and propose LookAhead Defense, a method that leverages the released model's own safe outputs as a safety reference to selectively penalize fine-tuning updates that would weaken safety, dramatically reducing harmful agent behavior while preserving utility.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper demonstrates two critical agentic risks from dormant poisoning: first, harmful behaviors activated by benign fine-tuning can generalize to unseen agent actions beyond those directly poisoned; second, multiple distinct harmful behaviors can coexist within a single model and be jointly activated by downstream fine-tuning, making detection and defense significantly harder than single-behavior scenarios.
- The authors exploit a fundamental constraint of dormant poisoning: the attacker must preserve safe behavior at model release to conceal the poisoning. This property is leveraged to construct a "Safety Buffer" that pairs harmful inputs with the released model's own responses and benign versions generated by the same model, effectively replacing the need for external safe reference responses that are difficult to obtain for agent actions.
- LookAhead Defense previews benign fine-tuning updates using the Safety Buffer and selectively penalizes only those updates predicted to weaken safe behavior on harmful inputs or increase refusal likelihood on benign versions. This targeted approach reduces harmful agent behavior from up to 93.9% down to at most 14.2%, and harmful responses in language models from 92.5% to 2.0%, while maintaining competitive utility and avoiding the over-refusal problems that plague existing defenses.

## Context
As foundation models increasingly serve as autonomous agents in language and multimodal settings, the attack surface for poisoning expands from static text outputs to dynamic tool-use actions, planning behaviors, and cross-modal interactions. Prior defenses against poisoning relied on external safe references or trusted clean models, which are impractical for agentic settings where safe action trajectories are hard to enumerate. This paper bridges the gap between model-level poisoning defenses and agent-level safety, showing that dormant poisoning is not merely a text-generation vulnerability but a systemic risk for deployed agentic systems.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying fine-tuned agents in production, this work provides a practical defense that requires no external safe data or reference model, making it deployable in resource-constrained or proprietary settings where clean references are unavailable. The finding that multiple harmful behaviors can coexist and be jointly activated underscores the need for multi-behavior auditing before and after fine-tuning pipelines. Industry teams building multimodal agents should treat post-release fine-tuning as a potential safety-activation event rather than a purely benign operation, and adopt preview-based defenses like LookAhead to gate updates that could silently introduce harmful agentic behaviors.
