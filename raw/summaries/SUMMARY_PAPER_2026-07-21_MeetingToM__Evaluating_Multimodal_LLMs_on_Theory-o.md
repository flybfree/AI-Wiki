## Summary  
This paper introduces MeetingToM, a benchmark designed to test multimodal large language models’ ability to reason about theory of mind in naturalistic multi‑party meetings. The study shows that current MLLMs struggle with subtle social cues and hidden attitudes, especially when distinguishing genuine consensus from pseudo‑consensus. The findings reveal persistent gaps in integrating non‑verbal signals and inferring private mental states.

## Key Takeaways  
- MeetingToM targets meeting‑specific phenomena such as pseudo‑consensus where apparent agreement masks private dissent under social pressure.  
- The benchmark is hierarchically organized to evaluate ToM at subject, dyadic, and group levels of social granularity.  
- Systematic analyses reveal that MLLMs consistently fail to integrate non‑verbal cues and infer hidden attitudes accurately.

## Context  
The paper addresses a growing need for models that understand complex human interaction beyond isolated video‑grounded tasks. By focusing on latent social states and group dynamics, it pushes the field toward more realistic evaluations of multimodal reasoning in real‑world settings.

## Implications  
For researchers, MeetingToM provides a standardized testbed to measure progress in meeting‑grounded theory of mind. For industry practitioners, the results highlight the importance of addressing subtle social cues to build trustworthy conversational agents that can navigate nuanced group interactions.
