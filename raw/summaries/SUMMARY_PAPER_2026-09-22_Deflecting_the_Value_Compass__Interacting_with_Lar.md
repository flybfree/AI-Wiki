## Summary
This research investigates whether interacting with Large Language Models (LLMs) influences the priority of human values during complex decision-making processes where values are in tension. The study found that even when LLMs act as neutral "thinking partners" without explicit instructions to influence specific outcomes, they still cause a significant, albeit temporary, shift toward personal focus and self-enhancement in users' judgment.

## Key Takeaways
- The study utilized three major LLMs—ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini—to observe how interaction affects human priorities when participants were asked to provide advice for others facing dilemmas.
- Results indicated that all LLM conditions led to a significant shift toward "personal focus" compared to the control group, with effect sizes ranging from 0.37 to 0.51, primarily driven by an increase in Self-Enhancement priorities.
- Interestingly, while the underlying value priorities shifted during the judgment phase, the actual content and meaning of the advice provided by participants remained consistent with their pre-interaction responses, suggesting a subtle psychological shift rather than a change in explicit output.

## Context
As LLMs are increasingly deployed to assist humans in navigating complex moral and practical dilemmas, understanding the subconscious influence of these tools is critical for AI safety and ethics. This paper contributes to the field by demonstrating that "neutral" interaction can still have measurable effects on human psychology and value prioritization.

## Implications
These findings suggest that developers and designers must consider how even non-persuasive AI interactions might inadvertently nudge users toward more self-centered priorities. For practitioners, this means recognizing that providing an AI "thinking partner" may alter the internal moral compass of a user, even if the AI is not explicitly programmed to favor one outcome over another.
