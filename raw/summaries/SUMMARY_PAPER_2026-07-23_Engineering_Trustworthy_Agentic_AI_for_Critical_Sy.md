## Summary
The paper proposes a trustworthiness framework for agentic AI in critical engineering domains, treating trust as an engineering property rather than just capability. It maps five dimensions—safety, constraint satisfaction, robustness, reliability, transparency, interpretability, accountability, auditability, and privacy—to an assurance workflow from perception to audit and surveys architectures across power systems, autonomous vehicles, HPC, and networks.

## Key Takeaways
- Trustworthiness is defined by safety, constraint satisfaction, robustness, reliability, transparency, interpretability, accountability, auditability, and privacy, forming a holistic model for evaluating agentic AI.
- The framework links each dimension to concrete trust mechanisms such as verification logs, explainable models, and audit trails that can be applied throughout the assurance workflow.
- Cross‑domain analysis reveals shared failure modes like perception drift and tool misuse while highlighting domain gaps in HPC where resource isolation is critical.

## Context
Current AI research often measures agents by performance metrics alone, overlooking how their behavior impacts real‑world engineering outcomes. This work fills that gap by aligning trustworthiness with the rigorous standards of safety‑critical systems.

## Implications
Engineers and developers can adopt this assurance framework to certify agentic solutions, reducing risk in high‑stakes applications. The reusable model supports standardized testing and certification similar to existing graded certification regimes.
