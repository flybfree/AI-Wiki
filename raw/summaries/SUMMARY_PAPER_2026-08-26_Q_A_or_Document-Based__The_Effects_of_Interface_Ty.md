## Summary
This study compares a Question‑Answer Interface (QAI) with open‑ended conversational queries and a Document Interface (DI) that relies on traditional document navigation among 16 blind‑low‑vision screen reader users. The DI enabled participants to explore more documents, build larger mental models, and apply knowledge more effectively than the QAI did.

## Key Takeaways
- Participants visited more distinct documents with the DI and formed larger and more correct mental models compared to those using the QAI.
- Knowledge transfer was higher on the DI, yet many users still preferred the QAI despite lower actual performance.
- The preference for the QAI may stem from its conversational flow, even though it does not support deeper knowledge construction.

## Context
The paper addresses a growing need for inclusive AI interfaces that enable screen reader users to navigate and interlink documents. As large language models become more accessible, understanding how interface design influences cognitive outcomes is crucial for equitable technology use.

## Implications
Designers must prioritize structured navigation over conversational prompts when building knowledge‑building tools for BLV users. This research highlights the risk of misaligned expectations and suggests a shift toward interfaces that directly support document exploration and integration.
