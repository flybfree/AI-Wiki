## Summary
The paper proposes a two-stage stacking model that combines patient-reported swallowing symptoms with structured clinical variables to predict dysphagia risk in head and neck cancer survivors without using videofluoroscopic imaging. The study shows that individual MDADI responses add predictive value beyond those captured by composite scores and reveals specific symptom patterns linked to impairment.

## Key Takeaways
- Individual MDADI responses contain predictive information beyond those captured by composite or global summary scores.
- Interpretability analyses reveal symptom patterns and clinical risk factors associated with swallowing impairment.
- The two-stage stacking model provides a clinically interpretable, imaging-free approach for dysphagia risk stratification in HNC survivorship.

## Context
This work advances AI-driven health prediction by integrating heterogeneous data sources into a transparent decision framework. By leveraging PROs alongside structured variables, the model illustrates how machine learning can support clinical judgment without requiring expensive imaging equipment.
