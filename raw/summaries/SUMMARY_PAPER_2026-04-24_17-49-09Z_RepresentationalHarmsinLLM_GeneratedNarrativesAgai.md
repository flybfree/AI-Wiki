## Summary
This paper investigates how large language models generate narratives about national origin identities and shows that these outputs often reinforce harmful stereotypes, erasure, or one‑dimensional portrayals of Global Majority groups. The study finds that minority nationalities are far less likely to appear in neutral stories than in subordinated character roles, a disparity that is especially pronounced when US nationality cues are present.

## Key Takeaways
- Minoritized national identities are underrepresented in power‑neutral narratives and overrepresented in subordinate character portrayals, with the latter occurring roughly fifty times more often than dominant portrayals.  
- The presence of US‑specific terms such as “American” amplifies these harms, indicating that US‑centric biases persist even when non‑US national cues are used.  
- The identified representational harms cannot be attributed to sycophancy or cultural bias toward the US, suggesting a deeper structural issue in how LLMs encode dominant narratives.

## Context
The rapid deployment of LLMs across diverse applications raises concerns about their impact on representation and fairness. This research contributes to the growing body of work that examines algorithmic bias beyond simple demographic metrics, focusing instead on narrative consequences for marginalized groups worldwide.

## Implications
Practitioners must adopt evaluation frameworks that center Global Majority perspectives to prevent misrepresentation in AI‑driven tools used for surveillance or classification. Ignoring these harms could lead to systemic discrimination and undermine trust in AI systems.
