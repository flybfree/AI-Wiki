## Summary
This paper introduces a legitimacy theory for constitutive priors in machine intelligence, arguing that the artificial physical world provides a structured exception to the cold‑start deadlock. It derives a testable criterion: prior extraction is legitimate only when an object domain is intentionally constituted and leaves a readable archive, with violations interpreted as world errors rather than model revisions.

## Key Takeaways
- The legitimacy criterion requires both intentional constitution of the domain and a durable, readable archive that precedes its instances.  
- Any valid framework must contain at least four layers — syntax, concept, knowledge, instance — because their construction goals are mutually incompatible.  
- Deployment claims span five industrial domains using a 32‑class failure‑mode vocabulary, with falsifiable predictions such as the impact of archiveless rule coverage on system reliability.

## Context
Machine intelligence excels in symbolic reasoning but struggles to generate reliable data for physical applications, creating a structural deadlock. This work offers a theoretical framework that treats constitutive priors as normative artifacts within engineered environments, providing a bridge between abstract models and real‑world deployment.

## Implications
The theory clarifies when AI can legitimately rely on prior knowledge in physical systems, guiding engineers to design archives that support consistent operation. It also sets measurable standards for failure prediction, encouraging industry adoption of structured data practices across critical infrastructure.
