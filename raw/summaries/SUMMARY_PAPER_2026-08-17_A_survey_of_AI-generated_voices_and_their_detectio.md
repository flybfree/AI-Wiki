## Summary
The paper surveys AI-generated voice synthesis and detection methods, highlighting both technical advances and practical challenges in identifying synthetic speech. It reviews recent research on generation models, evaluation metrics, and detection techniques, concluding that robust detection remains difficult due to the nuanced nature of phonetics and prosody.

## Key Takeaways
- Detection methods struggle with subtle variations in phonetic patterns and prosodic cues, making reliable identification hard even when synthetic voices mimic natural speech. 
- The survey identifies limited benchmark datasets as a bottleneck, preventing consistent comparison across studies. 
- Open challenges include the lack of standardized evaluation protocols for both generation quality and detection accuracy.

## Context
Voice cloning technologies have become central to accessibility tools and creative applications, yet they also pose societal risks such as fraud and disinformation. The rapid evolution of generative models outpaces the development of reliable safeguards, highlighting a gap between capability and control in AI research.

## Implications
For practitioners, this survey underscores the need for interdisciplinary efforts combining signal processing, machine learning, and ethical oversight to mitigate misuse. Industry adoption must prioritize detection integration into voice services while adhering to emerging regulatory standards.
