## Summary
This paper argues that while neural theorem provers have achieved notable feats, they represent only a fragment of what AI can contribute to mathematics. The authors advocate for a broader vision where AI augments human reasoning across diverse mathematical domains rather than merely solving isolated problems.

## Key Takeaways
- Neural theorem provers excel at pattern recognition but often lack the ability to generalize beyond training data, limiting their impact on deep theoretical work.
- Integrating AI with human mathematicians can foster new collaborative frameworks that expand research horizons and inspire innovative problem formulations.
- The optimism expressed in this essay suggests that future breakthroughs will arise from interdisciplinary synergy rather than isolated algorithmic improvements.

## Context
The rapid progress of neural theorem provers has sparked debates about the role of artificial intelligence in formal verification. This paper situates these advances within a larger narrative where AI is positioned as a catalyst for rethinking mathematical practice and pedagogy.

## Implications
For researchers, this outlook encourages experiments that blend symbolic reasoning with machine learning to unlock deeper insights. Practitioners should view AI tools not just as calculators but as partners in exploring uncharted territories of mathematics.
