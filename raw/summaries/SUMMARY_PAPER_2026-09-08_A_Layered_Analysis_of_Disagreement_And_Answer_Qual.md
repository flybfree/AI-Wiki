## Summary  
The paper investigates how multi‑agent LLM debate influences answer quality and persistent disagreement, measuring agreement reports, textual pushback, stance persistence, and token‑level log‑probabilities. Across 750 debates under three tones, it finds that tone strongly reshapes reported agreement but does not improve final answer accuracy.

## Key Takeaways  
- Tone reshapes the self‑reported agreement by about 50 percentage points between friendly and hostile settings.  
- The textual pushback is recovered even when only reading replies, indicating genuine disagreement occurs despite self‑report bias.  
- Persistent dissent shifts toward agreement after removing hostile instructions, showing condition‑dependent attitude changes.

## Context  
Multi‑agent debate is a popular technique to surface conflict among large language models, yet empirical evidence on its impact remains scarce. This study fills that gap by systematically measuring both reported and actual disagreement across diverse conditions.

## Implications  
For practitioners, the findings suggest that encouraging debate may not reliably boost answer quality or stable stance. Researchers should focus on mechanisms beyond self‑reporting to assess true model alignment.
