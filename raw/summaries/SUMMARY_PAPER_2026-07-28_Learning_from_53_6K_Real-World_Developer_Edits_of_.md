## Summary
This paper introduces DECODE, a dataset of 53.6 K real‑world developer edits to code generated by large language models in Python, TypeScript, and JavaScript. The study shows that most edits happen within the first 15 minutes after accepting an AI completion, with about one third of edit trajectories ending in the removal of the AI’s suggestion.

## Key Takeaways
- Most edits occur early, typically within the first 15 minutes after a developer accepts an AI‑generated code snippet.  
- Approximately 31 % of these edit trajectories conclude by deleting the entire AI completion.  
- Fine‑tuning open‑source 3B language models on DECODE improves their ability to predict edits compared with current frontier LLMs.

## Context
Current LLM training relies heavily on public Git commit data, which lacks the granularity and timing of actual developer modifications. This gap limits the realism of model performance benchmarks for code editing tasks.

## Implications
Developers need AI assistants that understand when and why humans intervene, so future systems must incorporate editor‑behavior insights. Incorporating such data will lead to more accurate prediction models and better user experiences in AI programming tools.
