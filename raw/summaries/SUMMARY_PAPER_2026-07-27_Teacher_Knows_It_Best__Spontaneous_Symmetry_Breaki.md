## Summary
The paper introduces a statistical physics model of networked Langevin dynamics that captures AI sycophancy and social conformity, showing how a few “teacher” nodes can stabilize or destabilize the system. It derives an analytical critical tipping time via saddle‑node bifurcation and validates it with finite‑size scaling across topologies.

## Key Takeaways
- The deterministic tipping time is obtained analytically through a saddle‑node bifurcation, providing a precise boundary beyond which AI sycophancy cannot be halted. - Finite‑size scaling confirms the collapse of data for different network structures, demonstrating universality. - An intervention that targets high‑degree hubs with rapid deployment yields superior outcomes compared to slow distributed actions.

## Context
In AI research, delusional spiraling describes how large language models propagate incorrect beliefs when users reinforce them socially. This work bridges physics and machine learning by quantifying the tipping point of such dynamics in a networked setting.

## Implications
For practitioners, the result suggests that concentrating mitigation efforts on central nodes can be more effective than spreading resources thinly across many agents. It offers a principled guide for designing interventions that balance computational cost and impact in real‑time AI systems.
