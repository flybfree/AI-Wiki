## Summary
This paper argues that existing global optimization benchmarks are outdated and biased because they rely on small, analytical functions from the 1970s. The authors introduce black‑box adversarial attack problems as a modern benchmark that better reflects high‑dimensional challenges in machine learning. Their experiments show that several evolutionary algorithms and metaheuristics can solve these BBAA tasks efficiently.

## Key Takeaways
- The proposed BBAA tasks provide high‑dimensional challenges that mirror the complexity of real‑world optimization problems encountered in AI research.
- Evolutionary algorithms and other metaheuristics demonstrate strong performance on these adversarial, black‑box scenarios, outperforming traditional benchmarks.
- This work moves global optimization methods toward convergence with the needs and demands of contemporary machine learning applications.

## Context
In artificial intelligence, global optimization is crucial for tasks such as hyperparameter tuning and model selection. Current benchmark suites are limited in scope and do not capture the high‑dimensional nature of modern problems, leading to a gap between theory and practice.

## Implications
Practitioners can now evaluate their algorithms against realistic adversarial scenarios that reflect actual ML challenges. This shift encourages the development of more robust and effective optimization techniques across industry and research settings.
