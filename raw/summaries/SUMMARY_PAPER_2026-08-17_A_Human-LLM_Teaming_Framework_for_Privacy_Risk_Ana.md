## Summary
This paper introduces PRIAM, a human‑LLM teaming framework designed to systematically analyze privacy risks associated with Central Bank Digital Currency welfare schemes. By combining the LLM’s ability to process vast documentary data with human expertise that interprets and refines outputs, the authors demonstrate an iterative collaboration that yields more accurate risk assessments than either approach alone.

## Key Takeaways
- The LLM generates initial data categories and assigns provisional values to attributes, but these outputs are subject to human evaluation for factual alignment.  
- Human experts identify information gaps, distinguish documented evidence from inference, and flag ambiguous or unsupported statements.  
- The iterative loop allows continuous refinement of the privacy risk analysis, ensuring a balanced blend of scale‑processing power and contextual judgment.

## Context
The rapid rollout of CBDC welfare programs creates complex data ecosystems where privacy risks like surveillance and discrimination are amplified. Existing AI tools often lack the nuanced human oversight needed to interpret subtle evidence or resolve ambiguities, highlighting a gap that this work addresses within the broader AI research agenda for responsible risk assessment.

## Implications
Practitioners in fintech and public policy can adopt PRIAM to improve privacy safeguards without sacrificing efficiency. The framework offers a replicable model for human‑AI collaboration, encouraging industry standards that balance automated data handling with expert scrutiny.
