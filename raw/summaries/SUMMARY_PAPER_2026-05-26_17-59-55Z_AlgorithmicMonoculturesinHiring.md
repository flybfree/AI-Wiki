## Summary  
The paper investigates how hiring algorithms from a single vendor create algorithmic monocultures that produce racial disparities in applicant outcomes, finding higher rejection rates for Asian and Black applicants despite similar qualifications.

## Key Takeaways  
- The dataset shows 14.74% of applications by Asian applicants are rejected under U.S. discrimination standards, compared with 25.87% for Black applicants.  
- A small fraction (4%) of applicants who apply to ten positions receive rejection from all positions, a rate higher than chance.  
- Applying widely does not guarantee consideration; the deterministic nature of algorithms means outcomes depend on total applications.

## Context  
This research highlights how centralized algorithmic design can amplify existing biases in hiring practices. It underscores the need for diverse data and evaluation across multiple vendors to mitigate unfair outcomes.

## Implications  
For AI practitioners, the findings suggest that reliance on a single vendor’s solution risks perpetuating discrimination. Companies should adopt auditable, multi-vendor approaches to ensure fairness and compliance with employment laws.
