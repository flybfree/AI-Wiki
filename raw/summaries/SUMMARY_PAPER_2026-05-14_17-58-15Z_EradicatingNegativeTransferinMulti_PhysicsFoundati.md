## Summary
The paper introduces Shodh-MoE, a sparse‑activated latent transformer that mitigates negative transfer in multi‑physics foundation models by routing latent patches to specialized experts while preserving shared components. It achieves exact mass conservation and low reconstruction MSEs on both open‑channel fluid dynamics and porous‑media flows.

## Key Takeaways
- The model operates on compressed 16³ physical latents generated by a physics‑informed autoencoder with an intra‑tokenizer Helmholtz velocity parameterization, guaranteeing divergence‑free velocities (~2.8 × 10⁻¹⁰) and exact mass conservation.
- A top‑1 soft‑semantic router autonomously assigns open‑channel tokens to Expert 0 and porous‑media tokens to Expert 1, demonstrating domain bifurcation during the 20 000‑step distributed pretraining run.
- The model converges simultaneously across both regimes with low validation MSEs (2.46 × 10⁻⁵, 9.76 × 10⁻⁶) and decoded physical MSEs (2.48 × 10⁻⁶, 1.76 × 10⁻⁶).

## Context
This work tackles the bottleneck of universal neural operators in scientific machine learning, where co‑training disparate PDE regimes leads to gradient conflict and instability. By separating physics‑specific parameter paths, Shodh-MoE enables scalable training across multiple domains without sacrificing performance.

## Implications
Sparse expert routing offers a practical architectural mechanism for reducing interference between physical mechanisms, which could accelerate research and deployment in complex simulation environments where multi‑physics AI is needed.
