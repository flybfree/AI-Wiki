## Summary
The paper investigates how large language model adoption reshapes scientific research practices by linking publication data, collaboration histories, and CRediT role statements across a large dataset of scientists. It finds that after 2022 scientists become more interdisciplinary, their team roles diversify, and the division of labor becomes less rigid.

## Key Takeaways
- Interdisciplinary publishing rises especially among established scientists in non‑English low‑middle‑income countries.
- AI‑writing signals amplify earlier interdisciplinary tendencies, widening the gap with authors who use LLMs less.
- Team role sets narrow, coauthor role overlap shrinks, and conceptual/management roles decline while software/validation roles grow.

## Context
The rapid diffusion of large language models has prompted speculation about their impact on scientific workflows. This study provides empirical evidence linking model usage to measurable shifts in research direction and team organization across a global dataset.

## Implications
Scientists may need to adapt collaboration frameworks as AI tools automate routine tasks, freeing capacity for broader conceptual work. Practitioners should monitor role evolution to maintain effective interdisciplinary teams and avoid over‑specialization.
