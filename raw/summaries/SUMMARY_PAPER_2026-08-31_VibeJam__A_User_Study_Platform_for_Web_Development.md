## Summary
This paper introduces VibeJam, a browser‑based platform that lets users collaborate with AI agents on web development tasks. The study shows that experienced programmers find the system enjoyable and intuitive, while junior students produce higher‑quality websites than the agents alone, highlighting both usability benefits and quality gains.

## Key Takeaways
- VibeJam provides an online user study environment where AI agents can be customized and interact with users through diff review, chat, plan, and live preview modes.  
- The pilot experiment with 55 released game‑based website tasks demonstrates that five experienced programmers rate the system as fun, simple, and comparable to commercial tools.  
- Junior students using VibeJam achieve websites of higher quality than those generated by agents in the same task, indicating a positive impact on learning outcomes.

## Context
The rapid integration of AI agents into programming workflows has shifted research focus from offline metrics to real‑world usage dynamics. Existing evaluations often ignore online interactions, limiting understanding of how programmers actually engage with agentic tools during collaborative coding tasks.

## Implications
VibeJam’s open‑source release encourages extensions that can support diverse user groups and task types, fostering more nuanced studies on AI‑assisted development. For practitioners, the platform offers a practical model for evaluating agent usability without sacrificing quantitative rigor.
