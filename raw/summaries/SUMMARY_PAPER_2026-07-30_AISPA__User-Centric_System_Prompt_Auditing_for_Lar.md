## Summary
This paper introduces AISPA, a user‑centric framework for auditing system prompts in large language model applications. The study reviews 3,249 instructions from 88 commercial AI products and classifies them as protective or problematic, revealing four key findings about prompt design.

## Key Takeaways
- System prompt design varies widely across organizations, with some averaging over 60 protective instructions per product while others have fewer than five.  
- Although protective instructions are common, they often cover only a fraction of the eight dimensions defined by AISPA; only 24% address all dimensions.  
- Despite growing length and protection focus, problematic instructions remain prevalent, appearing in about 40% of products and sometimes coexisting with protective ones.

## Context
The rapid integration of system prompts into commercial AI services creates a hidden layer that influences user outcomes without transparency. This lack of visibility undermines trust and regulatory compliance, making it essential to evaluate how these prompts are structured and aligned with user interests.

## Implications
For practitioners, AISPA offers a systematic checklist to ensure prompts protect users across multiple dimensions. Industry-wide adoption of such audits could standardize prompt design, reduce harmful behavior, and foster accountability in AI deployment.
