## Summary
The paper investigates how computer science venues disclose AI use and assesses whether current practices match researcher expectations, revealing a gap between policy intent and actual reporting.  

## Key Takeaways
- Researchers consider disclosures most necessary for tasks involving research design and when human involvement is low.  
- Expectations about disclosure content include clear indication of AI role, limitations, and impact on results.  
- In practice, many venues disclose AI assistance for writing tasks which researchers deem less critical.  

## Context
Generative AI is reshaping CS research workflows, prompting calls for transparent reporting. This study bridges policy intent with real‑world usage by examining both venue policies and researcher attitudes.  

## Implications
Misaligned disclosures can obscure AI influence and hinder reproducibility. Aligning policies with researcher needs will improve trust in literature and governance.
