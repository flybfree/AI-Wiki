## Summary
This paper examines how multimodal language models handle calibrated ambiguity compared to human communicators by using the parlor game Dixit as a controlled testing environment. The authors find that while humans intentionally craft open yet interpretable clues rich in cultural allusion, AI systems consistently suffer from ambiguity collapse and cultural flattening. These findings highlight a fundamental disconnect between human creative expression and current multimodal model capabilities.

## Key Takeaways
- Ambiguity is reframed not as a system bug to be resolved but as a generative resource that enables multiple legitimate interpretations, a quality humans naturally leverage across humor, politics, and art.
- Multimodal language models exhibit ambiguity collapse by producing over-specified outputs that leave no room for alternative readings, fundamentally limiting their capacity for open-ended creative or interpretive tasks.
- AI-generated clues demonstrate cultural flattening, almost entirely avoiding culturally situated knowledge or figurative language even when explicitly prompted to use allusions, revealing a significant gap in symbolic and contextual reasoning.

## Context
Current multimodal AI systems are typically optimized for literal accuracy and unambiguous instruction following, which often strips away the nuanced, open-ended nature of human communication. This research situates itself within the growing discourse on AI creativity and cultural representation, challenging the assumption that clarity should always be the primary objective in generative tasks. By introducing a novel coding rubric for calibrated ambiguity, the study provides a measurable framework for evaluating how well models capture the symbolic depth inherent in human visual-language interaction.

## Implications
For developers and practitioners, these findings suggest that current training paradigms may inadvertently suppress figurative reasoning and cultural nuance, which could limit AI applications in creative industries, marketing, and interactive storytelling. Addressing ambiguity collapse will require deliberate curation of training data that preserves open-endedness rather than enforcing literalism. Ultimately, bridging this gap is essential for building AI systems capable of meaningful cultural engagement and adaptive human-AI collaboration.
