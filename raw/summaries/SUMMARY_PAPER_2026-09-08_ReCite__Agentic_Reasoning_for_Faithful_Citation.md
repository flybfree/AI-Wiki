## Summary
This paper introduces ReCite, an agentic framework that replaces similarity‑based citation retrieval with claim‑level reasoning to improve factual citation accuracy. Experiments show the lightweight system surpasses large generative models in strict citation correctness while maintaining efficiency.

## Key Takeaways
- The authors argue that accurate citations require active, claim‑level reasoning rather than passive semantic matching.
- ReCite uses a decoupled agentic architecture that combines location perception, intent‑aware query planning, and reflective verification to detect logical gaps.
- Their lightweight framework outperforms state‑of‑the‑art massive generative models in citation accuracy metrics.

## Context
Current automatic citation tools depend on large language models that generate plausible references but often fabricate or misattribute sources. This reliance creates a risk of scholarly fraud and undermines the integrity of AI‑assisted writing.

## Implications
For researchers and industry practitioners, ReCite offers a reliable alternative to black‑box generative systems, fostering trustworthy academic output. The approach could be adopted in citation assistants, plagiarism detection tools, and any system requiring precise source attribution.
