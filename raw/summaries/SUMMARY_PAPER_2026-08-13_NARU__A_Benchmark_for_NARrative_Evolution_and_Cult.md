## Summary
The paper introduces NARU, a benchmark for evaluating narrative evolution and cultural understanding in Japanese long‑form video. It contains 1,481 questions across 155 videos totalling 146.8 hours. Models show limitations in long‑range integration and culturally grounded reasoning.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark spans four narrative dimensions and five cultural dimensions, providing a holistic test of MLLM performance.
- Evaluation across eight model configurations reveals persistent gaps in both long‑range narrative integration and culturally grounded reasoning.
- Two native‑speaker verification stages with 68 annotators ensure high‑quality annotation.

## Context
This work addresses the need for benchmarks that evaluate joint narrative and cultural comprehension, especially for non‑English media. The growing importance of Japanese media in global AI research makes this benchmark particularly relevant.

## Implications
Practitioners can use NARU to benchmark and improve models for long‑form video understanding in Japanese contexts. Future systems may integrate cultural nuance to deliver richer user experiences, especially in culturally specific domains.
