## Summary
This paper conducts a systematic literature review of 212 studies to examine how physics‑informed machine learning (PIML) is applied in prognostics and health management (PHM), introducing a four‑class classification scheme that highlights observational, inductive, learning, and hybrid biases. The authors find that PIML consistently improves predictive performance over conventional data‑driven baselines across various assets, though the research remains skewed toward lithium‑ion batteries and bearings.

## Key Takeaways
- PIML integrates physical laws directly into ML models, reducing reliance on purely statistical patterns.
- Studies show measurable gains in prediction accuracy for battery degradation and bearing wear compared to baseline methods.
- The literature is dominated by niche, problem‑specific solutions rather than transferable frameworks.

## Context
The integration of physics into AI addresses longstanding challenges such as limited generalization and interpretability in machine learning. By embedding domain knowledge, PIML offers a pathway toward more robust and trustworthy models that can operate reliably on complex industrial systems.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, this review suggests that while PIML delivers tangible benefits, further work is needed to develop standardized benchmarks and lightweight, uncertainty‑aware models suitable for real‑time deployment. The field must move beyond isolated case studies toward scalable design patterns that benefit a broader range of assets.
