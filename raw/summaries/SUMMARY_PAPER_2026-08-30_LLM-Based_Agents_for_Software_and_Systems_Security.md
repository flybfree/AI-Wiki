## Summary
The paper conducts a systematic literature review of LLM-based agents in software and systems security from 2023 to 2026, categorizing technical approaches, applications, and assessment methods. It finds that while agents can perform multi-step workflows, their authority remains unchecked and behavior is not auditable.

## Key Takeaways
- The field has developed agents capable of planning, using tools, retaining state, and revising actions across complex security tasks.
- Current assessments lack comparable protocols, making it hard to compare safety outcomes across studies.
- Most work focuses on automation without establishing bounded authority or auditability for the agents.

## Context
LLM-based agents are rapidly being integrated into security workflows where human analysts traditionally perform inspection and decision-making. This trend raises questions about trustworthiness and accountability in automated systems.

## Implications
For practitioners, the lack of standardized evaluation means security solutions may be deployed without reliable safety guarantees. Industry must prioritize developing auditable agent designs to ensure responsible automation.
