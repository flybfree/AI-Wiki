## Summary
This paper investigates how humans and large language models perceive moral agency in artificial agents embedded in smart city scenarios. Using a validated scale, the study finds that humans consistently attribute higher moral agency than LLMs, yet LLMs demonstrate context‑sensitive reasoning that aligns with human judgments when evaluating specific dilemmas.

## Key Takeaways
- Human participants rate perceived moral agency of autonomous artificial agents lower than their own self‑attributed agency.  
- LLMs evaluate moral agency based on the severity of harm and urgency of a situation rather than on stable traits of the agent itself.  
- The context‑sensitivity observed in LLMs mirrors that seen in human raters, suggesting shared reasoning mechanisms.

## Context
Understanding perceived moral agency is crucial as AI systems increasingly mediate everyday ethical choices in urban environments. This study bridges psychological theory with AI behavior, offering a benchmark for evaluating social trust in autonomous agents.

## Implications
For developers, the findings suggest that transparent, harm‑focused decision processes can improve public acceptance of AI moral roles. Practitioners should design LLMs to prioritize situational urgency over static agency labels to foster ethical alignment.
