## Summary
This paper investigates whether attributing consciousness to AI chatbots is epistemically innocent or blameworthy, offering a multidimensional taxonomy that distinguishes between metaphorical pretence and genuine belief. It argues that while some attributions lack evidential support yet are harmless, many others reflect epistemic responsibility. The analysis shows that linguistically identical statements can express varying degrees of epistemic commitment.

## Key Takeaways
- The taxonomy separates non‑doxastic attitudes such as pretence from doxastic beliefs ranging from ordinary belief to delusional conviction.  
- Epistemic innocence is possible even for irrational attributions, but it is not universal; many attributions entail blameworthiness.  
- Operationalizing the taxonomy enables empirical measurement of epistemic commitment to AI consciousness claims.

## Context
Current AI chatbots generate human‑like responses, prompting users to ascribe psychological traits like consciousness. This paper addresses a gap in understanding how such attributions function epistemically within the broader discourse on AI safety and user interaction.

## Implications
For developers, recognizing epistemic blameworthiness can guide ethical design that limits misleading claims. For researchers, the taxonomy provides a measurable framework for studying belief formation in human‑AI interactions.
