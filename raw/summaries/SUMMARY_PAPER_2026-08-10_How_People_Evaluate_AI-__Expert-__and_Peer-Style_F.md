## Summary
The paper investigates how people rate financial advice generated by AI, an expert, and a peer forum when the source attribution is correctly labeled, unlabeled, or mislabeled. It finds that expert advice receives higher ratings than AI advice across most outcomes, even without labels, while correct labeling adds little differentiation but mislabeling boosts ratings for AI advice on fit and quality.

## Key Takeaways
- Expert advice consistently outperforms AI advice in nine of ten outcome measures with effect sizes ranging from 0.20 to 0.47, indicating a measurable trust advantage that persists when source labels are absent.
- Correct labeling provides limited differentiation between sources, whereas mislabeling increases ratings for AI advice on situational fit and overall quality by about 0.42 standard deviations, suggesting label effects amplify perceived relevance.
- The visibility of style differences is strongest under an AI label, implying that the way a source is presented interacts with its message to shape evaluations.

## Context
As generative AI becomes embedded in everyday financial decision‑making, understanding how users interpret and trust different advice formats is essential for designing responsible systems. This study contributes to the literature by separating attribution effects from intrinsic communication cues, offering a nuanced view of trust formation in AI‑driven services.

## Implications
Practitioners should treat disclosure as an interpretive frame rather than a neutral transparency tool, recognizing that its accuracy and interaction with message content can shape user reliance. Policies and design choices must account for how labeling influences perceived expertise to mitigate overreliance on AI recommendations.
