## Summary
This paper investigates the challenges that cause failures in data, machine learning, and software delivery pipelines and evaluates existing solutions for detecting, diagnosing, and automatically fixing these issues. It concludes that while many tools exist, they are often costly, vendor‑specific, or hard to combine, revealing an architectural gap rather than a technological one. The authors propose a low‑cost, open‑source reference architecture that integrates monitoring, metadata, AI‑assisted diagnosis, approval workflows, and controlled remediation.

## Key Takeaways
- Existing off‑the‑shelf AI‑assisted pipeline tools are expensive and tied to specific vendors, limiting adoption by smaller teams.  
- The core components needed for self‑healing pipelines already exist but remain fragmented across different platforms and open‑source libraries.  
- A vendor‑agnostic architecture built on open‑source software can combine monitoring, metadata, AI diagnosis, approvals, and remediation to reduce manual effort.

## Context
The rapid growth of data engineering, machine learning operations, and CI/CD pipelines has created complex workflows where failures are frequent and costly. Traditional incident response relies heavily on expensive commercial platforms that add operational overhead without delivering scalable benefits across heterogeneous environments.

## Implications
This research offers a practical blueprint for organizations seeking affordable self‑healing capabilities, encouraging the adoption of open‑source tools to streamline pipeline resilience. By lowering barriers to integration, it can accelerate innovation and reduce downtime in AI and data pipelines worldwide.
