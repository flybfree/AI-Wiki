## Summary
The paper introduces ACME, a cross-cultural multi-embodiment dataset for social navigation, collected in eight sites across five countries using seven robot bodies. It provides 29.35 hours of onboard robot data and 43.5 hours of overhead pedestrian tracking, focusing on goal-driven behavior with explicit robot speech.

## Key Takeaways
- ACME captures more challenging scenarios and a broader distribution of pedestrian behavior than previous datasets.
- The dataset includes both human-readable data for each sensor modality as well as raw binary data to facilitate learning navigation policies and predicting trajectories.
- It provides 3D and 2D scene features, odometry, interaction information, and human‑annotated trajectory labels.

## Context
Social robot navigation remains a critical challenge because effective behavior depends on cultural norms, geographic contexts, and human‑robot interaction dynamics. Existing datasets often ignore these variations, limiting the applicability of learned policies to diverse environments.

## Implications
For researchers, ACME offers a more realistic benchmark for training robust social navigation models. For industry practitioners, it enables deployment in multicultural settings where adaptability is essential.
