## Summary
The paper proposes a framework to link AI model behaviour to system‑level harms in complex sociotechnical systems. Using Systems Theoretic Process Analysis and probabilistic modelling the authors show that simple adversarial inputs can trigger cascading failures in financial infrastructure. Their case study of the UK RTGS system demonstrates how AI‑driven loss scenarios increase bank failure risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Simple adversarial manipulation of LLM trading recommendations causes measurable behavioural shifts where AI advice is followed, creating a pathway from component to system outcome.
- The probabilistic contagion model shows these shifts raise systemic resilience thresholds, making the system more vulnerable to shocks under widespread or monopolistic AI adoption.
- The framework provides a traceable method for quantifying how observed AI actions translate into tangible harms across multiple organisational and technical layers.

## Context
AI integration in critical infrastructure is growing but current evaluation tools focus on models rather than real‑world impacts. This work highlights the need to connect technical outputs with organisational processes to understand systemic risk. The RTGS example illustrates a concrete instance where AI failures could disrupt financial stability.

## Implications
Practitioners can use this framework to anticipate and mitigate cascading harms, supporting evidence‑based governance. Industry leaders should adopt component‑level testing combined with system modelling to inform policy and design decisions for resilient AI deployment.
