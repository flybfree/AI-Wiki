## Summary
The paper introduces ASTELD, a six‑axis classification framework for autonomous AI agents that organizes platforms along architecture, security, tool integration, execution, autonomy, and deployment. It evaluates the framework by mapping eight existing frameworks and using OpenClaw as a case study, revealing distinct configuration patterns and an empty region where local‑first deployment meets enterprise‑grade security.

## Key Takeaways
- ASTELD separates all eight platforms under their dominant configurations, showing that security and execution are tightly coupled across systems. 
- The analysis uncovers three cross‑platform patterns: a security‑accessibility diagonal, strong execution‑architecture coupling, and capability convergence with persistent architectural differentiation. 
- Innovation in OpenClaw derivatives focuses on the Security, Execution, and Deployment axes, indicating where ecosystem fragmentation occurs.

## Context
Autonomous AI agent platforms vary widely in design choices yet lack a unified taxonomy to compare them, creating challenges for research reproducibility and industry adoption. This paper addresses that gap by proposing ASTELD, which offers an operational framework grounded in observable platform properties.

## Implications
ASTELD enables practitioners to select or develop frameworks aligned with specific risk profiles and deployment needs. It also highlights unoccupied design regions, guiding future work toward safer, more integrated autonomous systems.
