## Summary
This paper introduces a comprehensive framework for evaluating how artificial intelligence augments human work at the workflow level rather than focusing narrowly on task automation or adoption metrics. The authors define genuine AI augmentation through six essential conditions, including durable net value, meaningful human control, accountability and recovery mechanisms, and long-term professional development. By applying this framework to an AI-mediated social survey case study, they demonstrate how organizations can systematically assess and optimize sustained human-agent collaboration for transformative workplace outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- Current industry metrics for measuring AI's workplace value are fundamentally limited because they prioritize automation capabilities and public adoption rates, completely overlooking the deeper organizational impacts of long-term human-agent collaboration across integrated workflows.
- The proposed framework establishes six precise conditions for meaningful AI augmentation: ensuring durable net value, maintaining meaningful human control, guaranteeing robust accountability and recovery protocols, and fostering long-term human development through continuous learning, clear career pathways, and preserved job purpose.
- When applied to a case study of AI-mediated social surveys, the framework reveals how structured workflow integration can simultaneously capture efficiency gains while safeguarding ethical oversight and workforce growth, providing a practical evaluation template for future AI deployments.

## Context
As artificial intelligence rapidly evolves from isolated task automation to deeply integrated system design, researchers are increasingly recognizing that measuring success purely through productivity metrics or deployment scale misses critical societal and organizational impacts. This paper addresses a significant gap in the literature by shifting the analytical lens toward workflow-level augmentation, aligning with growing academic and industry efforts to develop sustainable AI integration strategies that prioritize human agency alongside technological capability.

## Implications
For practitioners and policymakers, this framework provides a structured methodology for designing AI systems that enhance rather than replace human roles, ensuring that automation initiatives support long-term workforce resilience and skill development. Organizations can leverage these six conditions to audit existing workflows, mitigate accountability risks, and align AI deployment with strategic career pathways. Ultimately, the research offers actionable guidance for building collaborative environments where technology amplifies human potential while preserving meaningful work in an increasingly automated economy.
