## Summary
The paper presents a low‑power wearable respiratory sensor that uses an abdominal force‑sensitive resistor and Bluetooth Low Energy to capture breathing patterns for stress detection. In a pilot study the system achieved 88 % accuracy in distinguishing stress‑induction phases from relaxation using time‑domain features.

## Key Takeaways
- The sensor records consistent amplitude changes and peak‑to‑peak timing across various breathing maneuvers even when the user moves lightly, indicating robustness to motion.
- A five‑phase stress protocol produces a model that reaches 88.0 % test accuracy, showing that extracted respiratory signals reliably separate stress from relaxation states.
- The integrated platform functions well in both stationary and mildly moving conditions without requiring analog amplification.

## Context
Continuous non‑invasive monitoring of physiological states is essential for affective computing applications that aim to provide real‑time feedback without intrusive devices. This work contributes to the field by demonstrating a simple, low‑cost sensor architecture that can be deployed at scale.

## Implications
The approach enables scalable integration of stress detection into consumer wearables and smart environments, supporting mental health apps and ambient monitoring systems. By reducing development complexity compared with optical or photoplethysmographic methods, it lowers barriers to entry for researchers and industry practitioners.
