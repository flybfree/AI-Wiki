## Summary
This study challenges the assumption that persona prompting in large language models achieves selective control, demonstrating that modifying a target user attribute often inadvertently shifts responses on unrelated, non-target attributes through a mechanism termed trait-conditioned completion. The authors identify a "neutrality gap" where explicitly declaring a neutral state for non-target attributes fails to prevent cross-attribute influence, revealing that successful persona following does not guarantee stable control over unspecified preferences. A three-state diagnostic framework highlights this disparity, showing that directional declarations can mask underlying biases that persist even when models report adherence to neutral constraints.
