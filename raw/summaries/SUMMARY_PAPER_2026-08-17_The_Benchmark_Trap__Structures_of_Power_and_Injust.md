## Summary
This paper argues that AI benchmarks are socio‑technical artefacts that reinforce existing power structures and can perpetuate systemic harms, using Iris Marion Young’s framework of oppression. It shows how standardised leaderboards privilege industry‑funded labs while marginalising alternative research pathways, thereby creating structural injustice.

## Key Takeaways
- Benchmarking normalises practices that are individually defensible yet collectively oppressive, aligning with Young’s four faces of oppression such as exploitation and marginalisation.
- The creation of leaderboards amplifies prestige, citations, and institutional influence, concentrating power among well‑funded entities at the expense of diverse contributors.
- These mechanisms generate structural injustice by embedding network effects that limit research trajectories toward epistemically robust yet socially beneficial outcomes.

## Context
AI evaluation is increasingly central to funding, hiring, and collaboration decisions in the field. As competition intensifies, benchmarks become gatekeepers that shape what gets researched and who benefits from visibility.

## Implications
For researchers, reliance on mainstream benchmarks may unintentionally steer work away from socially beneficial innovations. For industry and policymakers, recognizing benchmarking as a source of structural injustice is essential to fostering equitable AI development.
