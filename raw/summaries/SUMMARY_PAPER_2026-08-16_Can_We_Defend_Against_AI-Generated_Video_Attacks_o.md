## Summary
This paper introduces RA‑Bench, a benchmark that evaluates detectors and generators on real‑world crisis videos, revealing that current methods fail to generalize across diverse generation conditions and social contexts. The study shows that detectors are unreliable during dissemination and that generated videos can mislead audiences despite high realism.

## Key Takeaways
- Detector families show inconsistent performance across RA‑Bench instances, indicating poor generalization rather than a lack of capability.
- Generation quality, conditioning information, and sampling seeds affect detector behavior differently, while source‑level patterns remain stable regardless of seed variations.
- Human judgments and social dissemination amplify the impact of misleading videos, making detection more challenging as content spreads.

## Context
The rapid advancement of AI video generators creates new threats to public trust during crises such as wars or natural disasters. Existing benchmarks often rely on synthetic data that does not reflect real‑world distribution, limiting insights into practical detectability and societal impact.

## Implications
For researchers, the findings call for detectors that are robust to evolving generation techniques and can operate across varied social contexts. Practitioners must consider both technical reliability and human perception when designing detection systems for crisis information.
