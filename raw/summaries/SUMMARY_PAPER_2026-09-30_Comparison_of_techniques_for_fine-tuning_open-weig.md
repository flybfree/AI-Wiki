## Summary
This study investigates whether a fine-tuned open-weight model, Gemma-3-12B, can achieve performance parity with the proprietary GPT-4o model for extracting multi-label intracranial hemorrhage acuity from non-contrast head-CT radiology reports. Using a 2x2 experimental design that varies adaptation strategies and training data sources, the authors find that an instruction-tuned model trained on distilled real-world labels matches GPT-4o's macro-F1 score of 0.850 with statistical equivalence. The results underscore that high-quality distilled data is the primary driver of success, enabling open models to rival hosted solutions while offering significant advantages in privacy and cost.

## Key Takeaways
