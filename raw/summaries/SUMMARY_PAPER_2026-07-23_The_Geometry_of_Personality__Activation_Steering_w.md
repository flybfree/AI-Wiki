## Summary
This paper proposes using Jungian Cognitive Functions to represent personality as dynamic cognitive processes rather than static traits and demonstrates that activation steering can control these functions within a large language model. The framework integrates a Jungian evaluation protocol with a dataset of role‑playing narrations to explore controllability.

## Key Takeaways
- Personality information is concentrated in middle transformer layers, meaning the representation of cognitive functions emerges primarily around the central part of the network.
- Steering vectors exhibit structured geometric relationships consistent with distinctions between rational and irrational functions, indicating that control over different function types follows a non‑linear geometry.
- Effective multi-dimensional steering directions cannot be recovered as linear combinations of single-function directions, showing personality control requires independent vector components.

## Context
In AI research, personality modeling often relies on fixed trait dimensions like the Big Five, which limit interpretability and controllability. This work shifts focus to dynamic cognitive processes that can be steered in real time within transformer architectures.

## Implications
For practitioners, this framework offers a more nuanced way to align LLM outputs with specific personality traits without retraining models. It also opens avenues for ethical AI design where character consistency is crucial.
