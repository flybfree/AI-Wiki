## Summary
The paper evaluates Wiktionary as an ethically crowdsourced lexicon for English dialects by first analyzing its coverage across 12 national varieties and then testing it on geo‑referenced social media data. It finds that the lexicon matches or exceeds traditional dictionaries like the OED in regional and outer‑circle varieties, with high alignment demonstrated through statistical measures such as R = 0.883 for New Zealand English.

## Key Takeaways
- The lexical coverage of Wiktionary is comparable to the Oxford English Dictionary across many national English dialects, indicating a robust crowdsourced resource.
- High statistical alignment (R = 0.883) between Wiktionary and OED on word‑formation patterns in New Zealand English shows strong predictive power for dialect‑specific usage.
- The lexicon performs well when applied to geo‑referenced social media language, suggesting real‑world utility despite macro‑level challenges like language contact and register effects.

## Context
This work contributes to the growing interest in multilingual AI tools that require accurate representation of regional linguistic variation. Accurate dialect modeling is essential for natural language processing systems aiming to serve diverse user bases without bias or exclusion.

## Implications
For linguists, the study validates crowdsourced lexicons as viable alternatives to costly traditional dictionaries. Practitioners can leverage Wiktionary’s coverage to improve dialect‑aware models, reducing development costs and expanding accessibility in AI applications that require regional language support.
