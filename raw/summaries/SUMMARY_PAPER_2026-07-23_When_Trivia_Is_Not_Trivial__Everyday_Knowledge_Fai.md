## Summary  
This paper introduces TriviaRoomQA, a multilingual benchmark of 8,640 multiple‑choice questions across six European languages to test large language models on everyday cultural knowledge. Experiments show that while models excel at academic subjects like history and mathematics, they perform poorly on popular‑culture topics such as celebrities and movies, and their performance varies by language even for identical questions.

## Key Takeaways  
- Models are strong on knowledge‑intensive domains but weak on everyday pop‑culture facts.  
- Performance differences persist across languages, indicating non‑independent factual access.  
- Existing benchmarks overlook this gap in real‑world, multilingual trivia.

## Context  
The study highlights a limitation of current AI evaluation that focuses on academic or technical tasks while ignoring the diverse knowledge required for everyday interactions and multilingual settings. This gap affects trustworthy deployment of LLMs in global user experiences.

## Implications  
Developers must design benchmarks that capture real‑world cultural knowledge to ensure fair model performance across languages. Industry practices should incorporate these insights when evaluating or fine‑tuning models for diverse audiences.
