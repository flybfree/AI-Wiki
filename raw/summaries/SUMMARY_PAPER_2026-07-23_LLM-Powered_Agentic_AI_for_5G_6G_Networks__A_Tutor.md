## Summary
This paper presents a tutorial and survey that integrates large language model powered agentic AI with 5G/6G network architectures, protocols, and standardization efforts. It bridges the gap between autonomous AI control and next‑generation telecommunications by formalising control planes and mapping capabilities onto standardised interfaces.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper formalises the control, management, and AI‑native planes of both 5G and 6G to provide a common foundation for agentic systems.  
- It maps core agentic capabilities such as reasoning, planning, tool use, multi‑agent coordination, and evaluation onto specific network functions and standards.  
- It highlights open challenges in protocol integration, evaluation methodology, and alignment with emerging 6G initiatives.

## Context
The rapid advancement of large language models has enabled autonomous AI agents that can reason, plan, and interact with external tools, creating a new paradigm beyond rule‑based automation. Integrating these capabilities into telecommunications networks requires explicit mapping to existing control planes and standards, which this work addresses.

## Implications
This integration could accelerate the deployment of self‑optimising network slices, reduce latency through autonomous fault handling, and set a precedent for future AI‑driven telecom services. Practitioners will need new evaluation frameworks and collaborative standards bodies to realise these benefits safely.
