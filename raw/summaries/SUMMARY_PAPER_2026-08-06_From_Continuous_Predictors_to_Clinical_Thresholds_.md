## Summary
The paper investigates whether continuous predictors can be replaced by guideline‑aligned categorical encodings for 90‑day outcome prediction in acute ischaemic stroke. It finds that performance is preserved in two treatment cohorts but drops significantly in one, while global feature importance remains unchanged.

## Key Takeaways
- Continuous predictor accuracy matches fully categorized models in two cohorts, showing no loss of predictive power.
- One cohort experiences a significant drop in predictive accuracy after discretisation, highlighting potential subgroup differences.
- Feature importance rankings remain stable across both continuous and categorical models, indicating that guideline‑based categories preserve the prognostic hierarchy.

## Context
This work addresses a key challenge in AI for healthcare—making model outputs interpretable to clinicians. By aligning thresholds with clinical guidelines, researchers demonstrate that interpretability can be maintained without sacrificing predictive utility.

## Implications
The findings suggest that AI tools can adopt clinically driven categorical representations, supporting adoption by clinicians who rely on guideline‑based decision making. This could streamline integration of stroke outcome models into routine care pathways.
