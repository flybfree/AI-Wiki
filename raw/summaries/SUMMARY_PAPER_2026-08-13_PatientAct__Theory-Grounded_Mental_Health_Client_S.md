## Summary
The paper introduces PatientAct, a theory‑grounded framework that simulates mental health clients more realistically than current LLM simulators. It outperforms baselines in generating diverse profiles with high clinical plausibility and stronger resistance. The study uses four clinical scenarios to validate the approach.

## Key Takeaways
- Profiles integrate the 5Ps case formulation to add causal depth without fixing a therapy.
- The dynamic memory layer assigns trust thresholds, making some symptoms available early while others need sustained alliance.
- Responses are modeled for emotional reaction before generation, allowing varied resistance in quantity, content and style.

## Context
Current LLM therapist simulators often produce overly cooperative clients that lack realistic therapeutic dynamics. This limits their utility for training novices or evaluating models. These limitations affect both research and practice, highlighting a need for deeper simulation realism.

## Implications
The framework can improve AI‑driven mental health training by producing more authentic client interactions. Practitioners could integrate PatientAct‑style profiles into counseling curricula to better prepare students.
