## Summary  
This paper investigates whether the internal signal that distinguishes correct from incorrect code generated by large language models is a robust property of the model or an artifact of how it is extracted. The authors systematically vary extraction methods and examine program pairs that differ only in subtle faults, showing no single configuration yields consistent results. Their findings suggest that the internal correctness representation is fragile and not universally reliable.

## Key Takeaways  
- No single way to pull hidden states reliably predicts code correctness across different configurations.  
- Isolating a specific fault does not improve the signal’s quality or stability.  
- The model’s confidence signals remain poorly calibrated even when internal representations are examined.

## Context  
Understanding whether language models possess genuine internal reasoning about program logic is central to advancing safe AI deployment. This work contributes by probing the limits of self‑evaluation methods that avoid costly execution, offering a clearer view of what can be learned from hidden states without running code.

## Implications  
For developers relying on model confidence as a proxy for correctness, this research warns against overtrusting internal signals and highlights the need for complementary verification techniques. Practitioners should treat these findings as a cautionary note rather than a definitive benchmark for model reliability.
