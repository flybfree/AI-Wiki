## Summary
The paper investigates how prompt construction influences the detection of an LLM’s political stance beyond simple writing assistance tasks. It finds that fully synthetic prompts generated by LLMs are perceived as more realistic than templated ones and carry their intended intent more clearly, while templated prompts can mislead model evaluations under neutral framings.

## Key Takeaways
- Fully synthetic prompts produced by LLMs are judged no less realistic than real chat logs but significantly more realistic than human‑crafted templates.  
- Human annotators rank LLM‑generated prompts higher than templated ones, yet the distinction is sharper for the models themselves.  
- Templated prompts systematically overstate a model’s leaning on neutral topics because filler text masks the true stance.

## Context
Current political stance detection relies heavily on closed‑ended surveys that do not reflect natural human‑AI interactions and can be gamed. This study expands IssueBench to include open tasks, highlighting how prompt design affects ecological validity in measuring AI bias.

## Implications
Recognizing that synthetic prompts improve realism could lead to more honest assessments of LLM political alignment. Practitioners should adopt fully synthetic methods to avoid overestimating model neutrality and ensure fair evaluation across contested topics.
