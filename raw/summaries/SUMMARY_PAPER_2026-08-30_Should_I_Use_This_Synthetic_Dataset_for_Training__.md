## Summary
The paper tackles the problem of deciding whether augmenting a scarce real dataset with synthetic data improves model performance, while minimizing the amount of real test data needed. It proposes two testing approaches: one that directly compares mean loss differences and another that uses paired loss differences via an adaptive e-process sign-flip test (aeSFT). Experiments demonstrate that aeSFT identifies useful synthetic data using far fewer real samples than traditional methods.

## Key Takeaways
- The adaptive e-process sign-flip test provides anytime‑valid Type‑I error control without pre‑specifying a fixed test‑set size, allowing the procedure to stop early when evidence is sufficient.  
- aeSFT achieves comparable power to fixed‑sample sign‑flip testing and paired t‑tests while keeping the false‑positive rate below the target level, making it efficient for limited data budgets.  
- The method successfully identifies synthetic datasets that meaningfully augment real data in three domains: a synthetic classification task, a digital twin‑aided wireless packet scheduling scenario, and a radio‑map prediction problem.

## Context
In artificial intelligence research, obtaining large amounts of labeled real data is often impractical or costly. Researchers rely on synthetic datasets generated by simulations or digital twins to augment training, but the benefit is uncertain due to the simulation‑to‑reality gap. This work addresses that uncertainty with a principled statistical test that balances computational cost and statistical rigor.

## Implications
For practitioners, aeSFT offers a practical tool to evaluate whether synthetic data truly helps before committing expensive real‑world experiments, accelerating research cycles. In industry, it can reduce wasteful testing and improve the reliability of AI models deployed in safety‑critical systems such as wireless networks or predictive maintenance.
