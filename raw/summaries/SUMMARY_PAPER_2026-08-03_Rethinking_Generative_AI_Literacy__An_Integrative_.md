## Summary
The paper proposes a new framework called Responsible AI Literacy in Education (RAIL-Ed) to address the gap between rapid GenAI adoption and teacher readiness, integrating technical fluency with ethical commitment across K‑12 preparation.

## Key Takeaways
- RAIL-Ed defines six interdependent pillars—Technical Fluency, Critical Evaluation, Human-AI Collaboration, Contextual Awareness, Ethical Reasoning, Empowered Agency—each marked by three commitments; missing any pillar leads to pedagogical failure.
- The framework is developmental with a three‑level rubric (Emerging, Competent, Advanced) that maps each pillar’s growth across the K‑12 teacher continuum.
- It treats ethics, equity and agency as constitutive rather than supplementary, making them core to curriculum design.

## Context
Generative AI tools are proliferating in classrooms while existing AI literacy models predate large language models and treat ethical considerations as add‑ons; this gap creates a readiness lag that can widen educational inequities.

## Implications
RAIL-Ed offers educators a theoretically grounded roadmap for integrating GenAI responsibly, aligning with UNESCO and OECD AI competency standards; it can inform teacher training programs and policy to ensure equitable, agency‑preserving use of AI in education.
