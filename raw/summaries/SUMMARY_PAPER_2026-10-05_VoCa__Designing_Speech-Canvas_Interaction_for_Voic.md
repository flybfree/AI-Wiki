## Summary
This paper introduces VoCa, a voice-based conversational agent that integrates a visual canvas alongside spoken dialogue to support multi-turn interactions. The authors conducted a two-part formative study—an observational study of how pairs coordinate speech with boardwork, followed by a design workshop—to establish a design space for speech-canvas interaction, then deployed VoCa with 18 participants over five days to evaluate usability, interaction patterns, and user experiences. The findings reveal both promising opportunities for speech-canvas interaction in learning, work, and daily life, and significant challenges in coordinating what agents say and show in ways users can follow and influence.

## Key Takeaways
- The research is grounded in a formative two-part study: first, an observational study examining how human pairs naturally coordinate speech with boardwork (writing, sketching) to explain, organize, and develop content together, and second, a design workshop that translated these observations into a structured design space for speech-canvas interaction with voice agents. This methodology ensures the system design is informed by authentic human collaborative practices rather than purely technical assumptions.
- VoCa specifically coordinates speech with three canvas functions: visual object creation, annotation, and attention guidance. This tripartite design allows the agent to not only speak but also draw, label, and direct the user's visual focus during conversation, mirroring how humans use whiteboards or shared documents while talking. The five-day deployment with 18 participants provided empirical data on usability, patterns of use, and areas needing improvement.
- Participants identified concrete opportunities for speech-canvas interaction across learning, work, and daily life contexts, but also surfaced a core challenge: coordinating what agents say and show in ways that users can follow and influence. This highlights that the synchronization between verbal and visual channels remains a critical unsolved problem in voice-agent design.

## Context
Voice-based conversational agents have traditionally been limited to purely auditory interaction, constraining their ability to support complex reasoning, spatial explanation, or collaborative sense-making tasks that humans naturally handle through multimodal channels like whiteboards, diagrams, and shared documents. This paper addresses a gap in the human-computer interaction and conversational AI literature by treating the canvas not as a peripheral display but as an integral component of conversational design. It bridges research on human collaborative practices, interaction design, and voice agent development, contributing to the broader effort to make AI assistants more capable of supporting the rich, multimodal ways people actually think and communicate together.

## Implications
For practitioners building voice assistants, this work suggests that purely speech-based interaction is insufficient for tasks requiring explanation, organization, or collaborative development, and that integrating visual canvases into voice agents could unlock new interaction paradigms in education, professional collaboration, and everyday productivity. For the research community, the identified challenge of coordinating spoken and visual outputs in ways users can follow and influence points to a fundamental open problem in multimodal agent design that future systems must address through better attention management, turn-taking protocols, and user control mechanisms. The design space framework developed here provides a reusable foundation for subsequent work on speech-canvas systems.
