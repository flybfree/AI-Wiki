## Summary
This paper introduces an AI‑assisted workflow that extracts and structures hazardous operational scenarios from NASA’s Aviation Safety Reporting System (ASRS) to support aviation safety analysis. By generating candidate scenarios linked to plausible event sequences, the authors demonstrate how large language models can produce traceable hazard hypotheses that improve upon traditional functional assessments.

## Key Takeaways
- The approach creates structured hypothesis vectors of categorical factors and narrative descriptions for each adverse outcome, enabling systematic hazard exploration.
- Plausibility scores are derived from historical co‑occurrence data, providing a quantitative link to the most similar ASRS reports and enhancing traceability.
- Conditioning narrative generation on evolutionary abduction‑derived hypotheses reduces variability and increases correctness compared with unconditioned LLM outputs.

## Context
The integration of AI into aviation safety analysis reflects growing interest in leveraging natural language processing for extracting actionable insights from large corpora. This work situates such methods within the broader effort to automate hazard identification, moving beyond static checklists toward dynamic, data‑driven scenario generation.

## Implications
Practitioners can use these traceable scenarios to enrich operational safety reviews and inform design improvements in aircraft systems. The methodology offers a scalable way to augment human expertise with AI‑generated hypotheses, potentially lowering the risk of missed hazards in complex aviation environments.
