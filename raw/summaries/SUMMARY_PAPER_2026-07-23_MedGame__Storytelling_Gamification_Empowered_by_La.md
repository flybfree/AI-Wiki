## Summary
This paper introduces MedGame, a framework that turns static clinical cases into interactive storytelling games using large language models. The authors demonstrate that fine‑tuning open‑source LLMs on their benchmark improves performance and makes learners perceive the game more engaging than text‑only alternatives.

## Key Takeaways
- MedGame employs a dual‑engine design where a Medical Narrative Designer creates case‑grounded storylines with decision nodes and a Story Director orchestrates them into multimodal, dependency‑aware plans for an interactive platform.  
- The authors release MedGame Bench, a 5,000‑case dataset and evaluation protocol that enables systematic comparison of narrative generation and story direction across models.  
- Task‑specific fine‑tuning on this benchmark substantially boosts open‑source LLMs and narrows the performance gap with commercial models.

## Context
Current medical education systems rely heavily on isolated question answering or single‑turn feedback, which limits the depth of learning. Integrating storytelling into AI‑driven tools could provide richer, decision‑centered experiences that mimic real clinical reasoning. This work contributes to the growing effort to embed narrative structures within generative models for educational purposes.

## Implications
For educators and developers, MedGame offers a scalable way to embed interactive case studies into LLM pipelines without sacrificing performance. Industry adoption could lead to more immersive training modules that improve clinician decision‑making skills and patient engagement.
