## Summary
The paper proposes a capability-sustaining emotional dialogue (CSED) framework that treats emotional support as a longitudinal process aimed at preserving users' emotion regulation capacities across interactions. It finds that most existing system-building papers focus on short-term relief rather than sustained capability, and only a small fraction addresses long‑term outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- 95% of 60 system‑building papers pursue relief‑oriented goals while none evaluate capability or longitudinal outcomes, highlighting a gap in research.  
- In 300 ESConv supporter turns, capability‑relevant functions appear in 43.0%, compared with only 4.0% reappraisal and 6.7% self‑efficacy support, showing underuse of advanced functions.  
- Only 1 paper considers dependency, autonomy, or termination risk, indicating limited attention to safety and user agency.

## Context
Emotional dialogue research has traditionally divided into empathetic understanding and supportive conversation strategies, both focused on immediate relief. This work extends the paradigm by treating emotional support as a sustained capability that must be maintained over time, aligning with longitudinal AI evaluation practices.

## Implications
For practitioners, CSED offers a testable agenda that integrates repeated use, non‑use, transition, and termination into system design, training, and governance. The field should shift from short-term relief metrics to long-term capability assessment to ensure ethical, effective emotional AI interactions.
