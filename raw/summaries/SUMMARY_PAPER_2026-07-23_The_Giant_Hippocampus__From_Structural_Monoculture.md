## Summary
The paper argues that the current dominance of Transformer architectures reflects a structural mismatch between AI models and biological neural organization. It shows that distinct cognitive functions are implemented by different brain structures, unlike the uniform scaling of Transformers across tasks. The authors propose a Heterogeneous Topological Network as an alternative design.

## Key Takeaways
- Distinct cognitive functions are realized by qualitatively different neural structures rather than by rescaling one template.
- The Transformer’s success is due to the “Hardware Lottery” and not principled choice, with Mixture‑of‑Experts still using identical experts.
- A functional analog of the hippocampal formation is identified for Transformers, highlighting a mismatch when applying them to tasks like audition or executive gating.

## Context
AI researchers have long treated models as interchangeable, ignoring biological inspiration. This paper contrasts that view with empirical neuroanatomy, showing that structural diversity matters. The debate over architectural design versus empirical optimization is central.

## Implications
Practitioners should prioritize modularity and inductive bias in system design rather than chasing performance alone. Designing AI as a System of Systems could yield more efficient and task‑appropriate solutions, aligning with real cognitive principles.
