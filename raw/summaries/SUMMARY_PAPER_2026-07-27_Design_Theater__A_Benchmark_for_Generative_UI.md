## Summary
The paper introduces the concept of Design Theater and presents a benchmark to evaluate whether generative UI tools implement their stated design rationales. It finds that over 25% of user‑facing rationale is missing, with functional requirements failing at 34%, while tools recognize about half of UX principles.

## Key Takeaways
- The average interface omits more than a quarter of the design rationales provided by generative tools, indicating a significant gap between explanation and implementation.
- Functional requirement failures are especially high, reaching 34% unmet, showing that complex logic is less reliably reflected in the generated UI.
- Tools only capture roughly half of embedded UX principles on average, with four out of five tools implementing six percent or fewer functional principles.

## Context
Generative UI systems aim to replace manual design work by interpreting natural language prompts into visual interfaces and accompanying rationales. This paper addresses a critical gap: the lack of systematic verification that these rationales translate into usable designs, which is essential for trustworthy AI‑driven creative tools.

## Implications
For designers and developers, the findings warn against relying solely on generated rationales without validation. For researchers, the benchmark provides a foundation to compare models and guide improvements in alignment between reasoning and output.
