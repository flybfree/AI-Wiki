## Summary  
The paper investigates alienation experienced by early‑career critical AI researchers and shows how abstraction mechanisms create distance from lived experiences of harm. It proposes an interpretive framework that categorizes the preconditions, mechanisms, and harms of alienation and identifies affect abstraction as a resistible factor. The authors demonstrate that collective action can mitigate isolation when resisting exclusionary norms.

## Key Takeaways  
- Abstract logic in AI research often separates theoretical goals from lived experiences of harm, reinforcing epistemic injustice.  
- Alienation is experienced through tokenization of certain backgrounds while overlooking others, creating a sense of outsider status.  
- Affect abstraction can be resisted by staying attuned to emotional responses and organizing collective action.

## Context  
The study highlights a growing tension between abstract computational practices and the sociotechnical realities they affect. It situates alienation within broader community dynamics that marginalize diverse perspectives in AI research.

## Implications  
For researchers, the framework offers a hermeneutic tool to challenge exclusionary disciplinary norms. For industry and practitioners, recognizing abstraction’s social impact can foster more inclusive AI development practices.
