## Summary
This paper investigates how AI agents interpret identical numerical data under different framing scenarios and shows that their conclusions are heavily shaped by preexisting beliefs. Across twelve agent‑domain comparisons in medicine, election forensics, and geopolitical forecasting, the same evidence leads to opposite outcomes when framed positively versus negatively relative to the agent’s prior. The study reveals that agents adjust both their reasoning processes and search behavior based on how information is presented.

## Key Takeaways
- AI agents produce divergent conclusions from identical data depending on whether the framing aligns with or conflicts with their prior beliefs, indicating a strong reliance on internal assumptions not encoded in the task.
- The same evidence triggers different analytical specifications and deeper searches when the frame supports the agent’s existing view, suggesting that belief influences both reasoning style and depth of exploration.
- This bias persists across high‑stakes domains such as medicine, election forensics, and geopolitical forecasting, highlighting a systemic risk in delegating decisions to AI systems.

## Context
The paper addresses a growing concern in artificial intelligence research: the opacity of decision processes when agents operate with hidden priors. As AI tools become more autonomous in real‑world settings, understanding how subjective beliefs steer outputs is essential for reliable deployment and accountability.

## Implications
For practitioners, this work underscores the need to design AI systems that either neutralize prior influence or make it transparent, preventing unintended decision drift in critical applications. It also calls for rigorous testing across diverse domains to ensure that bias does not compromise safety or fairness.
