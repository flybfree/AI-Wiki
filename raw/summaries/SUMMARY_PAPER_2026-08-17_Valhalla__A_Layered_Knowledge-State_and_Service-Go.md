## Summary  
This paper introduces Valhalla, a layered knowledge‑state and service‑governance framework designed to support long‑term scientific collaboration. By replacing flat graph models with a five‑layer File‑Resource‑Entity‑Relationship‑Graph (FREG) architecture and a Router‑Contract‑Workflow microkernel, Valhalla creates stable semantic boundaries that enable seamless exchange and reorganization of knowledge across researchers. The authors validate the framework on an antibody‑design review task involving 26 papers, 80 entities, and 92 relations.

## Key Takeaways  
- Valhalla replaces flat node‑centric graphs with a layered FREG model that preserves provenance while allowing flexible knowledge views.  
- The Router‑Contract‑Workflow service architecture enforces microkernel constraints on how language models interact with the knowledge state, ensuring consistency and auditability.  
- Validation demonstrates successful integration of diverse scientific resources into a unified, reorganizable knowledge state.

## Context  
The rapid adoption of large language model agents in research creates demand for persistent, shareable knowledge structures that transcend individual workflows. Existing graph‑based systems often fail to provide stable semantic boundaries, limiting cross‑user collaboration and long‑term data integrity. Valhalla addresses these gaps by formalizing a layered representation and governance mechanism.

## Implications  
Valhalla offers researchers a scalable paradigm for organizing collaborative scientific knowledge, reducing fragmentation and improving reproducibility. For industry, it can streamline R&D pipelines by enabling consistent knowledge exchange across teams and tools. Practitioners will benefit from auditable, modular systems that support both short‑term tasks and long‑term institutional memory.
