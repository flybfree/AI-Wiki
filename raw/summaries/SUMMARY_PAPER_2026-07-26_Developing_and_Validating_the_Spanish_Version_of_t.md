## Summary
This study validates the Spanish version of the Large Language Model Dependency Scale (LLM-D12‑SP) for a culturally diverse sample. The scale measures two dimensions—Instrumental and Relationship dependency—and confirms its original factor structure through confirmatory factor analysis. Internal consistency is high, with Cronbach’s alpha values above 0.85 across both subscales. External validation links both dependencies to internet addiction and trust in LLMs while showing no significant link to need for cognition.

## Key Takeaways
- The LLM-D12‑SP retains its two-factor structure when applied to Spanish speakers, indicating cultural equivalence of the instrument.  
- High internal consistency is observed, with Cronbach’s alpha exceeding 0.85 for both Instrumental and Relationship subscales.  
- Both dependency dimensions are positively associated with internet addiction and perceived trustworthiness of LLMs, but not with need for cognition.

## Context
The rapid adoption of large language models in organizational settings has sparked interest in psychological impacts across different linguistic groups. This validation addresses a gap by providing psychometrically sound tools for Spanish‑speaking populations, supporting cross‑linguistic research on AI‑related dependency.

## Implications
Researchers can now use the LLM-D12‑SP to study how LLMs affect work‑related behavior in Spanish‑language contexts. Practitioners may leverage these findings to design interventions that mitigate excessive reliance while preserving functional benefits of AI assistance.
