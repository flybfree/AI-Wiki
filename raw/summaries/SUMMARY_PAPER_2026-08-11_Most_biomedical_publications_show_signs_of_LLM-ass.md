## Summary
The paper investigates how often large language models are used to generate biomedical articles and finds that by end of 2025, nearly nine out of ten papers contain an excess of LLM-associated vocabulary. It also reveals that LLMs are twice as likely to be employed in Discussion paragraphs than Methods paragraphs, with over half the Methods sections still showing LLM usage.

## Key Takeaways
- By the end of 2025, 89% of open‑access biomedical papers exhibit an excess of vocabulary typical of large language model generation. - The study shows that LLMs are used about twice as often in Discussion paragraphs (68%) compared to Methods paragraphs (32%). - Even within Methods sections, the overall prevalence of LLM usage exceeds 50%, indicating widespread adoption across all sections.

## Context
The rapid integration of large language models into academic writing raises concerns about authorship integrity and the need for transparent monitoring. This research provides a methodological framework that can be applied to other scholarly domains beyond medicine, offering a baseline for detecting AI‑generated content.

## Implications
These findings will inform policy makers and journal editors on how to detect and address LLM‑assisted manuscript production. Practitioners should consider implementing detection tools and clear guidelines to maintain trust in scientific literature.
