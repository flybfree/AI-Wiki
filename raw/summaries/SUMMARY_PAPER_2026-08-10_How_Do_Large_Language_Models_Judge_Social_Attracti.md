## Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models can reliably judge social attraction by comparing their ratings of persona profiles constructed from psychological constructs with human evaluations. The study finds that LLMs consistently rank profiles into three tiers, show high stability across runs, and agree in relative ordering, while humans also reproduce the tier structure but rate attractive and unattractive profiles more strongly than LLMs.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs exhibit strong stability and consistent three-tier ordering of socially attractive, mixed, and unattractive personas across multiple evaluations.  
- Gender presentation does not significantly affect LLM ratings or human judgments in the tested scenarios.  
- Human participants reproduce the tier structure but show stronger positive bias toward attractive profiles and negative bias toward unattractive ones compared to LLMs.

## Context
The work highlights a growing trend of delegating subjective social judgments to AI systems, raising questions about their reliability as proxies for human evaluation. By grounding persona ratings in psychological theory, the study provides empirical insight into how different models handle nuanced relational assessments.

## Implications
For industry practitioners, this research suggests that LLMs can serve as useful tools for initial screening but may require calibration to align with human preferences. It also underscores the need for transparent bias checks when deploying AI in social interaction contexts.
