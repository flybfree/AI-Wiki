## Summary
The paper describes a collaboration between DGT and EMT to localise the MMLU dataset into eleven European languages, creating an inclusive benchmark for evaluating large language models. It also provides master’s students with real‑world translation projects that involve revision, project management, coordination and methodological work.

## Key Takeaways
- The localisation effort expands the MMLU benchmark to eleven European languages, making it a more representative test set for multilingual LLMs across Europe.
- Students gain hands‑on experience in professional translation tasks such as adaptation, quality control and workflow planning, which mirrors industry practice.
- The project highlights methodological challenges like terminology alignment and administrative coordination that are often overlooked in automated evaluation.

## Context
The growth of large language models has driven the need for diverse linguistic benchmarks to assess performance across languages. This work contributes a European‑centric dataset that can be used to compare model capabilities on languages not well represented in existing corpora, supporting research on fairness and inclusivity in AI.

## Implications
For researchers, the localisation project offers a concrete resource to study how models handle regional linguistic nuances and cultural contexts. For industry practitioners, it demonstrates how translation tasks can serve as training grounds for multilingual coordination, offering a model for integrating evaluation with professional development.
