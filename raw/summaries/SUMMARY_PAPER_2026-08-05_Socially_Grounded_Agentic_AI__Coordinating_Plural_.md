## Summary
The paper argues that aligning AI requires recognizing multiple legitimate perspectives rather than a single set of values. It proposes using social theory to provide design resources for pluralistic alignment. The contribution is repositioning the problem as socially grounded coordination beyond output diversification.

## Key Takeaways
- Social theory offers conceptual tools to understand how roles, interaction, and power shape perspectives in AI systems.
- Pluralistic alignment must coordinate role activations, deliberative traces, aggregation rules, and feedback loops beyond just output diversity.
- The paper outlines a design space for agents that engage multiple perspectives through structured and accountable mechanisms.

## Context
AI deployment now spans varied social settings where values differ, making traditional alignment insufficient. This work highlights the need to integrate sociological insights into AI design to handle contested and distributed values.

## Implications
Practitioners can develop role‑based representations and context‑sensitive evaluation frameworks that reflect real‑world power structures. Implementing these could lead to more accountable and socially aware agents in industry.
