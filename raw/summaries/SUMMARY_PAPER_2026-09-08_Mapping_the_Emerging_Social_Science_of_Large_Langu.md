## Summary
The paper maps the emerging social science of large language models by analyzing 198 full-text papers and a field-scale corpus of 47,719 papers across five databases. It proposes three domains — LLM as Social Minds, LLM Societies, and LLM‑Human Interactions — each with subcategories covering reasoning, bias, creativity, education, etc., showing high stability in classification.

## Key Takeaways
- The taxonomy is stable under resampling (adjusted Rand index 0.952) indicating reliable grouping of papers.
- K‑means assignments align with author full‑text classifications for 77.8% of papers, validating the method’s internal consistency.
- LLM‑Human Interactions dominates domain mass at 78.0% but high‑citing venues concentrate on Social Minds and LLM Societies (66.4%), highlighting venue bias.

## Context
This work situates LLMs within broader AI research by providing a systematic, reproducible framework that links model behavior, agent interaction, and institutional context to social outcomes. It fills a gap where fragmented studies lack comparable synthesis across scales.

## Implications
Practitioners can use the taxonomy to guide policy, design interventions, or evaluate impact, ensuring attention is directed toward high‑impact areas. The framework also supports interdisciplinary dialogue between AI researchers, sociologists, and educators.
