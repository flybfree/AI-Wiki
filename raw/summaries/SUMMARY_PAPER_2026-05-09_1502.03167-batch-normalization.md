## Summary
The paper introduces batch normalization as a technique to stabilize deep neural network training by normalizing layer inputs across mini‑batches, thereby reducing internal covariate shift. This allows the use of higher learning rates and faster convergence compared to standard training methods.

## Key Takeaways
- Batch normalization computes mean and variance per feature across the batch and scales each input by a learnable affine transformation, which stabilizes gradients.
- It mitigates internal covariate shift caused by changing layer inputs during training, reducing the need for low learning rates.
- The method enables deeper networks with saturating nonlinearities to train effectively.

## Context
Deep neural networks often suffer from unstable training due to shifting input distributions across layers. This phenomenon hampers performance and limits model depth.

## Implications
By enabling faster convergence and higher learning rates, batch normalization has become a standard preprocessing step in modern deep learning pipelines, accelerating research and industry deployment of large models.
