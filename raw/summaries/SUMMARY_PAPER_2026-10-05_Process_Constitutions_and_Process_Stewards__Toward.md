## Summary
This paper argues that traditional Business Process Management (BPM), built on the assumption that organizations are primarily composed of human actors, is fundamentally inadequate for AI agent ecosystems that autonomously execute, coordinate, and adapt organizational work. The authors propose a constitutive shift in BPM toward governing autonomous agents through two novel concepts: the Process Constitution, a machine-interpretable framework defining admissible agent behavior, and the Process Steward, a governance agent that interprets and enforces those constraints. The paper's central thesis is that the next generation of BPM must prioritize organizational legibility—keeping agentic organizations accountable, contestable, and humanly understandable—rather than the traditional focus on process efficiency.

## Key Takeaways
- The foundational assumption of BPM—that organizations are populated primarily by human actors whose work can be made visible, governable, and improvable through process models—is depreciating as AI agent ecosystems increasingly operate with limited human direction, challenging not only BPM's methods but its core conception of what a process is.
- The Process Constitution is proposed as a machine-interpretable, value-laden framework that defines the space of admissible agent behavior, representing a shift from modeling human work to governing autonomous agents through explicit normative constraints rather than descriptive process models.
- The Process Steward is introduced as a governance agent tasked with interpreting and enforcing the Process Constitution, creating a layered governance architecture where one agent monitors and constrains the behavior of other agents within defined value boundaries.
- The central value proposition reframes BPM's purpose from efficiency optimization to organizational legibility, meaning the capacity to keep agentic organizations accountable, contestable, and humanly understandable to external stakeholders and human oversight bodies.

## Context
This paper sits at the intersection of Business Process Management, multi-agent systems, and AI governance research, addressing a critical gap as agentic AI systems move from experimental prototypes into production organizational deployments. The broader AI field is rapidly advancing toward autonomous agent ecosystems that coordinate complex workflows, yet governance frameworks have not kept pace with this architectural shift. By reframing BPM as a governance discipline for autonomous agents rather than a modeling discipline for human workflows, the authors position themselves within the growing discourse on AI accountability, alignment, and institutional legitimacy in the age of agentic systems.

## Implications
For BPM practitioners and enterprise architects, this work signals that existing process modeling tools, notation standards, and governance methodologies will require fundamental rethinking as AI agents become primary actors in organizational workflows. Industry stakeholders deploying agentic systems will need new governance infrastructures—Process Constitutions and Process Stewards—that can encode organizational values in machine-interpretable form and provide human-legible accountability mechanisms. The research agenda opened by this paper suggests that future BPM research must integrate normative AI alignment, multi-agent governance theory, and organizational sociology, moving the field from a technical optimization discipline toward a socio-technical governance discipline essential for trustworthy agentic organizations.
