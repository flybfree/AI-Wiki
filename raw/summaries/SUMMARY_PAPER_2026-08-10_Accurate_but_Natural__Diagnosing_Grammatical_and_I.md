## Summary
The paper introduces a layered LLM-correction pipeline that isolates structural errors from unnaturalness by generating literal corrections and idiomatic revisions for 3830 English writing samples collected from Japanese junior high school students, thereby quantifying both accuracy gaps and idiomatic gaps.

## Key Takeaways
- definite articles, third-person singular -s, and modals such as would, could exhibit significant accuracy difficulties.  
- the -ing forms and hypothetical modals like would show the largest idiomatic underuse relative to native norms.  
- simple present verbs, subject-verb-object patterns, and modal can demonstrate the most pronounced overuse in learner writing.

## Context
This study advances AI research in language assessment by providing a two-dimensional diagnostic that separates grammatical correctness from naturalness, moving beyond simplistic accuracy metrics. It highlights how machine models can capture nuanced preferences and avoidance strategies that affect intelligibility.

## Implications
For educators, the typology enables targeted feedback, allowing teachers to differentiate between corrective practice for errors and production-focused tasks for overused forms. The framework supports evidence-based interventions in EFL curricula, aligning AI tools with pedagogical goals.
