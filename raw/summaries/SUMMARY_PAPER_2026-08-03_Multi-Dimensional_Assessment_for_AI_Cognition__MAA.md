## Summary
The paper proposes the Multi-Dimensional Assessment for AI Cognition (MAAC) as a theoretical framework that shifts evaluation from outcome‑based benchmarks to process‑oriented cognitive analysis of text‑based AI systems. It defines nine cognitively motivated dimensions and demonstrates their coherence through five analytical approaches, offering a testable model for evaluating reasoning, memory integration, complexity handling, and hallucination control.

## Key Takeaways
- MAAC introduces nine dimensions—Cognitive Load, Tool Execution, Content Quality, Memory Integration, Complexity Handling, Hallucination Control, Knowledge Transfer, Processing Efficiency, and Process‑Outcome Alignment—to capture how AI systems think rather than just what they produce.  
- The framework is grounded in established cognitive science theories such as Marr’s tri‑level hypothesis, Baddeley’s working memory model, Sweller’s cognitive load theory, and unified cognition models, ensuring theoretical consistency.  
- MAAC provides a diagnostic illustration that maps performance gaps to specific dimensions, highlighting non‑redundant coverage and identifying gaps in current evaluation practices.

## Context
Current AI assessment relies heavily on accuracy metrics that ignore the underlying mental processes driving those results. This limitation hampers our understanding of why models fail or succeed under varying conditions, limiting both research progress and practical deployment decisions.

## Implications
For researchers, MAAC offers a structured way to design experiments that probe cognitive mechanisms, enriching theoretical knowledge. For industry practitioners, it enables more nuanced model selection by prioritizing systems that manage complexity and avoid hallucinations, ultimately leading to safer and more reliable AI applications.
