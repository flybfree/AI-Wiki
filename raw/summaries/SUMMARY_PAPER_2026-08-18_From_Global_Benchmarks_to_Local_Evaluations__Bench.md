## Summary
This paper introduces MÖVE, a holistic evaluation framework for German public sector organizations to assess large language models beyond traditional performance metrics. The study finds that energy consumption varies over 60‑fold across models and is not solely size‑dependent, provider transparency differs systematically, and European models do not outperform others in knowledge of German party positions.

## Key Takeaways
- Energy consumption estimates vary more than 60‑fold and cannot be explained by model size alone.  
- Information disclosure about model capabilities varies systematically across providers, indicating uneven transparency.  
- Models from Europe show no stronger understanding of German political party positions compared to other models.

## Context
The paper addresses a growing gap between global benchmarking practices that focus on English‑language tasks and the need for context‑specific evaluations in European public administration. It highlights how current benchmarks may mislead decision‑makers by ignoring non‑technical governance factors such as environmental impact and transparency.

## Implications
For public sector practitioners, this research suggests moving away from single‑metric rankings toward multi‑dimensional assessments that align with local sustainability and accountability goals. The findings encourage the development of regionally adapted evaluation standards to support responsible AI deployment.
