## Summary
This paper chronicles the rapid evolution of language models from BERT in 2018 to frontier agents capable of solving complex coding problems and writing software by mid‑2026. It highlights a dramatic improvement in problem‑resolution speed, a steep decline in cost per token, and the emergence of task‑specific models that outperform generic systems.

## Key Takeaways
- The ability to resolve real coding issues improved by nearly six times each year since late 2024, driven by larger, more efficient models such as OpenAI’s GPT‑5.6 Luna which delivers flagship capabilities for just $1–$6 per million tokens.  
- Top performance is now fragmented across specialized models: Claude Opus excels at frontend coding, Claude Fable dominates repository‑level tasks, and GPT‑5.6 Sol leads terminal operations, showing a shift from monolithic to modular architectures.  
- In a grade‑school math test the Qwen 2.5 model solved 58 of 100 basic problems with simple methods, while advanced sampling reached 79 correct answers, and a confidence ranking tool correctly identified 47 right answers in its top 50 choices.

## Context
The rapid progress described reflects broader trends in AI research where efficiency gains outpace raw model size, prompting a reevaluation of how capabilities are distributed across specialized agents rather than single large models. This shift influences training strategies, benchmark design, and the economic viability of deploying advanced language systems.

## Implications
For researchers, the cost‑capability curve’s collapse suggests that future breakthroughs may come from architectural innovations or data curation rather than sheer scale alone. Practitioners should consider task‑targeted models for deployment to maximize performance while minimizing expense, aligning with industry demands for affordable and reliable AI solutions.
