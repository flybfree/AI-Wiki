## Summary
This paper proposes a conceptual Agentic AI architecture specifically designed to assist the Brazilian Armed Forces in navigating the complexities of modern multi-domain environments, including land, air, sea, cyber, and electromagnetic spectrums. It identifies that current command-and-control (C2) systems are increasingly overwhelmed by data volume and velocity, necessitating a shift from reactive, isolated tools toward autonomous agents capable of planning, executing actions, and providing auditable decision support across various military domains.

## Key Takeaways
- Transitioning from Reactive to Agentic Systems: The paper argues that current AI applications in defense are largely limited to isolated tasks where human operators still perform the bulk of information synthesis; the proposed architecture seeks to create agents capable of proactive planning and autonomous tool execution to streamline the OODA decision cycle.
- Multi-Domain Decision Support: The framework targets four specific application fronts—decision support, situational analysis, feasibility studies, and countermeasure suggestions—to help commanders interpret complex data streams from diverse sources simultaneously across multiple domains.
- Security and Permission Frameworks: A critical component of the proposed architecture is the integration of robust security and permission safeguards to ensure that AI actions remain responsible and appropriate across different levels of command, including administrative, strategic, operational, and tactical contexts.

## Context
This research arrives at a time when the rapid expansion of data in warfare has outpaced human cognitive capacity, making "information overload" a primary threat to effective command-and-control. It contributes to the broader field of Agentic AI by applying these concepts to high-stakes, mission-critical environments where reliability and explainability are as important as raw processing power.

## Implications
For defense practitioners and researchers, this work suggests that the future of warfare will rely on "human-in-the-loop" systems where AI agents handle the heavy lifting of data synthesis and tactical planning. This shift requires a fundamental rethink of military doctrine, focusing on how to trust and audit autonomous agent decisions while maintaining human oversight in high-stakes environments.
