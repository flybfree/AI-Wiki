## Summary
This paper conducts a longitudinal analysis of seven engineering metrics from merged pull requests in two high‑velocity AI infrastructure repositories, vLLM (2023‑2026) and SGLang (2024‑2026). The study finds that AI‑assisted development has driven substantial increases in throughput, collaboration signals, and contributor diversity while human activity remains the dominant driver.

## Key Takeaways
- PR throughput rose 21× in vLLM and 17.9× in SGLang, yet bot‑authored PRs contributed less than 0.2% of this growth, indicating overwhelmingly human‑driven expansion.
- Median cycle times are short (1.04 days for vLLM, 0.62 days for SGLang) but the P90 values reach 16.8 and 14.3 days respectively, reflecting occasional long delays in AI‑assisted workflows.
- PR comment density increased 4.2× in vLLM and 3.8× in SGLang, with bot comments accounting for roughly 15–20% of the rise.

## Context
The rapid adoption of AI coding assistants and autonomous agentic development systems is reshaping open‑source software engineering at a pace that traditional metrics cannot capture. This longitudinal study provides empirical evidence of how these technologies influence team dynamics and pipeline efficiency, which is essential for understanding broader trends in AI‑augmented development.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that while AI can boost output volume, it also introduces variability in cycle times and requires careful monitoring of human collaboration signals. In biomedical AI agents and bioinformatics pipelines, this balance is critical to maintain reliability without sacrificing speed.
