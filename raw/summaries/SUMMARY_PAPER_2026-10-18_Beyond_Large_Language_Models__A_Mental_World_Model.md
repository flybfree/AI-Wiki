## Summary
This paper introduces the Mental World Model (MWM) framework and its implementation, the Multi-Branch Mental Evolution Architecture (MMEA), designed to explicitly construct subjective mental worlds, model interactions between cognition and mental states, and simulate continuous mental evolution shaped by historical experiences. The framework achieves 96.48% and 94.00% accuracy on DynToM and RecToM benchmarks respectively, surpassing leading large language models and specialized Theory of Mind methods, while also delivering substantial improvements in mental state prediction, long-sequence prediction, and implicit mental state inference.

## Key Takeaways
- The MMEA framework addresses a fundamental gap in existing AI approaches by moving beyond surface-level linguistic inference of mental states to explicitly constructing subjective worlds, modeling cognitive-psychological interactions, and incorporating dynamic evolution with historical dependencies, which current LLMs and Theory of Mind methods lack entirely.
- On established benchmarks, MMEA achieves 96.48% accuracy on DynToM and 94.00% on RecToM, outperforming leading LLMs and specialized ToM methods. Additionally, it demonstrates improvements of 28.7, 39.3, and 16.6 percentage points in mental state and behavior prediction, long-sequence prediction, and implicit mental state inference respectively, indicating broad and significant performance gains across multiple evaluation dimensions.
- The framework is explicitly designed for interpretability, enabling AI systems to infer individuals' underlying mental states from linguistic information and dynamically simulate their internal cognitive processes and evolution over time, providing a transparent and traceable mechanism for understanding subjective cognition rather than relying on opaque statistical pattern matching.

## Context
Current large language models and Theory of Mind methods operate primarily by inferring mental states from linguistic patterns without unified mechanisms for subjective world construction, cognitive-psychological interactions, dynamic evolution, or historical dependencies. This paper matters because it proposes a structural shift from reactive semantic processing toward proactive mental modeling, positioning mental state simulation as a first-class architectural concern rather than an emergent byproduct of language modeling. The work sits at the intersection of cognitive science, developmental psychology, and AI architecture design, addressing a recognized limitation in the path toward general intelligence.

## Implications
For AI practitioners and researchers, MWM and MMEA offer a concrete, interpretable foundation for building systems capable of causal reasoning about human behavior, personalized AI interactions, and sustained human-AI relationships that account for evolving mental states over time. For the broader field, this framework suggests that advancing toward artificial general intelligence may require explicit modeling of subjective cognition and its temporal evolution rather than scaling existing language models, potentially reshaping how researchers approach social cognition, developmental AI, and systems that must adapt to individuals whose internal states change across interactions.
