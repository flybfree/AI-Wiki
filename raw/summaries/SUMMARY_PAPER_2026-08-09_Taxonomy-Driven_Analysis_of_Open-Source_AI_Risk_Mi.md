## Summary
The paper presents a taxonomy-driven analysis of open-source AI risk mitigation tools, mapping their capabilities to the MIT AI Risk Mitigation and Response Taxonomy. It evaluates 21 tools across 32 subcategories using an LLM‑assisted pipeline and finds moderate inter‑rater reliability (Fleiss’ Kappa = 0.509). The analysis shows a skewed landscape where technical controls dominate while governance, legal and financial risks are largely unaddressed.

## Key Takeaways
- Tools cluster around technical and operational controls, leaving governance, legal and regulatory categories largely untouched.
- The mapping protocol achieved an F1 score of 75.5% after majority voting, indicating partial but useful coverage.
- Human oversight remains necessary for risk categories that lack automated tool support.

## Context
The rapid deployment of large language models in enterprises creates complex risk profiles that current tooling cannot fully capture. Existing tools are fragmented and designed for specific tasks without alignment to broader risk taxonomies, hindering scalable mitigation strategies.

## Implications
This framework provides a practical bridge between enterprise AI risk categories and open‑source mitigation capabilities, guiding practitioners toward layered approaches that combine technical controls with organizational processes. It highlights the need for governance mechanisms beyond tooling to address unmet risk areas.
