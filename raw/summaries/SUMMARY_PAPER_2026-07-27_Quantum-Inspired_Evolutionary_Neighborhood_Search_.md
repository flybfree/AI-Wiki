## Summary
The paper introduces a quantum‑inspired evolutionary neighborhood search algorithm to adjust arrival‑departure track allocations at railway stations affected by short‑term disturbances. Using GTFS timetable data from Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof, the method reduces total delay compared with CP‑SAT and demonstrates lower mean delays across ten random perturbation instances.

## Key Takeaways
- QEA‑NS achieves a 25.2 % reduction in total delay (388 min vs 519 min) while still satisfying all resource compatibility constraints.
- The algorithm lowers the average delay of delayed trains from 4.99 minutes to 3.73 minutes, indicating improved passenger experience despite longer computation time.
- Across ten random instances QEA‑NS consistently yields lower total delay than CP‑SAT, with mean delays of 390.5 min (σ = 35.945) versus 673.8 min (σ = 105.739).

## Context
This work advances AI‑driven optimization for real‑time railway resource management by integrating quantum‑inspired evolutionary principles with local search mechanisms, offering a practical alternative to traditional exact solvers that struggle with large‑scale stochastic constraints.

## Implications
The findings suggest that hybrid quantum‑AI approaches can deliver tangible operational benefits in logistics and transportation networks where timely recovery is critical. Practitioners may adopt QEA‑NS as a scalable solution for dynamic scheduling problems beyond rail, such as airport slot allocation or urban transit resource balancing.
