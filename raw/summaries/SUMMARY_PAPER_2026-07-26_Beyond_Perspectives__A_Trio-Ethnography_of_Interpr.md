## Summary
The paper reports a trio-ethnography that follows two computing educators with contrasting philosophies and an undergraduate student as they discuss AI‑supported learning over three conversations. The study shows that the student’s narratives expose learning processes invisible to classroom observation, prompting teachers to revise their assumptions about AI use, assessment, transparency, and instruction.

## Key Takeaways
- The student’s lived experiences revealed learning patterns that were not captured by teacher observations of AI‑assisted work.
- Educators shifted from viewing AI as a tool for compliance to seeing it as a catalyst for deeper conceptual engagement.
- The dialogue highlighted the need for transparent assessment criteria that accommodate generative AI involvement in programming tasks.

## Context
Generative AI is rapidly integrated into computer science curricula, yet existing evaluation frameworks often rely on visible student outputs. This gap creates misconceptions about how students actually engage with AI support and what it means for pedagogical goals.

## Implications
For educators the findings suggest a reflective practice that values narrative insight over behavioral metrics. Practitioners can adapt instruction to nurture authentic learning while maintaining rigorous assessment standards in an AI‑enhanced environment.
