## Summary
This paper proposes a conceptual framework that examines how Natural Language Processing can be integrated into Algeria’s mental healthcare system, highlighting four structural barriers and mapping existing NLP capabilities to each barrier. The analysis demonstrates that while global NLP research focuses on high‑income English contexts, North Africa remains largely absent from the literature despite its unique linguistic and cultural landscape.

## Key Takeaways
- The language‑of‑care gap persists because most mental health chatbots are built for English, leaving Algerian users without culturally appropriate digital support.  
- Geographic inequities in access mean that only urban centers have reliable internet, limiting the reach of NLP‑enabled services to those who can afford connectivity.  
- Stigma‑related barriers persist as patients may distrust AI tools or fear judgment, requiring community‑driven design and education.

## Context
The broader AI landscape shows a concentration of mental health NLP research in English‑speaking, high‑resource environments, creating a gap for multilingual, low‑resource settings like Algeria. This paper fills that gap by synthesizing global insights with local healthcare data to propose a practical model.

## Implications
For researchers and practitioners, the framework offers a roadmap prioritizing data resources, multilingual language technologies, evaluation tools, and regulatory capacity. It underscores the need for responsible AI deployment in underrepresented regions, encouraging ethical integration of NLP into mental health services worldwide.
