## Summary
This paper introduces AUDITS, a benchmark for studying image manipulation detection across four axes: domain‑shift, quality, type, and size. The authors evaluate existing methods on over 530 K images spanning user and news photos, showing that current approaches struggle under varied shifts.

## Key Takeaways
- AUDITS provides a large, diverse dataset enabling systematic analysis of manipulation detection across multiple visual axes, not just a single type or domain.  
- Experiments reveal significant performance drops when models encounter domain‑shifted inputs, highlighting robustness gaps in existing detectors.  
- The benchmark demonstrates that manipulation types and sizes affect detection difficulty, underscoring the need for multi‑factor evaluation.

## Context
The rapid rise of generative AI has made image editing tools more powerful yet harder to trust, raising concerns about misinformation spread. This work addresses the lack of a unified framework to assess how detectors perform under real‑world variations in manipulation artifacts.

## Implications
For researchers, AUDITS offers a roadmap for building robust, generalizable detection systems across diverse visual contexts. For industry practitioners, it signals that reliable image verification must account for multiple manipulation factors rather than focusing on isolated cases.
