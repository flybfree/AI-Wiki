## Summary
This paper proposes an evaluation framework to compare the design and implementation readiness of national AI policy portfolios across seven countries plus the EU. It scores each portfolio on whether it governs serious AI risks, ensures responsible institutions can act, covers the full AI lifecycle, protects affected people, and balances public benefit with innovation.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework treats a set of official instruments as a single policy portfolio rather than focusing on any single law or strategy.  
- It assesses both substantive coverage of serious risks and practical capacity for responsible implementation.  
- Each sub‑criterion is linked to specific provisions, producing a traceable comparison that highlights category differences.

## Context
National AI governance varies widely, yet few systematic tools exist to compare them objectively. This work fills that gap by offering a structured method to evaluate how comprehensive the policy portfolios are on paper and how feasible their implementation may be.

## Implications
Policymakers can use this framework to benchmark their approaches and identify gaps before launching new regulations. Practitioners in AI development will benefit from understanding which jurisdictions provide robust risk governance and responsible oversight.
