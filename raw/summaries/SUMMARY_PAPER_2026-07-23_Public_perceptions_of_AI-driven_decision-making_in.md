## Summary
This paper investigates how the public views automated decision‑making in healthcare, focusing on perceived helpfulness, riskiness, and fairness. Using structural equation modeling of a large survey sample (3,915 respondents), it finds that familiarity with AI, confidence in clinicians’ ability to spot AI output, and use of conversational health agents shape these perceptions.

## Key Takeaways
- Greater familiarity with different forms of AI and higher confidence that clinicians can distinguish AI‑generated from human content are linked to a stronger perception that ADM is helpful.  
- Use of conversational agents for health information reduces perceived risk, while reliance on traditional digital health sources increases perceived risk.  
- Perceived fairness is most strongly tied to confidence in clinician oversight, with modest positive links to AI familiarity and use of conversational agents.

## Context
AI tools are being embedded in clinical workflows to aid diagnosis and decision support, yet their acceptance hinges on public trust. Understanding the drivers of these perceptions helps researchers design interventions that align technology rollout with societal expectations.

## Implications
Healthcare providers should prioritize transparent communication about AI use and ensure clinicians can clearly identify algorithmic outputs. Policymakers need guidance on balancing technological benefits with public risk concerns to foster equitable adoption.
