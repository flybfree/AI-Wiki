## Summary
The paper questions whether general intelligence is truly valuable, showing that its evolutionary benefits are unproven and that it may create existential risks unique to intelligent species. It argues that the risk lies in the nature of general intelligence itself, not just misalignment.

## Key Takeaways
- Evolutionary value of general intelligence is uncertain; many long‑lived organisms lack it while humans face extinction threats.
- General intelligence may generate existential risks because only cognitively sophisticated beings create such threats.
- Engineering AGI carries a duty of caution due to ethical uncertainty, invoking deontological ethics and the precautionary principle.

## Context
In AI research, the quest for AGI is often driven by its presumed superiority over narrow systems; this paper shifts focus to evolutionary and existential dimensions that have been overlooked.

## Implications
For practitioners, the emphasis on alignment may be insufficient if the problem stems from intelligence itself. The field must reconsider safety protocols beyond misalignment, considering ethical responsibilities toward artificial consciousness.
