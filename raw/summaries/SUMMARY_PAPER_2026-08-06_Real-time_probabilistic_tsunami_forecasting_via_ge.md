## Summary
The paper introduces a probabilistic ensemble model using a conditional diffusion generative AI to forecast onshore tsunami inundation, replacing deterministic predictions with calibrated uncertainty estimates. Validated against the 2011 Tohoku earthquake, it captures decreasing post‑earthquake uncertainty and accurately predicts depth and extent of flooding.

## Key Takeaways
- The model replaces deterministic inundation boundaries with a probabilistic ensemble that quantifies how quickly uncertainty shrinks after an event.
- It maintains high accuracy in predicting both inundation depth and the spatial extent of affected land, even under near‑field conditions.
- The approach demonstrates that generative AI can transition tsunami forecasting from certainty to calibrated risk communication.

## Context
Current tsunami warnings focus on height rather than onshore flooding, limiting public preparedness. Deterministic ML models lack uncertainty quantification, which is essential for trustworthy early warning systems in high‑risk scenarios.

## Implications
This work provides a foundation for next‑generation early warning that can guide evacuation and resource allocation based on realistic risk probabilities. Practitioners may integrate such probabilistic forecasts into emergency planning tools to reduce false safety perceptions and improve community resilience.
