## Summary
The paper DuplexWorld introduces a comprehensive evaluation framework for voice agents across six real‑world domains, showing that current systems perform poorly on conversational and analytical tasks despite high speech naturalness. The best agents achieve only 49% pass@1, 65% turn‑taking accuracy, and a DNSMOS score of 3.38, indicating substantial room for improvement.

## Key Takeaways
- The evaluation spans 156 scenarios over 350+ hours, testing agentic tool calling against databases in banking, insurance, travel, healthcare, logistics, and Pathfinding, revealing that voice agents often fail to handle everyday conversational diversity.  
- Pass@1 of 0.490 shows low success in retrieving correct information from the database, indicating limited analytical capability beyond simple retrieval.  
- Turn‑taking accuracy of 0.653 highlights poor dialogue flow and turn management, a critical factor for natural interaction.

## Context
Voice agents are gaining traction as daily companions and customer service tools because speech is more accessible than typing. Existing benchmarks focus on narrow tasks like database queries, ignoring the broader conversational dynamics of real‑world interactions.

## Implications
Practitioners must redesign evaluation metrics to capture both agentic competence and natural dialogue flow. The findings urge industry adoption of holistic testing across diverse domains to ensure voice agents can reliably assist users beyond simple information retrieval.
