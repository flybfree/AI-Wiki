## Summary
This paper introduces CAi Copilot, an expert‑oriented agent that converts broad molecular‑design intent into traceable workflows across three linked layers: a Research Interface Layer, an Agent Reasoning Layer, and an Execution Substrate. Across 45 tasks the system achieves an overall outcome score of 84.59, outperforming the next‑best approach by 18.07 points. The results demonstrate that intent‑driven coordination can generate transparent, evidence‑based molecular design pipelines.

## Key Takeaways
- CAi Copilot integrates multiple AI functions—generation, optimization, property prediction, docking and synthesis planning—into a single adaptive workflow guided by interim results.  
- The three‑layer architecture enables the system to turn high‑level research goals into concrete execution plans while maintaining traceability of each decision to candidate evidence.  
- Benchmarks reveal that long‑horizon coordination remains a limitation, highlighting the need for better handling of multi‑step planning.

## Context
Current AI tools for molecular design operate in silos, requiring human experts to manually stitch together disparate outputs and assessments. This fragmentation hampers efficiency and reproducibility, making large‑scale drug discovery costly and time‑consuming. CAi Copilot addresses this by providing a unified agentic platform that aligns diverse computational capabilities under one coherent intent.

## Implications
For researchers, CAi Copilot offers a scalable way to automate iterative design cycles without sacrificing scientific rigor or traceability. Industry adoption could accelerate lead discovery, reduce experimental waste, and lower development timelines, ultimately bringing novel therapeutics to market faster.
