## Summary
The paper investigates whether strictly positive marginal values guarantee that an envy‑free allocation up to one good (EF1) and Pareto optimal (PO) can coexist for two agents. It finds a sharp threshold: any instance with at most seven goods admits such an allocation, while eight goods can break it even under submodular valuations.

## Key Takeaways
- At most seven strictly increasing integer‑valued goods always allow an EF1 and PO allocation without requiring submodularity.
- An explicit counterexample exists for eight goods where every EF1 allocation is Pareto dominated, showing the necessity of the threshold.
- The three‑agent NP‑hardness result by Chandramouleeswaran and Nimbhorkar (2026) holds even when zero marginals are limited to eight fixed agent‑good pairs involving a single agent.

## Context
This work extends classic list‑partition problems in social choice theory, where compatibility between fairness and efficiency is a central concern. By focusing on the exact number of goods that can be allocated fairly, it clarifies the limits of known algorithms for two agents.

## Implications
For practitioners designing resource distribution systems, knowing this threshold helps set realistic expectations about fairness guarantees. It also informs algorithmic research by highlighting where NP‑hardness emerges, guiding practical simplifications.
