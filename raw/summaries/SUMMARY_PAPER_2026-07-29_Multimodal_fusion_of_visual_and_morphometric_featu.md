## Summary
This study introduces a multimodal framework that combines convolutional neural network image analysis with osteometric measurements to classify avian bones. The integrated model reached 86% accuracy on the test set, showing reliable recognition of skeletal elements and promising results for family-level classification.

## Key Takeaways
- The fused visual‑morphometric architecture achieved 86% accuracy on bone-type classification tasks.
- Family-level classification yielded 51% top‑1 accuracy but 75% top‑3 accuracy, indicating that correct taxa often appear among the most probable predictions.
- A feature‑level multimodal design merges EfficientNet_V2_S visual features with standardized osteometric data for unified processing.

## Context
Artificial intelligence remains underutilized in zooarchaeology due to limited integration of traditional measurements. This paper provides a proof‑of‑concept that demonstrates how deep learning can complement classic osteometry, offering scalable solutions for large museum collections and research databases.

## Implications
The results establish a methodological baseline for future AI‑assisted identification tools, enabling practitioners to develop interpretable models that balance accuracy with archaeological relevance. As zooarchaeological datasets grow, such multimodal approaches may become essential for efficient taxonomic analysis.
