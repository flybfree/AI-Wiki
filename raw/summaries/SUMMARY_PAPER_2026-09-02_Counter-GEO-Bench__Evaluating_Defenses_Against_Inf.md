## Summary
The paper introduces Counter-GEO-Bench, a benchmark that tests defenses against misinformation generated by generative engine optimization attacks on large language models. It demonstrates that off‑the‑shelf safety guardrails are largely ineffective and that a lightweight baseline can cut attack success dramatically while preserving utility.

## Key Takeaways
- The benchmark pairs 247 human‑verified queries with GEO rewrites that either preserve or distort information, enabling precise measurement of defense performance across ASR, false positive rate, and answer quality. - Three widely used defenses (Granite Guardian, Llama Guard 3, NeMo Self‑Check Fact‑Checking) only reduce attack success by up to 5.7% relative, showing limited effectiveness, with Granite Guardian’s improvement not statistically significant. - A proposed lightweight baseline C‑GEO Guard reduces ASR by 47.6% relative while incurring near‑zero utility loss, proving that the threat can be mitigated without major trade‑offs.

## Context
Generative search engines increasingly rely on large language models to synthesize answers from web content, creating a vulnerability where adversarial rewrites of ordinary pages can produce deceptive information. Existing safety tools are designed for policy violations and often treat GEO‑generated content as harmless, leaving misinformation unchecked. This work fills the gap by providing controlled evaluation conditions that separate genuine threats from benign optimizations.

## Implications
Practitioners must recognize that current guardrails cannot reliably block GEO‑driven misinformation, prompting a shift toward lightweight, query‑specific defenses like C‑GEO Guard. The findings suggest that future AI systems should incorporate specialized checks for information distortion rather than relying solely on broad safety policies to maintain trust and accuracy in automated responses.
