## Summary
This paper introduces a platform‑adaptation model that treats governance interventions as dynamic shifts in an adaptive multi‑actor information system, measuring how creators, sellers, moderators and users respond to rule changes such as monetization thresholds or ranking algorithms. The authors evaluate the model on 72 public case studies across nine methods, achieving a mean adaptation quality of 0.836338, which outperforms all existing baselines in every comparison.

## Key Takeaways
- The model captures actor best‑response behavior, strategic gaming opportunities and moderation burden as separate dimensions that together explain platform governance outcomes.  
- Simulations on real‑world cases consistently exceed risk‑register, causal‑loop and generic critique benchmarks, demonstrating superior predictive accuracy for how actors will adapt to new rules.  
- The framework integrates external public‑case data with a formal information‑systems approach, showing why treating policy as static control leads to systematic underestimation of platform stability.

## Context
In the rapidly evolving AI ecosystem, digital platforms continuously reshape incentives through algorithmic governance, creating complex feedback loops among multiple stakeholders. Understanding these adaptive dynamics is crucial for designing policies that align with user behavior and long‑term system health, a challenge that current static risk assessments often fail to address.

## Implications
Practitioners can use this model to forecast the impact of rule changes before implementation, reducing unintended consequences such as market fragmentation or moderation overload. The framework also provides a benchmark for evaluating governance tools, encouraging research and industry collaboration on more responsive platform policies.
