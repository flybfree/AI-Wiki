## Summary
The paper investigates how multilingual and low‑resource NLP research is framed, arguing that many studies present community benefit as a given without solid evidence. It shows that researchers often emphasize resource creation over real structural change, using participatory language to mask limited impact.

## Key Takeaways
- Framing of low‑resource language work frequently claims decolonisation and equity but lacks empirical support for actual community outcomes.  
- The research narrative prioritises benchmarking and dataset building rather than demonstrating tangible improvements in users’ lives.  
- Community participation is highlighted as a key objective, yet the paper finds that most outputs remain abstract resources without clear accountability mechanisms.

## Context
This work matters because AI development often celebrates multilingual models while overlooking the lived realities of under‑represented speakers. The article adds a critical lens to evaluate whether these models truly serve linguistic minorities or merely reinforce existing power imbalances in the field.

## Implications
For researchers, reviewers and practitioners, this study calls for transparent justification of community impact and a shift from tokenistic inclusion to measurable benefits. Adopting such standards could steer AI innovation toward genuine equity rather than superficial representation.
