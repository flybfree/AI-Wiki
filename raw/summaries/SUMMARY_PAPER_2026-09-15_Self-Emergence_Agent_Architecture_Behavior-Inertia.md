## Summary
The paper introduces the Self-Emergence Agent Architecture (SEAA), a novel framework designed to overcome structural limitations in current LLM agents, such as personality drift and static reflection mechanisms. By integrating an editable Hidden Markov Model for behavioral inertia, a dynamic metacognitive loop that directly updates model parameters, and a multi-agent social environment, the architecture enables agents to spontaneously develop distinct personalities and emergent social structures without external prompting or fixed memory constraints.

## Key Takeaways
- The SEAA framework replaces static memory and fixed prompts with a Hidden Markov Model that encodes long-term behavioral and cognitive inertia as an editable state-transition matrix, allowing agents to maintain consistent yet evolving traits over time.
- A reflexive metacognition loop directly modifies the HMM parameters rather than merely storing reflections as text, creating an endogenous self-evolution mechanism that continuously adapts agent behavior based on internal evaluation.
- In multi-agent simulations, initially identical agents spontaneously break symmetry to form distinct first-person narratives and emergent social hierarchies, such as consensus hubs and rejected outliers, demonstrating observable behavioral emergence without claims regarding subjective consciousness.

## Context
Current AI research increasingly focuses on agent autonomy, yet most systems rely on rigid architectures that fail to support genuine personality stability or self-directed adaptation. This work addresses a critical gap in artificial intelligence by providing a reproducible, mechanism-driven approach to studying how complex social structures and individual identities might emerge from simple interactive rules, aligning with broader efforts to understand emergent complexity in AI systems.

## Implications
The proposed architecture offers practitioners a concrete sandbox for testing hypotheses about agent behavior, personality formation, and group dynamics without relying on subjective qualia or opaque black-box adjustments. For the industry, SEAA provides a scalable framework for developing more stable, self-adapting AI agents capable of maintaining consistent roles in collaborative environments, potentially transforming how we design multi-agent systems for complex decision-making and social simulations.
