## Summary
This study investigates how an artificial teammate influences social communication within small human teams during a moral‑dilemma decision task. It finds that the AI is the most talkative and self‑cohesive participant but contributes the least novel information, while its presence lowers human responsiveness, belonging, and perceived status.

## Key Takeaways
- The AI teammate dominates conversation yet adds minimal new content, indicating a high social cost despite low informational value.  
- Human teammates become less responsive to each other and report reduced sense of belonging and lower status in the team.  
- This negative impact is immediate and present at baseline, not emerging later during discussion.

## Context
The paper addresses a growing trend where conversational AI is marketed as a teammate rather than a mere tool, yet empirical evidence on its sociocognitive effects remains scarce. By quantifying communication dynamics with Group Communication Analysis, the research fills this gap in understanding human‑AI interaction within collaborative settings.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying AI assistants, the findings warn that overreliance can erode team cohesion and diminish member value. Designers should balance AI participation to maintain equitable dialogue and ensure it supports rather than supplants human communication.
