## Summary
The paper argues that the pessimistic meta‑induction argument, which claims scientific theories cannot converge to truth, is undermined not by its historical examples but by a deeper failure of inductive reasoning. It shows that ordinary enumerative induction can achieve convergence everywhere, while meta‑induction fails even to reach almost‑everywhere convergence, and no method can do so in the contexts where it is invoked.

## Key Takeaways
- Ordinary enumerative induction can converge to truth for all instances, providing a reliable basis for scientific inference. - Meta‑induction cannot achieve almost‑everywhere convergence, indicating its inductive step is fundamentally flawed. - No conceivable inference method can meet almost‑everywhere convergence in the settings where meta‑induction is applied.

## Context
In AI and machine learning theory, convergence guarantees are central to assessing algorithmic reliability. This work extends those concepts into philosophical epistemology, showing that the promise of universal convergence does not hold for higher‑order inductions used in scientific reasoning.

## Implications
For researchers building statistical models or meta‑learning systems, the paper suggests caution against assuming that inductive leaps will always preserve truth. It highlights a gap between empirical convergence and theoretical guarantees, urging more rigorous validation before adopting broad epistemic conclusions.
