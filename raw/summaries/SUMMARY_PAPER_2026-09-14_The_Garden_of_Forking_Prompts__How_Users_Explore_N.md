## Summary
This paper investigates how users iteratively revise and expand story generation prompts when interacting with large language models, revealing a dynamic, exploratory creative process that static benchmarks currently fail to capture. By analyzing over 275,000 real-world chatbot interactions, the authors introduce WildStories and WildEdits datasets alongside a structured framework of edit types and targets. Their findings demonstrate how users navigate narrative space through branching edits and propose automated permutations derived from this framework to improve future story generation evaluation.

## Key Takeaways
- Users dynamically iterate on story prompts by adjusting characters, redirecting plots, and swapping fictional universes, creating complex edit trees that reflect real-world creative exploration rather than one-shot requests.
- The authors construct WildStories (275,635 labeled prompts) and WildEdits (24,291 edit trees), providing a rich dataset to study how narrative preferences evolve across four directional operations: adding, removing, changing, and extending.
- Current story generation benchmarks rely on static prompts that miss iterative user behavior, but the proposed edit framework enables automated permutations that can significantly enhance how we evaluate and benchmark LLM
