## Summary
This study investigates how generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) reshapes course‑based undergraduate research experiences by providing personalized scaffolding, supporting team collaboration, and preserving human judgment in scientific inquiry. The authors report that GenAI enabled students to pursue self‑directed projects, facilitated distributed communication across expertise levels, and allowed them to retain responsibility for validating AI outputs.

## Key Takeaways
- Personalized, on‑demand scaffolding let students move beyond instructor expertise and create distinct self‑directed research topics.  
- GenAI integrated into team cognition helped novices communicate and coordinate without erasing specialization.  
- Expanded capabilities did not replace disciplinary judgment; students routinely validated or rejected AI contributions, maintaining intellectual responsibility.

## Context
Generative AI is rapidly becoming a tool for augmenting educational support in STEM fields, offering scalable, adaptive assistance that can respond to individual learner needs. This paper contributes by grounding those advances in a longitudinal qualitative analysis of a real CURE, highlighting the nuanced role AI plays within authentic research processes.

## Implications
For educators, GenAI can be leveraged as an extension of instructor expertise rather than a replacement, fostering deeper student engagement and scientific reasoning. Practitioners should design CUREs that embed AI responsibly to expand intellectual reach while safeguarding human judgment at the core of inquiry.
