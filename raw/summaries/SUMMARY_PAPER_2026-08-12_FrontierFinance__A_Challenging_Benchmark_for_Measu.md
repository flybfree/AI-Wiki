## Summary
The paper introduces FrontierFinance, a benchmark designed to test AI agents on complex investor workflows beyond simple data extraction. It evaluates 220 expert queries against 11,543 rubrics across six use cases using publicly available data. The results show that the tool harness influences performance more than model capabilities.

## Key Takeaways
- FrontierFinance spans the entire analyst journey, not just data extraction, and includes open-ended long-form answers that current models struggle with.
- Evaluation shows Samaya's in‑house system achieves 56.0% quality at roughly twice the cost of Claude Fable 5 (49.2%), highlighting harness impact over model alone.
- The best open-weight model Kimi K3 reaches 46.4%, matching proprietary performance while being four times cheaper, underscoring efficiency gains.

## Context
AI research increasingly focuses on narrow tasks like financial data parsing, but real‑world finance agents must handle multi‑step, nuanced queries that combine reasoning and domain knowledge. FrontierFinance addresses this gap by providing a holistic benchmark that mirrors the full workflow of professional investors.

## Implications
For practitioners, FrontierFinance offers a reliable metric to compare both model quality and operational efficiency in investment research tools. It also signals that infrastructure matters as much as algorithms, guiding investments toward robust harnesses rather than marginal model improvements alone.
