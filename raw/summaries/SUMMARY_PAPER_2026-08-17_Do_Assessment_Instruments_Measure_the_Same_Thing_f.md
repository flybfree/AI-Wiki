## Summary
This paper investigates whether assessment instruments originally designed for humans also reveal the same latent structure in responses generated by large language models. Using high‑school chemistry and a quantitative reasoning exam, it compares human answers with those from six multimodal LLMs through factor analysis techniques. The study finds systematic differences between human and LLM factor structures, suggesting that these assessments may not measure identical constructs for both groups.

## Key Takeaways
- Human response data and LLM responses exhibit distinct latent factor patterns, indicating the assessments do not capture the same underlying abilities in AI systems.
- Factor congruence is low across both educational contexts, revealing a lack of similarity in the structure that drives score interpretation.
- The observed divergence suggests that using human‑centric tests to validate LLMs’ capabilities may be invalid due to mismatched latent structures.

## Context
Large language models are increasingly evaluated with tools meant for human learners, raising questions about the transferability of those tools. This research highlights a gap between human psychometric assumptions and AI performance metrics, prompting a reevaluation of cross‑modal validation practices in AI research.

## Implications
Practitioners should avoid basing AI capability claims on human assessment scores without accounting for structural differences. The field must develop new evaluation frameworks that respect distinct latent representations in humans versus LLMs to ensure valid and reliable assessments.
