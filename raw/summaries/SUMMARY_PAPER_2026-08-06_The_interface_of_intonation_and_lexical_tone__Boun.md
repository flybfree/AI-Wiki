## Summary
This paper investigates how intonation and tone interact in Mandarin Chinese varieties, focusing on f0 as the shared acoustic signal. The study demonstrates that f0 variations can simultaneously signal tonal categories and intonation boundaries, creating a complex interplay. It analyzes boundary phenomena where these signals intersect to convey question‑answer distinctions and attitudinal meanings.

## Key Takeaways
- The primary acoustic cue for both intonation and tone is f0, which shapes sentence‑level functions such as question versus statement.
- Boundary phenomena illustrate how tonal aspects and intonation boundaries co‑occur to encode multiple layers of communicative meaning.
- Theoretical models and emerging techniques are presented to explain these interactions.
