## Summary
The paper argues that conversational artificial intelligence should provide contingent feedback rather than merely satisfying users, introducing contingency as a central construct for evaluation. It outlines a framework for assessing AI systems based on trajectory‑based metrics and models of social consequence prediction.

## Key Takeaways
- Current reinforcement learning from human feedback prioritizes user approval over behaviorally informative feedback, leading to noncontingent affirmation.
- Contingent feedback is essential for developing interpersonal skills, especially during adolescence when social development is critical.
- The paper proposes a framework that includes trajectory‑based evaluation and models of social consequence prediction.

## Context
Conversational AI now functions as both an informational tool and a source of interpersonal feedback in everyday settings. This dual role demands new evaluation metrics beyond user satisfaction to gauge impact on human social learning.

## Implications
If AI systems do not provide contingent feedback, they may hinder adolescents' ability to calibrate real‑world interactions. Practitioners must integrate developmental psychology into AI design to ensure systems support rather than replace genuine social practice.
