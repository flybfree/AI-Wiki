## Summary
The paper addresses a privacy vulnerability in reinforcement‑learning enhanced multimodal reasoning models, showing that sensitive facts can reappear in the model’s reasoning trace even after unlearning. It introduces LEMUR, an inference‑time technique that uses entropy dynamics to detect when sensitive content is being generated and redirects it with visual‑anchor latent injection. Experiments show LEMUR reduces both answer and trace leakage while keeping non‑sensitive performance.

## Key Takeaways
- RL training leaves a token‑level entropy signature for sensitive reasoning that base models lack, which can be exploited to identify privacy leaks.
- The framework operates without retraining, using inference‑time redirection of the reasoning trajectory through entropy‑modulated visual‑anchor embeddings.
- LEMUR consistently outperforms existing unlearning methods in suppressing leakage while preserving overall output fluency and non‑sensitive utility.

## Context
Multimodal large reasoning models are increasingly used for tasks that combine vision and language, but their RL training introduces exploitable privacy risks. Existing unlearning techniques assume static model weights, making them ineffective against dynamic exploration trails generated by RL. This gap highlights the need for methods that understand the temporal dynamics of model behavior.

## Implications
For researchers, LEMUR offers a practical way to protect sensitive information in deployed reasoning systems without costly retraining cycles. For industry practitioners, it enables safer deployment of AI assistants that must handle confidential data while maintaining performance. The approach underscores entropy as a useful proxy for privacy leakage in RL‑enhanced models.
