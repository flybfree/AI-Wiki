## Summary
The paper introduces a principal‑agent model to design liability for physicians who may rely on AI recommendations while their diagnostic quality is private. It finds that an optimal uniform liability level can achieve the first‑best outcome even with heterogeneous skill, especially when standard care or AI are reliable.

## Key Takeaways
- The optimal mechanism is a simple uniform liability for all physicians who deviate from standard care, achieving full‑information efficiency despite hidden quality differences.
- AI accuracy does not monotonically lower liability; instead it follows an inverted‑U pattern that depends on the uncertainty of standard treatment.
- Welfare loss from information asymmetry only occurs when both standard care is unreliable and AI is inaccurate, with losses rising then falling as AI improves.

## Context
Medical AI is reshaping clinical practice by offering decision support that can augment or replace physician judgment. Designing liability mechanisms must account for the hidden skill of clinicians to prevent unintended incentives.

## Implications
Policymakers should consider uniform liability as a baseline, recognizing its effectiveness under certain conditions. The findings highlight that AI performance alone does not dictate regulatory flexibility; context matters.
