## Summary
The paper investigates how a defendant’s courtroom statement influences simulated juror decisions in jury trials using large language models. It finds that emotional persuasion can backfire, background similarity strongly affects verdict severity, and ideological alignment also matters. The study uses JuryBench with 20 frontier LLMs generating 432K decisions.

## Key Takeaways
- Emotional persuasion in a defendant’s statement may be perceived as evidence of guilt or inconsistency, leading to harsher judgments.
- Jurors are more severe toward defendants from opposite backgrounds and lenient toward those sharing the same background, indicating strong affinity effects.
- Ideological profiles of simulated jurors significantly shape severity judgments, showing that personal beliefs drive verdicts.

## Context
This research addresses a gap in AI‑driven legal simulation by exploring how language models reproduce human jury dynamics. It highlights that LLMs can mirror biases and decision processes present in real trials, offering insight into the limits of automated reasoning.

## Implications
For developers, these findings suggest that LLM‑based juror simulations must account for emotional tone, demographic alignment, and ideological context to avoid misleading outcomes. Practitioners should evaluate such tools critically, recognizing they can amplify or mask biases rather than neutralize them.
