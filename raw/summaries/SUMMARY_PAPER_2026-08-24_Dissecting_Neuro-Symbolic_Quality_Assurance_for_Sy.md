## Summary
The paper investigates how neuro-symbolic quality assurance components affect the validity of synthetic oncology data generated by large language models. It finds that symbolic gating is essential for maintaining clinical correctness, while retrieval augmentation only helps one generator and does not improve overall corpus quality.

## Key Takeaways
- The symbolic gate alone rejects 148 out of 512 records due to schema failures, showing it is the primary filter.
- Ontology validation adds another 24 rejected records, confirming its role in ensuring medical terminology coverage.
- Retrieval augmentation improves one generator’s compliance by 12.5 points but has no effect on others and can cause output collapse.

## Context
Neuro-symbolic approaches aim to combine neural generation with symbolic constraints to produce reliable data, especially important where clinical errors are costly. This study isolates each gate to understand their independent impact on data quality in a high‑stakes domain.

## Implications
For AI researchers, the findings suggest that symbolic validation should be prioritized over retrieval tricks when building synthetic medical datasets. Practitioners must evaluate retrieval augmentation case‑by‑case and avoid reporting ontology density as a proxy for dataset validity.
