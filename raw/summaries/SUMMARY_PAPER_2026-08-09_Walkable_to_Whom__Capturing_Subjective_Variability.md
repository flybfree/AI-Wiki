## Summary
The paper aims to capture how walkability perception varies among individuals by linking sidewalk-view images with detailed rater attributes and building a user-conditioned deep learning model that outperforms image-only approaches. It demonstrates that the source of imagery influences ratings and that incorporating user-specific factors improves agreement with observed scores.

## Key Takeaways
- Sidewalk-view images receive significantly higher walkability ratings than matched street-view images, showing that visual perspective matters in perception surveys.
- The user-conditioned model raises rank agreement with observed ratings by 65% compared to an image-only baseline, indicating respondent characteristics add predictive value beyond image content.
- Existing studies aggregate individual judgments into uniform scores, which this work challenges by preserving variability across users.

## Context
Capturing subjective variability in urban perception is a growing challenge for AI that aims to model human experience. This research contributes to multimodal learning frameworks that integrate visual data with personal attributes, advancing the field toward more inclusive and realistic models.

## Implications
For urban planners and designers, this work suggests that evaluating walkability must consider both environment imagery and user diversity. Practitioners can use such models to create personalized feedback tools, improving accessibility assessments and informing inclusive city planning decisions.
