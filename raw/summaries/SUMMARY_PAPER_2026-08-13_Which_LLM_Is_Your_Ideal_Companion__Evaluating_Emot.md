## Summary
The paper introduces using adult attachment theory to evaluate LLMs' emotional companion capabilities, applying the Experiences in Close Relationships-Revised (ECR-R) scale to characterize attachment anxiety and avoidance. It presents an emotional companionship benchmark, ECBench, spanning four scenarios—emotional support, collaborative tasks, conflict resolution, and social guidance—across friendship and romantic relationships. The study evaluates 32 LLMs using 11 dialogue-quality metrics and three evaluation methods.

## Key Takeaways
- The study maps LLM behavior onto attachment anxiety and avoidance dimensions via the ECR-R scale.
- It proposes ECBench, a four-scenario benchmark measuring dialogue quality in emotional support, collaborative tasks, conflict resolution, and social guidance across friendship and romantic contexts.
- Prompting can influence how LLMs exhibit attachment tendencies within multi-turn interactions.

## Context
This work bridges psychological theory with AI evaluation, offering a nuanced framework beyond generic personality metrics for intimate contexts. It highlights the need to consider relational dynamics when deploying LLMs as companions in therapeutic or social settings.

## Implications
Practitioners and developers should use such frameworks to select models that align with desired attachment styles, improving user trust and safety in emotionally supportive applications. This could guide ethical deployment and personalization of LLM companionship services.
