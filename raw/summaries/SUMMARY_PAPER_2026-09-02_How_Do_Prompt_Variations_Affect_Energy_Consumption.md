## Summary
The paper investigates how two prompt characteristics—cognitive load and phrasing pattern—influence energy consumption during on‑device large language model inference. By profiling prefill and decode phases across diverse prompts, datasets, models, and devices, the authors find that cognitive load drives per‑token energy cost while phrasing patterns affect total token usage.

## Key Takeaways
- Cognitive load primarily increases the energy cost per token generated by the model.
- Phrasing pattern influences overall energy consumption through changes in the number of tokens produced.
- Model‑specific frontiers for energy efficiency are reshaped by prompt design, indicating a need for tailored prompts.

## Context
Mobile deployment of LLMs is constrained by limited battery life and power budgets. Understanding prompt effects on inference energy helps researchers optimize models for real‑world use without sacrificing performance.

## Implications
Practitioners can reduce device power draw by selecting low‑cognitive‑load prompts, which directly cuts per‑token energy. This insight supports the development of model‑aware design tools that guide users toward energy‑efficient prompt strategies.
