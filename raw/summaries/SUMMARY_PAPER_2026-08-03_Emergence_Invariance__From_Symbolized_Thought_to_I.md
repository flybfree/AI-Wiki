## Summary
The paper investigates how large language models' emergent capabilities arise from an incomplete symbolic substrate and whether scaling can compensate for missing distinctions. It formalizes a thesis linking symbolization to substructure and introduces the concept of emergence invariance, showing that interface refinement is essential beyond mere scale. Experiments demonstrate improved reasoning when distinct cognitive distinctions are available.

## Key Takeaways
- Scale reduces the compensation gap Cs while preserving a positive interface floor Rs* which remains constant across models.
- Under fixed input law one interface is universally no less informative than another exactly when its completed information σ-field refines the other.
- Total compensation occurs only when both the interface floor and asymptotic compensation gap vanish, yielding exact equivalence of interfaces.

## Context
This work situates LLM scaling within a philosophical framework that distinguishes between formalized thought and situated cognition. It connects emergent abilities such as grounding, memory, attention and reasoning control to a unified mathematical condition involving information refinement across task interfaces. The approach bridges AI theory with cognitive science by treating interface design as a critical variable.

## Implications
For practitioners the findings suggest that improving model performance may require explicit interface design rather than only larger models. Industry efforts should focus on providing richer distinctions in training data and API interactions to enable full compensation of missing cognitive structures. This could reshape research priorities toward interface refinement over pure scaling.
