## Summary
The paper introduces Persuasio, a multi‑agent dialogue platform grounded in formal argumentation theory that adjudicates logical winners during free‑text debates between humans and LLMs. It generated 192 UK political topic debates with human‑LLM interlocutors and evaluated them through automated adjudication and crowdsourced judgments across many instances. The main finding is a consistent decoupling: LLMs rank highly subjectively yet perform poorly under formal argumentation, where humans remain competitive.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs dominate subjective persuasiveness rankings despite their weaker performance in formal argumentation adjudication.
- Multi‑agent and retrieval‑augmented variants widen the divergence between human‑like persuasion and logical correctness.
- The systematic decoupling reveals that sounding persuasive does not guarantee winning a debate according to argumentation theory.

## Context
This work addresses a longstanding concern about LLM outputs in real‑world interactions where credibility matters. By measuring both subjective appeal and formal reasoning, Persuasio provides empirical evidence of the limitations of current language models in complex discursive settings.

## Implications
For researchers, the findings suggest that evaluating LLMs should include argumentation metrics beyond fluency. Practitioners may need to design safeguards that prevent persuasive but logically unsound responses from influencing decisions.
