## Summary
This paper investigates how social biases appear in different visual formats generated by text‑to‑image models, comparing photo generation with storyboard and comic outputs across six T2I systems. The authors find that bias rates rise significantly in narrative formats—by 9.6 percentage points for storyboards and 18.2 percentage points for comics compared to photos.

## Key Takeaways
- Proprietary T2I models produce on average 25.9% biased outputs in photo generation, but this rises to 35.5% in storyboard generation and 43.7% in comic generation.  
- Photo bias is often encoded through subtle visual cues, whereas storyboards and comics expose biases more explicitly via event sequencing, character positioning, narrative resolution, and accompanying text.  
- The study demonstrates that evaluating T2I systems only with photo outputs can underestimate the extent of bias when narrative visual formats are considered.

## Context
Current AI research often evaluates text‑to‑image models using static photographs, which may not capture how biases manifest in dynamic or sequential storytelling. As generative models become integral to creative and educational applications, understanding bias across varied visual contexts is essential for responsible deployment.

## Implications
Practitioners must adopt evaluation frameworks that include storyboard and comic generation to detect hidden biases early. Ignoring narrative formats could lead to products that appear fair on paper yet perpetuate inequities in real‑world user experiences.
