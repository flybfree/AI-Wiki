## Summary
This paper introduces AUV-Bench, a comprehensive evaluation framework designed to assess the aesthetic understanding and generation capabilities of multimodal foundation models in user interface design by integrating four interconnected tasks across 1,395 executable web interfaces. The study reveals that current models exhibit significant capability imbalances, particularly a "Judgment-Action Gap" where identifying aesthetic flaws does not reliably translate into successful remediation or high-quality text-to-UI generation, indicating a lack of fine-grained coherence required for professional design workflows.

## Key Takeaways
- AUV-Bench addresses the fragmentation in existing evaluations by providing a unified benchmark with four aligned tasks that share aesthetic principles and 660 controlled-degradation instances, enabling precise instance-level analysis to measure whether models possess shared aesthetic understanding or merely fragmented task-specific competence.
- Evaluation of 12 models exposes a critical Judgment-Action Gap; while models show moderate agreement with professionals on holistic scoring, exact diagnosis-chain success peaks at only 24.7%, and correct judgments frequently fail to result in successful repairs, demonstrating that identifying problems does not equate to acting on them effectively.
