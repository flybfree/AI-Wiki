## Summary
The paper introduces MARS-RA, a framework that treats credit assignment as a rank aggregation problem using contribution‑based pairwise comparisons among agents generated by multimodal models. By converting these relative contributions into scores, the method yields robust reward shaping even when feedback is noisy or agent participation changes. Experiments on various cooperative tasks show improved alignment with human expectations.

## Key Takeaways
- MARS-RA reformulates credit assignment as a rank aggregation problem using contribution‑based pairwise comparisons among agents generated by large multimodal models.
- The framework converts comparison results into contribution scores that serve as potential‑based rewards, providing robustness to noise and dynamic agent participation.
- Theoretical analysis demonstrates convergence properties, and Shapley values are proposed as an interpretive reference for the aggregated contributions.

## Context
Credit assignment remains a central difficulty in multi‑agent reinforcement learning because agents often receive delayed or sparse feedback. Traditional methods assume fixed participation and absolute reward estimates, which can break down under real‑world variability. This work addresses those limitations by leveraging relative judgments that are more stable across changing agent sets.

## Implications
The approach offers practitioners a principled way to design reward functions without relying on precise credit calculations. By using multimodal models to generate pairwise comparisons, it scales to large teams and adapts automatically as agents join or leave. This could accelerate the development of cooperative AI systems in robotics and multi‑agent simulations.
