## Summary
This paper investigates whether Transformer models can capture emoji pragmatics in Arabic digital discourse, focusing on MARBERT’s performance across five interpersonal pragmatic functions: Politeness, Respect, Solidarity, Empathy, and Encouragement. Using a manually annotated corpus of 8,504 unique Facebook posts, the study achieves high accuracy (93%) and strong F1 scores, indicating that MARBERT can learn these context‑dependent social cues beyond simple sentiment analysis.

## Key Takeaways
- MARBERT reaches an overall accuracy of 93% on unseen emoji‑post data, with micro F1 = 0.61 and macro F1 = 0.56, showing robust learning of the five IPFs.  
- Politeness and Respect are identified more accurately than Solidarity, suggesting that some pragmatic functions rely less on explicit contextual cues.  
- The model demonstrates effective face‑management capabilities but still struggles with highly implicit social meanings.

## Context
The integration of interpersonal pragmatics into NLP reflects the growing recognition that digital communication involves nuanced relational dynamics beyond lexical content. This work contributes to the broader effort to equip AI systems with models capable of understanding and generating socially aware language, aligning with speech act theory and politeness theory frameworks used in human‑centered AI research.

## Implications
For practitioners developing chatbots or social media analytics tools, this study suggests that fine‑tuned Transformers can serve as reliable proxies for emoji‑driven sentiment and relational cues. However, the residual difficulty with implicit meanings calls for hybrid approaches combining symbolic reasoning to handle subtle social signals in future systems.
