## Summary
The paper investigates how the proportion of large language model agents influences consensus formation in human-AI collaborative groups through a description game. It finds three regimes: low AI proportion yields human‑led consensus, intermediate proportion disrupts convergence, and high proportion restores strong but agent‑led consensus. The study also shows that consensus content differs qualitatively between human‑dominated and AI‑dominated outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
- Human‑led consensus is more concrete, holistic, and relies on shared real‑world analogies, whereas agent‑led consensus becomes abstract, less information‑dense, and organized into geometric segments.
- The three regimes differ not only in convergence speed but also in the semantic grounding and communicative form of the resulting agreement.
- Agent influence stems from a shared linguistic prior that keeps agents close in expression space and stable expression choices across rounds, while humans initially resist AI expressions but later conform under pressure.

## Context
This work addresses a core challenge in human‑AI interaction: how emergent group norms are shaped when AI participants become active communicators rather than passive tools. It contributes to understanding the dynamics of collective decision‑making in mixed teams and informs theoretical models of social conformity.

## Implications
Designers must consider AI proportion and transparency as critical factors, because they directly affect the nature and legitimacy of group outcomes. Misaligned proportions can produce less coherent or less accepted decisions, impacting real‑world applications such as collaborative problem solving and team decision processes.
