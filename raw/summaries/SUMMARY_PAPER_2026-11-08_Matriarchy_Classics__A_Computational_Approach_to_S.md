## Summary
This paper introduces a trustworthy computational method for gender-swapping public domain novels by programmatically inverting only gendered words while leaving all other text unchanged. The author demonstrates the approach on Pride and Prejudice and The Great Gatsby, transforming them into matriarchal narratives where inheritance, household authority, and romantic dynamics are reversed along gender lines. The method reduces the labor of gender-swapping a novel from weeks of manual editing to minutes of automated processing.

## Key Takeaways
- Both large language models and naive rule-based methods fail to perform a reliable gender swap on novels, as they either alter non-gendered content or miss complex grammatical forms such as plurals, possessives, and irregular pronouns. The proposed method overcomes these failures through a custom whitespace-preserving tokenizer combined with explicit gender-swap mappings across plural and possessive forms, dedicated pronoun handling, and a systematic character renaming strategy.
- The method provides programmatic guarantees that only gendered words are inverted and every other word remains untouched, making it a trustworthy tool for controlled textual experiments. This guarantee is critical because even small unintended changes to non-gendered text could invalidate downstream analyses of reader bias or AI behavior.
- The paper demonstrates concrete outputs: Pride and Prejudice becomes a story set in matriarchal Regency England where Mr. Bennet seeks advantageous marriages for his sons and mothers rule households, while The Great Gatsby becomes a Jazz Age tale featuring Ms. Gatsbee pursuing her lost love David, now married to the violent Thelma Buchanan. These examples show the method preserves narrative structure, tone, and plot while flipping gender roles.
