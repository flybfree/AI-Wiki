## Summary
This paper investigates the macroscopic dynamics of a closed-loop human-AI learning system by analyzing adaptive tutoring histories from 297,915 learners. It demonstrates that the collective trajectories exhibit reproducible basin-like flow and metastable kinetics, confirming an externally anchored leading-order effective field that links empirical behavior to learned states.

## Key Takeaways
- The study identifies semantic order variables defined before model fitting as predictors of population drift with high reliability (r = 0.946).  
- Shuffling the training order reverses the learned-plane flow, showing that order is essential for reproducing observed dynamics.  
- Model‑specific residual directions persist even when cross‑model fitting is omitted, indicating unique contributions beyond shared leading trends.

## Context
Understanding how human and AI learning processes interact in real time remains a central challenge in adaptive tutoring research. This work moves beyond isolated model comparisons to reveal underlying dynamical principles that govern collective learner behavior across large datasets.

## Implications
For practitioners developing closed-loop educational platforms, these findings suggest that preserving the original order of learning events can stabilize system performance and improve predictive accuracy. The identified effective field offers a conceptual framework for designing interventions that align with natural learning dynamics rather than imposing artificial constraints.
