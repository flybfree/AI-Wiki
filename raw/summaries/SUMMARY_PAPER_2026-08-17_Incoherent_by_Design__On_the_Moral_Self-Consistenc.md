## Summary
This paper investigates whether large language models can maintain moral consistency when faced with rephrased ethical scenarios, finding that inconsistency rates can reach 78% across responses. The study contrasts deontological, utilitarian, and virtue‑ethical framings to reveal epistemic instability in generative AI outputs.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs produce contradictory logical statements about the same moral principle when the scenario is reframed, with contradiction rates up to 78%.
- The inconsistency persists across multiple models such as GPT, Mistral, and Llama, indicating a systemic issue rather than model‑specific error.
- This epistemic instability undermines the reliability of AI‑mediated moral decision‑making and makes value alignment a shifting target.

## Context
Generative AIs are deployed in high‑stakes ethical domains where consistent reasoning is crucial. Existing research often assumes models adhere to a single normative framework, but this paper shows that framing variations can trigger divergent outputs, challenging the assumption of stable AI behavior.

## Implications
The findings warn developers and policymakers that current alignment strategies may be insufficient if models cannot preserve internal coherence. Addressing this incoherence is essential for trustworthy AI systems that influence human moral judgments.
