## Summary
The paper reviews 65 peer‑reviewed studies linking artificial intelligence to social‑emotional learning and finds a severe gap in policy discussion. It shows that most papers ignore governance issues, leaving AI‑SEL innovation without clear regulatory guidance. The authors argue that this deficit hampers evidence‑based decision making.

## Key Takeaways
- Nearly three quarters of the studies do not mention any policy implications at all.
- The research suggests academic incentives favor technical novelty over explicit engagement with governance and regulation.
- A “techno‑solutionist” trap emerges where potential is highlighted but implementation conditions are under‑specified.

## Context
Artificial intelligence is rapidly being embedded in educational tools aimed at fostering social‑emotional competencies. Yet scholarly work often treats policy as an afterthought, reflecting a broader trend of technical focus over societal impact.

## Implications
This gap threatens the practical integration of AI into SEL curricula and creates uncertainty for educators, developers, and policymakers alike. Addressing it will require redefining research priorities to embed governance from the outset, ensuring that innovations translate into actionable educational policies.
