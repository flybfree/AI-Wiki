## Summary
This paper introduces Ansatz, a mathematical research agent built around Continual Graph Memory, a graph-based, evolvable, cross-problem memory system designed to organize and reuse intermediate proof results across long-horizon mathematical search processes. The system explicitly represents facts, plans, counterexamples, and their relationships in a unified graph, enabling dependency-aware retrieval and evidence-sensitive curation. Ansatz successfully closed all ten First Proof Second Batch research tasks and independently solved the Jamison caterpillar conjecture and multiple Erdős Problems without human intervention.

## Key Takeaways
- Continual Graph Memory represents the entire proof search process as a unified graph containing facts, plans, and counterexamples connected by explicit relational edges, solving the critical challenge of organizing enormous volumes of intermediate results generated by parallel agent exploration over extended periods.
- The system employs three core mechanisms: dependency-aware retrieval that supplies precisely targeted local context, an evidence-sensitive curator that updates the research frontier and distills lessons from prior attempts, and scoped recall that surfaces earlier statements and negative findings for local re-proving rather than uncritical reuse, preventing agents from blindly reusing potentially flawed intermediate conclusions.
- Ansatz demonstrated closure on all ten First Proof Second Batch problems, proving its capacity to sustain and resume long-horizon mathematical search, while also producing complete solutions to the Jamison caterpillar conjecture and Erdős Problems 289, 348, and 488, and making partial progress on several additional open problems entirely without human intervention.

## Context
This work addresses a fundamental bottleneck in AI-driven mathematical research: frontier problems require massive parallel agent exploration generating enormous intermediate results, but existing agent harnesses lack principled mechanisms for organizing, retrieving, and reusing knowledge across long-horizon proof searches and across different problems. By building on the First Proof benchmark and extending to genuinely open conjectures, the paper bridges the gap between benchmark evaluation and real mathematical discovery, positioning graph-based memory as a critical infrastructure component for autonomous research agents.

## Implications
For the AI research community, Continual Graph Memory provides a reusable architectural pattern for any long-horizon multi-agent search task beyond mathematics, including software verification, scientific hypothesis generation, and automated theorem proving at industrial scale. For mathematicians and practitioners, the demonstrated ability to independently solve open Erdős Problems and conjectures signals that autonomous agents are approaching genuine research capability, potentially accelerating discovery timelines and reducing the human labor required for proof construction. The cross-problem memory reuse mechanism also suggests a path toward agents that compound knowledge over time rather than starting from scratch for each new problem.
