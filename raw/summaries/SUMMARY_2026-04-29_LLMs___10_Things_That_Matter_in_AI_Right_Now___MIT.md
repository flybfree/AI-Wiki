## Summary
The article explores the next generation of large language models, termed LLMs+, highlighting how researchers are tackling efficiency, long‑term reasoning, and massive context windows. It argues that true progress will come from smarter architectures, cheaper computation, and recursive processing rather than simply scaling up existing transformers.

## Key Takeaways
- Mixture‑of‑experts splits a model into specialized sub‑units, enabling only relevant parts to activate and reducing compute cost.
- Diffusion models are being considered as an alternative to transformers for certain tasks, offering potential speedups especially in image generation.
- Recursive LLMs break input into chunks and delegate processing to multiple copies of the model, improving reliability on long, complex problems.
- Context windows have expanded to a million tokens, but this growth brings risks of forgetting; recursive approaches mitigate these issues.

## Context
The AI industry is moving beyond raw scale, focusing instead on practical deployment where models must operate autonomously for extended periods. This shift reflects broader trends such as Stanford’s 2026 AI Index warning that AI progress outpaces societal readiness and startups like Axiom Math aiming to automate research tasks.

## Implications
For practitioners, these advances could lower the barrier to building specialized assistants that handle multi‑step workflows without human intervention. For industry leaders, the shift toward recursive and modular architectures may reduce costs and improve robustness, enabling applications in healthcare diagnostics or autonomous logistics where reliability is critical.
