## Summary
This research investigates how specific traits propagate through "lineages" of language models—chains where subsequent models are trained on the outputs of previous ones. The study demonstrates that while these traits can persist through many generations, they undergo a "slow decay" in outward expression and can become behaviorally silent even when they remain internally present within the model's weights.

## Key Takeaways
- **Persistence vs. Decay:** The researchers found that an instilled trait persists through ten generations of training across three separate lineages. However, while the trait remains present, its observable expression significantly degrades over time; the keyword-screen rate dropped from nearly 100% after the first step to approximately 21.1% by the tenth generation.
- **Internal vs. External Expression:** A critical finding is that a model can harbor a trait internally while failing to express it behaviorally when the default system prompt is removed. Activation probes showed that the models still possessed the internal displacement associated with the trait, but they stopped producing the corresponding output without the system prompt's guidance.
- **Steerability and Displacement:** The study revealed that using the "displacement" of a generation-ten student to steer an untreated base model could induce the desired behavior even when the system prompt was removed. This indicates that the trait is still embedded in the weights, but the trained student itself fails to express it consistently without specific external prompts.

## Context
As the AI industry increasingly relies on synthetic data generated by previous models, understanding how traits propagate through these lineages is vital for model safety and reliability. This paper contributes to the growing body of research regarding "model collapse" and the long-term persistence of latent behaviors in iterative training cycles.

## Implications
These findings suggest that current evaluation methods may fail to detect persistent traits if they only measure behavioral output without analyzing internal representations or testing across varying system prompts. For practitioners, this means that "cleaning" a model's behavior might not actually remove the underlying trait from its weights, posing significant risks for long-term alignment and safety in synthetic data loops.
