## Summary
The paper investigates how German political elites' rhetorical use of evidence versus intuition changes over time, especially after the far‑right AfD entered parliament. It analyzes a large corpus of tweets and parliamentary speeches from 2015 to 2025 using a distributed dictionary representation. The study finds that intuition‑based language is rising and right‑leaning actors have lower evidence minus intuition scores.

## Key Takeaways
- Intuition‑based rhetoric is rising across both social media and official speeches, indicating a shift away from data‑driven arguments.
- Right‑leaning politicians, especially the AfD, show the lowest Evidence Minus Intuition (EMI) values, suggesting they rely more on intuition than evidence.
- The parliamentary breakthrough of AfD in 2017 is linked to sharp declines in EMI across the chamber, while Twitter shows a gradual decline.

## Context
This research matters for AI because it demonstrates how large‑scale language models can detect shifts in epistemic stance within political discourse. It provides empirical validation that intuition‑driven messaging becomes dominant when populist actors gain visibility, highlighting challenges for automated fact‑checking systems.

## Implications
For policymakers and media analysts, the findings suggest that monitoring EMI scores could help identify emerging trends in public trust. Practitioners of AI should incorporate epistemic metrics into content analysis to anticipate changes in political communication strategies.
