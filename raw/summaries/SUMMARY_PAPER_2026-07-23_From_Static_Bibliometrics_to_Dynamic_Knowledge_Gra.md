## Summary  
The paper introduces a hybrid symbolic-first framework that merges static bibliometric indicators, dynamic knowledge graphs, and LLM‑assisted semantic augmentation to modernize STI analytics. It treats LLMs as provisional generators whose outputs are validated through structured checks before inclusion in the analytics layer.  

## Key Takeaways  
- The framework requires all candidate enrichments from LLMs to pass structural, evidentiary, comparative, and expert validation before becoming analytically admissible.  
- Validation is recorded at every stage to ensure full provenance and reproducibility of STI analytics results.  
- The approach enables trend detection, science‑to‑technology pathway mapping, and policy gap analysis while preserving epistemic discipline.  

## Context  
Current STI analytics rely on citation metrics that lag behind emerging knowledge and lack semantic depth. While static knowledge graphs capture relationships, they cannot adapt to new topics, and LLM pipelines risk hallucination without grounding. This work addresses both limitations by integrating verification mechanisms into the pipeline.  

## Implications  
Practitioners can obtain more timely, nuanced insights into scientific innovation without sacrificing rigor. The framework supports policy makers in identifying gaps and guiding investment, while researchers benefit from a transparent, auditable method for expanding bibliometric data with AI‑generated content.
