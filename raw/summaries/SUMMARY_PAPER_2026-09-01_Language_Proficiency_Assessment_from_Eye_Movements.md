## Summary
This paper evaluates eye‑movement based language proficiency assessment by extending the method from single sentences to naturalistic passage reading in English as a second language. The authors demonstrate that the approach yields valid, reliable scores across multiple proficiency measures and information‑seeking tasks, while also identifying and correcting scoring biases toward L1s linguistically close to English.

## Key Takeaways
- Eye movement scores are biased toward native languages that share linguistic features with English, which can affect test validity.  
- The proposed debiasing method successfully mitigates this bias, producing more equitable proficiency estimates.  
- Reliability analyses show eye‑movement based scores are more consistent than traditional language tests.

## Context
The paper contributes to the growing interest in using cognitive traces such as eye movements for AI‑driven educational assessment, offering a non‑invasive alternative to conventional linguistic tasks that can capture real‑time comprehension dynamics. This aligns with broader efforts to integrate multimodal data into machine learning models for personalized learning.

## Implications
For educators and language learners, the findings suggest that eye movement analysis could provide early, objective indicators of proficiency without requiring extensive written output. In industry settings, these scores may be integrated into adaptive tutoring platforms to tailor content difficulty in real time, enhancing user engagement and outcomes.
