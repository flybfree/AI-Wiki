## Summary
This paper investigates how social framing influences motor imitation in autistic versus neurotypical adults using 3D motion capture data. By applying Dynamic Time Warping to quantify movement consistency, the authors introduce a Social Context Sensitivity Index (SCSI) that measures variability modulation under solo versus socially‑framed duet conditions. A classifier based on these features achieved 79.2% balanced accuracy in distinguishing the two groups.

## Key Takeaways
- Neurotypical adults showed increased upper and lower limb movement variability when imitation was performed in a social duo context, whereas autistic adults maintained consistent motion across both solo and social settings.  
- The Social Context Sensitivity Index (SCSI) successfully captures this context‑dependent modulation of motor variability as a measurable biomarker.  
- Classification using SCSI features yielded 79.2% balanced accuracy, indicating the method’s potential for reliable diagnostic discrimination.

## Context
Understanding how social cues shape motor behavior is crucial for developing human‑centric AI systems that adapt to diverse user needs. This work bridges neurobiological findings with computational modeling, offering a framework that can inform sensorimotor interfaces and assistive technologies.

## Implications
The identified SCSI provides a concrete biomarker for autism‑related motor variability, enabling more accurate assessments in clinical and research settings. Practitioners can leverage this metric to design inclusive human‑machine systems that respond sensitively to social context, thereby improving user experience across diverse populations.
