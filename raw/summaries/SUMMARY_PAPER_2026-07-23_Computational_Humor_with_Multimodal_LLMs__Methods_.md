## Summary
The paper surveys multimodal humor understanding and generation focusing on memes cartoons comics. It organizes the literature into recognition interpretation reasoning and generation capabilities and discusses shift to large-model approaches based on alignment evidence grounding and controlled generation.

## Key Takeaways
- Recognition of visual humor relies heavily on non‑literal cues such as incongruity and cultural references which are hard for AI to capture.
- The field is moving from task‑specific fusion models toward large multimodal language models that employ evidence‑grounded reasoning and controlled output generation.
- Evaluation remains problematic because benchmarks often use shortcuts and limited cultural coverage leading to unreliable performance metrics.

## Context
Multimodal humor is a niche but growing area within AI research where understanding human‑like joke mechanisms such as surprise and shared knowledge is essential. This paper situates the work within broader MLLM surveys highlighting its contribution to capability hierarchy design.

## Implications
For practitioners this means building models that can handle nuanced, context‑dependent humor rather than relying on literal descriptions. Industry adoption will require robust evaluation frameworks and careful handling of cultural and safety issues.
