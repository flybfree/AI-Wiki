## Summary
The paper explores how to simplify a single-equation interpretable classifier into more readable forms for financial decision making. It demonstrates that pruning can be done with minimal loss of predictive performance and that simpler rules remain effective classifiers. Human assessments show improved readability, though rule preferences differ by professional background.

## Key Takeaways
- Pruning an equation to a monomial or if‑then rule typically causes only minor changes in accuracy because the original model is already interpretable.
- The fidelity loss can exceed predictive performance loss, allowing simpler rules that still rank outcomes correctly.
- Human readers find simplified representations easier to understand, yet professionals vary in their preference for equations versus rules.

## Context
Interpretable AI models are essential in regulated sectors like finance where regulators demand explainability. Traditional interpretable methods often produce complex formulas that hinder adoption. This work shows a systematic path from equation to rule that respects both regulatory needs and practical usability.

## Implications
Simpler, readable rules can be deployed faster without sacrificing compliance, accelerating model rollout. Practitioners should balance simplification with performance trade‑offs, using the derived bounds to anticipate impact. The study offers a toolkit for creating finance‑friendly AI explanations that are both trustworthy and accessible.
