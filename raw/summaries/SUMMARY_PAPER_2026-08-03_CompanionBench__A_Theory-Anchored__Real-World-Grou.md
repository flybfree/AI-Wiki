## Summary  
The paper introduces CompanionBench, a bilingual interactive benchmark that uses de‑identified real‑world data to evaluate AI emotional companions across ten capabilities derived from psychology theories; it combines a subjective rubric and a deterministic deeper disclosure metric with reproducible rankings in both Chinese and English.

## Key Takeaways  
- CompanionBench grounds scenarios and simulators in de‑identified real‑world data, eliminating handcrafted prompts and mitigating same‑family favoritism through a cross‑family panel.  
- It measures ten capabilities such as holding ambiguity, selfobject responsiveness, positive resonance and calibrated challenge using both a subjective rubric and a deterministic deeper disclosure metric.  
- The benchmark’s theory‑driven structure reveals capability differences that aggregate scores hide, showing emotion regulation and calibrated challenge are common weaknesses.

## Context  
The rapid deployment of LLM companions in personal settings demands rigorous evaluation beyond simple empathy scores; existing benchmarks lack real‑world grounding and ignore judge biases, limiting trustworthy assessments. This work addresses those gaps by integrating theory with authentic data.

## Implications  
CompanionBench provides a reproducible framework for industry practitioners to benchmark relational competence, guiding design toward substantive support rather than surface warmth; the open release of parallel pairs enables community validation and further research into AI emotional companionship.
