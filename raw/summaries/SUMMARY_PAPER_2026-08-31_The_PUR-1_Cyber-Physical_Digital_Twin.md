## Summary
This paper introduces the Purdue University Reactor One Digital Twin (PUR‑1 DT), a cyber‑physical model that merges high‑fidelity physics‑based and data‑driven components to provide real‑time diagnostics, forecasting, predictive control, and actionable recommendations for nuclear reactors. The authors demonstrate synchronized state estimation and short‑term predictions across a full operational cycle while maintaining sub‑cycle latency through two‑way communication.

## Key Takeaways
- The digital twin integrates neutronics, thermal‑hydraulic, and point kinetics models into an AI‑enhanced stack that delivers closed‑loop explainable diagnostics.  
- Real‑time synchronization is achieved with a time constant shorter than the reactor’s operational cycle, enabling immediate feedback to control systems.  
- Benchmarking experiments confirm high accuracy in state estimation and forecasting while keeping latency within acceptable limits.

## Context
The integration of AI with cyber‑physical twins represents a frontier in industrial automation, where explainable models must operate alongside traditional physics‑based simulations without compromising safety or responsiveness. This work exemplifies how machine learning can augment deterministic frameworks to handle complex, high‑dimensional data streams in real time.

## Implications
For nuclear operators, the PUR‑1 DT offers a pathway to enhance decision support and reduce downtime through proactive fault detection. Industry practitioners can adopt similar twin architectures across other critical infrastructure, improving reliability and operational agility.
