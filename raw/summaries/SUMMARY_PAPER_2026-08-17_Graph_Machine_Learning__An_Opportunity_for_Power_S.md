## Summary
The paper surveys nearly eight hundred papers linking graph machine learning to power systems, highlighting GML as a fast alternative to model‑based methods for real‑time grid operation. It identifies open challenges such as lack of standardized benchmarks and the need for interpretable models in safety‑critical settings.

## Key Takeaways
- The survey shows that GML can provide scalable, topology‑aware approximations that complement classical solvers while meeting operational speed requirements.
- Real‑world deployment remains limited because most studies rely on synthetic or small datasets, making reproducibility difficult.
- A structured catalog of ML‑ready benchmark data is proposed to guide future dataset development and improve scientific credibility.

## Context
Graph machine learning has become a focal point in AI research for incorporating structural information into models. In power systems, where physical constraints and multi‑scale dynamics intersect, GML offers a unique opportunity to model topological dependencies that classical solvers cannot capture efficiently.

## Implications
Practitioners can leverage GML to accelerate grid forecasting and control without sacrificing accuracy. The call for open datasets and benchmarks will foster reproducibility and accelerate innovation across the field.
