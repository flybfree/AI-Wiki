## Summary
The paper argues that federated learning does not alone solve extractive AI problems because who controls data storage, model circulation, and learning determines outcomes. It shows that creator governance is established at storage and circulation but limited to consent at learning, leaving the resulting model under external control.

## Key Takeaways
- Creator governance is built into how data are stored and shared, yet it ends when contributors give consent for training; they have little influence over the final federated model or its deployment. - The paper maps technical challenges of open federated models onto human questions about agency and stewardship, highlighting a gap between consent mechanisms and real power over outputs. - It proposes four design principles—governing the model not just the dataset, making contribution terms legible at the moment of sharing, designing for refusal as a first‑class state, and deciding stewardship openly—to create a creative data commons.

## Context
Federated learning is promoted as a privacy‑preserving alternative to centralized training, but its governance remains opaque. This work situates these technical practices within broader ethical debates about who owns AI outputs and how community values are encoded in system design.

## Implications
For practitioners, the paper calls for transparent consent frameworks that extend beyond data sharing to model stewardship. Industry adoption of federated models will benefit from policies that embed creator agency, ensuring that governance is not a one‑time checkbox but an ongoing practice.
