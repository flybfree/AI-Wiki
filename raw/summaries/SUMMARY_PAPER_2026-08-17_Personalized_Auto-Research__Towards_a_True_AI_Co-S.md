## Summary
This paper introduces personalized auto‑research, a framework that conditions every stage of AI‑driven scientific work on the unique context of an individual researcher. The authors argue that personalization is essential for true co‑scientist behavior and present a graph‑grounded approach that spans hypothesis generation to review.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper defines personalized auto‑research as conditioning each step—retrieval, hypothesis search, experimentation, writing, and review—on a researcher representation, emphasizing that novelty, value, and feasibility are context‑specific.  
- It highlights a one‑size‑fits‑all failure mode where different researchers given the same goal produce nearly identical research, erasing tacit knowledge that fuels innovation.  
- The framework is built around three components: graph‑grounded researcher representations, personalization across the full pipeline, and evaluation grounded in the individual’s context.

## Context
Current AI co‑scientist systems optimize for generic metrics such as novelty or reviewer score, ignoring how each scientist’s background shapes what counts as valuable work. This gap limits the practical impact of these tools, which remain useful only as generic instruments rather than true collaborators.

## Implications
Personalized auto‑research could transform research workflows by aligning AI outputs with scientists’ expertise and community norms, fostering more effective collaboration. For industry and academia, this means AI can become a strategic asset that enhances productivity and breakthrough potential.
