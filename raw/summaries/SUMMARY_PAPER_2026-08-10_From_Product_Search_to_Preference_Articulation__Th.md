## Summary
The paper investigates how generative AI changes digital commerce from browsing to agentic search, comparing manual inspection with AI‑mediated preference refinement. It finds that manual search collapses at high complexity while agentic search sustains positive revenue. The study also shows a revenue advantage for platforms before consumers adopt the richer process.

## Key Takeaways
- Manual search stops when product complexity exceeds a finite threshold because users cannot inspect more items, leading to zero platform revenue.
- Agentic search remains profitable as refinement depth increases; mismatch stays below the no‑search benchmark but effort and mismatch may rise.
- Platforms rank regimes by conversion revenue while consumers incur search cost, causing an adoption lag where manual inspection is cheaper than agentic refinement.

## Context
Generative AI enables agents to interpret vague consumer preferences through noisy representations. This shift moves digital commerce from static browsing to dynamic preference articulation, a trend that challenges traditional e‑commerce models built on product catalogs.

## Implications
For practitioners, the paper suggests designing platforms that balance refinement depth with user attention budgets to maintain revenue and fidelity. It also highlights the need for transparent cost structures so consumers can rationally choose between manual and agentic search.
