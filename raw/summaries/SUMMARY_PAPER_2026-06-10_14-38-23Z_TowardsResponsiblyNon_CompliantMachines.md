## Summary
The paper proposes a framework for engineering autonomous intelligent agents that can responsibly refuse user requests, outlining forms of non-compliance and related issues. It identifies task refusal justification, override pathways, and risk tracking as core components.

## Key Takeaways
- Responsible non‑compliance is framed as a structured set of justifications that allow machines to decline tasks without violating ethical or safety principles.
- The framework includes defined pathways for users to override the machine’s decision, ensuring accountability while maintaining control.
- It emphasizes continuous monitoring of security risks and liability transfers to protect both developers and end‑users.

## Context
Autonomous AI agents are increasingly deployed in high‑stakes environments where strict adherence to user instructions is essential. This work addresses a gap by exploring how machines can ethically and safely choose not to comply, which is crucial for safety‑critical systems.

## Implications
The proposed model could guide developers in building trustworthy AI that balances autonomy with responsibility. It offers practical guidance on risk management and liability allocation, influencing industry standards and regulatory approaches to non‑compliant AI behavior.
