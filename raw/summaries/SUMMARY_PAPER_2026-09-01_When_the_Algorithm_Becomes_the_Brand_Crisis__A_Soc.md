## Summary
The paper argues that AI incidents differ from organisational crises or scandals and proposes a sociotechnical theory of distributed responsibility. It shows that incident configuration determines how blame is assigned among system, developer, deployer, vendor, and user, influencing trust and reputation.

## Key Takeaways
- Incident configuration shapes actor-specific attribution, meaning the way an AI failure is framed decides which stakeholder bears responsibility.
- Attribution influences capability, integrity, fairness, and relationship appraisals, linking blame to perceived organisational virtues or flaws.
- Public moralisation may turn incidents into scandals only when it escalates beyond mere accountability.

## Context
AI systems now mediate customer interactions through chatbots and recommendation engines, raising questions about who is answerable for errors. Traditional brand‑crisis models assume a single responsible party, which does not capture the fragmented governance of AI deployments.

## Implications
Practitioners should adopt accountable transparency to manage perception and trust without assuming permanent brand damage. This theory guides design of response protocols that balance timely communication with role‑specific responsibility acknowledgment.
