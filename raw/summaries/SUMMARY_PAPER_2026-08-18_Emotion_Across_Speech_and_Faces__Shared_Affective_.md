## Summary
This paper investigates whether multimodal foundation models recognize speech and facial emotion through shared affective functional units or modality‑specific pathways. By probing three large models with speech and face recognition tasks, the authors identify emotion‑sensitive neurons (ESNs) that are causally linked to both modalities.

## Key Takeaways
- Visual ESNs can be deactivated to impair recognition of their associated facial emotions while steering them enhances those emotions relative to others.  
- Acoustic and visual ESNs show overlapping activation patterns and similar layer‑wise distributions, suggesting partial structural alignment across speech and face representations.  
- Cross‑modal interventions reveal bidirectional causal transfer: neurons identified from one modality produce emotion‑specific effects when applied to the other.

## Context
The study contributes to understanding how foundation models integrate heterogeneous sensory inputs at a granular level, moving beyond black‑box performance metrics toward interpretable affective mechanisms. It highlights that even large multimodal systems contain sparse components whose behavior can be manipulated without retraining.

## Implications
These findings suggest that emotion recognition in AI can be refined by targeting specific neural units rather than relying solely on overall model capacity. Practitioners may leverage this insight to develop more transparent and controllable affective interfaces for voice assistants, video analysis, and mental‑health applications.
