## Summary
The paper investigates trust as a subjunctive epistemic state and examines three representational strategies for modeling it, showing how each handles betrayal differently while agreeing on realizability of finite common trust but not the completed fixed point.

## Key Takeaways
- The lexicographic assumption representation treats trust as ordinal closeness and a betrayal can invert level orderings causing inconsistency.  
- The ordinal closeness representation shows that betrayal contaminates closeness proportional to betrayer deliberateness, altering perceived proximity.  
- Under strong belief representation the betrayal only shifts conditioning hypothesis without breaking belief coherence.

## Context
In AI we often model trust between agents or systems as a rational assumption; this work extends epistemic modeling beyond rationality to incorporate trustworthiness, offering tools for designing robust collaborative environments where deception is possible.

## Implications
Understanding how each representation reacts to betrayal helps engineers choose appropriate trust frameworks, ensuring that system designs can either adapt gracefully or expose hidden flaws in trust assumptions.
