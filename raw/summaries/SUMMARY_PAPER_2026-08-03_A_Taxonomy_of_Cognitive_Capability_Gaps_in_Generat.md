## Summary
This paper presents a taxonomy that maps the persistent cognitive capability gaps in generative and agentic AI systems. By organizing these gaps into five dimensions—persistent state modeling, goal‑directed autonomy, self‑monitoring and control, environment interaction, and learning and adaptation—the authors highlight where current models fall short of sustained reasoning and adaptive behavior. The work also introduces the Adaptive Cognitive Intelligence Architecture (ACIA) as a conceptual framework for addressing these gaps.

## Key Takeaways
- Persistent state modeling remains weak because systems cannot maintain coherent representations over long time horizons, leading to loss of context during task execution.  
- Goal‑directed autonomy is limited by a lack of reliable self‑regulation mechanisms that prevent drift from intended objectives.  
- Self‑monitoring and control are underdeveloped, resulting in difficulty detecting and correcting errors without external supervision.

## Context
Generative and agentic AI have achieved impressive performance on narrow tasks, yet they still struggle with the integrated cognitive functions required for genuine intelligence. This gap underscores a need for research that bridges the divide between task‑specific automation and holistic reasoning capabilities.

## Implications
For researchers, the taxonomy offers a clear roadmap to prioritize which gaps to tackle first in developing Cognitive AI. For industry practitioners, understanding these limitations can guide more realistic expectations and investment in systems capable of long‑term adaptive performance.
