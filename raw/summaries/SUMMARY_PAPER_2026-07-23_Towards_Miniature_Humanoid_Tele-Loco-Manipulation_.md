## Summary  
The paper proposes a tele‑presence control system for miniature humanoids that combines virtual reality for upper‑body manipulation with reinforcement learning for lower‑body balance and locomotion, enabling remote operators to move objects and walk autonomously; experiments on ROBOTIS OP3 achieved 0.45 m/s walking speed and relocated two 40 g cubes in ten minutes.

## Key Takeaways  
- The system integrates virtual reality for arm control while using reinforcement learning to manage lower‑body balance, allowing independent movement of the robot's legs.  
- Tele‑loco‑manipulation was demonstrated by moving two small cubes over a distance of five meters within ten minutes, showing real‑time object relocation without human physical contact.  
- The teleoperated miniature humanoid walked at up to 0.45 m/s while the arms remained stationary, indicating that lower‑body locomotion can be decoupled from upper‑body operations.

## Context  
Miniature humanoids are increasingly common in research but their control systems lag behind full‑sized robots, which rely on VR and RL for teleoperation; this gap limits accessibility to researchers and industry practitioners seeking compact, cost‑effective solutions.

## Implications  
The approach could democratize the use of humanoid robots by enabling affordable, remote‑controlled miniaturized platforms for education, logistics, and assistive applications; it also paves the way for future research on scalable control architectures that combine immersive VR with learning‑based locomotion.
