## Summary
The paper introduces a hybrid framework that uses large language models to design bounded behavioural rubrics and structured scenario specifications for an agent‑based model of solar PV adoption among Irish dairy farms. By integrating these LLM‑generated rules with the original techno‑economic mechanism, the authors achieve up to 13% higher adoption while keeping outcomes stable and monotonic across different policy settings.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework augments an existing ABM with interpretable behavioural rubrics that are conservative, balanced, or optimistic, preserving the core model structure.
- Scenario specifications are fixed and rule‑validated, allowing systematic exploration of future market conditions without altering the underlying adoption logic.
- Experimental results across multiple Monte Carlo worlds show stable, economically plausible adoption outcomes bounded by the original logistic case.

## Context
Large language models now enable rapid generation of structured policy rules that can be plugged into simulation frameworks. This work demonstrates how AI‑generated specifications can coexist with traditional agent‑based modeling while maintaining scientific rigor and reproducibility.

## Implications
Practitioners in energy modelling can adopt this approach to explore behavioural nuances without sacrificing model stability, offering a scalable method for scenario analysis across diverse policy environments.
