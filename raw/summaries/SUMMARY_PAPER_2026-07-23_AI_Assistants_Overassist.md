## Summary
The paper introduces Int-Bench, a benchmark that simulates a student solving problems while an AI teacher decides when and how to intervene. Experiments across code debugging, mathematics, and brain teasers show LLMs intervene more frequently and earlier than humans and tend to give full solutions rather than targeted hints. This suggests LLM tutors may prioritize short‑term success over deeper learning.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs intervene more often and at earlier stages than human teachers, which could reduce cognitive engagement.
- The interventions are typically complete solutions instead of focused hints, limiting the development of problem‑solving skills.
- These patterns affect both immediate task performance and later generalization to new problems.

## Context
AI assistants are being deployed as learning companions in education and professional training. Understanding how they guide users is crucial for designing effective tutoring systems that promote genuine skill acquisition rather than shortcuts.

## Implications
If LLMs continue to favor early, comprehensive answers, learners may become dependent on AI assistance and miss opportunities to develop reasoning abilities. Practitioners should redesign intervention strategies to encourage self‑guided problem solving and long‑term mastery.
