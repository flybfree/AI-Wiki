## Summary  
The paper investigates how generative AI and agentic AI reshape both research production and scholarly evaluation, showing that changes in one domain trigger cascading effects on the other. Using a taxonomy of six dynamics across 230 publications and institutional records, it finds an emerging progression where faster production pressures evaluation, leading to scalable AI‑driven reviews, manipulation, institutional safeguards, evasion, and feedback loops.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Production scaling drives demand for automated evaluation, prompting institutions to deploy AI tools that can process large volumes of papers quickly.  
- Participants exploit regularities in evaluator behavior, causing measurable manipulation of scores and outcomes.  
- Institutional responses such as technical safeguards create new evasion strategies, redistributing workloads and altering the scholarly record used by future systems.  

## Context  
This research matters because it moves beyond isolated AI capability studies to examine systemic interactions within academic publishing. The paper highlights that AI’s impact is not linear but embedded in feedback loops between production speed and evaluation quality, a pattern increasingly relevant as generative models become mainstream.  

## Implications  
For scholars, the findings suggest that designing AI tools must consider downstream effects on peer review incentives. For industry, it underscores the need for robust governance to prevent manipulation and maintain trust in scholarly records.
