## Summary
This paper investigates the emergence and diffusion of toxic language within Mastodon’s decentralized network using machine‑learning classifiers. The study reveals that toxicity clusters around specific server cultures and that certain linguistic patterns are more predictive than others, offering a clearer picture of how moderation gaps affect community health.

## Key Takeaways
- Toxicity is not uniform across servers; it varies with local norms and moderator policies, indicating the need for context‑aware detection.  
- Machine‑learning models trained on a subset of posts overestimate toxicity in high‑moderation environments, suggesting that algorithmic bias can amplify perceived problems.  
- The spread of toxic content follows diffusion curves similar to viral memes, highlighting the importance of early intervention before cascading effects occur.

## Context
The work contributes to AI research by applying supervised classification to real‑world social media data, demonstrating how decentralized platforms introduce heterogeneity that challenges one‑size‑fits‑all models. It underscores the difficulty of standardizing toxicity metrics across unmoderated ecosystems and raises questions about model calibration in dynamic environments.

## Implications
For platform designers, these findings suggest that automated moderation tools must incorporate server‑specific baselines to avoid false positives. Practitioners should also consider community feedback loops to refine detection thresholds, ensuring AI supports rather than undermines decentralized governance.
