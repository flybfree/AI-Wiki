## Summary
This paper investigates how large language models can be integrated into partial differential equation workflows, focusing on three stages: model discovery and formulation, solver generation and revision, and feedback‑driven control or optimization. The authors highlight that current LLM systems function mainly as interface tools but face significant obstacles such as limited high‑quality datasets and a gap between simulated results and real‑world applications.

## Key Takeaways
- High‑quality datasets and benchmarks are scarce, making knowledge discovery especially difficult because expert annotation and executable problem construction demand substantial domain effort.  
- The persistent mismatch between simulation outcomes and actual scientific or engineering systems hampers direct transfer of numerical solutions, control policies, and optimized designs to practice.  
- LLM‑assisted PDE workflows remain largely at the level of workflow interfaces rather than fully autonomous decision‑making agents.

## Context
The integration of LLMs into scientific computing is part of a broader trend toward AI that can translate natural language into executable code and interpret complex simulations, aiming to reduce reliance on manual coding. This paper situates such advances within the challenge of aligning computational models with real‑world constraints, a persistent problem in both academia and industry.

## Implications
For researchers, the findings suggest that without robust datasets and feedback loops, LLM tools will continue to assist rather than replace human expertise in PDE workflows. For practitioners, the gap between simulation and reality underscores the need for more realistic benchmarking before deploying AI‑driven optimization or control solutions.
