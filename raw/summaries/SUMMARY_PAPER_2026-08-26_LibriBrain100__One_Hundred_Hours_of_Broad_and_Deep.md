## Summary
This paper presents LibriBrain100, a new MEG dataset containing over 100 hours of high‑quality neural recordings from naturalistic speech. The authors report state‑of‑the‑art performance on a word‑classification benchmark and demonstrate that both deep within‑subject data and broad multi‑subject fine‑tuning can achieve top results.

## Key Takeaways
- LibriBrain100 provides over 100 hours of high‑quality within‑subject MEG from naturalistic speech, far exceeding the size of any comparable dataset.  
- Using an existing decoding model, the authors achieve state‑of‑the‑art performance on a word‑classification benchmark, confirming that deep neural data at scale yields strong results.  
- The dataset includes roughly 40 minutes per subject for multi‑subject fine‑tuning, showing that broad data can compensate for limited per‑subject information.

## Context
The field of brain‑computer interfaces relies on large, standardized datasets to train and evaluate decoding models. Providing such resources accelerates progress toward reliable, non‑invasive speech decoding and supports reproducible research across the community.

## Implications
LibriBrain100 will benefit researchers, industry developers, and practitioners by offering a benchmark that can be used for rapid prototyping and competition. Its open release and competition framework promote transparency and drive innovation in practical neural communication technologies.
