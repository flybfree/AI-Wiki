## Summary
This paper introduces event-to-action process mining, a new agenda that transforms heterogeneous operational event data into governed actions rather than just providing retrospective insights. The authors argue for four mineable artifacts—event-object representations, action evidence packages, governance contracts, and benchmarks—and propose a framework where agents can decide to act, defer, ask, or refuse based on evidence, privacy constraints, authority, and risk.

## Key Takeaways
- Event-to-action process mining shifts the focus from discovering past processes to generating accountable actions that respect organizational rules and privacy limits.  
- The four artifacts—event-object representations, action evidence packages, governance contracts, and benchmarks—are required to support a complete decision-making pipeline for agentic BPM.  
- The agenda aligns with emerging trends such as LLM‑assisted mining, object‑centric standards, causal monitoring, and privacy‑preserving learning.

## Context
Agentic AI is moving beyond passive analysis into proactive decision making within business processes. As event logs become richer and more heterogeneous, traditional process mining tools struggle to produce actionable outputs that incorporate governance and risk considerations. This research addresses the gap by integrating these emerging capabilities into a unified mining agenda.

## Implications
Practitioners can leverage this framework to build systems that not only monitor but also recommend or execute actions in compliance with organizational policies and privacy regulations. The approach may become standard in enterprise AI platforms, enabling trustworthy automation across industries.
