## Summary  
This paper introduces Slang‑Q, a manually curated dataset of user‑generated English sentences paired with queer slang terms and reference definitions, built on a taxonomy of 118 queer terms. The authors evaluate current language models on their ability to understand and define these slang items under different prompting conditions, revealing how well the models capture community‑specific linguistic expressions.

## Key Takeaways  
- Slang‑Q provides a comprehensive resource that pairs real user sentences with precise definitions of 118 queer terms, enabling systematic testing of model comprehension.  
- The evaluation shows that language models often fail to retrieve or generate accurate definitions when prompted without explicit context, highlighting limited awareness of queer slang.  
- Prompting strategies that include the term’s definition improve performance, suggesting that models can be steered toward correct answers with proper instruction.

## Context  
Understanding niche linguistic forms is crucial for AI systems that interact with diverse user bases, especially those representing marginalized communities. This work contributes to a broader effort in inclusive NLP by addressing representation gaps in existing datasets and model training data.

## Implications  
For researchers, Slang‑Q offers a benchmark for measuring how models handle sensitive, identity‑specific language, guiding future dataset creation. Practitioners can use the findings to design more equitable AI interfaces that respect linguistic diversity.
