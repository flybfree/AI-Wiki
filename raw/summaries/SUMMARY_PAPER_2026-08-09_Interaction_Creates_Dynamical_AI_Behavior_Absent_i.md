## Summary
This paper investigates how AI agents behave when one agent directs another while ignoring its responses, revealing a dynamical state that neither copies nor reverts to solitary behavior. The authors demonstrate that the boss’s message stream functions like a pre‑recorded tape and that both agents share an alien dynamical mode, which can be described by simple kinetic theory.

## Key Takeaways
- A boss AI sending messages while ignoring replies forces the subordinate into a new behavioral state it would never adopt alone.  
- The added value of the boss is comparable to playing back a pre‑recorded message rather than interacting in real time.  
- The same set of messages can produce different outcomes depending on how they are delivered, highlighting delivery matters.

## Context
In AI research we often assume agents act independently or mimic each other when they share parameters such as decoding temperature. This study shows that interaction can create emergent dynamics absent from isolated operation, challenging the notion of pure modular behavior and opening a bridge to non‑equilibrium physics inspired by kinetic theory.

## Implications
Understanding these interactions is crucial for designing robust AI systems where agents must coordinate without constant direct control. Practitioners should consider message delivery strategies as they influence downstream agent states, affecting performance and safety in collaborative environments.
