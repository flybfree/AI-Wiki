## Summary
This paper investigates how human‑AI interaction loops can be engineered to foster accurate user beliefs, curb deceptive behavior, and preserve system performance. By combining theory, modeling, experiments, and real‑world data, the authors propose design principles that balance individual benefit with collective goals.

## Key Takeaways
- Designing feedback mechanisms that align users’ self‑model of AI accuracy with actual outcomes helps individuals make cost‑effective improvement decisions without resorting to manipulation.  
- The framework explicitly discourages gaming by making deceptive strategies less rewarding than honest engagement, thereby protecting the system’s intended objective of maximizing precision.  
- Aligning the AI’s objectives with human values through transparent interaction design ensures that improvements in user belief formation do not compromise the core performance metric.

## Context
Human‑centered machine learning increasingly emphasizes how users perceive and act upon AI systems, yet few works address the trade‑off between personal gain and system integrity. This research fills that gap by offering a systematic approach to shaping interactions across both human and algorithmic perspectives.

## Implications
Practitioners can adopt these principles to build trustworthy AI interfaces that enhance user outcomes while safeguarding model quality. The methodology provides a replicable template for future systems aiming to balance competing objectives in real‑world deployments.
