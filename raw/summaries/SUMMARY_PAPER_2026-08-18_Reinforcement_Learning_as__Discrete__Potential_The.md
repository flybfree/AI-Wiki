## Summary
The paper reviews the link between reinforcement learning theory and potential theory under a fixed-policy assumption. It shows how potential-theoretic frameworks can improve sample efficiency and provide formal constraints. Extending to non‑fixed policies yields a nonlinear extension of the linear framework.

## Key Takeaways
- Potential theory offers a probabilistic foundation that can be applied directly to RL algorithms, reducing reliance on Markov chain assumptions.
- The fixed-policy assumption enables a linear potential approach that enhances sample efficiency by focusing on energy minimization rather than stochastic exploration.
- Extending the framework to non‑fixed policies introduces a nonlinear potential model that maintains theoretical consistency.

## Context
In reinforcement learning research, connecting algorithmic design to classical mathematical fields such as potential theory is rare. This work bridges those domains, offering new analytical tools beyond standard Markovian analysis. The interdisciplinary perspective may inspire future methods that unify diverse optimization landscapes.

## Implications
Practitioners can leverage these constraints to build more sample‑efficient policies without extensive simulation. Researchers gain a unified language for exploring both linear and nonlinear RL spaces, potentially accelerating algorithm development across industries.
