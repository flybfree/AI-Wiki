## Summary
The paper proposes a new metric called Content Depth Score to measure how much short videos stimulate higher-order thinking. It evaluates 13 recommendation systems on a benchmark dataset and finds they still favor shallow content despite deep content being theoretically better. The results show that recommending cognitively deep videos only marginally improves performance over random selection.

## Key Takeaways
- The Content Depth Score quantifies video depth using a seven-level scale based on cognitive psychology theories.
- Benchmark SCOPE-Bench provides annotations for 150K short videos enabling systematic evaluation of recommendation models from a cognitive perspective.
- Existing RSs consistently prefer shallow-content videos and deep‑content recommendations yield only marginal gains over random selection.

## Context
Short‑video recommender systems are optimized for immediate attention capture, often ignoring deeper cognitive engagement. This work addresses the gap between engagement metrics and long‑term user well‑being by introducing a content depth perspective in AI research.

## Implications
For practitioners, integrating Content Depth Score could lead to more balanced recommendation pipelines that consider both engagement and cognitive health. The field may shift toward models that balance attention capture with deeper learning outcomes, influencing future platform design and ethical AI development.
