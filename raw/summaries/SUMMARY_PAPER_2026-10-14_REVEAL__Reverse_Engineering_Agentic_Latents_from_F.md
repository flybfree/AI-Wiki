## Summary
REVEAL introduces a training-free framework that transfers the problem-solving experience of frontier LLM agents to smaller, frozen open-source agents without requiring access to the source model's internal weights or any fine-tuning of the target model. By reverse-engineering "Agentic Latents"—anchored states and dynamic feedback-response profiles extracted from replaying frontier-model trajectories in the target model—REVEAL constructs target-specific steering directions that are injected before each response, yielding measurable accuracy improvements on agentic benchmarks such as Terminal-Bench 2.1 and Humanity's Last Exam across three different target models.

## Key Takeaways
- REVEAL operates entirely without training: it does not fine-tune the target agent, nor does it require access to the frontier model's hidden states or weights. Instead, it treats textual trajectories from a frontier model as a kind of protocol specification, reverse-engineering reusable local control signals (anchored states and dynamic profiles) that can be replayed within a frozen target agent to guide its behavior.
- The core mechanism contrasts successful and failed rollouts of the same task generated by the same source model. By matching and selecting local events across these rollouts, REVEAL extracts response-transition contrasts that encode what distinguishes productive reasoning steps from unproductive ones. These contrasts are then translated into a target-specific steering direction applied before each response, effectively acting as a local activation control rather than a global policy update.
- Empirical validation spans two demanding agentic benchmarks—Terminal-Bench 2.1 and Humanity's Last Exam with tool use—across three distinct target models, consistently showing higher task accuracy compared to the unmodified agents. Representation analyses and behavioral case studies further support the interpretation that frontier-model experience can be decomposed into reusable, local activation-level controls rather than requiring end-to-end distillation.

## Context
This work sits at the intersection of model distillation, agent scaffolding, and interpretability. As frontier models grow in cost and capability, the community increasingly seeks ways to compress their reasoning competence into smaller, open-source agents suitable for deployment. Traditional approaches—knowledge distillation, chain-of-thought transfer, or latent-space alignment—typically demand either training the target model or inspecting the source model's internals, both of which are impractical for closed frontier APIs. REVEAL reframes the problem through the lens of protocol reverse engineering, treating observable textual trajectories as sufficient to extract actionable control signals, thereby sidestepping the need for weight-level access or gradient-based optimization.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying agentic systems under budget constraints, REVEAL offers a practical pathway to improve small-model performance by mining high-quality trajectories from expensive frontier models without any retraining pipeline, making it attractive for production environments where model weights are proprietary or locked behind APIs. For the broader research community, the framework suggests that agent behavior can be decomposed into reusable local control primitives—anchored states and response-transition contrasts—opening new directions in agent interpretability, modular agent composition, and the systematic transfer of reasoning strategies across heterogeneous model families.
