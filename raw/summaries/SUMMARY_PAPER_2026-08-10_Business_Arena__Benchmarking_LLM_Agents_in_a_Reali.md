## Summary
The paper introduces Business Arena, a marketplace simulation where AI agents act as cross‑border sellers and buyers to test end‑to‑end business intelligence. It evaluates fifteen frontier language models by measuring final net worth after delayed sourcing, pricing, and recovery decisions. The results show a ninefold difference in profit between the best model and human strategies.

## Key Takeaways
- Business Arena creates a realistic environment that captures delayed and coupled market consequences, allowing agents to face uncertainty similar to real trading.
- The study finds that even top models underperform human‑designed strategies, highlighting persistent gaps in LLM business competence.
- Skill‑level analysis uncovers distinct operating styles such as premium sellers versus high‑turnover wholesalers, while action‑level attribution pinpoints sourcing, pricing and recovery choices that drive profit.

## Context
This work addresses a gap in AI benchmarking by focusing on commercial decision making rather than pure language tasks. It demonstrates how LLM agents struggle with multi‑step economic interactions where outcomes are not immediate. The environment leverages real Alibaba.com data to ground simulations in actual market dynamics.

## Implications
For practitioners, Business Arena provides a transparent testbed to compare agent capabilities against human expertise. For the industry, it underscores that current frontier models need stronger reasoning and planning before being trusted for commercial deployment.
