## Summary
The paper introduces a method to train attention graph neural networks by minimizing finite-volume residuals of thermo-fluid equations directly on mesh data without labeled examples. The approach yields low prediction errors and eliminates the need for costly CFD training data generation. Results show state‑of‑the‑art performance across both steady‑state and transient benchmarks.

## Key Takeaways
- The FVM loss provides a direct, unlabeled signal that can be evaluated on mesh points to train attention graph neural networks.
- On two steady‑state problems the model reaches an all‑field normalized root‑mean‑square error of 2.3–2.8% matching CFD references including buoyancy‑energy coupling.
- In parametric transient cases the FVM‑loss model outperforms a data‑supervised baseline while completely avoiding computational cost of generating training data.

## Context
Current scientific machine learning relies on large labeled datasets generated by expensive numerical simulations, limiting rapid prototyping. This work demonstrates that physics‑based residuals can serve as an alternative training signal, aligning with the trend toward efficient surrogate modeling in fluid dynamics.

## Implications
Practitioners can develop fast predictive tools for thermo‑fluid applications without prohibitive data acquisition budgets. The method reduces development time and cost, encouraging broader adoption of AI surrogates in engineering design cycles.
