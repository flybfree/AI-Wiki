## Summary
This paper evaluates whether large language models can reliably nowcast major U.S. macroeconomic indicators by producing hourly forecasts before official releases, using a contamination‑resistant benchmark called LiveMacroEval. The results show that state‑of‑the‑art LLM agents achieve accuracy comparable to institutional and professional nowcasts across many indicators.

## Key Takeaways
- LLM agents produce nowcasts for sixteen U.S. macroeconomic indicators over the pre‑release window, with performance measured by LiveMacro Score relative to equity returns and simulated Polymarket bets.  
- The aggregate accuracy of these models is broadly comparable to regional‑bank nowcasts, Bloomberg ECOS consensus, and an auto‑ARIMA baseline.  
- Accuracy varies widely across individual indicators, indicating that some metrics are better suited for LLM estimation than others.

## Context
The rapid advancement of large language models enables them to combine extensive knowledge with real‑time web search, allowing higher frequency updates than traditional expert nowcasts. This capability is especially relevant as central banks and financial markets increasingly rely on timely macroeconomic signals.

## Implications
For policymakers and market participants, LLM‑based nowcasting could provide faster, cheaper estimates that complement human expertise without compromising accuracy. Practitioners may integrate these models into automated trading or policy monitoring pipelines to stay ahead of official releases.
