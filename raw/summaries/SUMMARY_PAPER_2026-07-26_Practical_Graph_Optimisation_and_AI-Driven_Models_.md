## Summary
The paper proposes game‑theoretic and optimisation models to improve Active Directory security hardening. It introduces a honeypot placement model, a dynamic decoy strategy, an adaptive prioritisation framework, and a generalised edge‑removal policy. The authors demonstrate that all underlying problems are computationally intractable.

## Key Takeaways
- Existing AD attack graph models treat the graph as static, ignoring its frequent changes in real environments.
- Most solutions only remove vulnerabilities (edge removal) without considering active defence mechanisms or admin feedback.
- The proposed adaptive model reduces system‑admin approval effort by generalising decisions to similar risk features.

## Context
The paper contributes to AI research on security optimisation by applying game theory and dynamic graph models to a critical enterprise infrastructure. It highlights the gap between theoretical attack graphs and practical, time‑varying AD environments.

## Implications
Practitioners can use these models to prioritize remediation efforts while minimizing administrative overhead. The work underscores that effective AI‑driven security requires both dynamic modelling and scalable decision‑making pipelines.
