## Summary
This paper introduces the Analyze‑Experiment‑Resituate (AER) framework designed to help digital artists explore new artistic styles rather than merely replicate existing ones. The study shows that AER increases artists’ agency and reflective practice compared with a standard style‑transfer approach, demonstrating its effectiveness in fostering creative experimentation.

## Key Takeaways
- AER breaks down artworks into interpretable stylistic elements, allowing artists to understand the components of reference material before applying them.
- The framework guides controlled experimentation where artists choose specific stylistic possibilities, ensuring that exploration is intentional and not random.
- Resituating emerging styles through simulated social perspectives helps artists anticipate how their new directions might be received by others.

## Context
Generative AI has become a powerful tool for style replication, yet most existing systems focus on producing visually appealing outputs without encouraging deeper creative inquiry. This paper addresses the need for tools that support the full cycle of artistic exploration—analysis, experimentation, and reflection—within an AI‑assisted workflow.

## Implications
For artists, AER offers a structured yet flexible method to experiment with new aesthetics while maintaining personal control over their work. For developers, the framework highlights how AI can be designed to augment human creativity rather than replace it, opening pathways for more meaningful artistic collaboration in the industry.
