## Summary
The paper introduces a systematic method for identifying language areas that can be defined by any arbitrary size and location, aiming to replace ad‑hoc expert‑based macroareas with algorithmic clustering. The resulting worldwide macroareas largely match existing continental groupings while also revealing finer local clusters such as the well‑known sprachbund. This approach provides a reproducible way to separate universal linguistic patterns from regionally specific ones.

## Key Takeaways
- Macroareas are defined geographically and serve to group languages for typological analysis, distinguishing those with potential contact from those without.
- Current macroarea definitions rely on expert judgment and mainly follow continental boundaries, lacking a systematic algorithmic framework.
- The proposed clustering method can generate language areas of any size, producing both global groupings that align with existing macroareas and localized clusters like the sprachbund.
