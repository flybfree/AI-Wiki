## Summary
The paper addresses a paradox in goal reasoning within Non-Axiomatic Logic where avoiding an event is mistakenly treated as pursuing its negation. It introduces anti-goal reasoning and the prevent operation to differentiate avoidance from pursuit. The framework yields four distinct cases: pursuit, passive avoidance, active prevention, and withholding action.

## Key Takeaways
- Avoidance is represented by a separate concept called anti‑goal rather than simply using ¬G, preventing confusion between negating an event and avoiding it.
- The prevent operation links anti‑goal reasoning to ordinary goal reasoning, allowing active prevention of undesired outcomes without treating the negation as a goal itself.
- Minimal case studies demonstrate that the rules correctly distinguish pursuit, passive avoidance, active prevention, and withholding action.

## Context
Non‑Axiomatic Logic seeks adaptive systems that achieve goals with limited knowledge. Traditional goal representation often conflates logical negation with behavioral intent, leading to ambiguous or paradoxical behavior in reinforcement learning and decision making.

## Implications
This distinction improves the design of AI agents that must both avoid harmful events and preserve beneficial ones without unintended side effects. Practitioners can implement clearer intention specifications and prevent paradoxes in goal‑driven algorithms.
