## Summary
This paper investigates how personality‑conditioned large language model agents change after 11 major life events using the Big Five trait framework. It finds that agents exhibit measurable shifts in traits that align with human psychological expectations, though effect sizes are smaller than those observed in people. The study introduces BFI‑Adapt to benchmark these changes and ranks models accordingly.

## Key Takeaways
- PC‑Agents show consistent trait shifts for event‑trait pairs, both with and without documented human change directions.  
- Even when direction matches expectations, the magnitude of shift is typically below human effect‑size ranges.  
- Gender and cultural‑region prompts have little moderating influence, while persona‑level dispersion is compressed three to four times compared with human samples.

## Context
The rapid adoption of AI personas in emotional support and role‑playing creates a need for agents that evolve plausibly over time. Understanding the limits of personality evolution in LLMs helps set realistic expectations for user interaction design.

## Implications
Researchers can use BFI‑Adapt to evaluate model performance on personality change, guiding developers toward more coherent agent behavior. Practitioners should recognize that current AI personas reflect average human dynamics rather than full personality complexity.
