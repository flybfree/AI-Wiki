## Summary
The paper investigates whether Harris County magistrate judges follow algorithmic patterns in misdemeanor bail hearings using court data and machine learning models. It finds that decisions can be captured by simple formulas but also reveals notable inconsistency among judges.

## Key Takeaways
- Judges’ outcomes are often predictable through small, interpretable formulas based on discrete factors like criminal history and charge type.
- Significant differences exist between judges leading to unequal treatment of similarly situated defendants.
- Machine learning models highlight variable importance that aligns with algorithmic explanations rather than individualized discretion.

## Context
This study contributes to the debate on algorithmic governance by showing that human judicial behavior can be modeled as rule‑based systems, challenging assumptions about AI’s role in law. It highlights the need for transparent decision processes when integrating technology into legal practice.

## Implications
Practitioners should monitor cases where algorithms fail to explain outcomes, focusing resources on those instances. The findings suggest that improving consistency may require addressing hidden individual biases rather than solely relying on algorithmic fairness metrics.
