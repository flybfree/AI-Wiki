## Summary
The paper introduces a unified framework for safety in urban mobility by creating hazard-informed safety envelopes that integrate symbolic, spatial, and dynamic models. It demonstrates how these envelopes can be transformed across layers to enforce runtime constraints. The approach enables physical AI systems to operate safely amid complex human-robot interactions.

## Key Takeaways
- Hazard-informed safety envelopes combine symbolic, spatial, and dynamic world models into a single representation that guides safe behavior.
- The framework bridges systematic hazard analysis with runtime enforcement via cross-layer transformation processes.
- This unified model interfaces directly with physical AI runtime harnesses to guarantee safe urban mobility operations.

## Context
In the rapidly expanding domain of autonomous urban mobility, ensuring safety across heterogeneous robotic systems is paramount. Traditional safety mechanisms often remain siloed and fail to adapt to dynamic environments. The proposed hazard-informed envelopes address these limitations by providing a holistic view of hazards.

## Implications
This research shifts safety from static constraints to an adaptive, model-driven process that can be integrated into existing AI pipelines. Practitioners can leverage the framework to design safer robotic deployments without sacrificing performance. It also sets a new standard for cross-layer safety in physical AI systems.
