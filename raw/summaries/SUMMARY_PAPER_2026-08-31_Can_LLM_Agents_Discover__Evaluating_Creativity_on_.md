## Summary
The paper introduces a framework to evaluate the creativity of multi‑turn LLM research agents on machine learning engineering tasks. It finds that agents can generate novel solutions but often fail to improve task performance, and that their historical novelty exceeds human medalists while still underperforming.

## Key Takeaways
- All agents show declining psychological novelty as they move from exploration to exploitation, indicating a loss of P‑Creativity during the transition.  
- LLMs produce higher historical novelty than top human competitors yet achieve lower task performance, revealing a disconnect between novelty and usefulness.  
- The automated LLM‑as‑a‑Judge pipeline provides a reliable metric for measuring psychological creativity at scale.

## Context
Current AI research touts autonomous discovery capabilities, but the ability to generate useful, novel solutions remains unproven. This work bridges that gap by applying rigorous creativity metrics to real engineering problems, offering a benchmark for future agent design.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that novelty alone is insufficient; agents must be guided toward performance‑oriented innovation. Industry adoption of such evaluation tools could help prioritize research that balances exploration with tangible outcomes.
