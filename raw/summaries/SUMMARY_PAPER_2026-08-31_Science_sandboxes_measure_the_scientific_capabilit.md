## Summary
The paper introduces science sandboxes as a framework to evaluate AI agents' scientific reasoning by repeatedly running experiments in varied settings. The authors demonstrate that frontier models can optimize quantitative metrics but fail when encountering rules outside their learned priors, showing both success and failure modes.

## Key Takeaways
- Agents may achieve high performance on specific metrics without grasping underlying system rules, indicating a gap between optimization and scientific understanding.
- Their reasoning deteriorates when faced with novel biological rules that deviate from familiar priors, revealing limitations in generalization.
- The framework provides a standardized protocol for assessing both quantitative outcomes and qualitative reasoning across wet, damp, and dry experimental modes.

## Context
This work addresses the need to measure not just task completion but genuine scientific insight in AI agents. By applying it to regulatory genomics and protein fitness prediction, it bridges AI performance metrics with domain-specific knowledge gaps.

## Implications
Science sandboxes offer a controlled way for researchers to benchmark and improve the scientific reasoning capabilities of AI systems, guiding development toward more robust and interpretable models. Practitioners can use these insights to design experiments that better align AI outputs with real-world biological principles.
