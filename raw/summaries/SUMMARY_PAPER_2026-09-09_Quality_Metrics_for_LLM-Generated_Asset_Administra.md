## Summary
This paper introduces a perturbation‑based evaluation framework to assess the quality of asset administration shells (AAS) generated by large language models such as GPT‑4o‑mini, Qwen3, and DeepSeek‑R1. By systematically degrading AAS outputs across property names and values, the authors identify which metrics most reliably capture quality loss. Their experiments on 6 400 AAS instances from 200 products reveal that exact matching of property names combined with value‑based recall and name‑based F1 scores are the strongest indicators.

## Key Takeaways
- Exact matching of property names together with value‑based recall provides the most reliable signal for detecting quality degradation in AI‑generated AAS.  
- Name‑based F1 score also remains a strong indicator, especially when property values diverge from expected ranges.  
- Perturbation types such as missing properties or incorrect values have distinct impacts that can be quantified using these metrics.

## Context
The rapid adoption of large language models in industrial settings creates new challenges for quality assurance without ground truth references. Traditional evaluation methods rely on static benchmarks, but real‑world AAS generation involves continuous variation and uncertainty. This work bridges the gap by proposing a dynamic, perturbation‑driven approach that can be applied to any LLM pipeline producing structured asset data.

## Implications
Practitioners can now select metrics that align with specific degradation patterns, enabling more robust tuning of LLM pipelines for AAS generation. The findings support integration of AI‑generated AAS into manufacturing workflows where interoperability and accuracy are critical, reducing reliance on manual verification processes.
