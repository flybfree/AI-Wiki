## Summary
The paper introduces a clinician-facing chatbot that integrates consensus guidance with PubMed literature, registered interventional trials, and evidence from living systematic reviews to answer Long COVID questions. In an exploratory evaluation on 50 clinician-facing queries, the chatbot performed comparably to OpenEvidence but achieved numerically higher scores and lower score variability in LLM‑judged comparisons.

## Key Takeaways
- Consensus guidance is always included to frame responses.
- Retrieval of PubMed literature, registered trials, and systematic reviews occurs in parallel when selected by the user.
- The chatbot showed numerically higher scores and lower score variability than OpenEvidence on LLM‑judged comparisons.

## Context
This work tackles the fragmentation of Long COVID evidence across sources with different update cycles, emphasizing the need for AI systems that can seamlessly blend authoritative consensus with current literature. It showcases how retrieval‑augmented chatbots can deliver clinically relevant information while preserving consistency and trustworthiness.

## Implications
Clinicians gain access to a unified source that respects both established guidelines and emerging research, enhancing decision‑support accuracy. The approach is adaptable to other chronic conditions with heterogeneous evidence, potentially fostering broader adoption of AI tools in clinical practice.
