## Summary
This paper conducts a large‑scale bibliometric and topic‑based analysis of 7,120 Arabic NLP papers from 1960 to 2026, revealing a sharp increase in publications after 2020 linked to transformer models and LLMs. The study identifies 19 major research themes, with text, speech, translation, and recognition dominating the output.

## Key Takeaways
- Publication surge after 2020 driven by transformer models and large language models.  
- Topic modeling uncovers 19 substantive themes, the largest centered on text, speech, translation, and recognition.  
- Task‑dialect gap matrix highlights understudied Maghrebi, Iraqi, and Sudanese dialects.

## Context
Arabic NLP remains underexplored compared to global AI trends, despite rapid digital adoption in the region. This quantitative study fills a critical data gap by providing measurable insights into publication patterns, citation dynamics, and research focus areas across decades.

## Implications
Researchers should prioritize under‑resourced dialects and develop culturally aligned benchmarks to close the task‑dialect gap. Practitioners can leverage these findings to allocate resources toward high‑impact Arabic NLP tasks that align with regional linguistic diversity.
