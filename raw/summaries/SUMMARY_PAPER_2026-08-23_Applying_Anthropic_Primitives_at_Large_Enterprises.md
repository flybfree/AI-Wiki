## Summary
This paper introduces the harness paradigm as a scalable infrastructure for knowledge work in large enterprises, arguing that it outperforms more complex architectures while collapsing review and maintenance costs. The authors present four mechanisms—credential‑scoped tooling, external authorization logic, side‑effect registration, and a unified codebase—that enable consistent deployment across multiple use cases.

## Key Takeaways
- Harnesses can serve as task‑level infrastructure that surpasses elaborate architectures in enterprise settings, reducing the need for bespoke solutions.  
- The choice of harness dominates variance in agent benchmark results more than model selection does, highlighting its architectural importance.  
- Governance remains a barrier to adoption; solving it requires a single unmodified harness with shared code and documentation.

## Context
The rapid rise of large language models has lowered the cost of generating custom code, yet enterprises still struggle with fragmented, high‑maintenance solutions. This research situates the harness paradigm within that landscape, offering a pragmatic alternative to both off‑the‑shelf products and fully custom architectures.

## Implications
For practitioners, adopting a harness reduces cognitive load and accelerates deployment across teams. For industry, it could standardize AI workflows, lowering operational costs while maintaining flexibility for diverse knowledge work tasks.
