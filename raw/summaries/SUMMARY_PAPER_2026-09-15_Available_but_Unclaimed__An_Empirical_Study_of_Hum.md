## Summary
This empirical study investigates how humans interact with and benefit from large language models when solving complex reasoning tasks. The research demonstrates that while AI assistance improves human performance, the degree of improvement depends heavily on model competence, task type, and the extent to which individuals defer to AI advice. Ultimately, only about half of the accuracy gains provided by LLMs are successfully transferred to human-AI collaborative outcomes, highlighting the need for better interaction design.

## Key Takeaways
- Human performance improvements when assisted by LLMs scale with the model's inherent competence on specific reasoning tasks, yet complementary AI capabilities do not automatically guarantee that combined human-AI output will outperform both components independently.
- Deference to AI advice varies significantly across different cognitive domains and increases as task difficulty or model reliability rises, though post-advice confidence is a weaker predictor of answer correctness compared to unaided self-assessment.
- Approximately fifty percent of the accuracy advantage offered by LLMs successfully translates into assisted human performance, with this transfer rate differing substantially across models, underscoring the importance of evaluating AI systems within actual human-AI interaction loops rather than in isolation.

## Context
As large language models become increasingly integrated into cognitive workflows, understanding their true synergistic potential with human reasoning remains a critical research frontier. This study addresses a gap in current literature by empirically measuring how well humans absorb and apply AI-generated insights across standardized cognitive benchmarks, moving beyond isolated model evaluations to real-world collaborative dynamics.
