## Summary
This paper examines the deployment of AI‑generated text watermarks under the EU AI Act and finds that neither the claimed quality benefits nor the alleged inescapability can be verified with current tools. The authors argue that the core governance failure lies in the unverifiable nature of these claims rather than the absence of watermarking.

## Key Takeaways
- The measured impact on prose is comparable to random sampling seed changes, indicating negligible quality loss.  
- On code generation, watermarks cause three errors per model and detection rates remain near chance, suggesting detectability limits are more critical than quality degradation.  
- The lack of public testing tools forces reliance on vendor assurances that cannot be independently confirmed.

## Context
The EU AI Act mandates visible AI‑generated content marking, prompting major models like Claude and Gemini to embed watermarks such as SynthID‑Text. This rapid rollout raises questions about transparency, user trust, and the ability of regulators or users to verify claims made by providers.

## Implications
If verification remains impossible, regulatory oversight will be ineffective, leaving AI systems vulnerable to misuse and eroding public confidence in generative AI. Practitioners must therefore prioritize open‑access evaluation protocols and shared standards to ensure accountability beyond vendor promises.
