## Summary
This paper investigates how recommendation markets evolve when users act as autonomous agents who specify needs before selecting platforms, creating a competition between access and attention. Experiments across three domains show that platform strategies shift dramatically under agentic interaction, with positive explanations dominating early rankings but losing favor once user feedback is considered.

## Key Takeaways
- Positive explanations occupy 73–78% of first‑ranked positions when the user agent does not relate platform actions to subsequent feedback.  
- When agents link their queries to later user outcomes, that share drops to 36–41%, and the likelihood of a purchase rises.  
- The user agent functions as both ranker and decision maker, shaping attention allocation, scarcity, and evaluation through its querying, ranking, and feedback mechanisms.

## Context
The study highlights a shift from platform‑centric recommendation systems to user‑driven marketplaces where agents compete for limited attention. This aligns with broader AI trends toward personalized, interactive experiences that treat users as active participants rather than passive recipients of curated content.

## Implications
For practitioners, designing agentic recommendation requires joint optimization of access, attention, and accountability to maximize utility while mitigating strategic manipulation by platforms. The findings suggest new research directions in mechanism design for collaborative filtering systems.
