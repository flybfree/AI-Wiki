## Summary
The paper investigates when a density flux pair solving a no-flux continuity equation in a bounded domain can be generated by a regular Lagrangian flow that stays inside the domain and respects prescribed marginals. It provides sufficient conditions involving interior BV regularity, boundary collar control, divergence bounds, and vanishing normal trace of velocity. The authors also construct an explicit counterexample showing that relaxing these assumptions leads to no such flow.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper proves that interior bounded-variation regularity together with a bounded-variation bound on a boundary collar, a one‑sided bound on the absolute value of the divergence and vanishing normal trace of the velocity are sufficient for existence of a regular Lagrangian flow confined to the domain.
- It shows that these four assumptions cannot be jointly relaxed; any relaxation allows a boundary current mechanism which precludes a smooth interior flow.
- An explicit smooth density flux pair is constructed whose characteristics transport marginals but fail to admit a regular Lagrangian flow because the compressibility bound breaks down near the initial time.

## Context
In AI research, reflected diffusion models are often sampled using ordinary differential equations that assume only mild coefficient regularity. This work supplies rigorous mathematical criteria for when such ODE samplers can be justified and highlights their limitations when boundary conditions or singular drifts appear. The study bridges PDE theory with stochastic modeling, offering a theoretical foundation for sampling algorithms.

## Implications
For practitioners developing reflected diffusion samplers, the results clarify which coefficient classes admit safe ODE integration and warn against using them near boundaries where divergence may blow up. This can guide algorithm design to avoid breakdowns and improve reliability of generated samples.
