## Summary
This study investigates whether the unspaced em‑dash (word---word) in U.S. congressional press releases is a measurable sign of large‑language‑model assistance, comparing 2021‑2025 data across House and Senate offices. The analysis shows a sharp rise in frequency from about 0.10–0.12 per thousand characters in the first four years to 0.217 in 2025, with the proportion of releases containing such dashes doubling from roughly 13% to 24.8%.

## Key Takeaways
- The unspaced em‑dash density increased by more than a factor of two between 2021‑2024 and 2025, crossing the pre‑registered threshold of 1.5×.
- The rise was consistent across party lines and chambers, with 75.6% of offices showing an increase and the effect persisting in a closed panel of 224 offices.
- Falsification tests revealed no step at the ChatGPT cut‑off or at the 2024/2025 boundary, indicating that the surge is not tied to a specific model release.

## Context
The paper addresses a growing body of research on subtle stylistic artifacts left by large language models in human‑written text. By focusing on a population‑level metric rather than individual authorship, it contributes to the field’s shift toward quantifying AI influence without requiring explicit disclosure.

## Implications
For journalists and media analysts, this finding suggests that unspaced em‑dashes may serve as an early indicator of LLM assistance in political communications. Practitioners should consider such markers alongside other stylistic cues when evaluating the authenticity or origin of press releases.
