## Summary
This study examined whether having students evaluate generative AI (GenAI) solutions was as effective as solving problems directly in a junior algorithms course, finding no statistically significant differences in grades but higher homework scores when evaluation tasks were used.

## Key Takeaways
- Students received significantly higher homework scores when evaluating GenAI-generated solutions.  
- There were no statistically significant differences between groups in midterm scores, final exam scores, overall course grades, or structural alignment of exam problems with the interventions.  
- Survey data showed most students reported no change in study habits; however, those who adapted their strategies rated the evaluation assignments as significantly more helpful.

## Context
The paper contributes to AI education research by testing a pedagogical approach that shifts emphasis from solution construction to verification and judgment within theory‑heavy courses. As GenAI tools become commonplace, understanding how they affect learning outcomes is crucial for curriculum design.

## Implications
Incorporating GenAI evaluation can be incorporated into algorithms coursework without causing broad performance losses, but meaningful learning gains may require deliberate scaffolding that moves students beyond simple error diagnosis. Practitioners should therefore design tasks that encourage deeper conceptual transfer rather than relying solely on the novelty of tool use.
