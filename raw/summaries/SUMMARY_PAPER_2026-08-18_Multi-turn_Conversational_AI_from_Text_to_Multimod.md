## Summary
This paper surveys the state of multi‑turn conversational AI that integrates text, audio, speech, and multimodal inputs, highlighting how systems handle memory, grounding, tool use, and cross‑modal adaptation. The authors find that while perception, generation, and actuation across modalities have improved rapidly, persistent session coherence remains a major challenge.

## Key Takeaways
- Multi‑turn dialogue requires robust memory mechanisms to retain context across turns, yet current models often lose track of earlier information.  
- Grounding responses in multiple modalities—such as linking spoken cues to visual or textual data—is still limited and prone to misalignment.  
- Evaluation frameworks that assess full‑session performance are scarce, making it hard to measure true cross‑turn reliability.

## Context
The rapid progress of multimodal AI has driven research toward seamless human‑computer interaction, but the literature often treats each modality in isolation rather than as an integrated conversational loop. This gap hampers real‑world deployment where users expect continuous, context‑aware dialogue.

## Implications
For practitioners, the need for persistent memory and cross‑modal grounding signals a shift from isolated model upgrades to holistic system design. Industry adoption will depend on tools that can reliably maintain conversation state across modalities and cultures.
