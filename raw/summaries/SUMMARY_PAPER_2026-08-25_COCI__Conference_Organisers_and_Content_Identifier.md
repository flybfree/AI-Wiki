## Summary
The paper introduces COCI, an AI framework that extracts fine-grained structured metadata from unstructured calls for papers and links them to existing scholarly knowledge graphs. By combining large language models with semantic mapping, COCI achieves high accuracy in identifying authors, topics, conference series, and organizer affiliations, thereby converting grey literature into machine‑readable resources.

## Key Takeaways
- COCI uses a multi‑stage pipeline that integrates LLMs with semantic mapping to pull entities from raw CfP texts. 
- The framework disambiguates authors and aligns topics with established knowledge bases such as OpenAlex, DBLP, TIB ConfIDent, and the AIDA Dashboard. 
- This integration bridges informal scholarly dissemination with structured Semantic Web resources for systematic analysis.

## Context
The growing volume of grey literature, especially unstructured calls for papers, creates a challenge for automated extraction and linking to existing knowledge graphs. In AI research, models that combine language understanding with semantic mapping are emerging as a way to handle heterogeneous data sources. COCI exemplifies this trend by demonstrating how LLMs can be guided toward precise entity recognition in real‑world academic contexts.

## Implications
For scholars and researchers, COCI enables systematic study of conference series and author participation without manual annotation. For publishers and knowledge platforms, the framework offers a scalable pipeline to enrich grey literature with structured metadata, improving discoverability and interoperability across scholarly ecosystems.
