## Summary
This paper investigates how inference‑time defenses can curb the hallucination of non‑existent software packages generated by large language models (LLMs). The authors evaluate seven defense strategies across eight model‑language configurations and show that Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) and Self‑Refine significantly reduce package hallucination rates, while Greedy decoding offers a strong trade‑off between mitigation and utility. They also reveal that adversarial prompts can inflate hallucinations up to 45 percentage points, especially in Ruby.

## Key Takeaways
- Prior evaluation methods overestimate Python’s hallucination rate by about nine percentage points because standard‑library modules are misclassified as hallucinations.  
- Among the defenses tested, Retrieval‑Augmented Generation (RAG) and Self‑Refine reduce PHR in 18 of 32 model‑language setups, whereas Greedy decoding provides the best average mitigation–utility balance.  
- Under adversarial prompts containing fabricated package names, PHR spikes by up to 45 percentage points, with Ruby showing the highest vulnerability (80.9–95.2 %).  

## Context
The rise of LLMs for code generation has introduced new supply‑chain risks, as hallucinated packages can be exploited. Existing defenses often focus on decoding strategies that ignore external knowledge sources or iterative verification, limiting their effectiveness against malicious inputs.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that robust package safety requires grounding in trusted repositories and self‑verification loops rather than relying solely on prompt engineering. Industry adoption of such multi‑layered defenses could mitigate supply‑chain attacks and improve trust in automated code generation.
