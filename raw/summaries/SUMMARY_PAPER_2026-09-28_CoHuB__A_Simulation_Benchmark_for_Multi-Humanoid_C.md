## Summary
CoHuB introduces a novel simulation benchmark designed to evaluate and advance multi-humanoid collaboration under egocentric visual observations. By providing ten diverse tasks involving two or three humanoids, along with synchronized VR teleoperation demonstrations, the benchmark addresses the critical gap in existing research that predominantly focuses on single-agent capabilities. Experimental evaluations demonstrate significant challenges in coordinated perception and control, establishing a robust foundation for future multi-robot collaboration research.

## Key Takeaways
- CoHuB fills a major gap in humanoid robotics by shifting focus from isolated single-humanoid tasks to collaborative scenarios requiring joint manipulation and partner assistance under egocentric visual constraints.
- The benchmark features ten distinct tasks, including eight two-agent and two three-agent configurations, supported by synchronized multi-operator VR teleoperation demonstrations that capture realistic human coordination patterns.
- Initial testing with state-of-the-art visuomotor policies reveals substantial difficulties in achieving coordinated perception and control, highlighting the complexity of multi-humanoid collaboration and identifying key areas for algorithmic improvement.
