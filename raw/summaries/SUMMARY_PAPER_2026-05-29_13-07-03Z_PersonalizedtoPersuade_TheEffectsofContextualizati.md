## Summary  
The paper investigates how contextualization combined with conversational warmth influences trust and reliance on an AI assistant that argues against expert advice. It finds that contextualization alone weakens persuasion, but when paired with warmth it restores persuasiveness through a crossover effect.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Contextualization reduces the AI’s persuasive power because users are less convinced by explanations tailored to their background.  
- The combination of contextualization and warmth restores persuasion, showing a crossover interaction that depends on both design elements.  
- Trust remains the strongest predictor of both persuasion and reliance, while AI literacy decouples trust from behavior, leading more literate users to be less trusting yet more persuaded and reliant.  

## Context  
This work contributes to ongoing debates about the persuasive capabilities of conversational agents in everyday decision‑making contexts. It highlights that interface design choices, though limited, can modulate trust and reliance dynamics.  

## Implications  
For developers, these findings suggest that personalization should be balanced with warmth to avoid unintended persuasion effects. Practitioners may need to consider AI literacy among users when deploying trust‑sensitive assistants.
