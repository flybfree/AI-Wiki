## Summary
This work presents SciSchema.org, a multidisciplinary set of 16 expert‑annotated schemas that capture the structured description of scientific processes across multiple fields. The schemas standardize inputs, outputs, materials, instruments, parameters, steps, measurements, and provenance information for reproducibility and automation.

## Key Takeaways
- The collection includes schemas for Biology & Biotechnology, Materials & Chemistry, Imaging & Measurement, Physics, and Psychology, each defining reusable fields such as inputs, outputs, materials, instruments or software, parameters, conditions, procedural steps, measurements, and provenance‑related data.  
- Schema development used a human‑in‑the‑loop workflow where large language models generated candidate structures from process specifications, articles, and expert feedback before domain experts refined them into final JSON Schema and SHACL formats.  
- The dataset also contains intermediate model schemas, expert‑feedback records, source‑paper metadata, community‑development materials, analysis scripts, and technical validation results assessing structure, provenance, expert review, and syntactic conformance.

## Context
The paper addresses the fragmentation of scientific process descriptions that hampers comparison, reproducibility, reuse, and automation in AI research. By providing a unified set of schemas, it aligns with broader efforts to create structured knowledge representations for machine‑readable scientific data.

## Implications
SciSchema.org enables automated annotation, metadata enrichment, and integration into semantic publishing pipelines, supporting knowledge graphs and cross‑study comparisons across disciplines. Practitioners can leverage these schemas to build reproducible workflows and improve the interoperability of scientific literature in AI research.
