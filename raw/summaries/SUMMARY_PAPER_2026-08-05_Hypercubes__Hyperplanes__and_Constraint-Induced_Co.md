## Summary
This paper revisits higher‑arity atomic concept learning by analyzing the geometry of hypercubes and hyperplanes formed from ground instances. It shows that most hyperplanes collapse into a finite number of elementary equivalence classes, while only the full diagonal grows unboundedly, revealing an asymmetry rooted in reduction theory.

## Key Takeaways
- The logical complexity of r‑dimensional hypercubes is organized by hyperplanes, with each non‑full diagonal collapsing to finitely many classes independent of term depth.  
- This geometric asymmetry mirrors the reduction‑theoretic structure of concepts, producing a taxonomy that localizes complexity rather than spreading it uniformly.  
- The three‑dimensional case already demonstrates orthogonal families and partial diagonals alongside the exceptional full diagonal.

## Context
Higher‑arity atomic concept learning remains a challenge in AI because traditional models assume uniform complexity across instance space. This work provides a geometric‑logical lens that explains where complexity concentrates, offering a fresh perspective on constrained hypothesis spaces.

## Implications
For practitioners, understanding hyperplane collapse can guide the design of more efficient constraint‑induced learners and improve model interpretability. The insights may inform industry applications seeking robust classification under limited computational resources.
