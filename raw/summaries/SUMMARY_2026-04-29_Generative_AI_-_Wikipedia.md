## Summary
Generative AI is a subfield of artificial intelligence that uses generative models to create text, images, videos, audio, code and other data from prompts. The article outlines its rapid growth since the 2020s boom, key applications across industries, ethical concerns such as copyright and bias, and regulatory responses worldwide.

## Key Takeaways
- Generative AI tools like ChatGPT, DALL‑E and Stable Diffusion have become mainstream, enabling text‑to‑image and code generation.  
- The technology is used in high‑impact sectors including healthcare drug discovery, finance report drafting, entertainment content creation and customer service automation.  
- Major concerns include copyright infringement lawsuits, deepfake misuse for cybercrime, environmental costs of large data centers, and potential job displacement in creative professions.

## Context
Generative AI builds on transformer architectures introduced in 2017, which replaced older recurrent networks with self‑attention mechanisms. Since 2020 the field has seen explosive adoption driven by cloud services, open‑source models such as LLaMA and Stable Diffusion, and a surge of private investment, especially in Asia‑Pacific markets where adoption rates exceed global averages.

## Implications
For developers, integrating generative AI raises questions about data privacy, model licensing and the need for human oversight to avoid biased or copyrighted outputs. Companies must also consider regulatory compliance, such as watermarking generated content and adhering to emerging AI legislation in the EU and China, while stakeholders like educators and artists grapple with impacts on academic integrity and creative livelihoods.
