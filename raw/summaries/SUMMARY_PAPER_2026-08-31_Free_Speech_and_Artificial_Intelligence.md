## Summary
This paper examines how artificial intelligence reshapes free speech by focusing on two AI technologies: social media recommendation algorithms and conversational chatbots powered by large language models. It argues that algorithmic control over content visibility raises philosophical questions about freedom of expression, while also questioning whether chatbot regulation can be justified given users’ right to receive information.

## Key Takeaways
- Social media platforms wield influence through recommendation systems, which shape what speech is seen and heard, thereby implicating corporate decisions in the public sphere’s discourse.  
- The paper distinguishes between granting speech rights to AI agents versus protecting users’ right to access information, suggesting that regulation may be illegitimate if it only targets one side of the interaction.  
- It raises uncertainty about whether corporations or developers possess speech rights, highlighting a gap in existing legal frameworks.

## Context
The rapid deployment of AI tools is altering how societies communicate and what counts as protected expression. Scholars have long debated free speech principles, but new technologies like recommendation engines and chatbots introduce novel challenges that traditional theories do not fully address. This work situates those debates within the current discourse on digital public spheres.

## Implications
For policymakers and industry leaders, the findings suggest a need for nuanced regulation that balances corporate autonomy with user rights. The paper’s emphasis on algorithmic influence may prompt legal reforms to clarify who holds speech rights in AI‑driven platforms.
