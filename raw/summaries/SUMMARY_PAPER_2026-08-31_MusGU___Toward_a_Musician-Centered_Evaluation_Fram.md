## Summary
This paper introduces MusGU+, a musician‑centered evaluation framework that assesses generative music AI models across Adaptability, Usability, and Controllability. The authors evaluate ten representative systems and present an interactive discovery tool that lets musicians filter models based on these criteria.

## Key Takeaways
- Adaptability is defined as the model’s ability to be trained or fine‑tuned on a user’s personal data without compromising privacy or requiring extensive retraining, which directly affects how customizable the system can become for individual workflows.  
- Usability captures whether the interface integrates smoothly into existing music production pipelines, offering intuitive controls that reduce cognitive load and enable real‑time collaboration with other musicians.  
- Controllability refers to the extent to which users can shape musical output in musically meaningful ways, such as adjusting timbre, rhythm, or harmonic structure through expressive parameters rather than arbitrary sliders.

## Context
Generative music AI is rapidly advancing, but existing evaluation methods focus on openness and qualitative user feedback rather than systematic comparison. This gap hampers the ability of creators to choose models that fit their specific needs, limiting both research reproducibility and practical adoption in creative industries.

## Implications
MusGU+ provides a concrete benchmark for evaluating generative music tools, encouraging developers to design systems with clear adaptability, usability, and controllability features. By offering an interactive discovery tool, the framework empowers musicians to make informed decisions early in model selection, fostering innovation and reducing friction between AI capabilities and creative practice.
