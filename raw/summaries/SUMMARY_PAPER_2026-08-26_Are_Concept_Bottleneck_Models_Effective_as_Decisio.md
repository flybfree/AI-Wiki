## Summary
The paper investigates whether Concept Bottleneck Models (CBMs) can serve as effective decision‑support tools by conducting two large‑scale user studies involving 705 participants and 6,959 observations. The results indicate that CBMs, especially their interactive component, improve the accuracy of human‑AI teams compared to unaided humans or non‑interpretable AI support.

## Key Takeaways
- CBMs can boost human‑AI team accuracy relative to both unaided human performance and performance with non‑interpretable AI when classification tasks are perceived as difficult and concepts are easily identifiable.  
- The improvement only emerges under specific conditions: tasks that users find challenging, concepts that are clearly recognizable, and active interaction with the model’s concept explanations.  
- Inaccurate concept detection may undermine users' trust in the model, which can diminish the overall effectiveness of CBMs as decision‑support systems.

## Context
Concept Bottleneck Models aim to make neural networks interpretable by extracting human‑understandable concepts from inputs and using those concepts for predictions. As AI systems become more opaque in high‑stakes domains, there is a growing need for tools that can provide clear explanations without sacrificing performance. This study adds empirical evidence that interpretability can translate into real performance gains when paired with user engagement.

## Implications
Practitioners should prioritize CBMs that are both accurate and interactive for tasks where human expertise is critical, while monitoring concept detection errors to maintain trust. The findings guide the integration of explainable AI in collaborative decision workflows, emphasizing that effective decision‑support depends on reliable concept extraction and active user interaction.
