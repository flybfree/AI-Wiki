## Summary
The paper investigates how a humanoid robotic head is accepted in three public spaces and evaluates its multimodal natural language interaction. It reports that users generally found the robot useful and easy to use, though response time occasionally hindered dialog flow. The TAM2 questionnaire supports these observations across locations.

## Key Takeaways
- Users were motivated to use the robot and rated it as useful and easy regardless of location, indicating strong acceptance.
- Public spaces such as tourist information desks and city libraries proved more suitable than office environments like the building authority where interaction willingness was lower.
- Every fifth user experienced slow response times that disrupted dialog flow, a performance issue needing improvement.

## Context
This study contributes to affective computing by integrating emotion simulation into real‑world robotic dialogue, showing how human‑like responses affect acceptance. It also highlights the importance of contextual fit in deploying interactive AI systems beyond controlled labs.

## Implications
For developers, the findings suggest that multimodal language capabilities should be paired with low latency processing for seamless user experience. Practitioners can prioritize public‑space deployments where users are more willing to engage, reserving office settings for specialized tasks requiring higher reliability.
