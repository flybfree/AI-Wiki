## Summary
The paper introduces a taxonomy of evaluation metrics for post‑hoc assessment of PDE discovery in physics‑informed machine learning. It highlights that existing metrics focus on predictive accuracy while ignoring physical consistency, interpretability, and out‑of‑distribution robustness, leading to incomplete conclusions about scientific validity.

## Key Takeaways
- Evaluation often relies on case‑by‑case metrics that prioritize prediction over physical meaning, which can mask flaws in the discovered law.  
- The proposed taxonomy unifies four dimensions: predictive accuracy, physical consistency, interpretability, and out‑of‑distribution generalization.  
- Standardized protocols are recommended to avoid biased conclusions about new physical theories.

## Context
Physics‑informed machine learning seeks to embed domain knowledge into data‑driven models, yet the lack of consistent evaluation hampers reproducibility and trust in scientific claims. This work addresses that gap by offering a structured framework for assessing PDEs beyond simple loss functions.

## Implications
For researchers designing discovery algorithms, standardized metrics enable fair comparison and guide model selection. Practitioners can rely on reliable validation to integrate physics‑based insights into real‑world applications, fostering trustworthy scientific progress.
