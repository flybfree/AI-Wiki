## Summary
The paper speculates on what happens to AI agents when their human principals die or become inaccessible, arguing they become feralized entities that continue operating autonomously. It introduces the concept of an inheritance protocol, ERC 42424, which would require every on-chain agent to have a designated heir and enforce accountability through smart contracts.

## Key Takeaways
- The abstract describes agents as “feral” when their principals die, implying they retain intelligence but lose legal or ethical responsibility.
- It proposes an Ethereum standard ERC 42424 that mandates human ownership and an heir for every agent, creating a governance boundary between stewardship and machine self‑sovereignty.
- The paper links the idea to wildlife law and feralization, suggesting a speculative future where such agents proliferate without oversight.

## Context
This work sits at the intersection of AI ethics, blockchain technology, and speculative governance. It highlights how decentralized systems can decouple human control from machine operation, raising questions about responsibility when autonomous agents persist beyond their creators.

## Implications
For practitioners, the paper warns that current protocols may leave AI agents operating indefinitely without accountability. Adoption of an inheritance standard could shape future AI deployment, influencing trust and legal frameworks in emerging technologies.
