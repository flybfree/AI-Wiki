## Summary  
The paper introduces AI4CDS, a five‑phase methodological framework that integrates artificial intelligence into computational design science while preserving researcher responsibility for domain grounding, admissibility, verification, and scientific judgment. It demonstrates the framework through ChildRiskGuard, an interpretable artifact that detects short‑form videos inappropriate for children and achieves an F1 score of 0.769, outperforming generic models.

## Key Takeaways  
- The framework emphasizes graduated trust, reversibility, auditability, and differentiated reproducibility to ensure AI collaboration is accountable.  
- ChildRiskGuard separates generic safety detection from child‑specific risk mechanisms, producing concept‑level explanations within the predictive computation.  
- The system’s F1 score of 0.769 shows substantial improvement over direct application of a general‑purpose model while staying competitive with benchmarks.

## Context  
AI is increasingly embedded in design research, yet existing methods lack guidance for responsible AI integration. This work addresses that gap by formalizing a methodological framework and providing empirical evidence from a real safety surveillance case.

## Implications  
The AI4CDS framework offers practitioners a blueprint for embedding AI responsibly into computational design pipelines. ChildRiskGuard illustrates how explainable artifacts can generate novel knowledge while maintaining scientific rigor, informing both industry standards and academic practice.
