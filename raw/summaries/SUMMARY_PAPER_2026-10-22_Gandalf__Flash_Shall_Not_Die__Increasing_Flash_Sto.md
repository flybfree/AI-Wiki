## Summary
This paper introduces Gandalf, the first SSD-based KV cache offloading system designed with explicit awareness of SSD lifetime constraints in LLM serving environments. The authors identify that production workloads such as multi-round conversations and LLM agents generate KV cache write volumes far exceeding SSD capacity, reducing drive lifespan from years to mere months. Gandalf addresses this by incorporating application-level attention-type information into the cache admission policy, prioritizing full-attention KV cache entries for SSD storage, which extends SSD lifetime by 7.3X on average while maintaining negligible impact on cache hit rates.

## Key Takeaways
- Production LLM workloads, including multi-round conversations and agentic systems, produce cumulative KV cache writes that significantly exceed the physical capacity of SSDs used for offloading. This write amplification shortens SSD operational lifetime from the expected multi-year range down to just a few months, creating a critical infrastructure reliability and cost problem for deployed LLM serving systems.
- A naive mitigation strategy of gating KV cache writes based solely on historical access patterns fails because KV caches generated by different attention mechanisms (e.g., full attention versus sliding-window attention) can exhibit identical historical access signatures yet diverge sharply in their future access patterns. This mismatch causes the gating approach to discard caches that will later be needed, substantially lowering hit rates and increasing overall serving costs.
- Gandalf's core design principle is to combine access-pattern analysis with application-level metadata, specifically the attention type associated with each KV cache entry. By prioritizing admission of full-attention KV cache into SSD over caches produced by other attention types, Gandalf achieves a 7.3X average extension in SSD lifetime across multiple workload traces and a wide range of hardware capacities, with negligible degradation in cache hit rate. The architecture is also designed to extend to other flash-based storage technologies such as High Bandwidth Flash.

## Context
As large language models scale in parameter count and context window length, the KV cache footprint grows rapidly, quickly surpassing the capacity of GPU HBM and host DRAM. The industry has responded by offloading overflow KV cache to NVMe SSDs to leverage their high I/O bandwidth, but this approach introduces a hidden durability crisis that prior systems have largely ignored. Gandalf represents a shift from treating SSDs as infinite-capacity storage tiers to treating them as finite-lifetime resources that must be managed with application-aware policies, bridging the gap between storage engineering and LLM serving architecture.

## Implications
For practitioners deploying LLM serving infrastructure at scale, Gandalf demonstrates that SSD lifetime degradation is not merely a hardware concern but a system-design problem solvable through smarter cache admission policies that respect application semantics. The 7.3X lifetime improvement directly translates into lower total cost of ownership, fewer drive replacements, and reduced operational disruption for production LLM services. Furthermore, the design's extensibility to emerging flash technologies like High Bandwidth Flash suggests that attention-type-aware admission policies could become a general principle for future heterogeneous memory hierarchies in AI serving stacks.
