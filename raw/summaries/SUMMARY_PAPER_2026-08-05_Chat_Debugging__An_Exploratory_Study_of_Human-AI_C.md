## Summary
The paper investigates how undergraduate students use chat-based debugging of analog circuits with large language models and finds that both humans and LLMs contribute valuable insights while revealing limitations in image reasoning and confidence. The study shows multimodal usage patterns and that LLM suggestions are often sensible but sometimes overconfident.

## Key Takeaways
- Students frequently combine visual sketches with textual queries, indicating a multimodal workflow where images trigger specific LLM prompts.
- LLMs provide domain knowledge and practical debugging steps, yet they struggle with interpreting 2D/3D circuit diagrams and may express certainty without justification.
- Undergraduate participants often lack foundational analog theory, leading to reliance on LLM answers that fill gaps but do not build deeper understanding.

## Context
This research aligns with growing interest in human-AI collaboration for technical problem solving, where LLMs are expected to augment rather than replace expert knowledge. The findings highlight the need for multimodal interfaces that can handle both text and image inputs effectively.

## Implications
For educators, integrating chat debugging into curricula could democratize access to circuit analysis tools while fostering critical thinking. Industry practitioners should design LLM prompts that acknowledge uncertainty and encourage human verification to ensure safety and reliability.
