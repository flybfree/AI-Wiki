## Summary
This paper introduces ANASSA, an agentic AI orchestration framework designed to overcome the fragmented integration of reasoning, execution, and evaluation that currently limits geographic information systems. By synthesizing recent advances in large language models and multimodal architectures, the authors propose a unified system architecture that embeds structured spatial reasoning, multi-agent workflow management, uncertainty handling, and human oversight into a cohesive blueprint. The study delivers an architecture-level specification rather than empirical results, establishing foundational components intended to make autonomous geospatial workflows traceable, reproducible, and accountable.

## Key Takeaways
- Existing agentic GIS systems suffer from fragmented integration of reasoning, execution, and evaluation, particularly when handling complex real-world spatial tasks that require robust validation and uncertainty management.
- ANASSA introduces a comprehensive architecture comprising eleven components across four distinct layers, alongside a six-step Geospatial AI Cognitive Loop designed to standardize how agentic systems interpret user intent and execute geospatial analyses.
- The framework emphasizes governance mechanisms including authoritative spatial validation, detailed provenance tracking, uncertainty quantification, and explicit human decision authority to ensure workflows remain traceable, reproducible, and accountable.

## Context
The rapid advancement of large language models and multimodal AI has catalyzed a shift toward autonomous agents capable of executing complex tool-based tasks across various domains. In geospatial analysis, this evolution is particularly significant as traditional GIS workflows have historically relied on manual expert intervention rather than automated reasoning pipelines. This paper addresses a critical gap in the broader AI landscape by formalizing how agentic systems can be reliably integrated into spatial intelligence applications without compromising accuracy or oversight.

## Implications
For practitioners and industry stakeholders, ANASSA provides a standardized architectural blueprint that reduces the friction of deploying autonomous geospatial agents in production environments. By embedding governance, provenance tracking, and human-in-the-loop controls directly into the system design, organizations can mitigate risks associated with AI hallucination and unverified spatial outputs. This framework lays the groundwork for future empirical studies and scalable implementations across urban planning, environmental monitoring, and infrastructure management sectors.
