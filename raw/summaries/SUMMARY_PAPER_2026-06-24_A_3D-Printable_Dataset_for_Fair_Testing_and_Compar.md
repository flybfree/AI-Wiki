## Summary
This paper presents a 3D‑printable texture dataset created from mathematically defined patterns to enable fair testing of tactile sensors. The study shows that print quality, especially peak sharpness and stringing, influences tactile variance, with high‑end printers yielding more consistent sensor readings across the textures.

## Key Takeaways
- Print quality significantly affects tactile variability, as higher‑resolution prints produce smoother surface signatures.
- Neural network classifiers achieve strong within‑printer generalisation but struggle with cross‑printer performance due to geometric inconsistencies.
- The dataset provides a reproducible benchmark that can be fabricated reliably across multiple 3D printers and filament types.

## Context
Tactile sensing is crucial for robotics and human‑machine interaction, yet existing texture datasets are limited by sensor‑specific interactions rather than describing textures objectively. This work addresses the need for a neutral reference that supports reproducible AI research in tactile perception.

## Implications
For researchers, the benchmark enables fair model comparisons across different hardware setups. For industry practitioners, it offers a practical tool to evaluate sensor performance under consistent physical conditions, accelerating development and deployment of reliable tactile systems.
