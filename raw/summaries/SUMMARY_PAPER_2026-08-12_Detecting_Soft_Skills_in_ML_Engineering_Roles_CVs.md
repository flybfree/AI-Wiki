## Summary
This paper investigates how machine learning engineers, data scientists, and software engineers describe soft skills in their CVs, moving beyond keyword extraction to capture narrative expressions of competencies. Using a balanced corpus of 300 curated resumes, the authors validate an LLM‑based pipeline that distinguishes explicitly listed from implicitly narrated soft skills and test eleven hypotheses about role signatures, seniority effects, and disclosure patterns.

## Key Takeaways
- Candidates disclose soft skills through narrative rather than keyword lists by roughly three to one, especially for leadership, coordination, and mentoring (88‑96% narrative).  
- Seniority nearly triples the odds of articulating leadership, indicating that this competency is not universally expressed across experience levels.  
- Technical candidates do articulate soft skills, but a keyword‑based screening systematically misses them.

## Context
The demand side literature on soft skills in ML engineering roles relies heavily on job advertisements and hiring manager surveys, which capture what employers seek rather than how candidates self‑present these competencies. Existing CV‑mining tools are limited to simple keyword matching or frequency rankings without statistical validation against candidate data.

## Implications
Understanding narrative disclosure can improve fairer hiring practices by recognizing that soft skills are often communicated implicitly in resumes. Practitioners should consider using LLM‑enhanced extraction methods to capture these subtle signals and avoid missing valuable candidates whose soft skills are not explicitly listed.
