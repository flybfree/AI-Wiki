## Summary
This paper evaluates six deep‑learning weather emulators and traditional baselines for predicting global near‑surface temperature and extreme heat up to 15 days in advance. The results show that several emulators match or exceed physics‑based forecasts in deterministic skill, though they lose spectral detail due to blurring.

## Key Takeaways
- Several AI emulators rival or surpass conventional models in short‑term temperature prediction but sacrifice fine spatial resolution because of the blurring effect inherent to deep networks.  
- All evaluated models underestimate peak heat intensities, meaning extreme warnings are often weaker than reality.  
- The integrated forecasting system (IFS) retains higher recall for extreme events compared with any of the AI emulators.

## Context
The study highlights how artificial intelligence can extend the reach of weather prediction beyond the traditional ten‑day window, offering a promising alternative to classical dynamical systems when data are limited. This work aligns with broader efforts to embed machine learning into climate services to cope with rapid warming and increasing heat extremes.

## Implications
For meteorological agencies, these findings suggest that AI emulators can be integrated as complementary tools rather than replacements for physics‑based models, improving long‑range forecasts at the expense of detail. Practitioners should monitor spectral fidelity and extreme event representation when deploying such systems in operational early‑warning frameworks.
