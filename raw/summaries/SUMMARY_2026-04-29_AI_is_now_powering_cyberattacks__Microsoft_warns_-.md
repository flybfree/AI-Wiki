Title: AI is now powering cyberattacks, Microsoft warns - Fox News
Article text:

## Summary
Microsoft Threat Intelligence reports that cybercriminals are now leveraging artificial intelligence to accelerate and simplify attacks, from phishing emails to malware development, effectively turning AI into a “force multiplier” for hackers while keeping human operators in control. The article highlights how AI lowers technical barriers, speeds up each stage of an attack, and enables nation‑state groups such as Jasper Sleet to infiltrate organizations through fake remote workers.

## Key Takeaways
- Generative AI helps attackers write convincing phishing emails, translate scams into multiple languages, summarize stolen data, generate malware code, and build scripts and infrastructure.  
- AI reduces the time between attack stages from hours or days to minutes by automating tasks such as creating realistic fake identities and job‑application resumes.  
- Attackers are experimenting with “jailbreaking” prompts to bypass AI safety rules and using agentic AI that can perform autonomous tasks, though Microsoft says current tools mainly assist rather than replace human operators.  
- The rise of AI also gives defenders new capabilities: security teams use AI to analyze billions of signals daily, detect anomalies faster, and respond more quickly.

## Context
Artificial intelligence has long promised efficiency gains across industries, but its integration into cybersecurity flips the script by empowering malicious actors. Microsoft’s findings underscore that while AI can automate routine hacking steps, it does not eliminate the need for sophisticated human strategy or intent. This trend reflects a broader shift where tools once exclusive to experts become accessible to anyone with basic prompting skills.

## Implications
The article suggests that as AI continues to lower the barrier for launching attacks, organizations must adopt stronger identity protections and monitor unusual remote‑worker activity to counter insider threats. For cybersecurity practitioners, the challenge is to balance the defensive advantages of AI detection systems against the offensive potential of AI‑assisted hacking, ensuring that vigilance remains a critical human element in the evolving arms race.
