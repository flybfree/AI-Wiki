## Summary
The paper proposes a taxonomy of verbal‑nonverbal mismatch categories and tests whether large language models can select context‑appropriate mismatched behaviors for virtual humans. It evaluates the model’s outputs in a human study to see if they produce intended social effects. The results show that LLMs can generate nonverbal cues that align with speaker roles, relationships, and hidden emotional states.

## Key Takeaways
- The taxonomy identifies multiple ways verbal and nonverbal channels may reinforce, weaken, qualify, or contradict each other, revealing internal emotional leakage beyond speech content.
- The study demonstrates that LLMs can select mismatched behaviors from a dialogue context that reflect speaker roles and social dynamics, not just lexical meaning.
- Human observers responded positively to the virtual human’s nuanced nonverbal cues, indicating they convey intended relational meanings.

## Context
Current virtual‑agent systems often treat nonverbal behavior as a deterministic function of speech, ignoring the complex interplay of role, context, and emotion that shapes real human interaction. This paper contributes by treating those factors as variables for model selection, aligning with broader AI research on multimodal coherence.

## Implications
For counseling simulations and other nuanced training contexts, integrating LLMs to generate socially aware mismatched cues can improve realism and therapeutic effectiveness. Practitioners may leverage these methods to create virtual humans that better simulate human relational dynamics.
