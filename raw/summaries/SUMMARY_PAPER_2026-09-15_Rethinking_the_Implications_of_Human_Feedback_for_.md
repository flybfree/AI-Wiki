## Summary
This paper addresses a critical limitation in human-robot collaboration where robots rely on fixed implication rules to translate direct human feedback into preference labels for unchosen actions. The authors demonstrate that these rigid rules frequently misalign with actual human reasoning, hindering effective reward model training. To overcome this, they introduce IMPLIED, a dynamic learning framework that treats initial implications as starting points and continuously refines them based on observed interactions, significantly improving preference estimation and robot behavior adaptation.

## Key Takeaways
- Standard fixed-rule implication methods in human-robot interaction frequently misrepresent how humans actually reason about unchosen actions, leading to suboptimal reward model updates during preference learning.
- The proposed IMPLIED framework dynamically learns and revises accepted or rejected action labels over time rather than relying on static rules,
