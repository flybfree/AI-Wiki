## Summary
The paper investigates the psychological impacts of AI adoption in software engineering after a year of implementation in a large services firm, using qualitative interviews with 21 professionals. It identifies anxiety, identity disruption, meaning erosion, workload intensification and uncertainty distress as key costs.

## Key Takeaways
- Accountability anxiety arises as professionals feel responsible for AI-generated outputs.
- Craft identity disruption occurs when AI replaces traditional coding tasks, threatening professional self‑concept.
- Meaning and satisfaction erosion is observed due to loss of purpose and increased stress.

## Context
AI integration in software engineering extends beyond code generation to include test case creation and review assistance, prompting organizations to adopt strategies focused on productivity gains. This shift disrupts established team norms and role identities that were previously stable.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings highlight the need for policies that preserve autonomy and identity while integrating AI tools. The study calls for viewing AI adoption as a human transition rather than merely a technological upgrade.
