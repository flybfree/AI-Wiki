## Summary
The paper introduces TRIBE, a domain‑independent framework that uncovers communication patterns within teams and classifies them into behavioral tribes that predict performance as early as 10% of task execution. Experiments on four datasets show that these communication‑based predictions improve team outcomes while traditional metrics lag behind. The temporal analysis also reveals that AI agents reshape team dynamics, whereas human advisors preserve natural trajectories.

## Key Takeaways
- Communication patterns can be used to predict performance early in a task, forming behavioral tribes that are visible even before 10% of work is completed.
- Prediction accuracy varies with how much freedom the task structure provides for individual behavior, indicating that structured tasks yield stronger signals.
- AI agents significantly alter team communication trajectories, while human advisors tend to align with existing natural dynamics.

## Context
In autonomous agent design, understanding subtle social and communicative cues is essential for effective collaboration. Traditional performance metrics often overlook these cues, leading to blind spots in early-stage interventions. TRIBE addresses this gap by leveraging observable behavioral data, offering a more holistic view of team health.

## Implications
For practitioners developing AI‑assisted teams, TRIBE provides actionable insights that can be integrated into advisory systems without task‑specific knowledge. This approach could enhance intervention timing and personalize support, ultimately boosting productivity across diverse domains.
