## Summary  
The paper evaluates six multimodal large language models on a standardized scientific visualization literacy test, revealing that Gemini outperforms human averages while open‑source alternatives fall short. The benchmark demonstrates uneven performance across different visualization techniques and tasks, highlighting a gap in current MLLM capabilities for scientific visual understanding.

## Key Takeaways  
- Current MLLMs do not exhibit uniform SciVis literacy; their abilities vary significantly depending on the type of scientific illustration or task they are given.  
- Gemini is the strongest model overall, achieving scores above the human mean across all evaluated subsets of the test.  
- Open‑source models remain below the human baseline, particularly struggling with texture‑based and integration‑based visualizations as well as quantitative estimation tasks.

## Context  
Multimodal large language models are increasingly deployed to interpret scientific visualizations, yet existing evaluations focus mainly on chart comprehension without measuring deeper literacy skills. This paper addresses that gap by introducing a comprehensive benchmark that spans multiple visualization techniques and task types, providing a more holistic view of AI performance in scientific contexts.

## Implications  
For researchers, the results underscore the need for standardized assessment metrics that capture fine‑grained understanding beyond surface‑level chart reading. In industry, developers must consider these limitations when selecting models for applications requiring precise quantitative or spatial reasoning from visual data.
