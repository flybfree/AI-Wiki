## Summary
The paper argues that data citation for large language models is an open challenge distinct from grounding citations in textual documents. It proposes three research directions to make model outputs verifiable, traceable, and creditable to data creators. The authors set out a framework linking training attribution, inference‑time citation, and knowledge graph referencing.

## Key Takeaways
- Training data attribution must convert influence estimates into explicit references for corpora that become part of the model’s parameters.
- Data citation at inference time needs to identify datasets, subsets, and query results with appropriate granularity and fixity.
- Citing knowledge‑graph facts requires defining what a reference to a single triple means and how credit propagates along provenance.

## Context
Large language models increasingly generate information without explicit citations, raising questions about accountability and reproducibility. This work highlights the gap between textual citation practices and the need for data‑level attribution in AI systems.

## Implications
If solved, these methods could enable transparent model audits, protect intellectual property of data curators, and improve trustworthiness of AI outputs across industries.
