## Summary
The paper introduces AfS (Agent for Science), a specialized platform designed for long-horizon scientific research projects that span tens of hours and numerous agent runs with minimal human intervention. Unlike general coding agents prone to fabrication or omission under pressure, AfS enforces research discipline through mechanically enforced laws and structural constraints that render non-compliant states unrepresentable. The system utilizes a three-tier temporal architecture comprising minimal research nodes, hash-chained artifact ledgers, and a two-tier knowledge base to ensure integrity without relying solely on model compliance.

## Key Takeaways
- AfS shifts the burden of credibility from prompting models to behave correctly to engineering a system where non-compliant states are structurally impossible, employing mechanisms like "commitment before measurement" and "unforgeable freezing" to prevent fabrication or smoothing over results.
- The platform organizes research across three time horizons: a minimal set of nodes within a single run, an inquiry contract with frozen closure conditions supported by a hash-chained artifact ledger for project-level integrity, and a two-tier knowledge base that promotes information via rewriting across multiple projects
