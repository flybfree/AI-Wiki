## Summary
The paper introduces READI, a multimodal benchmark designed to evaluate indirect speech act understanding in visual contexts. Experiments reveal that even state‑of‑the‑art models struggle with visually grounded ISAs, especially in high‑context languages like Korean, and their performance declines as indirectness increases.

## Key Takeaways
- Prior text‑based and existing multimodal benchmarks largely ignore context‑dependent pragmatic reasoning, instead focusing on explicit encoding or perceptual recognition.  
- READI frames the task as vision‑based pragmatic question answering (V‑PQA), allowing cross‑lingual evaluation in both English and Korean.  
- State‑of‑the‑art multimodal models show a clear degradation of performance when indirectness rises, underscoring the need for benchmarks that explicitly target contextual pragmatic reasoning.

## Context
This work addresses a gap in AI research where understanding speaker intent from multimodal cues—rather than surface form alone—is essential. It advances pragmatic NLP beyond traditional text‑only approaches and contributes to the broader challenge of grounding language meaning in visual context.

## Implications
For industry, systems must be able to interpret subtle conversational cues across languages to improve user interaction quality. Practitioners can leverage READI as a benchmark to develop more robust models that reliably handle indirect speech acts in real‑world applications.
