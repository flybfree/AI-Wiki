## Summary
The paper introduces a multi‑layer framework to map risks of AI agents in the workplace by linking agents, goals, and environment. Applied to 2,078 O*NET job tasks, it generates 8,356 labeled risk scenarios across augmentation and automation modes, validated with workers and an LLM judge. The resulting 15‑category taxonomy shows that augmentation can erode skills while erroneous actions dominate severe risks.

## Key Takeaways
- Augmentation is not inherently safe because overreliance on agents can gradually erode workers' skills and oversight.
- Erroneous Agent Actions accounts for the largest share of risk scenarios, with many occurring at the human‑agent boundary and being severe.
- Automation mainly creates organizational risks, whereas augmentation primarily threatens worker well‑being.

## Context
Current AI risk taxonomies are too broad to capture job‑specific hazards. This work bridges that gap by grounding taxonomy in a concrete interaction model of agents, goals, and environments, using real occupational data. The study thus offers a practical tool for assessing how AI deployment interacts with human labor processes.

## Implications
Organizations must design both safer agents and collaborative workflows to mitigate skill loss and misuse. Practitioners can leverage the 15‑category taxonomy to prioritize interventions, reducing organizational exposure while protecting employee capabilities.
