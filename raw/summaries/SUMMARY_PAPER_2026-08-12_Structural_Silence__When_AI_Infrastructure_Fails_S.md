## Summary  
This paper investigates how AI infrastructure disadvantages speakers of underrepresented languages, using Bengali as a case study in low‑connectivity education settings. It identifies four interlocking failures that create structural silence before any model can function effectively.

## Key Takeaways  
- Bengali accounts for less than 0.5% of global web content despite being spoken by nearly 4% of the world’s population, creating a severe web presence gap that limits data availability.  
- The training‑token deficit between English and Bengali is 67:1, meaning models receive far fewer Bengali tokens to learn from, which compounds the problem through higher token fertility in Bengali’s alphasyllabary script.  
- Rural internet penetration at 36.5% versus urban 71.4% excludes many speakers from online resources, illustrating connectivity exclusion that blocks real‑time AI services.

## Context  
AI tools are often promoted as scalable solutions for education gaps, yet their design defaults to dominant languages and high‑bandwidth environments. This study highlights how structural resource imbalances persist in datasets, tokenization, and deployment architectures, affecting model performance for marginalized linguistic communities.

## Implications  
Researchers must treat dataset scarcity as a systemic barrier rather than a technical hurdle, advocating offline‑first infrastructure to promote equity. Practitioners should prioritize inclusive data collection and low‑connectivity solutions to ensure AI benefits all language speakers.
