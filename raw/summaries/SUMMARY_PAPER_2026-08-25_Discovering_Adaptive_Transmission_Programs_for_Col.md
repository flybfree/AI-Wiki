## Summary
The paper proposes a framework for designing state‑aware transmission protocols that guide collective discovery tasks by routing information according to both agent and group states. Using LLM‑guided evolutionary search, the authors evolve protocols that outperform existing network‑only baselines by up to 37% in performance. Ablation studies show that only adding content‑dependence restores the advantage.

## Key Takeaways
- Evolved state‑aware protocols boost collective discovery outcomes compared with standard network‑based approaches.
- The improvement disappears when content dependence is removed, indicating that state awareness is essential.
- Protocols transfer across different domains and agent populations, showing robustness.

## Context
Collective intelligence research often treats networks as static structures, ignoring how agents’ knowledge influences sharing. This work introduces a formal model where protocols condition on both individual and collective states, aligning with emerging AI‑driven coordination tools.

## Implications
Designing such state‑aware programs could improve real‑world collaborative systems like crowdsourced problem solving or multi‑agent robotics. Practitioners can leverage evolutionary search to create adaptive coordination infrastructure that enhances human‑AI teamwork.
