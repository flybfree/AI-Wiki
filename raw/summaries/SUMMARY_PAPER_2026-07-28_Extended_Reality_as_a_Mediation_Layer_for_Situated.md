## Summary
The paper proposes Extended Reality as a mediation layer that enables situated human control in human‑robot teaming. It identifies four mediation functions and six design dimensions to make robot autonomy inspectable and adjustable in dynamic environments.

## Key Takeaways
- Situated human control requires the human collaborator to understand, shape, authorize, and interrupt robot action within concrete physical, social, and temporal contexts.
- Robot autonomy must remain inspectable and adjustable as people move, goals change, sensing is incomplete, roles shift, and plans become invalid.
- The six design dimensions—joint action possibilities, socio‑physical constraints, uncertainty and plan validity, multimodal control and correction, roles, handover, and accountability—provide a framework for XR systems.

## Context
This work situates XR within the emerging field of human‑robot collaboration, where trust and adaptability are critical. It contributes to AI research by formalizing how external interfaces mediate control between humans and autonomous agents in real‑world settings.

## Implications
Practitioners can use these dimensions to design safer, more responsive teaming systems that reduce errors and improve accountability. The framework supports industry adoption of XR as a strategic layer for human‑centric automation.
