## Summary
The paper investigates how on-demand AI assistance during logic puzzles influences skill development over time, finding that cheaper assistance leads to more frequent use and poorer later performance when assistance is removed. Using a Bayesian latent ability model the authors separate initial ability, post-AI ability, and individual skill change, showing that independent problem solving correlates with larger gains.

## Key Takeaways
- Lower-cost AI assistance results in higher frequency of requests during the AI-access phase.
- Participants who rely on AI while it is available perform worse afterward and overestimate their unassisted performance when predicted from earlier assisted scores.
- Independent reasoning effort is linked to greater increases in latent ability, indicating skill development weakens when AI substitutes for independent work.

## Context
This study addresses a central concern in human-AI collaboration: the trade‑off between immediate performance boosts and long‑term competence growth. By isolating the effect of assistance cost on learning trajectories, it contributes to debates about responsible AI deployment in educational settings.

## Implications
For educators and developers, the findings suggest that limiting AI support can preserve skill acquisition, while overly cheap assistance may erode learning benefits. Practitioners should design interfaces that encourage independent problem solving rather than automatic reliance on AI.
