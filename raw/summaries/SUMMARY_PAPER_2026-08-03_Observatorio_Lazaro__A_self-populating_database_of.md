## Summary  
The paper introduces Observatorio Lázaro, a self-populating database that tracks English anglicisms used in the Spanish digital press from 2020 to 2026. It details an end-to-end pipeline using neural sequence labeling and presents results showing over two million borrowings across millions of tokens.

## Key Takeaways  
- The system detects unassimilated anglicisms with a span-level F1 score of 0.86, indicating high accuracy in identifying borrowing spans.  
- Approximately two anglicisms per thousand tokens are used, and the rate remains stable over six years, showing an open growing class.  
- The vocabulary is most dense in fashion, technology, and lifestyle sections, while political news has lower usage.

## Context  
This work advances AI-driven linguistic monitoring by applying neural sequence labeling to large-scale digital corpora, demonstrating how such models can automatically generate diachronic lexical databases. It complements existing static borrowing dictionaries with a continuously updated record of neologisms in real time.

## Implications  
For language technology researchers, the database offers a benchmark for detecting and measuring anglicism trends across media outlets. Practitioners in digital publishing can use it to monitor emerging lexical shifts and adapt content strategies accordingly.
