## Summary
The paper introduces a human‑guided causal knowledge injection method for virtual cells that combines gene similarity awareness with causal graph visualization to improve interpretability of simulated cellular models. By employing a hybrid optimization algorithm and counterfactual analysis, the authors demonstrate that their approach yields scientifically meaningful insights and receives positive feedback from domain experts in two real‑world case studies.

## Key Takeaways
- The method creates a gene‑similarity‑aware causal graph visualization that jointly explores both the relationships between concepts and the similarities among genes, reducing errors inherent in unsupervised automatic constructions.  
- A hybrid optimization algorithm enables systematic exploration of these dual dimensions, allowing researchers to refine graphs based on empirical similarity measures.  
- Counterfactual analysis is supported by visualizations that validate and refine causal graphs, providing a feedback loop for expert‑driven improvement.

## Context
This work addresses the challenge of integrating interpretable causal knowledge into machine‑learned virtual cell models, where current unsupervised graph extraction often produces inaccurate or incomplete representations. By bridging AI‑generated similarity structures with human‑informed causality, the approach advances the field toward more reliable and actionable simulations in health and disease research.

## Implications
For researchers, this method offers a practical pathway to enhance model transparency without sacrificing predictive power, supporting evidence‑based decision making. Practitioners can leverage the counterfactual visualizations to validate causal claims before deployment, potentially accelerating drug discovery and therapeutic planning.
