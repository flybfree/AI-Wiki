## Summary
This paper surveys arXiv submissions from 2024 to 2026 that describe AI systems performing recursive self‑improvement and maps them onto a taxonomy of improvement axes and loop closure levels, highlighting the gap between bounded self‑refinement and open‑ended recursive self‑improvement.

## Key Takeaways
- Bounded self‑refinement is convergent, evaluable, and already practiced in industry, whereas RSI remains limited by grounding requirements, collapse dynamics, and compute constraints.  
- The taxonomy introduces a dedicated category for self‑evaluation where improvement loops claim that some signal can replace human judgment.  
- Demonstrated self‑improvement strength follows the hierarchy from formal verifiers (strongest) to intrinsic self‑assessment (weakest), with failure modes arising when this hierarchy is violated.

## Context
The rapid emergence of AI systems that modify their own behavior, policies, or research processes reflects a shift toward autonomous improvement loops. Understanding how these loops function and where they break down is crucial for aligning advanced AI with safety goals.

## Implications
Practitioners must design evaluation hierarchies carefully to avoid self‑confirming loops and model collapse. Governance frameworks need measurable metrics for RSI to track progress beyond human oversight, addressing a critical under‑researched area in frontier AI research.
