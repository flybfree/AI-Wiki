## Summary
The paper introduces a human‑in‑the‑loop AI agent that automatically compiles evidence for CTSA scholars and drafts one‑sentence Translational Science Benefits Model impact summaries, which staff review. In testing across ten KL2/K12 scholars, the agent produced 507 findings with an 81.7 % unanimous usable rate (accept or edit) while reviewers spent only a median of 14 minutes per scholar, cutting manual work from about 15 hours to under half that time.

## Key Takeaways
- The AI agent achieved an 81.7 % usable‑versus‑reject rate across two independent reviewers, indicating high acceptance or editing of its drafts despite moderate inter‑rater agreement (Cohen’s kappa 0.43).  
- Reviewers spent only a median of 14 minutes per scholar to edit the agent’s output, replacing an estimated 15 hours of manual data gathering and writing, demonstrating substantial time savings.  
- The agent’s evidence spanned all four TSBM domains and uncovered about a third of findings in non‑scholarly categories that routine processes often overlook.

## Context
This work contributes to the growing body of AI research on augmenting human workflows with automated documentation tools, showing how machine learning can reduce repetitive administrative tasks. By integrating real‑world data from multiple sources into concise impact statements, the approach aligns with broader goals of scaling scholarly impact reporting without sacrificing quality.

## Implications
For CTSA programs and similar research funding bodies, this AI agent offers a scalable solution that frees staff to focus on strategic oversight rather than manual compilation. Practitioners can adopt such agents to improve efficiency, ensure comprehensive evidence coverage, and maintain high‑quality impact summaries across large cohorts.
