## Summary
The paper introduces AEROBAT, an automated pipeline that turns a user‑specified AI behavior into a full scientific study from hypothesis generation to report writing. For 12 target behaviors it generated and tested 79 hypotheses, producing moderate‑to‑strong statistical evidence for 26 of them. The results demonstrate that automation can complement and extend the reach of manual research.

## Key Takeaways
- AEROBAT automatically designs controlled experiments and runs thousands of simulation rounds, turning a vague behavior request into a rigorous experimental plan.
- The system produced moderate‑to‑strong statistical support for many hypotheses, including novel insights that were not anticipated by human researchers.
- Automated generation of reports means the entire research cycle can be completed without manual intervention.

## Context
Current AI research often relies on manual experiments and subjective assessments, limiting scalability. This work shows a path to systematic investigation across many agents.

## Implications
For practitioners, AEROBAT reduces time and cost of behavior analysis, enabling rapid iteration. For the field, it opens possibilities for large‑scale behavioral benchmarking and hypothesis testing in AI development.
