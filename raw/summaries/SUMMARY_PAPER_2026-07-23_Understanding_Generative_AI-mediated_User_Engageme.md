## Summary
The paper examines how generative AI tools influence access to academic library resources between August 2023 and October 2025. It finds that AI‑mediated traffic spikes after linked citation features are added, with ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini as the main drivers.

## Key Takeaways
- AI retrieval mechanisms preferentially surface electronic theses and dissertations because these resources have structured metadata and stable permalinks that are Open Access and freely available.
- The integration of linked citation functionality in library catalogs appears to be a key catalyst for increased AI‑mediated traffic, indicating that discoverability improvements directly affect usage patterns.
- Users primarily reach institutional repositories through AI platforms, suggesting a shift from traditional search to AI‑generated pathways for accessing scholarly content.

## Context
This research contributes to the growing body of work on how generative AI reshapes information retrieval in academic settings. By linking library metadata practices with user behavior data, it offers insights into the practical implications of AI integration beyond theoretical models.

## Implications
Libraries must adopt structured metadata standards and stable permalinks to align with AI‑driven discovery trends. Practitioners should monitor AI platform usage and adjust catalog features accordingly to maximize resource visibility in an evolving digital ecosystem.
