## Summary
This study combines telehealth data from the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System and National Health Interview Survey with machine learning to classify self-reported chronic kidney disease status and uncover its drivers. The custom stacked ensemble model achieved balanced accuracy between 72.56% and 76.12% with AUROC scores ranging from 79.59% to 82.29%, demonstrating strong predictive performance.

## Key Takeaways
- The model identifies regular medical check-ups, age, blood pressure, and mental health stress indicators as top predictors of CKD risk.
- SHAP analysis combined with clinical review provides interpretable insights that translate into actionable risk stratification for clinicians.
- Balanced accuracy above 72% and AUROC above 79% show the approach can reliably separate CKD cases from non‑cases in large public datasets.

## Context
Machine learning models that integrate psychosocial factors with traditional health metrics are gaining traction to improve chronic disease management. This work exemplifies how federated data sources can be leveraged for population‑level risk prediction while preserving privacy and enabling scalable analysis.

## Implications
Clinicians can use these predictors to prioritize preventive care for high‑risk patients, reducing progression of CKD. The framework also offers a template for public health agencies seeking cost‑effective, AI‑driven surveillance tools that incorporate mental well‑being into chronic disease prevention strategies.
