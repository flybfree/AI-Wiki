## Summary
The paper proposes a framework to explore how LLMs encode personality traits as internal representations that interact with situations and produce behaviors. Using contrastive behavior pairs they uncover sparse features linked to trait poles, validate them across contexts, and show behavioral effects consistent with human personality research.

## Key Takeaways
- Contrastive behavior pairs reveal sparse internal features associated with opposing poles of personality traits through SAE decomposition.
- Feature-level interventions cause bidirectional shifts in trait-related responses across diverse situations while preserving response validity.
- Behavioral changes under the same interventions on social intelligence tasks show benefit-tradeoff patterns aligning with human personality findings.

## Context
This work extends AI research beyond surface outputs to examine whether models possess internal representations that mirror stable human traits, a longstanding challenge in understanding model behavior. By linking these representations to situational affordances and observable behaviors, the study bridges theory and practice in affective computing.

## Implications
For practitioners, this framework offers tools to diagnose and manipulate trait-like behaviors in LLMs, enabling more reliable and context‑aware applications. It also informs ethical considerations by exposing how controllable internal states can influence social interactions at scale.
