## Summary
This paper introduces AutoCog, an automated system that simulates human behavior using a foundation model and discovers decision‑theory hypotheses from simulated data. The findings show that several of these theories generalize to real human experiments, outperforming traditional models on held‑out tasks.  

## Key Takeaways
- AutoCog’s loop can surface theory‑discriminating hypotheses even when the simulator is imperfect because it only needs to capture distinguishing regularities rather than perfect behavior.  
- The simulated theories generalize to human data, matching or surpassing canonical models on ten held‑out experiments.  
- Imperfect simulators may actually broaden the search space of viable theories, allowing human data to act as a filter for generalization.  

## Context
Foundation models are increasingly used as behavioral stand‑ins in AI research, yet their capacity to mimic human cognition remains debated. This work demonstrates that automated discovery loops can produce hypotheses that are empirically testable beyond simulation, highlighting the potential of generative AI to inform cognitive science.  

## Implications
For researchers, AutoCog suggests a pathway to generate and validate theories without relying solely on human participants, accelerating hypothesis generation. Practitioners may leverage such tools to design experiments more efficiently, reducing costs while maintaining scientific rigor.
