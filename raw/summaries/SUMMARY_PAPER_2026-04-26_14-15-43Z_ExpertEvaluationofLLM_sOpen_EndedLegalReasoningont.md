## Summary
This paper introduces a dataset for evaluating large language models' open-ended legal reasoning on the Japanese bar exam writing task. It reports that expert evaluations show significant gaps in structured argument generation and frequent hallucinations. The findings highlight current limitations of LLMs in this specialized domain.

## Key Takeaways
- Expert evaluations reveal that LLM-generated responses often lack coherent, multi-step legal arguments required by the exam.
- Hallucinations are common when models fabricate precedents or statutes not present in the input narratives.
- The dataset demonstrates that current models struggle to identify and address multiple legal issues simultaneously.

## Context
Legal reasoning tasks demand precise synthesis of complex statutes and case law, a challenge for AI. This study fills a gap by applying LLMs to a real-world Japanese assessment, providing empirical insight into their performance in high-stakes environments.

## Implications
Practitioners should treat LLM outputs as drafts requiring human review before use. The results caution against deploying unvetted models in legal education or practice without rigorous validation.
