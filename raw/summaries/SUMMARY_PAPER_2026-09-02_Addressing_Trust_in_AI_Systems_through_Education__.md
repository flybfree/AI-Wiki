## Summary
This paper introduces ICE‑T, a didactic framework designed to reduce the opacity of machine learning and foster calibrated trust in AI systems. By integrating intermodal representation, computational thinking progression, and explanatory thinking, the authors demonstrate that these components directly support the cognitive mechanisms identified as drivers of appropriate reliance on AI.

## Key Takeaways
- Intermodal transfer rooted in Bruner’s enactive, iconic, and symbolic modes provides representational richness, allowing learners to experience ML concepts through multiple sensory channels.  
- The Use‑Modify‑Create progression operationalizes computational thinking, granting students graduated process control that mirrors the trust calibration literature’s emphasis on step‑wise understanding.  
- Explanatory thinking supported by a process model enables contextualization of errors, helping users situate failures within broader algorithmic contexts.

## Context
Machine learning education often treats algorithms as black boxes, limiting both learner comprehension and public confidence in AI applications. This gap is amplified across K‑12 curricula where diverse activities lack systematic integration of trust‑building pedagogies.

## Implications
Treating trust calibration as an explicit educational objective can transform how institutions design ML courses, leading to more transparent tools and responsible users. Practitioners should adopt scalable frameworks like ICE‑T to embed these cognitive mechanisms into curriculum design.
