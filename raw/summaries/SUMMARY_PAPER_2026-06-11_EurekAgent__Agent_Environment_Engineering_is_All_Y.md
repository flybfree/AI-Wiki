## Summary  
This paper introduces EurekAgent, a system that engineers the environment for autonomous scientific discovery to overcome the bottleneck of workflow design. The authors demonstrate that by tailoring permissions, artifact handling, budget constraints, and human‑in‑the‑loop interfaces, agents can achieve state‑of‑the‑art results across mathematics, kernel engineering, and machine learning tasks. Notably, EurekAgent discovered a 26‑circle packing solution with total API cost under $11.

## Key Takeaways  
- The paper argues that designing the execution environment is as crucial as prescribing agent workflows for autonomous discovery.  
- EurekAgent’s four engineering dimensions—permissions, artifact management, budget control, and human supervision—enable safe, open‑ended exploration and systematic collaboration.  
- Achieving breakthrough results at minimal cost shows that well‑engineered environments can outperform human‑designed approaches.

## Context  
Recent advances in large language models have enabled agents to propose and validate scientific solutions autonomously. However, the field still struggles with creating environments where agents can explore freely without excessive oversight or resource waste. This work addresses that gap by formalizing environment engineering as a core research direction.

## Implications  
Environment engineering will become essential for scaling autonomous research agents in industry and academia alike. By reducing human intervention and cost, it opens pathways to continuous, high‑impact scientific progress. Practitioners can adopt these four dimensions to build more reliable and efficient agent systems.
