## Summary
The paper introduces PsyBridge, a hybrid intelligent framework that integrates PHQ‑9, GAD‑7, cognitive and personality data to generate interpretable mental health risk assessments. Experimental results on 500 synthetic patient profiles show an overall accuracy of 0.84, outperforming standalone screening tools.

## Key Takeaways
- PsyBridge combines multiple clinical instruments into a single model using a weighted aggregation mechanism for improved interpretability.
- The framework achieves an accuracy of 0.84 across varying severity levels, which is higher than the performance of PHQ‑9 or GAD‑7 alone.
- Cognitive and personality components stabilize moderate‑risk predictions and reduce inconsistencies in classification.

## Context
Current AI mental health solutions often rely on isolated screening instruments, limiting both comprehensiveness and explainability. PsyBridge addresses this gap by merging quantitative scores with qualitative profiles within a modular architecture that supports multi‑dimensional evaluation.

## Implications
This framework provides scalable, transparent decision support for telehealth platforms, allowing clinicians to trust algorithmic recommendations while meeting regulatory standards and enhancing patient safety.
