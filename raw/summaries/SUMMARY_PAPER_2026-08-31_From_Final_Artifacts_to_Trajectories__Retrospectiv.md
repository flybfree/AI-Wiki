## Summary
The paper introduces RetroGen, a self‑improving framework for retrospective process supervision that reconstructs latent trajectories from high‑quality final artifacts such as literature reviews, analyst reports, and legal judgments. By training models on their own successful reconstruction data, RetroGen improves grounding, faithful synthesis, and long‑form evidence‑seeking agent tasks without requiring trajectory data from stronger models.

## Key Takeaways
- Expert trajectories are scarce in open‑ended tasks, yet high‑quality final artifacts are abundant in pre‑training corpora.  
- RetroGen reconstructs candidate latent trajectories from these artifacts and verifies them against both the artifact and supporting evidence.  
- The model is trained on its own successful reconstruction data, eliminating the need for trajectory data generated by stronger models.

## Context
Trajectory data is essential for training large language models to develop agentic abilities, but obtaining it reliably is challenging outside of well‑defined domains like coding or mathematics. This paper addresses that challenge by leveraging readily available final artifacts as compressed traces of evidence‑seeking processes.

## Implications
RetroGen offers practitioners a practical way to enhance model grounding and synthesis capabilities using existing data, reducing reliance on costly annotation pipelines. It could accelerate the development of long‑form reasoning agents in industries such as legal analysis, research summarization, and knowledge management.
