## Summary
This paper surveys the security, privacy, and ethical risks and safeguards associated with deploying Large Language Models (LLMs) in telecommunications. It argues that although LLMs are increasingly used for customer service, network analytics, and operational support, their adoption in telecom is complicated by the uniquely sensitive and longitudinal nature of telecom data. The authors introduce a telecom-specific taxonomy that connects privacy, security, and ethics concerns to concrete use cases, and they outline open research challenges including longitudinal data privacy, cross-border compliance, and scalable guardrails.

## Key Takeaways
- LLMs are moving into core telecom functions such as customer service, network analytics, and operational support, bringing efficiency and innovation but also introducing critical privacy, security, and ethical challenges.
- Telecom data is distinctive because it is both sensitive and longitudinal, meaning risks are not adequately captured by generic LLM safety discussions and require domain-specific treatment.
- The paper contributes a telecom-specific taxonomy that links privacy, security, and ethics to particular use cases, and it identifies open research problems: longitudinal data privacy, cross-border compliance, and scalable guardrails.

## Context
Large Language Models are rapidly expanding across industries, and telecommunications is one of the sectors where they are being piloted and deployed at scale. Telecom environments combine personal, location-adjacent, and network-level information over long periods, making them a high-stakes setting for AI. This paper matters because it grounds LLM risk analysis in telecom-specific constraints rather than treating the sector as a generic application area, helping connect AI safety concerns to the operational realities of networked services.

## Implications
For practitioners and industry teams, the survey signals that LLM adoption in telecom should be governed by domain-aware safeguards rather than off-the-shelf AI controls. It highlights that longitudinal data handling, cross-border regulatory expectations, and guardrails that scale across networks are not solved problems and require dedicated research and engineering. For researchers, it frames a clear agenda: build taxonomies, privacy-preserving methods, compliance-aware deployment patterns, and scalable guardrails so that LLM-enabled telecom services can be deployed responsibly without sacrificing user trust or regulatory alignment.
