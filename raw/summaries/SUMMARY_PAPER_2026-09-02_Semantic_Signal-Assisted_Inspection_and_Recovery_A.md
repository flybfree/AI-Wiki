## Summary
The paper introduces a semantic signal‑assisted decision support system that converts return notes into condition factors and signal‑quality scores to guide inspection depth and recovery allocation when labor is scarce. In three synthetic benchmarks it outperforms structured‑feature baselines in net recovery value while lowering inspection cost, though a risk‑blind approach can still yield higher economic returns under purely monetary objectives.

## Key Takeaways
- The semantic extractor improves net recovery by 53.9 thousand USD per batch in the aircraft scenario.
- Inspection cost is reduced across all three scenarios compared to full noisy inspection.
- Phrase and large language model extractors add further gains specifically for the aircraft benchmark.

## Context
This work demonstrates how natural‑language processing can turn unstructured return narratives into actionable metrics, addressing labor constraints in reverse logistics. It highlights the potential of AI to prioritize inspection effort where it yields the greatest economic impact.

## Implications
Practitioners can adopt these extractors to allocate scarce resources efficiently, balancing inspection intensity with recovery value. The approach may become a standard part of automated reverse‑logistics workflows, reducing waste and labor costs without sacrificing profitability.
