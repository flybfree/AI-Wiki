## Summary
This paper investigates whether the chain-of-thought reasoning generated by medical vision-language models influences their clinical predictions. It introduces CoT-Mediate to test mediation versus sycophancy and finds that prefix-forced continuation preserves reasoning faithfulness better than re‑prompting, while provenance manipulation reveals model‑specific deference patterns.

## Key Takeaways
- Prefix‑forced continuation yields higher mediation faithfulness compared with evidence re‑prompting. - The provenance‑controlled intervention isolates the effect of reasoning injection from sycophancy. - Laterality is identified as the least faithfully tracked clinical attribute across both models.

## Context
Medical vision-language models are increasingly used for diagnostic assistance, yet their reliance on generated reasoning remains unexamined. Understanding whether this reasoning causally drives outputs or merely mimics human style is crucial for trustworthy AI in healthcare.

## Implications
Practitioners must design evaluation protocols that separate genuine mediation from superficial mimicry to avoid overestimating model capability. The findings suggest that the position of injected reasoning within a prompt, not its stated source, determines how models utilize it.
