## Summary
This paper introduces ODRA, a framework that synthesizes cognitive behavioral therapy sessions using chain‑of‑thought reasoning while modeling patient resistance. The method combines foundational CBT guidelines with an orchestrator that steers dialogue to reflect realistic therapeutic resistance levels. Evaluations show ODRA outperforms existing approaches in therapeutic skill, CBT alignment, and patient fidelity.

## Key Takeaways
- ODRA employs a chain‑of‑thought strategy grounded in Beck’s 2020 CBT framework, ensuring each response follows sequential therapeutic logic rather than relying on static scripts.  
- The resistance orchestrator uses steering techniques to generate patient behaviors that match the intended resistance level, directly addressing sycophancy and producing more authentic dialogue.  
- Automated and expert assessments reveal superior performance across clinical metrics, with licensed psychologists preferring ODRA sessions in 12 of 13 evaluations.

## Context
The rapid development of AI‑generated therapeutic content raises concerns about maintaining clinical integrity and ethical standards. Existing methods either produce overly compliant dialogues or break the sequential structure required for CBT, highlighting a gap that this work addresses by integrating reasoning with resistance modeling.

## Implications
ODRA demonstrates that explicit resistance modeling in synthetic training data can improve downstream clinical robustness, offering a template for future AI‑assisted mental health tools. Practitioners may adopt such frameworks to create more realistic therapeutic simulations without compromising safety or efficacy.
