## Summary
The paper proposes WitnessSim, a deposition simulator that generates witness responses driven by controllable legal personas. It evaluates the model on behavioral realism and pedagogical usefulness, finding that realism is maintained while attorney preferences are neutralized. The study demonstrates that simulated behavior adapts to question form and attorney input without collapsing persona fidelity.

## Key Takeaways
- WitnessSim maintains plausible behavioral boundaries through adversarial testing, showing responses stay within assigned legal personas despite varied questioning.
- Attorneys do not systematically prefer original testimony over generated testimony, indicating the model does not bias perception of authenticity.
- Longitudinal analysis reveals that witness behavior changes meaningfully with question form and attorney intervention, yet persona integrity is preserved.

## Context
Legal AI research often measures models by factual correctness or response plausibility, overlooking realistic human-like interaction. This work bridges that gap by focusing on behavioral realism as a core evaluation metric for simulation tools.

## Implications
For legal training platforms, the findings suggest that realistic behavior can coexist with educational value, encouraging developers to prioritize persona fidelity in AI‑driven simulations. Practitioners may use this framework to assess whether their simulators improve learning without misleading users.
