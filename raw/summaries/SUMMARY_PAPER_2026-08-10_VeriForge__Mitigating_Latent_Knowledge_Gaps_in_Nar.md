## Summary
This paper introduces VeriForge, a mixed‑initiative writing system that helps fiction writers discover and integrate unfamiliar domain knowledge without imposing their own queries or limiting creativity. The authors demonstrate that the system can surface latent knowledge gaps while preserving author agency, producing passages with stronger domain grounding in a controlled study.

## Key Takeaways
- Proactive inline highlighting flags potential knowledge gaps as authors draft, prompting them to address missing details before they become visible in the final text.
- Dual‑stream querying combines conversational responses with source‑anchored Knowledge Cards, enabling direct extraction of facts from specialized sources without requiring explicit author prompts.
- The spatial Knowledge Canvas lets writers organize and connect discovered domain information across their narrative, fostering a coherent knowledge map that guides synthesis.

## Context
Current AI writing assistants often demand clear instructions or only work within the writer’s existing knowledge base, limiting their utility for complex fictional worlds. VeriForge addresses this by automating the discovery of obscure facts while leaving creative decisions to human authors, aligning with trends toward more autonomous and context‑aware generative systems.

## Implications
VeriForge suggests a new paradigm where AI acts as a knowledge broker rather than a directive tool, potentially enhancing the realism of AI‑generated narratives across publishing and education. Practitioners can leverage this approach to reduce author bottlenecks and produce richer, domain‑grounded content without sacrificing stylistic voice.
