## Summary
The paper explores gender bias in multimodal large language models by examining how ten diverse models associate 22 musical instruments with three gender categories across text, vision, and audio modalities. It finds that 92% of instrument-level outcomes align with established social‑science stereotypes, especially for the harp and drums, and notes that alignment is strongest in text, weaker in audio, and stronger in vision.

## Key Takeaways
- The study demonstrates that 92 % of instrument‑level gender associations across all models match prior social‑science findings, highlighting a consistent reinforcement of male/female stereotypes for instruments like the harp and drums. - Alignment with gendered stereotypes is weakest in audio representation, stronger in vision, and strongest in text, indicating modality can amplify or dampen bias. - The Symphony‑Bias dataset will be released publicly upon paper acceptance, providing a resource for further research into multimodal bias.

## Context
This work addresses a growing concern that AI systems may unintentionally encode societal stereotypes through their outputs, which can affect user trust and decision‑making in diverse settings. By focusing on musical instruments—a culturally gendered domain—it offers a concrete testbed to evaluate how different model architectures and data modalities propagate bias.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that modality choice may be leveraged to mitigate or exacerbate gendered associations, informing design choices for text‑heavy versus audio‑rich applications. The dataset will enable broader benchmarking, helping the community develop more equitable AI systems that respect diverse identities.
