## Summary
The paper introduces a hybrid LLM‑Enabled Agent Based Network‑Dynamic (LAND) model called GhostField and applies it to the AuraSight scenario, where 314,244 agents exchange messages over 30 days about a fictional song contest. The study examines four layers of social dynamics: ego‑network topology, semantic network evolution, coordination dynamics, and influence dynamics, showing that emergent coordination and influence arise from recursive interactions between network structure and narrative exchange.

## Key Takeaways
- The LAND model demonstrates that collective behavior such as coordination and influence does not stem solely from individual agents but is generated through the interplay of network topology changes and ongoing narrative exchanges.
- Empirical results reveal that semantic networks evolve in a way that reshapes ego‑network structures, creating feedback loops that amplify certain actors’ reach.
- The simulation shows that over 30 days, coordination patterns emerge as groups align around shared narratives, while influence metrics shift based on who controls the dominant storylines.

## Context
This work situates large language model capabilities within social science modeling, offering a framework where AI agents can simulate real‑world interaction dynamics without requiring explicit human data. It highlights how generative models can be used to study emergent group processes that are difficult to capture with traditional network analysis alone.

## Implications
For researchers, the LAND approach provides a testable method for exploring how narrative control influences social coordination in digital spaces. Practitioners may leverage this model to design interventions that steer discourse toward desired outcomes while minimizing unintended influence amplification.
