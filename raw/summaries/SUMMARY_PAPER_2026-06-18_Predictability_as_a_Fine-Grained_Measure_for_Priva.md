## Summary
The paper introduces predictability as a fine-grained privacy measure that quantifies how much an attacker can improve their ability to infer unknown individuals' sensitive information beyond what compromised data already reveals. It demonstrates that differential privacy and predictability are generally incomparable, but they align in the worst‑case regime where all but one individual is compromised, implying mutual‑information DP.

## Key Takeaways
- Predictability measures the incremental gain in an attacker's ability to predict sensitive information about unknown individuals after observing the algorithm's output, beyond what can already be inferred from the compromised data.  
- In the extreme case of all but one compromised individuals and binary queries considered sensitive, predictability equals mutual‑information differential privacy.  
- The framework uses the generalized method of moments to analyze asymptotic predictability when the compromised data is generated by a stationary, ergodic, mixing process.

## Context
This work addresses a limitation of differential privacy by offering a more nuanced metric that respects existing knowledge without sacrificing accuracy. It complements DP by providing calibrated perturbations tailored to specific sensitive attributes and attacker models, which is crucial as AI systems generate large datasets with stochastic noise.

## Implications
Practitioners can deploy predictability alongside DP to fine‑tune privacy guarantees per data type, reducing unnecessary over‑protection while maintaining security. The methodology supports responsible AI deployment where precise control over information leakage is required.
