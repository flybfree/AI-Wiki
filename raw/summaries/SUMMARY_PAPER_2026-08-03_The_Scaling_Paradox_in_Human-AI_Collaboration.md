## Summary
This paper investigates how the empirical scaling laws of AI translate into real‑world human‑AI collaboration outcomes. It shows that joint system performance improves with AI scale only when humans accurately perceive the AI’s capabilities; otherwise, misperception creates a paradox where larger AI reduces overall performance and profit.

## Key Takeaways
- When humans over‑perceive an AI’s ability, scaling up the model can actually lower joint performance and increase firm‑level losses.  
- Under‑perceiving the AI leads to slower but still positive gains as scale increases, highlighting a trade‑off in responsiveness.  
- Firms can mitigate these distortions through policies such as cost internalization and perception alignment, whose success hinges on deployment economics and the direction of human bias.

## Context
The scaling paradox challenges the assumption that bigger AI models automatically yield better outcomes, especially when deployed alongside humans who may misinterpret their capabilities. This work adds a behavioral layer to existing literature on AI performance, emphasizing that human cognition is a critical bottleneck in realizing theoretical gains.

## Implications
For practitioners, the paper suggests focusing on managing the human‑AI interface rather than merely investing in larger models. It underscores that organizational success depends on aligning beliefs about AI competence with operational policies, offering a strategic lever for sustainable value creation.
