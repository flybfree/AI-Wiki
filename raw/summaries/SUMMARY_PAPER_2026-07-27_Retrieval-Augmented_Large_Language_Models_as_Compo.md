## Summary
The paper investigates whether Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) can turn large language models into reliable components of cognitive computing for regulatory knowledge management. It demonstrates that locally deployed Polish LLMs combined with RAG improve factual consistency and traceability while avoiding unsupported content generation. The study validates the approach on consumer‑class hardware using Ollama, LM Studio, Bielik and PLLuM models.

## Key Takeaways
- Integrating RAG into local LLMs creates a hybrid cognitive architecture that enhances epistemic reliability by combining semantic interpretation with controlled knowledge retrieval.
- The solution reduces unsupported content generation risk while preserving domain specificity and normative precision through external knowledge sources.
- Auditability and dynamic updating of regulatory information are achieved without retraining the language model.

## Context
This work addresses the growing need for AI systems that can operate securely on limited hardware in regulated environments. By decoupling generation from high‑end GPUs, it aligns with practical deployment constraints while preserving domain expertise through RAG.

## Implications
For regulators and compliance teams, this architecture offers a transparent, updatable knowledge layer that supports continuous analysis of legal acts without model retraining. Practitioners can embed such modules into existing cognitive computing pipelines to improve decision‑making under high informational volatility.
