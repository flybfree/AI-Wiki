## Summary
Frontier Financial Judgement is a benchmark designed to test agents' ability to identify new, valuation‑relevant financial information among mixed news items. The strongest model matches expert labels in only 52.4% of cases, indicating limited performance.

- Rapidly identifying new information and its valuation impact remains a bottleneck for equity coverage, making it increasingly challenging as AI processes more data.
- False‑positive rates among frontier agents vary widely, from ~1% for GPT‑5.6 Sol to ~32% for Claude Sonnet 4.6, indicating inconsistent reliability.
- Constructing the benchmark with hybrid human‑designed and live news items yields 656 assessment items that reflect real‑world conditions.

## Context
This paper addresses a key challenge in deploying AI tools for financial information filtering, where accuracy and cost trade‑offs are critical. It highlights the gap between theoretical model performance and practical deployment reliability.

## Implications
The findings suggest that current large language models cannot reliably replace human analysts in news‑flow analysis, limiting trustworthy integration into investment workflows. Practitioners should consider cost versus false‑positive risk when selecting AI agents for financial coverage.
