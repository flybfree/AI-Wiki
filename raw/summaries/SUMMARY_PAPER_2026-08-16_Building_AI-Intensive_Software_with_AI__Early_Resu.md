## Summary
This paper reports early empirical results from a student team that built a full conversational onboarding assistant using pervasive AI assistance, and it examines the true cost of such AI‑intensive development through a three‑layer model. The initial cost ratio was reported as 19.4 × but a follow‑up correction reduced it to about 9.9 × after identifying two systematic errors.

## Key Takeaways
- Inflating AI spend via a flat‑rate subscription per‑token cost estimation leads to an overestimation of the total expense.
- Pricing the counterfactual with incorrect regional labor rates inflates the human effort component, further raising the ratio.
- Both errors are easy to make, invisible in the final number, and plausibly common in similar reports.

## Context
Accurate measurement of AI‑intensive software development costs is a growing concern as organizations adopt large language models and other generative tools. Most published cost analyses rely on assumptions or proxies that do not reflect real‑world usage patterns, creating a gap between reported figures and actual expenses.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings warn against presenting inflated cost ratios without transparent methodology checks. This encourages the development of more robust, replicable costing frameworks that can guide budgeting and investment decisions in AI‑driven projects.
