## Summary
This paper introduces AgentRecommender, a novel framework that empowers users to deploy customizable recommender systems directly on their own devices by leveraging the investigation capabilities and internal knowledge of Large Language Model (LLM) agents. The proposed method addresses the limitations of traditional platform-centric recommendation models, which often prioritize engagement metrics over user well-being, by enabling personalized system construction without requiring additional training data or extensive customization efforts. By utilizing LLM agents to autonomously gather relevant information and adapt recommendations to individual preferences, AgentRecommender offers a viable solution for mitigating issues such as filter bubbles, clickbait, and platform lock-in while maintaining high personalization standards.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper identifies significant drawbacks associated with traditional platform-based recommender systems, including the promotion of clickbait content, the creation of filter bubbles that limit exposure to diverse viewpoints, and the potential spread of misinformation, all of which serve platform interests rather than user needs; AgentRecommender shifts control back to users by enabling a user-side paradigm where individuals manage their own recommendation logic.
