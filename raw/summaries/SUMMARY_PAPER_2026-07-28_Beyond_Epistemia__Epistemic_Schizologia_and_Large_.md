## Summary
The paper argues that large language models generate fluent text but do not provide genuine knowledge; it introduces “epistemic schizologia” to describe the gap between linguistic output and responsible understanding. The authors propose viewing LLMs as techno‑semiotic machines that automate a phase of semiosis rather than epistemic agents.

## Key Takeaways
- Epistemia is defined as experiencing knowledge without performing justified judgment, a risk when LLM outputs are taken as proof.
- The phenomenon of epistemic schizologia splits signs into linguistic expressions and socially embedded interpretive circuits involving evidence, criticism, verification, and responsibility.
- Eikotic closure creates the illusion that a plausible continuation is an epistemic result, reinforced by algorithmic authority and self‑misrecognition.

## Context
Current AI discourse often conflates model output with knowledge, prompting calls for new frameworks to distinguish production from understanding. This paper contributes by grounding discussions in semiotic theory and practice philosophy, offering a lens for analyzing hybrid human‑AI interactions.

## Implications
For practitioners, the framework suggests designing systems that maintain inspectable genealogy and distributed responsibility, ensuring that AI‑generated text is used within contested, accountable practices rather than as epistemic authority. It highlights the need for interdisciplinary collaboration to prevent epistemic erosion in AI deployment.
