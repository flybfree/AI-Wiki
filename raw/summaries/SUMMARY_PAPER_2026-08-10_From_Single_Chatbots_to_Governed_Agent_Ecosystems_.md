## Summary
The paper introduces a compliance‑first Agentic AI pattern catalogue and orchestration framework designed for mission‑critical Hospital Information Management Systems (HIMS). It moves beyond isolated LLM chatbots toward a governed ecosystem of autonomous agents that can handle triage, documentation, scheduling and revenue‑cycle tasks. The framework demonstrates reduced documentation time, lower integration effort and higher pilot success rates while maintaining strict regulatory compliance.

## Key Takeaways
- A taxonomy of Agentic roles is defined to map each AI pattern to specific clinical functions such as patient triage or billing automation.
- A formal risk‑stratification model assigns each pattern a risk tier, embeds human‑in‑the‑loop checkpoints and provides governance hooks for auditability.
- The unified orchestration runtime uses vLLM inference, confidential computing and MCP deployment to coordinate multi‑agent workflows across Epic Cerner MEDITECH while enforcing end‑to‑end encryption.

## Context
Healthcare AI adoption is accelerating as hospitals seek to integrate large language models into daily operations. Yet most implementations remain siloed pilots that fail to scale due to fragmented governance and compliance gaps, creating operational fragility and financial risk. This work addresses the need for a systematic approach that aligns technical architecture with regulatory frameworks.

## Implications
The framework offers hospital leaders a blueprint to convert AI investment into sustainable ROI by reducing pilot attrition and cutting documentation overhead. By embedding compliance as code, institutions can meet HIPAA GDPR EU AI Act India DPDP and ISO standards while maintaining high performance, positioning AI as a reliable clinical asset rather than a risk.
