## Summary
This paper introduces CUE Bench, a Chinese Unsaid Emotion benchmark that focuses on Affective Stance to capture how explicit and implicit expressions interact in discourse. Experiments demonstrate that modeling Affective Stance boosts fine‑grained emotion recognition by 3.5 percentage points and pragmatic intent detection by 7.8 percentage points over strong baselines.

## Key Takeaways
- CUE Bench constructs nine interpretable affective stances derived from the interaction of explicit polarity and implicit affect, providing a structured view beyond simple sentiment labels.  
- The benchmark explicitly annotates intent and fine‑grained emotion, enabling models to reason about concealed or weakened emotional meaning in Chinese text.  
- Incorporating Affective Stance yields measurable improvements, showing that current models are limited when affective meaning is indirect or pragmatically reshaped.

## Context
Emotion understanding in natural language processing often relies on surface polarity or final emotion categories, which ignore the nuanced interplay of explicit and implicit cues. This paper addresses a gap by offering a benchmark that models how these cues combine to produce Affective Stance, reflecting real‑world discourse where affect is rarely straightforward.

## Implications
For practitioners, CUE Bench provides a concrete resource to evaluate whether their systems can capture subtle affective nuances in Chinese communication. In industry and research, the findings suggest that integrating stance modeling could lead to more accurate sentiment analysis, improving user experience in chatbots, mental health apps, and social media monitoring tools.
