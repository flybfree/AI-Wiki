## Summary
This paper introduces Graph Engineering as a framework for organizing LLM agents into system-level intelligence. It argues that complex tasks require distributed coordination beyond individual agent enhancements and proposes explicit graph structures to model tasks, agents, and evolving states.

## Key Takeaways
- The paper defines System Intelligence as the ability of an agent system to integrate heterogeneous components toward a shared goal.
- Graph Engineering creates dynamic graphs representing tasks, agents, and state changes to enable coordination and evolution.
- It emphasizes that adding more agents is insufficient; structured organization is essential for scalable agent systems.

## Context
The rapid advancement of large language models has led to autonomous agents performing long-horizon tasks. Traditional approaches focus on prompting or context engineering, but they lack mechanisms for managing complex, interdependent workflows across multiple specialized agents.

## Implications
Graph Engineering offers a systematic way to design modular and adaptive agent ecosystems, which could improve reliability and efficiency in real-world applications such as robotics, customer support, and scientific research. Practitioners may adopt graph-based orchestration tools to manage multi-agent projects more effectively.
