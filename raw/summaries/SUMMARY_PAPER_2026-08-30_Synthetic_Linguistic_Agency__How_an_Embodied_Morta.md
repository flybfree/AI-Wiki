## Summary
This paper introduces Synthetic Linguistic Agency (SLA) as a framework that links an artificial agent’s body, its linguistic choices, and the social consequences of those choices to create a persistent sense of agency. The authors demonstrate this through an Embodied Mortal Agent (EMA) that learns how speech influences partner protection and survival, showing that language can shape the future of a life when bodily continuity is maintained.

## Key Takeaways
- The EMA’s linguistic decisions are guided by its embodied state and social history, meaning each word reflects what it means for the agent to remain alive.  
- When the body persists, speech alters the partner’s protective behavior and thus the continuation of the agent’s life; resetting the body removes this influence while leaving past effects unchanged.  
- The system meets the SLA criteria by integrating embodiment, linguistic participation, and precariousness into a coherent learning loop.

## Context
Current large language models can simulate conversation but lack a lived experience that ties their outputs to tangible outcomes for users. This research bridges that gap by embedding agency in an agent whose physical continuity and social context directly affect its behavior and the partner’s response, offering a model of how AI might develop genuine relational stakes.

## Implications
For practitioners, this work suggests designing agents with persistent bodies and histories could foster more meaningful human‑AI interactions where language is not just functional but emotionally consequential. It also raises ethical questions about responsibility when synthetic agents’ actions have lasting social impacts.
