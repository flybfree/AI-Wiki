## Summary
The paper introduces a proof-of-concept for an agentic Just-in-Time Adaptive Intervention (JITAI) system designed to provide personalized sleep support by analyzing user data such as physical activity, smartphone usage, and bedtime routines. The study demonstrates that an AI agent can successfully automate the identification of behavioral patterns and generate context-aware reminders, offering a more flexible alternative to traditional rule-based systems which often require manual configuration.

## Key Takeaways
- The system utilizes an AI agent capable of reviewing 30 days of historical data—including sleep quality, physical activity, smartphone usage, and bedtime routines—to identify specific behavioral patterns rather than relying on static rules.
- The architecture is built on Home Assistant and uses a reusable "skill file," allowing the agent to autonomously create or update automated reminders based on specific user behaviors and context changes in real-time.
- Initial results confirmed technical feasibility by successfully completing data reviews and limiting interventions to three per day, while also recording decision logs for human oversight to ensure safety and reliability.

## Context
This research addresses a significant hurdle in digital health: moving from static, one-size-fits-all notifications to truly personalized, context-aware interventions. By leveraging agentic AI, the study explores how autonomous agents can handle the complexity of human behavior data more effectively than traditional rule-based systems, which struggle to scale across diverse user profiles.

## Implications
For practitioners and developers in digital health, this work suggests that agentic AI could significantly lower the barrier to creating highly personalized interventions without requiring manual configuration for every individual user. It provides a framework for scalable, adaptive health support that maintains human oversight while offering more nuanced feedback than current market solutions, potentially improving long-term adherence to sleep hygiene habits.
