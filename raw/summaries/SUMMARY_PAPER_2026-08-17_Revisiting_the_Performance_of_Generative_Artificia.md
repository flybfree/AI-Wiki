## Summary
This paper evaluates five contemporary generative AI models on authentic introductory object‑oriented programming assessments to determine how well they can generate correct and compilable code. The results show that all models outperform typical student performance, yet they still encounter systematic errors in advanced OOP topics such as interfaces, abstract classes, and inheritance.

## Key Takeaways
- The AI systems consistently produce higher scores than the average student cohort and often earn full marks on longer programming tasks, indicating strong baseline competence.  
- Despite these gains, models frequently generate non‑compiling code and repeatedly misapply OOP concepts like interface implementation and abstract class usage.  
- Performance has improved compared with the previous year, but recurring error patterns persist across multiple assessment items.

## Context
Generative AI has rapidly advanced in code generation, yet its real‑world impact on educational assessments remains understudied. This work fills a gap by applying current models to a standard OOP course, providing empirical evidence of both progress and persistent limitations.

## Implications
Educators can use these findings to design assessments that probe deeper OOP reasoning rather than relying solely on basic syntax. Companies may integrate AI tools cautiously, recognizing that while they assist with routine coding tasks, they still require human oversight for complex object‑oriented problems.
