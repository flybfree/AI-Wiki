## Summary
The paper evaluates Vibe Coding, an AI‑led conversational programming paradigm, comparing its performance, cognitive impact and responsible adoption to traditional coding and AI‑assisted environments. Thirty participants completed tasks under three conditions; the study found a 27 % reduction in task time with vibe coding but noted lower maintainability and higher security risks.

## Key Takeaways
- Vibe coding cut development efficiency by 27 % versus traditional methods, yet this gain correlated with reduced software maintainability scores.  
- Cognitive workload stayed moderate (NASA‑TLX 55.5) while syntactic effort dropped, indicating a shift toward linguistic reasoning that may affect long‑term learning.  
- Perceived loss of control was linked to higher security vulnerabilities because AI outputs were less transparent and harder to validate.

## Context
Conversational programming is rapidly moving into mainstream development tools, raising questions about how natural‑language interaction reshapes workflow efficiency and quality. This study adds empirical evidence that speed gains may be offset by subtle quality trade‑offs in maintainability and security.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that AI‑driven coding can boost productivity but must be paired with human oversight to preserve code health. Industry adoption should adopt hybrid models and transparent accountability mechanisms to mitigate hidden risks.
