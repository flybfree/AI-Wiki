## Summary
This paper introduces a multi‑agent large language model framework that automates the full lifecycle of literature analysis for interdisciplinary climate‑health research. The system screens documents, extracts structured information, and integrates results into standardized outputs, achieving high accuracy on a bilingual Chinese‑English corpus of 32,642 papers.

## Key Takeaways
- The framework uses three specialized agents to mimic expert thinking in document evaluation, extraction, and analytical review, enabling full‑process automation.  
- A four‑layer hallucination control strategy combined with manual verification ensures the reliability of extracted data.  
- Validation on a 1993‑2023 Chinese climate‑health corpus yields an F1 score of 0.92 for core extraction and produces 2,012 city‑literature association pairs.

## Context
Automated literature analysis has become essential as interdisciplinary research expands rapidly, yet existing tools struggle with multilingual scalability and domain specificity. This work addresses those gaps by integrating multiple language models into a coordinated pipeline tailored to climate‑health studies.

## Implications
The framework reduces manual effort and accelerates evidence mining for policymakers and researchers, supporting large‑scale studies that require precise, standardized data across languages. Its high extraction accuracy can improve the quality of interdisciplinary climate‑health interventions worldwide.
