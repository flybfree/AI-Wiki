## Summary
The paper disproves the Tang Zhang Schatten norm conjecture by providing explicit counterexamples at p=3/2 using real 2x2 rank-one matrices, showing the attained ratio exceeds the conjectured constant. It also confirms the sharp bound for families of rank‑at‑most‑one summands for all finite p>1 and proves endpoint results for p=∞, with equality cases classified.

## Key Takeaways
- The conjecture fails at p=3/2 because two explicit real 2×2 rank‑one matrices give a ratio above 207/200 while the conjectured constant is below that value.
- Sharpness holds for any family of rank‑at‑most‑one summands when 2≤p<∞, with equality cases fully classified.
- The endpoint case p=∞ also attains its sharp bound.

## Context
This work addresses a longstanding inequality in matrix analysis concerning Schatten norms and their behavior under finite sums. Understanding these bounds is crucial for applications in signal processing, data compression, and machine learning where norm estimates guide algorithm design.

## Implications
The failure of the conjecture signals that simple linear inequalities between sum norms are not universally valid, prompting researchers to seek more nuanced characterizations. Practitioners can rely on the proven sharp constants for rank‑one families, improving theoretical guarantees in optimization problems involving matrix sums.
