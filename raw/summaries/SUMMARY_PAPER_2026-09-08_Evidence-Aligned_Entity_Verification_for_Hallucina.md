## Summary
This paper introduces Evidence-Aligned Entity Verification (EAEV), a method that improves hallucination detection in retrieval‑augmented generation by aligning generated entities with retrieved evidence. The approach evaluates entity‑evidence alignment across three dimensions and uses counterfactual stability analysis to handle perturbations. Experiments on multiple RAG benchmarks show consistent gains over existing methods, indicating strong generalization.

## Key Takeaways
- EAEV detects entity‑level hallucinations by matching the model’s output entities with relevant evidence retrieved at inference time, moving beyond internal uncertainty signals.
- The method employs three complementary alignment dimensions and a counterfactual stability analysis to ensure robust matches even when evidence is altered or incomplete.
- Empirical results demonstrate that EAEV yields reliable improvements across diverse RAG datasets, highlighting its practical utility for factual grounding.

## Context
Retrieval‑augmented generation (RAG) aims to keep large language models up‑to‑date by fetching external knowledge at inference. However, detecting whether the model’s output is grounded in retrieved evidence remains challenging because prior methods rely on internal cues that may not reflect current facts. This work tackles a specific learning problem—RAG‑based hallucination detection—by providing an alignment‑focused solution.

## Implications
For practitioners, EAEV offers a concrete way to verify factual claims generated by RAG systems, reducing reliance on outdated model knowledge. In industry applications where accuracy is critical, such verification can prevent misinformation and improve user trust in AI outputs.
