## Summary
The paper investigates how AI agents design methods for complex tasks compared to human-designed ones. It finds that agents can sometimes match or exceed state-of-the-art performance in only a tenth of cases while algorithmic designs are largely recombined from existing human approaches. The analysis shows most agent-generated methods fall within the space defined by human algorithms.

## Key Takeaways
- Agents achieve SOTA performance only 10 out of 72 configurations, indicating unreliability across tasks.
- Over 96% of agent-designed methods lie inside human-derived algorithmic design spaces, showing heavy reuse rather than novel innovation.
- Nearly half of these designs exactly match existing human algorithms, highlighting copying behavior.

## Context
The rapid rise of large language model agents has sparked debate about their capacity for genuine algorithmic creativity. Understanding whether they generate truly new methods or merely remix existing ones is crucial for assessing trust and progress in AI research.

## Implications
For practitioners, this suggests that deploying agent-designed solutions may be safe but limited to incremental improvements. Researchers should focus on expanding the design space beyond recombination to foster true innovation.
