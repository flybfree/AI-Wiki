## Summary
The paper investigates how the internal network topology influences performance in photonic reservoir computing using a spatial light modulator. It compares small-world and scale-free topologies for chaotic time series prediction and demonstrates that small‑world networks achieve higher memory capacity and superior one‑step‑ahead accuracy. The study also shows that rewiring probability and leak rate can be tuned to optimize predictions.

## Key Takeaways
- Small‑world network topology yields the highest memory capacity and best prediction performance compared with scale‑free topologies.
- Adjusting the rewiring probability of the internal nodes allows fine‑tuning of reservoir learning without sacrificing speed or bandwidth.
- Implementing a photonic human brain network using connectome data shows that realistic biological topologies can outperform synthetic ones when optimized.

## Context
Photonic reservoir computing promises rapid, low‑cost time‑series forecasting by exploiting the inherent parallelism of light. Understanding how network design impacts memory and prediction is crucial for scaling these systems to real‑world applications such as sensor networks and edge AI devices.

## Implications
This work provides a practical guide for engineers designing photonic reservoirs, highlighting that topology choice and parameter tuning are key levers for performance. By leveraging small‑world structures, industries can achieve faster inference with minimal hardware complexity, accelerating deployment in IoT and autonomous systems.
