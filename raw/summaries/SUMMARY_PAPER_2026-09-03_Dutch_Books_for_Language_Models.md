## Summary
The paper evaluates the coherence of probabilistic forecasts generated by language models using a Dutch‑book approach that does not require observed outcomes. By constructing arbitrage opportunities from model probabilities across events derived from stock returns, it quantifies incoherence as the largest guaranteed profit an arbitrageur could earn. The study finds substantial evidence of incoherent predictions, especially when logical relationships between events are complex or when irrelevant context is introduced.

## Key Takeaways
- Language models generate probabilistic forecasts that can be exploited for Dutch‑book profits, indicating a lack of internal consistency in their world model.
- Incoherence rises sharply with richer logical dependencies among events and can increase by an order of magnitude due to extraneous contextual details.
- The method evaluates coherence without needing outcome labels, allowing assessment even when results are unknown or unresolved.

## Context
This work addresses a growing concern that AI‑generated probabilistic statements may mislead users who rely on them for decision making. By measuring incoherence through arbitrage potential, the study provides an objective metric that can be applied across various domains where forecasts are used.

## Implications
For practitioners, the findings suggest that current language model outputs should not be trusted as reliable risk assessments without further validation. The paper also hints at alternative training strategies that could improve internal consistency and reduce exploitable arbitrage opportunities.
