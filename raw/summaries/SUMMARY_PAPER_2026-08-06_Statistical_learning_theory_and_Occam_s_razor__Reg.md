## Summary
The paper justifies regularization as a methodological choice rooted in statistical learning theory, arguing that simplicity is preferred over fit to achieve reliable predictions and “what‑you‑see‑is‑what‑you‑get” guarantees. It builds on an earlier core argument to show that trading off fit for simplicity yields theoretical benefits.

## Key Takeaways
- Regularization embodies Occam's razor by favoring simpler models despite lower predictive accuracy, providing a principled trade‑off between complexity and performance.
- The justification is not merely pragmatic; it links simplicity to the reliability of predictions and the “what-you-see-is-what-you-get” principle.
- This methodological stance avoids collapsing into pure pragmatism or ontological assumptions about truth.
