## Summary
This paper introduces MigrationNarrate, the first multimodal dataset for detecting migration narratives in YouTube videos from the United Kingdom. The dataset contains 1,115 video transcripts annotated with a two‑level taxonomy of 12 super‑narratives and 53 narrative labels, enabling researchers to study how migration stories are framed across online video content.

## Key Takeaways
- MigrationNarrate provides a comprehensive collection of YouTube video transcripts that have been manually labeled using both super‑narrative categories and detailed narrative tags.  
- The dataset demonstrates the effectiveness of combining pre‑trained encoder models with open‑source and closed‑source large language models to detect migration narratives in video content.  
- Error analysis reveals systematic misclassifications, highlighting the need for richer annotation strategies and multimodal feature fusion.

## Context
The rapid shift from text‑only to video‑centric communication has created a new frontier for narrative analysis, yet existing tools lack domain‑specific data for migration stories. MigrationNarrate addresses this gap by offering a UK‑focused resource that can be leveraged for research on social discourse and automated content moderation.

## Implications
For AI researchers, the dataset enables more accurate detection of sensitive topics in video platforms, improving safety measures and personalization algorithms. Practitioners can adopt these findings to develop tools that responsibly surface migration narratives while respecting privacy and ethical concerns.
