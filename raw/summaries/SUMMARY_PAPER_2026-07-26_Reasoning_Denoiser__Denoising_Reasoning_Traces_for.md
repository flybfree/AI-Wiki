## Summary
This paper addresses the challenge of extracting reliable signals from long reasoning traces generated by large reasoning models to detect hallucinations. The authors introduce REDE, a learning framework that refines step-level representations using final-answer attention as supervision, thereby improving detection performance over existing methods.

## Key Takeaways
- Identifying irrelevant steps and repetitive steps in reasoning traces substantially degrades hallucination detection accuracy.  
- Confidence‑based scores and naive embedding‑based filtering cannot reliably separate noisy from informative steps.  
- REDE leverages final‑answer attention to shape the step‑level representation space, producing refined embeddings that allow reliable identification and removal of noisy steps.

## Context
Large reasoning models produce extensive intermediate traces that are essential for understanding model behavior but also contain noise that hampers downstream tasks such as hallucination detection. Current approaches often treat these traces as a single entity, limiting their utility and leading to suboptimal performance.

## Implications
The ability to filter out noisy reasoning steps directly enhances the trustworthiness of large language models in real‑world applications where accurate answers are critical. Practitioners can integrate REDE into existing detectors to produce more reliable outputs without major architectural changes.
