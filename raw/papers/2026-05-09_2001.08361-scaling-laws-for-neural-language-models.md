# Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models



**Source**: [Original Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)
**Kaplan et al., OpenAI (2020)**
arXiv:2001.08361

## Abstract

We study empirical scaling laws for language model performance on the cross-entropy loss. The loss scales as a power law with model size, dataset size, and the amount of computation used for training. We find that the performance of large models is limited not by the compute used to train them but by the number of tokens seen during training.

## Key Concepts

### The Power Law
The central finding: model performance follows a smooth, predictable power law with three factors:

| Factor | What it is | Effect on performance |
|--------|-----------|---------------------|
| Model size | Number of parameters | Larger = better, power-law |
| Dataset size | Tokens seen during training | Larger = better, power-law |
| Compute | FLOPs used for training | More = better, power-law |

None of the relationships have a "knee" or saturation point — it just keeps getting better.

### The Surprising Insight: Data is the Real Bottleneck
The paper's most important finding: **for models as large as GPT-3, performance is limited by data, not compute or model size**.

In other words:
- If you have the compute to train GPT-3, you should use it on MORE data rather than making the model bigger.
- Larger models on less data will UNDERPERFORM smaller models on more data (this is what Chinchilla later proved).

### The Three Laws
1. **Model size law**: Loss ∝ (model size)^(-α) — doubling parameters reduces loss
2. **Data size law**: Loss ∝ (data size)^(-β) — doubling data reduces loss more than doubling model size
3. **Compute law**: Performance ∝ (compute)^(-γ) — everything scales together

### Implications for Training Strategy
- **Data scaling is more important than model scaling** for models of GPT-3's size
- Keep making models bigger AND use more data — both help
- There is no "sweet spot" — the power law keeps holding all the way to the largest models tried

## Why This Changed Everything

### Guiding the AI Arms Race
Scaling laws gave the field a roadmap:
- Want better performance? Get more data, make the model bigger, train on more compute.
- Each one has diminishing but still-relevant returns.

### Justified the $100M+ Training Runs
The scaling laws showed that every additional dollar spent on training yields predictable improvements. This is what made it "rational" for companies to invest billions in foundation model development.

### The Foundation for Chinchilla
Hoffmann et al.'s Chinchilla paper (2022) built directly on Kaplan's work: "If data matters more than model size per the scaling laws, let's flip the ratio."
