# ImageNet Classification with Deep CNNs (AlexNet)



**Source**: [Original Paper](https://example.com/placeholder)
**Krizhevsky, Sutskever, and Hinton, University of Toronto (2012)**
Published in NeurIPS 2012. Often cited as arXiv:1409.1556.

## Abstract

We trained a large deep convolutional neural network to classify the 1.2 million high-resolution images in the ImageNet LSVRC-2010 contest into the 1000 different classes. Our model achieved top-1 error of 37.5% and top-5 error of 17.0%, which was substantially better than the previous best.

## Key Concepts

### The AlexNet Architecture
A deep convolutional network that won every image classification category:

1. **Convolutional layers**: Filter images at multiple scales to detect edges, textures, shapes, and objects
2. **Pooling layers**: Downsample the feature maps to reduce computation and provide some spatial invariance
3. **ReLU activation**: Use the rectified linear function — simple, fast, and surprisingly effective
4. **Dropout**: Randomly disable some neurons during training to prevent overfitting
5. **GPU parallelization**: Train on two GPUs simultaneously — unprecedented for the time

### The "Deep" Was the Key
What made AlexNet special wasn't a single innovation — it was making many working ideas MUCH bigger:

- 60 million parameters (vs. thousands in previous networks)
- Training on raw pixels (not hand-engineered features)
- Using GPUs to train a network too big for CPUs
- Using ReLU instead of sigmoid/tanh for faster convergence

### The Data + GPU + Deep Architecture Formula
AlexNet proved a new recipe: give a deep network lots of data and lots of GPU compute, and it discovers features better than human engineers can hand-craft them.

## Why This Changed Everything

### The Deep Learning Revolution
Before AlexNet (2012): "Let's design features by hand (SIFT, SURF) and feed them to a classifier."
After AlexNet: "Let the network learn features directly from pixels."

Every vision model since — ResNet, VGG, Inception, EfficientNet, YOLO, MobileNet — follows AlexNet's approach: train a deep network on raw pixels.

### GPU for ML
AlexNet popularized using GPUs for neural network training. Before this, GPUs were for gaming. After this, GPUs became the compute engine of AI.

### "Deep" ≠ "Better" Until AlexNet Showed It
Researchers had tried deep networks before and they failed. AlexNet showed that with enough data, enough GPU, and ReLU activations, deep networks could work spectacularly.

## For the Course

### Lesson 14 (Choosing the Right Architecture)
This paper is foundational because:
- It proves that CNNs are the right architecture for vision tasks
- It shows how to match problem type to architecture (image classification → CNN)
- It introduced the data/compute/training paradigm that applies to ALL architectures
- It shows "why vision needs different architectures than language"

### Lesson 7 (Convolutional Networks for Vision)
AlexNet is the first concrete example of why:
- Convolution works for images (spatial locality, shared weights)
- Different architecture for images vs text vs time series
- Why CNNs are special for spatial data (image pixels)
