# Attention Is All You Need

**Vaswani et al., Google Brain / Google Research (2017)**
arXiv:1706.03762

## Abstract

The dominant sequence transduction models are based on complex recurrent or convolutional neural networks that include an encoder and a decoder. The best performing models also connect the encoder and decoder through an attention mechanism.

We propose a new simple network architecture, the Transformer, based solely on attention mechanisms, dispensing with recurrence and convolutions entirely. Experiments on two machine translation tasks show these models to be superior in quality while being more parallelizable and requiring significantly less time to train.

## Key Concepts

### The Attention Problem
Before transformers, sequence models (RNNs, LSTMs) processed data one token at a time sequentially. This created a bottleneck:

- **Slow training**: Must wait for each token before processing the next
- **Memory limits**: Hard to connect distant context (even LSTMs struggle with very long sequences)
- **Compression bottleneck**: All information must be squeezed through a single hidden state

### The Attention Solution
Attention lets the model look at ALL tokens simultaneously and decide which ones matter most:

- **Parallelization**: No sequential dependency — all tokens processed at once
- **Long-range dependencies**: Directly attend to any token, regardless of distance
- **Weighted focus**: Not a binary "attend or not" — attention assigns different weights to different tokens

### Multi-Head Attention
Instead of one attention mechanism, use multiple "heads" that each attend differently:

- One head might focus on syntactic structure (subject-verb agreement)
- Another on semantic similarity (coreference resolution)
- Another on positional cues (nearby words)

This lets the model capture multiple types of relationships simultaneously.

### The Transformer Architecture
The Transformer has a remarkably simple design:

1. **Encoder**: Stack of self-attention layers + feed-forward networks
2. **Decoder**: Same, plus cross-attention to the encoder output
3. **Positional encoding**: Since there's no recurrence, add position information as a signal
4. **Residual connections + layer normalization**: Stabilize training

No recurrence. No convolutions. Just attention.

## Why This Changed Everything

### Training Speed
The parallel nature of attention means models train on GPUs/TPUs orders of magnitude faster than RNNs.

### Scalability
Because attention doesn't lose information over distance, scaling model size and training data leads to predictable improvements. This is the foundation of the scaling laws (Kaplan et al., 2020).

### Universal Architecture
Transformers work on text, images (ViT), audio, video, multimodal — and all future large models (BERT, GPT-3, Stable Diffusion, DALL-E, Claude, GPT-4) are built on transformer architecture.

When the syllabus says:
> "Recognize why transformers became the dominant language architecture"

This is the paper that answered it.

## For the Course

### Lesson 9 (Attention and Transformers)
This is the PRIMARY foundational paper. Every lesson on attention and transformers must reference it. It explains:

- The original problem (recurrence bottleneck)
- The solution (attention mechanism)
- Why attention beats recurrence for the tasks it addresses
- Why the architecture scales

### Lesson 11 (Large Language Models)
GPT-3 (and all decoder-only LLMs) is a transformer decoder. Understanding the Transformer is prerequisite to understanding LLMs. The Transformer is the engine; scaling laws are the fuel.

### Teaching Notes
- Don't try to explain the math of attention formulas — use the analogy of "looking up information" in a document
- Emphasize that the architecture is SURPRISINGLY simple — no recurrence, no convolutions, just attention
- The name "Self-Attention" (each token attends to all tokens including itself) is the key insight
- Mention that Google's original use case was machine translation, not language modeling
