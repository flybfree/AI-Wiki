# Conditional Endorsement: Simulating How Adults with Different Skills Evaluate Canada's National AI Strategy

## Abstract
Canada's 2026 national AI strategy, AI for All, commits to free AI training for everyone and to expanding business AI adoption, yet it is difficult to anticipate whether these policies will reach adults with uneven skills and circumstances. We ask how adults with diverse skills might rate these policies on support, effectiveness, and personal benefit, how their skills and circumstances shape these evaluations, and what implementation conditions they raise. We built 3,200 generative agents from a weighted sample of the Programme for the International Assessment of Adult Competencies (PIAAC) Cycle 2 respondents, collected their ratings and explanations for 20 policies, and analyzed dialogues between agents with contrasting ratings. The agents supported nearly every policy but were far less confident that they would improve adult AI literacy or benefit them personally. This gap widened as digital technology use declined: agents modeled on adults who had never used a digital device still endorsed the policies but rarely expected to benefit. Agents traced the gap to participation costs, hard-to-exercise rights, and limited community control, and their dialogues specified these conditions rather than resolving disagreement. We describe this pattern as conditional endorsement and argue that AI literacy policy should be designed and evaluated for adults with the fewest digital skills.
