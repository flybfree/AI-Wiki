[SK Telecom Opens Joint AI Program with Seoul National University, Current Developers to Teach]
Current experts leading SK Telecom's artificial intelligence (AI) development are taking to the podium at Seoul National University. They plan to share research achievements and commercialization experience accumulated in real industrial settings—from AI services to large language models (LLMs) and infrastructure—with graduate students.
SK Telecom announced on the 24th that it is launching the "SKT-Seoul National University AI Joint Program" for graduate students in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering in the second semester of this year. The course will run for 15 weeks starting on the 1st of next month.
The curriculum is structured around three areas—AI apps and services, AI modeling, and AI infrastructure—aligned with SK Telecom's full-stack AI business strategy. Rather than focusing on AI theory, the course emphasizes the technologies SK Telecom has acquired through actual research and development and real-world examples of how these have been implemented as services.
The first lecture will be delivered by Jung Jae-heon, CEO of SK Telecom. On the 1st of next month, he will give a special lecture on "Choices and Opportunities in the AI Era" to approximately 300 graduate and undergraduate students at Seoul National University. He is expected to discuss the changes AI technology will bring to industry and society, as well as SK Telecom's outlook on the future of the AI industry.
Following the opening lecture, current employees responsible for SK Telecom's AI business and R&D will take turns at the podium. Yoo Kyung-sang, head of AI CIC, and Kim Tae-yoon, head of foundation models, along with executives, team leaders, and PhD-level members of the AI development organization, will lead classes directly.
The AI education partnership between SK Telecom and Seoul National University marks its 10th year in 2026. SK Telecom has been operating courses taught by current employees at South Korean universities including Seoul National University, while running hands-on AI research programs for undergraduate and graduate students. The goal is to secure talent and expand South Korea's AI ecosystem by integrating industrial research achievements and commercialization experience into university education.
CEO Jung Jae-heon said, "It is deeply meaningful that the long-standing industry-academia collaboration with Seoul National University continues once again through this course. We will continue to cooperate with academia so that today's students can lead tomorrow's AI industry."
This joint program is notable as the first regular graduate school course introduced by SK Telecom since it reorganized its AI business around a full-stack strategy. SK Telecom is building a business structure spanning the entire spectrum from AI infrastructure to models and services, and through this course, the company plans to directly deploy its technological capabilities and field experience toward nurturing future talent.
The company expects that early talent identification and development through industry-academia collaboration will translate into long-term competitive advantage amid intensifying competition for AI talent. In particular, the approach of having current executives and PhD-level researchers participate extensively in a regular curriculum differentiates this program from existing one-off special lectures or internship programs.
Through this course, graduate students in Seoul National University's Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering will gain direct exposure to development cases of AI services actually operated by the telecom company, LLM training and optimization processes, and AI infrastructure build-out know-how. Practical industry knowledge that is difficult to obtain from theory-focused graduate curricula is the core of the lectures.
