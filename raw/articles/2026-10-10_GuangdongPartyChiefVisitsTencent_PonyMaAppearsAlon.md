On the first working day after China's National Day holiday, Huang Kunming, Communist Party secretary of Guangdong Province, made Shenzhen tech companies the first stop on his inspection tour. On October 8, he visited Tencent's headquarters, accompanied by founder Pony Ma, to review the company's AI research and development progress. At the discussion session, Ma sat next to Tencent's 28-year-old Chief AI Scientist Yao Shunyu — an image that quickly drew widespread attention online.
According to the
Nanfang Daily
, Huang spent the day visiting port hubs and platform enterprises. At Tencent's exhibition hall, he observed demonstrations of core product applications, focused on the company's AI R&D innovation and industrial deployment, and heard reports on digital technology innovation and its enabling role across multiple sectors.
During discussions with company executives and young scientists, Huang affirmed Tencent's achievements in driving steady business growth through independent innovation. He noted that AI development is still in its early stages of rapid expansion, and that Guangdong has explicitly designated AI as a key development area for the "15th Five-Year Plan" period and beyond. He expressed hope that Tencent would firmly seize the opportunities of the new era, commit to long-cycle investment in AI, deeply participate in Guangdong's AI infrastructure construction, continue expanding its computing power deployment in the province, strengthen both general-purpose and vertical large language models, and accelerate application promotion — helping Guangdong speed up intelligent transformation across the entire industrial chain.
A rare joint appearance
Video footage showed Ma (55) and Yao (28) seated side by side at the discussion session. The former is Tencent's founder, chairman, and CEO; the latter serves as Chief AI Scientist in the company's CEO/President's Office and heads both the AI Infrastructure division and the Large Language Model division.
Public records show Yao was born in 1998, earned his undergraduate degree from Tsinghua University's Yao Class, and went to Princeton University in 2019 to specialize in natural language processing and reinforcement learning, receiving his PhD in computer science in 2024. During his doctoral studies, he proposed the Tree of Thoughts framework to improve decision-making models and developed the CoALA modular cognitive architecture.
After graduation, Yao joined OpenAI in 2024 and quickly became one of its core researchers, contributing to projects including Operator and DeepResearch. In December 2025, Tencent announced his appointment as Chief AI Scientist in the CEO/President's Office, tasked with driving the company's AI-related businesses.
AI investment continues to accelerate
Tencent's investment in AI is expanding at a visible pace. First-half 2026 results released in August showed revenue of 401.243 billion yuan (approximately $60.0 billion), up 10% year-on-year; non-IFRS profit attributable to equity holders reached 136.32 billion yuan (approximately $20.4 billion), also up 10%. Second-quarter revenue came in at 204.785 billion yuan (approximately $30.6 billion), up 11% year-on-year, while non-IFRS net profit was 68.415 billion yuan (approximately $10.2 billion), up 9%. Excluding the impact of new AI products, non-IFRS operating profit grew 19% year-on-year to 86.1 billion yuan (approximately $12.9 billion).
The jump in capital expenditure is even more striking. First-half R&D spending reached 49.82 billion yuan (approximately $7.4 billion), up 27% year-on-year. Capital expenditure totaled 84.72 billion yuan (approximately $12.7 billion), with the second quarter alone accounting for 52.8 billion yuan (approximately $7.9 billion) — up 65% quarter-on-quarter and 176% year-on-year — primarily directed at AI computing power and data center infrastructure.

Tencent's business now spans large language model development, computing power infrastructure, and AI applications, with products including the Hunyuan LLM, Yuanbao, WorkBuddy, and CodeBuddy, exploring AI deployment in office productivity, programming, and other scenarios.
Pony Ma's stance on AI has also undergone a subtle shift. At a previous shareholder meeting, he responded to external concerns about Tencent's AI development pace, candidly acknowledging that the company had shortcomings in foundational AI capabilities in its early days. The recruitment of young scientists like Yao Shunyu and the redirection of capital expenditure toward computing infrastructure signal Tencent's sense of urgency and determination in the AI race.
For Guangdong Province, Tencent's AI strategy aligns closely with local industrial policy. Huang Kunming's visit sends a clear signal: Guangdong aims to make AI the core driver of full-industrial-chain upgrades during the "15th Five-Year Plan" period, and Tencent — as one of the province's largest platform enterprises — is expected to play a pivotal role.
