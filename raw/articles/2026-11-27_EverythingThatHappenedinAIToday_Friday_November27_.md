Around the Horn: Friday, November 27, 2026
China's AI development could be about to get slower and more expensive.
Reuters reported that Z.AI
has fallen behind schedule on its next flagship model. Meanwhile, Moonshot AI reportedly expects the time between major model releases could increase from six months to a year or longer if it loses access to overseas computing resources.
Chinese developers face restrictions on advanced American chips alongside efforts by OpenAI and Anthropic to prevent model distillation. Distillation involves training a model with responses generated by a more capable AI system, which can reduce the cost of developing competitive models.
The restrictions are also affecting the international cloud computing market:
Two California-based Nvidia customers
were reportedly Chinese-owned companies used to circumvent US export restrictions.
Nvidia chip sellers reportedly warned
specialized cloud providers that they could lose access to GPUs if they rented computing capacity to Chinese companies.
Nvidia instructed server partners
to suspend shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, and Indonesia, putting existing data center investments and financing arrangements at risk.

Chinese AI developers may need to rely more on domestic hardware and techniques that reduce their models' computing requirements. The next generation of Chinese AI releases could reveal how much progress remains possible under these restrictions.

OpenAI corrected a billing error
that caused receive incorrect discounts on GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol usage. Users will now reach their limits 40% faster on Astra and 25% faster on Sol compared with last week. Eligible subscribers will receive an additional usage reset, while Business users get free Fast Mode through the end of 2026. OpenAI is also testing a combined Chat and Work experience.
Huawei teased a next-generation
high-bandwidth memory chip that could compete with Samsung and SK Hynix. The technology gives AI processors rapid access to large amounts of data and represents an attempt to expand China's domestic chip manufacturing capabilities.
Canada added C$700 million
to its AI Compute Access Fund, bringing its total to C$1 billion. The government will
cover up to 80%
of eligible computing expenses for businesses that choose qualifying sovereign cloud providers.
AI reshaped Black Friday shopping
as retailers embraced AI-powered tools. However, recent cyberattacks have exposed the risks facing a retail industry with limited cyber insurance coverage.
The European Union could vote
on whether to authorize Tesla's assisted-driving system across the bloc during a December 15 meeting, according to a draft European Commission agenda.

Nvidia faced renewed credit concerns

that reads an application's environment configuration and provisions services such as databases, storage, and AI access keys. Developers can manage these resources through a central dashboard.
