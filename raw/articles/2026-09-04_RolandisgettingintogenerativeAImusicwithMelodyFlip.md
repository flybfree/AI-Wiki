It’s not quite the
“push button; get song” of Suno
, but Roland’s new
Melody Flip
tool marks the company’s foray into generative AI music. Available as a plug-in for your digital audio workstation (DAW), Melody Flip offers around 250 “Palettes,” which are essentially themed collections of musical ideas sorted by genre. You can start from scratch and ask it to generate any combination of melody, chord progression, bassline, or drums. Or you can feed it a reference track and have it build off the melodic ideas from that.
Unlike Suno or Udio, Melody Flip doesn’t spit out fully polished songs with vocals and full arrangements. Instead, it offers simple musical loops meant to serve as a creative spark for you to build on. There are fairly limited control over the outputs. You can’t feed Melody Flip a text prompt like “acoustic country rock with clean male vocals, literate singer-songwriter, gentle brushed drums, and sparse pedal steel at 98BPM” and get a
Jason Isbell ripoff
. Instead, you basically just choose a genre, note density, BPM, musical key, and take what you get. Though the genre options are pretty fine-grained, going from standard fare like “80s disco” and “90s R&B” to esoteric things like “Kawaii Future Bass” and “Anime World.”
The sound selection is further proof that you’re not supposed to use Melody Flip outputs out of the box. The presets available are mostly the sort of general MIDI tones you’d find in ‘90s video games. It seems like the intended use is to export the MIDI data from Melody Flip directly into your DAW to manipulate and pair with other, more powerful synth plugins.
After years of seeming lost in the woods and
alienating
customers
with its
messy
Roland Cloud
subscription service, the company was back on track recently. The
P-6 Creative Sampler
,
SH-4d
, and Gaia 2 synth all addressed major issues with the company’s lineup. And Roland finally
listened to customers
last year, paying loving tribute to its analog heritage with the
TR-1000
. But considering the
general
response
from the
music
community
to generative AI, Melody Flip is unlikely to garner it much goodwill.
