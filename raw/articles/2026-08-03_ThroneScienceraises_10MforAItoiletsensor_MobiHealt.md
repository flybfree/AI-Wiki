# Throne Science raises $10M for AI toilet sensor | MobiHealthNews

## Full Article

Throne Science, maker of an AI toilet sensor, closed a $10 million Series A funding round.

Will Ventures led the round, with participation from Emerson Collective, Workshop, LEAD VC, Salt VC, Accomplice, Moxxie, Ventures Together, Symphony, Felix Capital, Cosmic Venture Partners, Offscript, V1.VC and Morrison Seger.

The Austin, Texas-based company will use the capital to expand its workforce and advance research and development for the next generation of its sensor.

Throne said it ultimately aims to develop a version of the technology that can identify potential early signs of colorectal cancer and cancers of the urinary tract.

"We live in an age where tracking steps, heart rate, sleep and recovery has become the norm, and yet we disregard the vital insights that come with our bowel movements," Throne CEO and founder Scott Hickle said in a statement.

"Throne is building a platform that will not only provide valuable data about gut health and hydration, but in future iterations of the technology, will also sound alarms regarding more serious health conditions."

**WHAT IT DOES**

Founded in 2023, Throne Sensor launched its sensor to consumers in March.

The Throne One sensor device clips onto the rim of a standard toilet and uses computer vision and AI to track stool form and frequency, hydration based on urine color analysis, urinary flow habits and bathroom habits.

The information is sent to a companion app that displays users’ results and longer-term trends.

The company earlier this year released a beta version of its Gut Health AI coach, which allows users to ask questions about their health data and receive personalized feedback.

The Throne One sensor costs $399, plus a $5.99 monthly subscription.

**MARKET SNAPSHOT**

Throne Science was founded in Austin in 2023 by CEO Scott Hickle, CTO Tim Blumberg and John Capodilupo, a cofounder and former CTO of[Whoop](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/tag/whoop). It launched its Throne One sensor in March 2026.

Throne [raised $4 million in seed financing](https://www.mofo.com/resources/news/250604-mofo-advised-throne-on-4m) in 2025. That round was led by Moxxie Ventures.

Other companies have developed toilet-based systems for collecting health information.

Connected health company [Withings](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/tag/withings) unveiled [U-Scan](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/withings-announces-miniaturized-health-lab-urine-analysis), an in-home urine analysis platform designed to sit inside a toilet bowl and assess biomarkers associated with hydration, nutrition and reproductive health.

[Casana](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/tag/casana) received [FDA clearance for its Heart Seat](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/casana-scores-fda-clearance-health-monitoring-toilet-seat) in 2023. The connected toilet seat monitors heart rate and oxygen saturation and sends the information to healthcare providers.

Withings and Kohler began selling toilet-mounted health trackers in late 2025.

The devices generally cost between $199 and $599, with some requiring subscriptions or replacement testing cartridges. However, the companies have not disclosed unit sales, leaving the level of consumer adoption unclear.
