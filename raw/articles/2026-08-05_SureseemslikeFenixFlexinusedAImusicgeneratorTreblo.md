We were pretty sure that
Fenix Flexin’s “Rubberz”
was made using AI, but musician
Medasin
was confident that it was made using Treblo specifically. Now the company and a new
detection tool
seem to confirm it.
On Monday, the company announced the open-source
Treblo AI Music Classifier
, which detects when a song was generated using Treblo, though not other AI tools. According to a blog post, it has a false positive rate of less than 1 in 10,000. That’s not unimpeachable, but it’s notable that the company’s own Classifier determined that “Rubberz” was “very likely Treblo” with “high confidence,” adding more fuel to the PR nightmare for Fenix.
Treblo does seem to be relishing the attention a bit. “The classifier returns a very high probability score for Rubberz, indicating the released audio was generated almost entirely by our model. That would make it the first known AI-generated song to reach the Billboard Hot 100,” CEO Ryan Tremblay said in a statement to
The Verge
. He continued, “Someone appears to have used our model to find a sound that millions of people connected with and that’s exciting.”
[Screenshot of the Treblo AI Music Classifier.]
Unsurprisingly, “Rubberz” is “very likely” AI.
Screenshot: Terrence O’Brien / The Verge
Fenix has continued to deny that Rubberz was made using AI, and has posted clips of what he claims are the project files of the recording session. However, those low-quality videos have done little to dispel any allegations of the song being AI-generated. According to
Stereogum
, Medasin contended in a since-expired Instagram story that Fenix clearly just used an AI stem separator to fake the session.
Fenix did not immediately return a request for comment.
Correction August 5th:
This story originally stated that the false positive rate for Treblo’s detector was 1 in 1,000; it is 1 in 10,000.
