CHARLOTTESVILLE, Va. (CBS19 NEWS) -- Artificial intelligence is becoming an increasingly important tool in medical research and treatment, including here in Charlottesville.
Researchers are using AI to help make medical procedures more efficient, speed up the development of new treatments and support doctors and patients.
Will Mauldin, CEO of Rivanna Medical, said his team uses AI to help make epidural spinal anesthesia more efficient and safer for patients.
“So going from completely blind, you touch and feel and kind of poke around, to AI-driven, automated placement of those needles to make it more efficient and safer for patients,” Mauldin said.
Mauldin said the AI used in medical devices today is different from the generative AI systems making headlines.
“You develop your AI model, you test the performance, you validate it, it’s locked, it goes on the device and then it performs according to your validation,” he said.
That means the models are not designed to change on their own after they are deployed.
Researchers are also using AI earlier in the medical development process, with the goal of speeding up how quickly new treatments can reach patients.
Dr. Barrett W. R. Peters, whose company SonoFX is working on biomaterials for people with vocal cord paralysis and age-related voice problems, said AI can significantly shorten the development timeline.
“We’re building machine learning frameworks to compress that development timeline from years to just days,” Peters said. “And so we can get solutions to patients out faster than they would have previously.”
Peters said the potential benefits of AI need to be balanced with the risks.
The rapid development of AI is also raising questions on Capitol Hill about how the technology should be regulated.
“Before you release a new model, it has to be tested,” U.S. Sen. Mark Warner said. “Make sure that where these companies experiment is in what’s called the sandbox.”
Republican Rep. John McGuire also said AI needs guardrails, while emphasizing the competition between the United States and China.
“I know one thing, we can’t let China dominate the world with AI,” McGuire said. “We’ve got to be measured and smart about it. It’s incredible what it’s going to be able to do with medicine and everything else. There needs to be some guardrails.”
Back in Charlottesville, researchers say AI could help the United States move medical research forward, while also requiring caution about how the technology is used.
