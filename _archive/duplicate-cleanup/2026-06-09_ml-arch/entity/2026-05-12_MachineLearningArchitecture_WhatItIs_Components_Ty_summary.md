## Summary  
Machine learning architecture refers to the structural design of a system that orchestrates data ingestion, preprocessing, model training, evaluation, and prediction. It encompasses components such as data collection, cleansing, transformation, integration, sampling, splitting, and ensures scalability, maintainability, security, and continuous improvement.  

## Key Takeaways  
- A robust ML architecture is essential for building scalable, efficient, and maintainable machine learning applications.  
- Data ingestion stages—collection, cleaning, transformation, integration, sampling, and splitting—are critical to model performance and data quality.  
- The architecture must balance technical requirements with security, privacy, and the need for ongoing improvement.  

## Context  
In today’s AI landscape, organizations increasingly rely on automated ML pipelines that integrate multiple tools and services. Understanding architectural principles helps teams avoid bottlenecks in experimentation, deployment, and governance, aligning technical choices with business objectives.  

## Implications  
Designing a well‑structured machine learning architecture reduces time spent on debugging, improves model reliability, and supports regulatory compliance. It also enables faster iteration cycles, lower operational costs, and greater confidence in AI outcomes across industries.
