## Summary
SymptomAI studies conversational agents for everyday symptom assessment using a large-scale deployment inside the Fitbit app. The paper finds that dedicated symptom interviews can improve differential diagnosis accuracy relative to more user-guided conversations and even outperform blinded clinicians given the same dialogue in the study setting.

## Key Takeaways
- Deployed five AI agents to 13,917 participants in a real-world study.
- 1,228 participants reported clinician diagnoses; 517 were clinician-reviewed in depth.
- SymptomAI diagnoses were significantly more accurate than independent clinicians on the same dialogue.
- Agentic, interview-style conversations outperformed baseline user-guided symptom chats.

## Context
The work targets a gap between benchmark performance on curated medical vignettes and performance on everyday symptom reports from real users. It also extends analysis to wearable metrics across many conditions to study associations between symptoms and physiological changes.

## Implications
The results support designing symptom-assessment assistants that actively elicit missing information rather than waiting for the user to volunteer everything. The study also suggests wearable data may help connect diagnosis labels with broader physiological patterns, though the authors note limitations from self-reported ground truth.
