## Summary
This paper addresses the critical bottleneck of environment dynamics modeling for advanced AI agents that move beyond text generation to goal-oriented interaction. It introduces a structured framework—a "levels x laws" taxonomy—to categorize and unify disparate concepts of world models across various research domains (physical, digital, social, scientific). The core contribution is synthesizing over 400 works into a comprehensive roadmap, charting the evolution from simple next-step prediction to autonomous model revision.

## Key Contributions
1. **Levels x Laws Taxonomy:** Proposes a novel dual-axis framework defining three capability levels (L1 Predictor, L2 Simulator, L3 Evolver) and four governing law regimes (physical, digital, social, scientific).
2. **Systematic Synthesis:** Synthesizes over 400 works and analyzes more than 100 representative systems spanning model-based RL, video generation, web agents, and multi-agent simulations.
3. **Evaluation Guidance:** Outlines decision-centric evaluation principles and proposes a minimal reproducible package to standardize the assessment of world models across different level-regime pairs.

## Methodology
The authors employed a comprehensive literature review and taxonomy construction approach. By organizing existing research along two orthogonal axes—capability levels (L1, L2, L3) and governing law regimes (Physical, Digital, Social, Scientific)—they created a structured matrix to classify the state-of-the-art in world modeling.
