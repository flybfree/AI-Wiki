## Summary
This paper addresses the Plan Existence Problem, which determines if a sequence of epistemic actions can guide an agent from an initial state to satisfy a goal defined in modal logic. The primary contribution is proving that even under highly restrictive conditions—specifically, when action preconditions have a modal depth of at most 1 and there are no postconditions—the problem remains undecidable. This result establishes fundamental theoretical limits on the computational tractability of planning systems operating within epistemic logics.

## Key Contributions
1. **Undecidability Proof:** The paper provides a formal proof demonstrating that the Plan Existence Problem is undecidable, establishing a significant theoretical boundary for automated planning research.
2. **Relaxed Constraints:** It proves this undecidability
