## Summary

Generative AI is transforming what employers expect from knowledge workers—not by replacing expertise but by raising the bar for it. A study of three sectors that recruit the largest proportion of MBA graduates found that three capabilities will increasingly distinguish high-potential talent:

1. **Taking on broader, AI-enabled roles** — Professionals are expected to work alongside and leverage AI tools as core collaborators in their daily tasks.
2. **Synthesizing knowledge across functions to accelerate innovation** — The ability to integrate insights from multiple domains and apply them quickly is now a key differentiator.
3. **Redesigning workflows around intelligent agents** — Employees must be able to restructure processes to optimize how AI agents are embedded in their work.

### Context

The rapid adoption of generative AI in the workplace has been dramatic: one executive at a large multinational bank noted that three years ago, no one used gen AI tools, but today **100% of his department uses them every day for hours per day**. This shift means employers are looking for candidates who can not only use these tools but also **rethink how work gets done** in an AI-augmented environment.

### Implication

For new hires and MBA graduates, the message is clear: technical skills alone aren't enough. The competitive edge now lies in **AI fluency**, cross-functional synthesis, and workflow redesign—capabilities that turn AI from a tool into a strategic partner.
