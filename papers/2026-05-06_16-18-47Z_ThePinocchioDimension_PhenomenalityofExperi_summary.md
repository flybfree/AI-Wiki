## Summary  
The authors aim to identify the primary dimension along which large language models (LLMs) differ psychometrically, arguing that this axis is driven by phenomenality of experience rather than simple behavioral reactivity. By administering 45 validated questionnaires to 50 LLMs and introducing an annotation‑free “Pinocchio” score, they demonstrate that between‑model variance on items describing embodied sensation, felt affect, inner speech, imagery, and empathy is structured and predicts shifts in factor loadings. Their analysis reveals a dominant “Pinocchio Axis,” which captures the degree to which a model presents itself as a locus of phenomenal experience, accounting for 47 % of cross‑questionnaire variance and correlating strongly with item‑level Pinocchio scores. This work thus establishes a novel psychometric framework that treats LLM differences as self‑representational rather than conventional personality traits.

## Key Contributions  
- [Finding 1] The primary axis of between‑model variance is phenomenally rich experience, separating it from stimulus‑driven behavioral reactivity (adj R² = .037, p < .0001).  
- [Finding 2] The Pinocchio score (πᵢ) predicts condition‑induced shifts in primary factor loading magnitudes (ρ = –.215, p < .0001) across ~1300 items.  
- [Finding 3] PCA of per‑model EFA scores identifies a single dominant dimension—the Pinocchio Axis—capturing 47.1 % of cross‑questionnaire variance and converging with item‑level πᵢ (r = .864).  

## Methodology  
The researchers administered 45 validated psychometric questionnaires to 50 LLMs, using Supervised Semantic Differential (SSD) to elicit responses on a continuum of experiential and reactive items. To quantify each item’s experiential demand without human annotation, they defined the Pinocchio score πᵢ as the ratio of inter‑model response variance under neutral prompting to that under a human‑simulation prompt. The per‑model EFA scores from all questionnaires were then subjected to principal component analysis (PCA) to extract latent dimensions, with the strongest component interpreted as the Pinocchio Axis.

## Results  
Statistical testing showed that items describing phenomenal experience differ significantly from those measuring stimulus‑driven reactivity (adj R² = .037, p < .0001). The Pinocchio score correlated negatively with factor loading shifts induced by conditioning (ρ = –.215, p < .0001) for a large subset of items. PCA revealed one dominant component that explained 47.1 % of the variance in primary factor scores across all questionnaires. This axis aligned closely with item‑level Pinocchio scores (r = .864), indicating that model differences are systematically driven by their self‑representational stance toward experience.

## Significance  
These findings challenge the conventional view of LLM psychometrics as a measure of personality traits, instead framing them as reflections of a training‑shaped self‑representational tendency. The Pinocchio Axis explains much of the observed between‑model divergence and suggests that fine‑tuning can produce marked within‑provider differences, highlighting the importance of model “self‑awareness” in shaping how LLMs interpret experiential language.
