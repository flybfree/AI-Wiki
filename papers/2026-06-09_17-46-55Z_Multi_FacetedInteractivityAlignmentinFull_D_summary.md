## Summary  
Full‑duplex speech models can listen and speak simultaneously, but their current training only maximizes token‑level likelihood, which leaves interaction problems such as excessive silence, poor turn‑taking, absent backchanneling, and inability to handle user interruptions. This paper proposes a post‑training reinforcement‑learning (RL) alignment method that tackles all four canonical axes of interactivity and adds an LLM‑based reward for response quality. By optimizing each axis with short audio segments from human conversations, the authors achieve a systematic improvement in conversational flow without degrading semantic content.

## Key Contributions  
- Finding 1: A comprehensive post‑training RL framework that simultaneously optimizes pause handling, turn‑taking, backchanneling, and user interruption.  
- Finding 2: Extraction of axis‑specific short audio segments from human conversation corpora to define tailored reward functions for each interactivity dimension.  
- Finding 3: Integration of an LLM‑driven response‑quality reward to prevent semantic degradation while fine‑tuning the model.

## Methodology  
The authors collect multi‑turn spoken dialogues, segment them into short windows that correspond to each interactive axis (e.g., a pause window for silence detection, a turn‑taking window for response latency). For each segment they construct a reward: pause handling rewards early or appropriate silences; turn‑taking rewards responses that occur within a target time window; backchanneling rewards vocalizations during the speaker’s turn; interruption rewards the model’s ability to resume after an external interruption. These axis‑specific rewards are fed into a reinforcement‑learning fine‑tuning loop, while an additional reward generated by a large language model evaluates semantic quality of the output. The combined RL process is applied to two open‑source full‑duplex models—Moshi and PersonaPlex.

## Results  
Experimental evaluation shows that both offline (pre‑recorded audio) and real‑time multi‑turn dialogue tests yield consistent gains in interactivity metrics such as reduced silence duration, improved turn‑taking latency, increased backchanneling frequency, and better handling of user interruptions. The LLM reward ensures that response quality remains high, with no noticeable drop in semantic correctness compared to the baseline token‑level training.

## Significance  
This work moves full‑duplex dialogue from a purely supervised token‑likelihood objective to an interaction‑aware optimization pipeline, directly addressing longstanding usability issues that limit natural conversation. By providing a modular, axis‑specific RL alignment strategy and preserving semantic fidelity through LLM feedback, the method offers a scalable path toward more human‑like spoken interactions.
