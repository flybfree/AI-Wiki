## Summary
This paper introduces MathDuels, a novel self-play benchmark designed to evaluate frontier Large Language Models (LLMs) in both problem generation and solution capabilities. Recognizing that traditional static benchmarks fail to differentiate model abilities as LLMs approach performance ceilings, MathDuels forces models into a dual role: authors who generate adversarial math problems and solvers who tackle those generated by peers. The framework uses advanced generation pipelines and statistical modeling to provide a dynamic assessment of underlying mathematical reasoning skills, revealing capability separations invisible in single-role testing.

## Key Contributions
1. **Self-Play Dual-Role Benchmark:** Introduced MathDuels, which requires models to actively generate challenging problems (authoring) while simultaneously solving the problems created by other participants.
2. **Adversarial Problem Generation:** Developed a three-stage generation pipeline (meta-prompting, problem generation, and difficulty amplification) to ensure generated problems are rigorous, non-trivial, and validated against ill-posed questions.
