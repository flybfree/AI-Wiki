## Summary
ClinSeekAgent addresses a critical gap in clinical decision support systems by shifting the paradigm from passive evidence consumption to active, automated evidence acquisition. Unlike existing agentic frameworks that assume curated data is already available, this framework enables Large Language Models to dynamically seek, plan, and synthesize multimodal evidence from heterogeneous sources such as raw Electronic Health Records (EHRs), medical knowledge bases, and imaging tools. The authors introduce ClinSeek-Bench to evaluate this capability, demonstrating significant performance gains across various frontier models. Furthermore, the paper validates ClinSeekAgent as a robust training pipeline for distilling complex agentic trajectories into compact open-source models.

## Key Contributions
- **Active Evidence Seeking Framework**: The introduction of ClinSeekAgent, an agentic system that autonomously queries medical databases, navigates raw EHRs, and invokes imaging tools to gather necessary clinical evidence, rather than relying on pre-selected inputs.
- **Comprehensive Benchmarking**: The creation of ClinSeek-Bench, a novel evaluation suite that contrasts curated input reasoning with automated evidence-seeking, providing a rigorous testbed for dynamic clinical reasoning capabilities.
- **Effective Knowledge Distillation**: The development of a training-time pipeline that successfully distills high-quality agentic trajectories into the compact ClinSeek-35B-A3B model, significantly narrowing the performance gap with frontier proprietary models like Claude Opus 4.6.

## Methodology
The authors designed ClinSeekAgent to operate in two distinct modes: inference-time reasoning and training-time distillation. During inference, the agent is given only a clinical query and access to raw data sources. It employs an iterative loop to query medical knowledge bases, extract relevant information from unstructured EHRs, and analyze medical images. As new information emerges, the agent refines its hypotheses and integrates the collected multimodal evidence to form grounded clinical decisions. For the training component, the authors utilized the trajectories generated by this agentic process to fine-tune open-source models. They constructed ClinSeek-Bench to pair fixed pre-selected evidence with automated evidence-seeking tasks, allowing for a controlled comparison of model performance under different evidence availability conditions.

## Results
On text-only EHR tasks, ClinSeekAgent improved the overall F1 score of Claude Opus 4.6 from 60.0 to 63.2 and MiniMax M2.5 from 43.1 to 47.3, with positive risk-prediction gains observed in seven out of nine evaluated host models. The impact was even more pronounced in multimodal tasks, where Claude Opus 4.6 saw a substantial improvement from 47.5 to 62.6 (+15.1 points), with all evaluated models showing gains across three CXR-related task groups. Additionally, the distilled ClinSeek-35B-A3B model achieved an average F1 of 34.0 on the AgentEHR-Bench, representing an +11.9 point improvement over its Qwen3.5-35B-A3B baseline and approaching the performance of Claude Opus 4.6.

## Significance
This research is significant because it moves clinical AI from static, data-dependent models to dynamic, autonomous agents capable of handling real-world clinical workflows where evidence is scattered and heterogeneous. By demonstrating that active evidence seeking significantly boosts accuracy and that these complex behaviors can be distilled into smaller models, the work paves the way for more accessible, efficient, and robust clinical decision support systems that do not rely on expensive, large-scale proprietary models.
