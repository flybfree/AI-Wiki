## Summary
This research paper investigates the practical utility of integrating Large Language Model (LLM)-derived error highlights and automatic post-editing (APE) correction suggestions into the workflow of professional translators. The primary goal is to determine whether these advanced, AI-driven features can enhance the efficiency and quality of post-editing machine-translated text compared to traditional methods. The authors specifically compare a condition using APE-generated suggestions against standard post-editing and post-editing with Quality Estimation (QE)-derived highlights. The study aims to fill a critical gap in the literature by providing empirical evidence on the effectiveness of newer, LLM-based assistance tools in real-world translation scenarios.

## Key Contributions
- The study provides the first comparative empirical evidence regarding the impact of LLM-derived error highlights and correction suggestions versus traditional QE-derived highlights on professional translator performance.
- It demonstrates that while neither APE highlights nor correction suggestions significantly improved raw productivity or translation quality metrics compared to regular post-editing, they offered distinct advantages in user experience.
- The research highlights a clear preference among professional translators for APE-derived highlights over QE-derived ones, suggesting that the nature of the AI assistance matters more than the mere presence of assistance.
- It establishes that correction suggestions, while not boosting speed or accuracy, significantly improved the overall subjective user experience, indicating that perceived support is a valuable component of post-editing tools.

## Methodology
The authors conducted a controlled user study involving professional translators specializing in English-to-Dutch translation tasks. Participants were divided into three distinct groups to test different post-editing conditions. The first group performed regular post-editing (PE) without any additional AI assistance, serving as the baseline. The second group utilized post-editing interfaces equipped with Quality Estimation (QE)-derived error highlights, which indicate the likelihood of errors in specific segments. The third group employed post-editing features powered by Automatic Post-Editing (APE) and Large Language Models, which provided both error highlights and specific correction suggestions. The study measured three key dependent variables: productivity (measured by time taken per segment), quality (evaluated through standard translation metrics and expert review), and user experience (assessed via post-task surveys and subjective feedback). This multi-metric approach allowed for a comprehensive analysis of both objective performance and subjective satisfaction.

## Results
The experimental results revealed that neither the APE error highlights nor the correction suggestions led to statistically significant gains in productivity or translation quality when compared to the regular post-editing baseline. This suggests that for professional translators, the cognitive load of evaluating AI suggestions may offset the time saved by using them. However, in terms of user experience, the APE highlights were rated more favorably than the QE-derived highlights, indicating that LLM-based explanations are more intuitive or trustworthy to users. Furthermore, the inclusion of correction suggestions significantly improved the overall user experience, making the process feel more supportive and less tedious, even if it did not make the translators faster or more accurate.

## Significance
This study is significant because it challenges the assumption that more advanced AI features automatically lead to better performance in human-in-the-loop systems. It underscores the importance of user experience and trust in the adoption of post-editing tools, suggesting that usability and perceived helpfulness are just as critical as objective metrics. For developers of translation technology, the findings imply that focusing on intuitive, LLM-driven explanations and supportive suggestions may yield better adoption rates than focusing solely on speed or accuracy improvements.
