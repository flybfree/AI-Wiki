## Summary
This paper investigates the emerging adoption patterns of Large Language Models (LLMs) among professional data visualization practitioners, addressing a significant gap in existing literature regarding how these tools are integrated into creative and technical workflows. The authors introduce the concept of "vibe analysis," a new paradigm analogous to "vibe coding," where designers rely on intuitive interaction with LLMs rather than strict procedural adherence for generating visualizations. Through qualitative inquiry, the study highlights that while practitioners leverage LLMs for efficiency in data preparation and code generation, they face unique challenges related to domain-specific knowledge gaps and the verification of visual outputs. Ultimately, the research underscores the need for future visualization tools to incorporate built-in guidance and constraints to mitigate errors inherent in current LLM capabilities.

## Key Contributions
- The identification and definition of "vibe analysis" as a distinct workflow where data visualization designers use LLMs intuitively for both creative ideation and technical implementation, mirroring trends seen in software development.
- Documentation of specific challenges faced by practitioners, including significant gaps in visualization knowledge and the difficulty of verifying chart accuracy, which parallel but differ from general LLM adoption issues.
- A call to action for the development of next-generation data visualization tools that integrate LLM mediation with strict adherence to visualization best practices, constraints, and domain-specific guidance to enhance reliability.

## Methodology
The authors employed a qualitative research approach centered on semi-structured interviews. Participants were recruited from the Data Visualization Society, a global community comprising professional data visualization designers and practitioners. This method allowed for an in-depth exploration of individual experiences, workflows, and perceptions regarding the utility and limitations of LLMs in their daily tasks. The interview protocol focused on understanding how LLMs fit into existing processes, the types of tasks where they were most useful, and the specific pain points encountered during adoption.

## Results
The study revealed that data visualization designers actively utilize LLMs across various stages of the visualization pipeline, including data cleaning, exploratory analysis, and code generation for plotting libraries. However, the results also highlighted a critical reliance on human expertise to validate outputs, as LLMs often produce plausible but incorrect visual encodings or statistical interpretations. Participants noted that while LLMs accelerate the technical aspects of creation, they lack inherent understanding of visualization theory, leading to potential misinterpretations of data relationships. The "vibe analysis" workflow emerged as a dominant pattern where practitioners iterate rapidly with AI suggestions, trusting their own aesthetic and analytical judgment to filter out errors.

## Significance
This work is significant because it shifts the focus from technical benchmarking of LLMs in visualization to the human-centric experience of adoption within professional practice. By naming "vibe analysis," the paper provides a conceptual framework for understanding how non-expert programmers or designers navigate AI-assisted workflows. It highlights the urgent need for interdisciplinary research that bridges natural language processing with data visualization theory, ensuring that automated tools respect the semantic and aesthetic constraints necessary for effective communication of data.
