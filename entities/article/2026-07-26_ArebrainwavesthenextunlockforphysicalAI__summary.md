## Summary  
The article explores how measuring brain waves during physical tasks could generate richer training data for robotics, moving beyond traditional video capture to create a new class of annotated data that may overcome the scarcity bottleneck in AI model development. Encord and Zander Labs are piloting this approach by tagging human neural activity with robotic manipulation.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Brain wave sensors can provide additional cues (error, intent) for robot models.  
- The data‑generation market is expanding as real‑world physical training data is scarce.  
- Success depends on whether brain‑tagged data actually improves model performance before scaling.  

## Context  
In the broader AI landscape, the shortage of high‑quality physical interaction data limits progress in humanoid and warehouse robotics, much like LLMs rely on vast textual corpora. Companies are turning to egocentric video streams or remote robot operation, but both have limitations; generating synthetic or enriched datasets is costly. The brain‑wave approach represents a speculative yet promising alternative that could fill the gap between data availability and model capability.  

## Implications  
If validated, this method could democratize high‑quality training data, allowing smaller firms to compete with large players who otherwise must build massive proprietary datasets. It also signals a shift from purely algorithmic solutions toward multimodal sensing that captures both visual and neurophysiological states, potentially accelerating the development of embodied AI.
