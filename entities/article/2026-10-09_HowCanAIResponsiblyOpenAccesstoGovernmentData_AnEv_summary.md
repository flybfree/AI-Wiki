## Summary
This article details an evaluation conducted by the Urban Institute regarding the development of a National Secure Data Service (NSDS) data concierge, which utilizes agentic AI chatbots to provide researchers with direct access to scattered federal and state statistical data. The evaluation resulted in the establishment of four core principles designed to ensure that AI-driven data services remain accurate, transparent, and aligned with existing statistical governance standards. These recommendations aim to guide the responsible integration of AI into government data infrastructure, ensuring that technology augments rather than replaces official data sources.

## Key Takeaways
- **Augmentation over Replacement:** AI chatbots should serve as tools to augment existing statistical systems, not replace them. They must credit official sources and encourage users to review original materials to maintain data integrity and trust.
- **Structured Context Protocols:** Developers should co-develop agency-specific model context protocols with data stewards. These technical infrastructures ensure that AI models refer to predefined, accurate data collections and metadata, reducing the risk of hallucinations or misinterpretations.
- **Governance and Standards:** The lack of concrete federal standards for AI development creates uncertainty. The evaluation highlights the importance of interagency governance decisions, such as setting testing requirements and defining implementation standards, to ensure responsible AI deployment in public sectors.

## Context
The article addresses the growing challenge of siloed government data, which obscures availability and usage guidelines for researchers and policymakers. As AI-augmented chatbots proliferate across industries, the government faces the dual challenge of leveraging these tools for efficiency while maintaining rigorous standards for accuracy and accountability. This evaluation occurs within a broader industry context where the integration of agentic AI into public infrastructure is still nascent, lacking established best practices for handling sensitive statistical data.

## Implications
This research is critical for the field of public policy and data science as it provides a blueprint for how governments can responsibly adopt AI technologies. By establishing clear principles for AI-supported data concierges, the findings help mitigate risks associated with AI hallucinations and data misinterpretation in high-stakes environments. For the industry, this signals a shift toward collaborative development models where AI developers work closely with domain experts and data stewards. It underscores the necessity for regulatory frameworks and technical standards that ensure AI tools enhance evidence-based decision-making without compromising the reliability of official government statistics.
