## Summary  
Half of Europe’s towns and villages have fewer residents than 60 years ago despite overall EU growth, according to a CORRECTIV.Europe analysis based on new JRC data covering 1961‑2024. The study shows rural depopulation in many regions while only a handful of urban centers such as Madrid suburbs and Athens have expanded.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Rural depopulation: one‑in‑five rural municipalities lost >50 % of population between 1961 and 2024, driven by outmigration and economic decline.  
- Emigration from former Eastern Bloc countries has halved populations in many eastern German towns and Bulgarian regions such as Hoyerswerda and Vidin.  
- Growth is concentrated in a few urban hubs (Madrid suburbs, Athens, Vilnius), while the EU’s total population may peak around 2029 before gradual decline.

## Implications
