## Summary  
April 2026 saw a crowded launch of large language models, with OpenAI’s GPT‑5.5 and Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.7 emerging as the two leading public‑API options, while Google’s Gemma 4 series and Zhipu AI’s GLM‑5.1 offered free open‑source alternatives. The Fazm blog highlights these releases and introduces a local macOS agent that lets users plug any of these models into their Mac without leaving the device.

## Key Takeaways  
- GPT‑5.5 (Spud) and Claude Opus 4.7 now hold the public‑API top spot, offering 1M‑token contexts at $5 in / $30 out and $5 in / $25 out respectively.  
- Open‑source models such as Gemma 4 (31B dense), GLM‑5.1 (744B MoE) and Qwen 3.6‑Plus are freely available under permissive licenses, expanding competition with proprietary services.  
- The Fazm project enables a private, offline macOS agent that can run any of these models locally, eliminating reliance on external APIs.

## Context  
The AI industry is shifting toward incremental upgrades rather than major version jumps; OpenAI’s “Spud” was rebranded as GPT‑5.5 after a delayed launch to reflect modest gains over GPT‑5.4. Meanwhile, open‑source ecosystems are gaining traction with Google’s Gemma 4 series and Zhipu AI’s GLM‑5.1, both released under Apache 2.0 or MIT licenses. The rapid proliferation of models is driving competition in pricing, licensing, and performance metrics.

## Implications  
For developers and enterprises, the mix of high‑cost proprietary APIs and free open weights creates strategic choices that affect cost efficiency, data privacy, and customization. Local agents like Fazm reduce latency and compliance risks by keeping model inference on‑device, while also democratizing access to cutting‑edge capabilities without subscription fees. This landscape will likely accelerate adoption of AI tools across macOS platforms and influence future release calendars for both open and closed models.
