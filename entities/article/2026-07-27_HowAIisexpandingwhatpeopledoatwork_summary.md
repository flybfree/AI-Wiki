## Summary  
AI is expanding the range of tasks people perform at work, not just how they are performed. An analysis of over 800,000 ChatGPT messages shows that many users engage in activities linked to other occupations, indicating a shift from “who does what” to “what gets done.”  

## Key Takeaways  
- 43.5 % of occupation‑specific AI use involves tasks outside the user’s own job, revealing early evidence of task crossover before formal titles change.  
- Certain roles experience high crossover: customer‑experience workers (77 %), designers (75 %), human‑resources staff (69 %), legal professionals (56 %) and marketers (53 %).  
- Marketing and engineering tasks travel farthest across many occupations, suggesting that these activities are increasingly shared beyond their traditional sources.  

## Context  
This study leverages a unique window into real‑time ChatGPT usage to map how AI is reshaping job responsibilities, complementing traditional analyses of model capabilities with actual user behavior data. The findings provide empirical insight into the evolving division of work across industries and sectors.  

## Implications  
For employers and policymakers, task crossover signals a need for flexible role definitions, proactive skill‑sharing programs, and training initiatives that anticipate changes before formal job titles are revised.
