## Summary  
The EU’s copyright law, grounded in the principle that works must be the “author’s own intellectual creation,” does not extend protection to content created solely by AI systems. Because generative AI lacks personality and makes no free‑choice decisions, purely AI‑generated text or art receives no copyright status; only human‑edited works retain protection under EU law.

## Key Takeaways  
- **Human authorship required:** EU copyright mandates a personal creative act, which AI cannot provide.  
- **AI‑only works unprotected:** Purely machine‑produced output is excluded from copyright registration in the EU.  
- **Compliance focus:** The EU AI Act encourages watermarking to label AI content, but does not grant rights over the generated material itself.

## Context  
The article reflects a broader shift as generative AI becomes mainstream, prompting legal bodies worldwide to re‑examine intellectual property frameworks. In the EU, the AI Act aims to ensure transparency and mitigate risks of misuse, while copyright law remains anchored in traditional human‑centric authorship concepts established by the Court of Justice of the European Union (CJEU) in Infopaq.

## Implications  
For creators, businesses, and policymakers, this means a clear legal gap: AI can produce content at scale without infringing on existing copyrights, yet it cannot be owned or protected. The field must develop new mechanisms—such as attribution systems or licensing models—to balance creator rights with the rapid diffusion of machine‑generated material.
