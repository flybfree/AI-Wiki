## Summary  
OpenAI’s “full‑stack” approach integrates hardware, software, models and services to continuously improve AI performance across training, inference and deployment. The article highlights Jalapeño, OpenAI’s first custom inference chip, which delivers higher throughput per watt than competing systems while supporting a range of frontier models.

## Key Takeaways  
- Custom silicon such as Jalapeño can outperform third‑party accelerators in both speed and energy efficiency.  
- Building the entire stack—chips, memory, network and software—creates a Pareto‑frontier that balances capability, latency, cost and reliability.  
- A diversified portfolio of cloud providers and partners lets OpenAI match workloads to the best performance‑per‑dollar solution.

## Context  
The AI industry is moving toward tighter integration between hardware design and model serving, driven by the need for scalable, low‑latency inference at scale. Companies that control multiple layers can respond faster than those relying on external accelerators, influencing market dynamics and pricing strategies.

## Implications  
This integrated strategy could lower operational costs for large language models, accelerate product rollout of AI‑native devices, and set a new benchmark for efficiency in the sector, prompting competitors to invest more heavily in custom silicon and end‑to‑end ecosystem management.
