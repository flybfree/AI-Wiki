## Summary  
Google’s ATLAS study, which analyzed over 15 million de‑identified human‑AI interactions across Google’s Gemini App, AI Mode, and API, reveals that artificial intelligence is being adopted broadly across the workforce but remains largely superficial; only about one‑fifth of tasks are automated, with most usage focused on collaborative support such as ideation, strategy, information retrieval, and learning.  

## Key Takeaways  
- AI adoption spans all industry sectors and also 68 % of all occupations that collectively represent 90 % of total U.S. employment; however within jobs, people are using AI selectively, with only about 21 % of tasks being automated.  
- Most AI use at work is focused on collaborative uses such as ideation, strategy, information retrieval, and learning, rather than replacing human labor with full task automation.  
- The ATLAS v1.0 dataset covers more than 150 countries, 140 languages, 800 occupations, and 4,000 tasks, making it the most comprehensive snapshot to date of real‑world AI usage at scale.  

## Context  
Google’s ATLAS is a large‑scale, de‑identified analysis of human‑AI interactions across Google’s Gemini App, AI Mode, and API, providing empirical evidence on how generative AI is reshaping work and daily life worldwide.  

## Implications  
Understanding these patterns helps policymakers, employers, and researchers design interventions that maximize productivity while mitigating displacement risks, ensuring AI benefits are shared equitably across the global economy.
