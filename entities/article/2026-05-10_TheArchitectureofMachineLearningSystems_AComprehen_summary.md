## Summary  
This article introduces the foundational architecture of machine‑learning systems, emphasizing that modern AI relies on deep learning frameworks which abstract away the intricacies of non‑linear function approximation and representation learning. By outlining how these mathematical foundations are implemented within computational graphs, the guide sets the stage for understanding the high‑level structure that powers contemporary models.

## Key Takeaways  
- Deep learning frameworks encapsulate complex differentiation and computation graph construction, allowing practitioners to focus on model design rather than low‑level calculus.  
- Representation learning is enabled by non‑linear function approximation techniques such as neural networks, which transform raw data into meaningful features automatically.  
- The abstraction layer isolates hardware specifics, making the system portable across different computational environments.

## Context  
The rise of deep learning has reshaped AI research and industry, turning once‑theoretical concepts like function approximation into practical tools that drive applications in computer vision, natural language processing, and robotics. Understanding this architecture is essential for anyone involved in building or evaluating modern ML pipelines.
