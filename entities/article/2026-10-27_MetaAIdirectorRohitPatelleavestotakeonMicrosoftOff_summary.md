## Summary
Rohit Patel, a former director at Meta Superintelligence Labs, has left Meta after eight years to launch Ndoc, a startup positioning itself as an AI-native replacement for productivity suites like Microsoft Office and Google Workspace. The new platform aims to unify fragmented workflows by integrating presentations, analytical narratives, and underlying data models into a single, transparent workspace. This move highlights the growing trend of high-profile AI talent leaving major tech giants to pursue independent ventures focused on reimagining core productivity tools.

## Key Takeaways
- **Unified Workspace Architecture:** Ndoc consolidates three distinct functions into one interface: the "Stage" tab for presentations and dashboards, the "Narrative" tab for analyses with citations, and the "Model" tab for datasets and Python scripts, replacing fragmented tools like Excel, Word, and Tableau.
- **Transparency and Verifiability:** Leveraging Patel’s background in reinforcement learning and AI trustworthiness, Ndoc allows users to inspect the underlying code (e.g., Python scripts) used to generate outputs, ensuring that AI-generated insights are auditable and not hidden "behind the curtain."
- **Open Source Ambition:** Patel plans to open-source the ".ndoc.html" file format, allowing documents to be opened in any browser and enabling developers to build compatible apps without being locked into proprietary ecosystems like Microsoft or Google.

## Context
Patel’s departure is part of a broader wave of high-profile exits from Meta, including Chief Global Affairs Officer Joel Kaplan and AI researcher Andrew Tulloch, signaling potential internal shifts or strategic disagreements within the company’s AI division. The timing coincides with intense competition in the enterprise software market, where incumbents like Microsoft and Google are aggressively integrating generative AI into their existing suites. Patel’s approach contrasts with these incumbents by building a new platform from scratch using open-source models rather than layering AI onto legacy architectures, aiming for lower operational costs and greater flexibility.

## Implications
This development underscores a critical shift in how professionals interact with data and documents: moving from static files to dynamic, executable narratives where the logic behind AI outputs is visible and verifiable. If successful, Ndoc could disrupt the entrenched dominance of Microsoft Office and Google Workspace by offering a more transparent, cost-effective, and interoperable alternative. Furthermore, Patel’s emphasis on open-source file formats challenges the current walled-garden approach of major tech platforms, potentially fostering a more decentralized ecosystem for document creation and collaboration. This highlights the increasing importance of trust and auditability in AI-driven business tools, as users demand to understand not just the results generated by AI, but how those results were derived.
