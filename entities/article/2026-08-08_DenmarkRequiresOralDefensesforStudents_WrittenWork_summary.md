## Summary  
Denmark has introduced an immediate policy requiring all upper‑secondary students to provide oral defenses of their written assignments, aiming to curb AI‑assisted cheating. The regulation applies to about 9,000 students in the HF program and is part of a broader set of measures that include screen monitoring during exams and on‑campus work.  

## Key Takeaways  
- **Mandatory oral defense:** Students must verbally justify their written work, creating accountability for AI use.  
- **Immediate enforcement with future consultation:** The rule takes effect now, but schools will continue collaborating to refine the framework as AI evolves.  
- **Student involvement in long‑term solutions:** Educators and student representatives are urged to co‑design sustainable policies beyond these three initiatives.  

## Context  
The policy reflects a growing industry concern that generative AI tools can undermine academic integrity by producing high‑quality work without genuine learning. As schools worldwide grapple with similar challenges, Denmark’s approach combines immediate deterrence (oral defenses) with preventive measures such as exam monitoring and restricted access to resources during class time.  

## Implications  
For the education sector, this initiative signals a shift from purely punitive responses to proactive skill‑building that emphasizes critical thinking and independent learning. It also sets a precedent for other jurisdictions to adopt multi‑layered strategies, potentially influencing AI governance standards in higher education and professional training programs.
