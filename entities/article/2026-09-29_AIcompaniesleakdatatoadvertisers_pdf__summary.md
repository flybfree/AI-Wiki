## Summary
This academic study conducts a systematic privacy analysis of nine prominent web and mobile conversational AI services, revealing that these platforms increasingly integrate traditional third-party advertising and tracking services (ATSes) into their operations. The research identifies significant privacy risks, including the disclosure of sensitive conversation-derived artifacts—such as prompts, titles, and screenshots—to third parties alongside persistent user identifiers. These findings highlight a novel privacy attack surface where AI-mediated interactions become subject to extensive tracking and public exposure, often without adequate access controls or transparency for users.

## Key Takeaways
- **Integration of Traditional Tracking in AI:** Major conversational AI providers are adopting advertising-based business models that rely on third-party trackers, extending traditional web tracking practices into the domain of private user-AI interactions.
- **Exposure of Sensitive Artifacts:** The study found that multiple providers disclose sensitive data derived from conversations, including full prompts and screenshots, to third-party advertisers. This data is often linked with persistent identifiers, allowing for precise user attribution and profiling based on conversational content.
- **Lack of Access Controls on Permalinks:** Some AI services publicly expose conversation permalinks without proper access controls, enabling trackers and other third parties to read entire conversation histories, thereby compromising the confidentiality of user interactions.

## Context
The research situates itself within the broader industry shift where Large Language Model (LLM) providers like OpenAI are exploring new monetization strategies, including advertising-based models. As conversational AI services become integral to personal and professional activities, they inherit the data collection practices common in traditional web and mobile ecosystems. The study was conducted by researchers from IMDEA Networks and independent collaborators, focusing on compliance with European regulations such as the GDPR and ePrivacy Directive. It reflects a growing concern among regulators and privacy advocates regarding the opacity of data flows in emerging AI technologies, which have received limited scrutiny compared to established digital platforms.

## Implications
These findings underscore an urgent need for stronger safeguards governing AI-mediated interactions. The intertwining of traditional tracking technologies with conversational AI creates new pathways for sensitive user information to be collected, inferred, and disseminated without explicit user consent or understanding. For the industry, this highlights a critical gap in privacy protection as AI services scale; provider-generated artifacts become commodities for advertising, potentially violating data minimization principles under GDPR. Regulators and policymakers must address this novel privacy attack surface by enforcing stricter transparency requirements and access controls for conversational AI platforms to prevent the unauthorized dissemination of private user data.
