## Summary
The author argues against the practice of blindly relaying raw, verbatim outputs from Large Language Models (LLMs) in professional communications, labeling such behavior as being a "meat proxy" rather than adding genuine value. They contend that simply forwarding AI-generated text is inefficient and often introduces confusion due to verbose, jargon-heavy, or hallucinated content that requires unnecessary effort for the recipient to decipher. Instead, the author advocates for a workflow where humans actively read, validate, and synthesize AI outputs into their own words before sharing them with others.

## Key Takeaways
- **Eliminate the Middleman**: Directly forwarding AI responses adds no value because the recipient can interact with the model themselves, gaining better control over context and receiving faster, more tailored answers.
- **Prioritize Human Synthesis**: The true value of using AI lies in the human’s ability to process, understand, and rewrite information. This synthesis acts as a quality check and ensures clarity, whereas raw output is often dense with plausible nonsense or obscure technical jargon that hinders comprehension.
- **Rethink Code Review Practices**: In software development, relying on tools like Claude Code to generate code without human oversight creates a false sense of productivity. It shifts the actual cognitive load and quality assurance burden onto reviewers, who must then validate work they did not author, effectively making them proxies for an AI that performed the implementation.

## Context
This article reflects a growing tension in the tech industry as AI coding assistants and chatbots become ubiquitous. While these tools significantly lower the barrier to entry for generating code and text, they also risk degrading the quality of professional discourse and technical documentation. The concern is not with the technology itself, but with the erosion of critical thinking and accountability when humans treat AI outputs as final products rather than drafts or suggestions.

## Implications
For the industry, this highlights a critical need for updated workflows that emphasize human-in-the-loop validation. Organizations must recognize that efficiency gains from AI are negated if they result in lower-quality communication and increased cognitive load for colleagues. The implication is that professional standards must evolve to demand synthesis over replication, ensuring that AI serves as an augmentative tool for thought rather than a replacement for intellectual engagement. This shift is essential for maintaining high standards of clarity, accuracy, and accountability in software development and collaborative environments.
