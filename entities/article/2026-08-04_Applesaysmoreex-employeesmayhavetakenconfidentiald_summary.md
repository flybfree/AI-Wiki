## Summary  
Apple is now seeking a preliminary injunction in its trade‑secret lawsuit against OpenAI, arguing that the company has taken confidential Apple technology to develop an AI device or other products. The filing also alleges that more than the two former employees named in the original complaint—Chang Liu and Tang Yew Tan—may have been involved, with additional ex‑employees meeting with OpenAI to discuss proprietary information.

## Key Takeaways  
- Apple claims that several former staff members met with OpenAI before interviews and shared unannounced product details.  
- The company’s motion for a preliminary injunction is based on the suspicion of broader involvement beyond Liu, Tan, Yu‑Ting Peng, and other named individuals.  
- OpenAI publicly denies any theft, stating it has no trade secrets and that Apple’s request is “based on false information and completely unnecessary.”

## Context  
The dispute unfolds amid growing competition between AI firms and device manufacturers, where data security and intellectual‑property protection are critical concerns. Similar cases have highlighted the vulnerability of corporate trade secrets in fast‑moving tech ecosystems, prompting regulators to scrutinize how confidential information is managed across industry boundaries.

## Implications  
If Apple’s injunction succeeds, it could set a precedent that courts will intervene early to halt AI development built on stolen proprietary data, influencing how companies protect their innovations. For the broader AI field, such rulings may accelerate demands for stronger security protocols and stricter oversight of former employee access, potentially slowing product releases while raising questions about innovation versus legal compliance.
