## Summary  
Relativity Networks has secured $22 million in SAFE‑note funding to develop hollow‑core fiber that transmits data roughly 30 % faster than conventional fiber, cutting latency from five microseconds per kilometer to about three‑and‑a‑half. The round also includes a $40 million follow‑on order from a leading hyperscaler, signaling strong investor confidence in the technology’s potential to reshape large‑scale AI data‑center architectures.

## Key Takeaways  
- Hollow‑core fiber reduces signal latency from 5 µs/km to ~3.5 µs/km, delivering a 30 % speed boost over standard fiber.  
- The $22 million raise combines SAFE notes with a $40 million hyperscaler follow‑on, indicating robust market interest in faster networking solutions.  
- Faster fiber could enable AI compute to be distributed across multiple campus sites while operating as a single synchronized unit.

## Context  
Data‑center developers face mounting pressure to expand rapidly, constrained by power‑grid and regulatory limits on where new facilities can be built. While most assume fiber speed is sufficient, latency becomes critical as campuses sprawl over hundreds of acres and dozens of buildings, creating “geographical math” challenges for AI workloads that require ultra‑low inter‑node communication times.

## Implications  
A 30 % reduction in latency translates to the ability to span distances roughly 30 % farther without performance degradation, potentially allowing data centers to be co‑located across multiple campuses and operate as a unified AI platform. This could shift industry focus from purely GPU‑centric optimization to geography‑optimized compute, accelerating the next era of large‑scale artificial intelligence deployment.
