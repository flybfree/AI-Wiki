## Summary
OpenAI, in collaboration with AARP’s Older Adults Technology Services (OATS), has launched the "Older Adults AI Skills Jam," a series of free, in-person workshops designed to teach seniors how to use ChatGPT confidently and safely. The initiative aims to bridge the digital divide by providing practical training on tasks such as trip planning, interpreting complex documents, and pursuing new hobbies. Furthermore, the program emphasizes online safety by teaching older adults how to use AI as a tool to identify and mitigate potential scams and fraudulent communications.

## Key Takeaways
- **Increasing Adoption:** Data indicates that older adults (55+) are increasingly engaging with ChatGPT, with their share of messages growing from 6% to nearly 10% in just one year for practical guidance and information seeking.
- **Safety as a Priority:** A core component of the training involves teaching users to recognize red flags—such as urgent language and suspicious links—and using AI as an extra layer of scrutiny to evaluate the legitimacy of messages.
- **Community-Based Outreach:** The initiative is being rolled out across 10 different communities in the U.S., partnering with local organizations like Senior Planet and various public libraries to ensure localized, accessible support.
- **Broad Literacy Goals:** This program is part of a larger effort by OpenAI Academy to democratize AI literacy across diverse demographics, including non-profits, small businesses, and K–12 educators.

## Context
This initiative arrives at a time when the "digital divide" remains a significant hurdle in technology adoption. While younger generations often adopt new tools instinctively, older populations frequently face higher barriers regarding trust and technical navigation. By integrating AI literacy with cybersecurity education, OpenAI is addressing two of the most pressing issues for seniors: staying connected to society and protecting themselves from sophisticated cyber-fraud, which has become increasingly prevalent as digital interactions increase.

## Implications
For the AI industry, this represents a shift toward "inclusive AI" where success is measured not just by raw capability but by accessibility and safety for vulnerable populations. By providing structured, in-person education, OpenAI is acknowledging that technology alone cannot solve social isolation or fraud; human-led instruction is necessary to build the confidence required for adoption. This model of community-based education could serve as a blueprint for how AI companies can fulfill their social responsibility goals by ensuring that the benefits of generative AI reach underserved and high-risk demographics, ultimately fostering a safer and more inclusive digital economy.
