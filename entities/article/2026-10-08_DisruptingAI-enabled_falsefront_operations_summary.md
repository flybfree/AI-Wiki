## Summary
OpenAI has disrupted two sophisticated influence operations originating from Russia and Iran that utilized AI models to support "false front" entities, such as fake journalist personas and co-opted think tanks. These operations leveraged AI to streamline traditional disinformation workflows, including generating long-form articles, social media comments, and internal reports, while employing deceitful methodologies to exaggerate their effectiveness. The findings highlight how threat actors are increasingly integrating AI into established covert influence campaigns to enhance scale and efficiency.

## Key Takeaways
- **Hybrid Disinformation Tactics**: The disrupted operations combined AI-generated content with traditional human-driven tactics, creating multi-dimensional campaigns that included fake news outlets, leaked documents, and audio scripts to launder geopolitical messaging.
- **AI-Enhanced False Fronts**: The Iranian operation utilized seven AI-assisted "journalist" personas to pitch articles to global outlets, while the Russian operation co-opted unwitting individuals in Latin America to run a fake think tank, demonstrating how AI can lend credibility to deceptive entities.
- **Internal Misrepresentation**: Both threat actors heavily relied on AI to draft internal reports that exaggerated the success and reach of their operations, indicating that AI is not only used for external propaganda but also for internal performance inflation and resource justification.

## Context
This report is part of OpenAI’s ongoing series of transparency disclosures regarding malicious uses of its models by state-affiliated threat actors. It builds upon previous findings related to cyber attacks, scams, and covert influence operations (IOs) from various geopolitical actors. The article draws historical parallels to pre-AI influence campaigns, such as the Russian "Alice Donovan" fake journalist persona and the Internet Research Agency’s "PeaceData" outlet, suggesting that AI is being used to modernize and scale these legacy disinformation strategies rather than creating entirely new methods.

## Implications
The disruption of these operations underscores the evolving nature of information warfare, where AI serves as a force multiplier for traditional state-sponsored disinformation. For the industry, this highlights the critical need for robust detection mechanisms that can identify not just AI-generated text, but also the structural patterns of "false front" entities and the misuse of AI for internal metric inflation. It signals to regulators and peers that AI platforms must continuously monitor for hybrid threats that blend automated content generation with human-led deception strategies, requiring more nuanced policy responses to protect democratic discourse and global information integrity.
