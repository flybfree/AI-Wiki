## Summary
OpenAI argues that a critical "policy window" is currently open, urging governments and industries to act swiftly to establish mandatory safety regulations before AI capabilities outpace governance structures. The article outlines OpenAI’s specific policy advocacy efforts, including support for national safety requirements, state-level legislation in California, and the development of global industry standards. It emphasizes that while AI offers significant benefits, rapid advancements in machine intelligence require a bias toward meaningful action over policy perfection to ensure human control and safety.

## Key Takeaways
- **Advocacy for Mandatory National Regulation:** OpenAI is actively pushing for Congress to implement mandatory, capability-based national AI safety regulations, arguing that voluntary measures are insufficient given the pace of technological advancement.
- **Support for State-Level Legislation:** Until federal action occurs, OpenAI supports specific California bills (SB 813, AB 1405, SB 1119, and AB 1864) to strengthen independent safety assessments, auditor standards, protections for young people, and safeguards against biological threats.
- **Development of Global Standards:** The company commits to working with other frontier labs to create voluntary industry standards and advocates for international approaches to measuring capabilities and determining when AI development should slow or stop to preserve human control.

## Context
The article situates itself within a broader narrative of rapid AI advancement, referencing internal insights from Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki regarding the potential for recursive self-improvement in machine intelligence. It highlights that advanced AI models are no longer confined to frontier laboratories but are becoming broadly available through open models worldwide. This widespread accessibility increases the urgency for shared standards, as capabilities that drive utility—such as accelerating medical research or strengthening infrastructure—also introduce risks that require coordinated global management.

## Implications
This stance signals a strategic shift in how leading AI developers engage with public policy, moving from purely technical solutions to active legislative advocacy. By calling for a "defenders window" analogous to military strategy, OpenAI suggests that policymakers have a limited timeframe to establish durable safeguards before offensive capabilities become widespread. This implies that the industry is preparing for a future where safety protocols may intentionally slow model advancement to maintain control. For the field, this highlights a growing consensus among frontier labs that technical alignment work alone is insufficient without robust, shared regulatory frameworks and international cooperation.
