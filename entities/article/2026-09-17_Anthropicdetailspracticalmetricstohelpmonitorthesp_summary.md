## Summary
Anthropic has introduced three specific, practical metrics designed to help the AI industry monitor and potentially slow down the pace of frontier model development. Following a call by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei for a coordinated slowdown, these metrics aim to provide transparency regarding how quickly AI is advancing and who—or what—is driving that progress.

## Key Takeaways
- **AI-Led R&D:** Anthropic is measuring the extent to which AI models are being used to develop even more powerful versions of themselves, a trend that risks creating a "black box" where humans lose the ability to understand the capabilities or dangers of the systems they are building.
- **Oversight of Autonomous Agents:** The company is tracking how autonomous agents delegate tasks to sub-agents; this is critical because it marks a shift from AI as a collaborative tool to AI taking the lead on consequential decisions, such as directing research paths.
- **Compute Allocation:** Because compute power is the primary "fuel" for AI development, Anthropic identifies it as one of the most verifiable inputs. Monitoring how these resources are allocated allows for a clearer picture of where and how quickly developers are focusing their efforts.

## Context
This initiative follows a period of intense debate regarding the safety of frontier models. It builds upon Dario Amodei’s recent proposal to coordinate a slowdown in development, a move that has gained significant traction among industry leaders including Sam Altman (OpenAI), Elon Musk (SpaceX), and Demis Hassabis (Google DeepMind). The initiative comes amid stark warnings from researchers like Evan Hubinger regarding the rapid, potentially uncontrollable advancement of AI capabilities.

## Implications
By formalizing these metrics, Anthropic is attempting to bridge the information gap between private labs and the public. If the industry can successfully measure and report on these "hidden" developments—specifically autonomous agency and self-driven R&D—it provides a framework for society to make informed decisions about safety regulations. Ultimately, these metrics serve as a mechanism for accountability, transforming abstract fears about AI speed into quantifiable data points that could be used to implement a structured, industry-wide "pacing" strategy.
