## Summary  
Anthropic, a public‑benefit corporation, launches an initiative to collect the “hardest” questions people have about artificial intelligence and pledges to publicly track the actions it will take to answer them. The effort builds on existing surveys of 52 000 Americans, 81 000 Claude users across many languages, focus groups, and real‑world usage data, aiming to ensure AI’s benefits are realized while its risks are mitigated.

## Key Takeaways  
- Anthropic reinforces its public‑benefit mission by explicitly inviting the public to voice concerns about jobs, society, families, scientific impact, and misuse.  
- The company is aggregating diverse perspectives through large‑scale surveys, multilingual interviews, in‑person focus groups, and anonymized usage analytics.  
- Anthropic commits to transparent reporting: each question will be linked to specific actions taken, fostering accountability.

## Context  
Artificial intelligence is rapidly reshaping work, education, health, and culture, yet public trust hinges on visible oversight and responsiveness. The broader AI ecosystem—research labs, startups, policymakers—relies on stakeholder input to align technology with societal good, making Anthropic’s outreach a notable example of industry‑led engagement.

## Implications  
For the field, this initiative signals that responsible AI development must be coupled with genuine dialogue and measurable follow‑through. It could set a precedent for other companies to prioritize transparency, reduce speculation about risk, and embed public benefit into product roadmaps, ultimately influencing policy and consumer confidence.
