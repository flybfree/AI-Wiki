## Summary
A recent survey commissioned by Monday.com and conducted by Nielsen reveals that while 94% of managers are enthusiastic about AI’s potential, the technology is currently creating significant operational challenges for leadership. The primary issue is that AI-generated outputs are often polished and indistinguishable from human work, making it difficult for managers to evaluate employee performance accurately. Consequently, managers are spending more time reviewing and guiding team output, effectively adding a new layer of "invisible labor" to their daily responsibilities.

## Key Takeaways
- **Evaluation Difficulties:** Over 50% of senior leaders report increased difficulty in assessing employee work because AI tools produce polished outputs that obscure the origin of the work. Nearly half of these managers now spend more time reviewing and guiding team output than they did prior to AI adoption.
- **Shift in Performance Metrics:** 78% of respondents have altered how they evaluate employees, shifting focus from traditional output quality to skills such as prompt engineering, task delegation to AI, and experimentation with new workflows. This raises concerns about fairness and the ability to distinguish human contribution from machine assistance.
- **Bias and Supervision Risks:** There is a growing trend of managers using AI to draft performance reviews (39%) or inform decisions (46%), which risks introducing algorithmic bias into human resource processes. Additionally, 57% of managers report an increase in the number of AI tools in circulation, complicating management oversight and creating a need for constant supervision of AI agents.

## Context
This survey highlights a critical disconnect between the rapid advancement of AI capabilities and the slower pace of organizational adaptation. Michal Miller Levi, head of market research at Monday.com, compares the current excitement to the early internet era, noting that while the "magic" of AI is appealing, companies are struggling to integrate these tools into existing management structures. The data suggests that AI is moving faster than corporate processes can handle, leading to a situation where individual managers benefit from AI, but the organizational hierarchy struggles to maintain control and clarity.

## Implications
The findings suggest that the immediate impact of AI on the workforce is not just productivity enhancement but also a fundamental restructuring of managerial roles. Companies must address the "invisible labor" required to supervise AI agents and verify their outputs. Furthermore, the reliance on AI for performance reviews poses significant ethical and legal risks, as LLMs may perpetuate existing biases found in training data, particularly against marginalized groups. Organizations need to develop clear objective standards and governance frameworks to ensure that AI adoption does not compromise fairness in employee evaluation or overwhelm managers with unmanageable tool sprawl.
