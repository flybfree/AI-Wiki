## Summary  
Anthropic announced it will embed invisible watermarks in all text generated by its Claude models and related products to meet the EU AI Act’s Transparency Code, which took effect on August 2. The watermark is applied at the model level and persists when users copy‑paste or perform minor edits, ensuring that downstream systems can identify AI‑generated content.

## Key Takeaways  
- Anthropic will automatically watermark Claude outputs for compliance with EU regulations.  
- Watermarks travel with text when copied or edited, making removal difficult without extensive processing.  
- The initiative aligns with a broader industry trend of AI content attribution to avoid legal and reputational risk.

## Context  
The European Union’s AI Act mandates that any AI‑generated or altered content be marked in an unambiguous way so that downstream systems can detect its origin. Several other firms, including OpenAI, Google, Meta and Suno, have pledged similar measures, reflecting a growing regulatory pressure to increase transparency across the generative AI ecosystem.

## Implications  
For developers and users, watermarking may affect content reuse and plagiarism detection, potentially complicating downstream applications that rely on clean text. It also signals a shift toward mandatory provenance tracking in AI outputs, which could reshape market dynamics as companies invest in compliance infrastructure.
