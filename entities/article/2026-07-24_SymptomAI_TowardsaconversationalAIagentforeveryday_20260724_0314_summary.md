## Summary  
SymptomAI is a conversational AI agent built on Google’s Gemini Flash 2.0 that conducts end‑to‑end symptom interviews and differential diagnosis for everyday users. The project demonstrates how such agents can be benchmarked against real clinicians and physiological data in a large, randomized national study. The study involved 13,917 consented participants interacting with five experimental agents and followed up two weeks later for clinical correlation.  

## Key Takeaways  
- SymptomAI performs well in diagnosing infectious diseases when combined with participant Fitbit biosignals.  
- The AI’s diagnostic accuracy rivals that of human clinicians after expert annotation.  
- Real‑world symptom reporting includes variability in medical literacy, incomplete information, and natural conversation dynamics.  

## Context  
Current language models excel on curated medical vignettes but struggle to handle the messy, unstructured data typical of everyday patient interactions. This study addresses that gap by evaluating AI performance across a diverse population. This approach highlights the need for AI systems that can integrate multimodal data such as wearable biosignals into symptom assessment pipelines.  

## Implications  
If conversational AI can reliably flag potential conditions early, it could reduce unnecessary visits and improve public health surveillance while respecting privacy through research‑only labeling. Future deployments could be integrated into primary care platforms, offering patients a low‑cost triage tool while clinicians retain final authority.
