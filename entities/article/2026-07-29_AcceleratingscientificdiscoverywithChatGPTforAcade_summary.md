## Summary  
OpenAI is launching a program that will give free access to its frontier AI models—including GPT‑5.6 Sol Pro—to up to 100 000 researchers at selected academic institutions by 2027, starting with 10 000 participants this summer. The initiative aims to empower scientists across the sciences, mathematics and engineering to accelerate discovery, improve productivity and collaborate while maintaining data privacy.

## Key Takeaways  
- OpenAI will provide free access to its most advanced models for 100 000 researchers at partner institutions, with an initial rollout of 10 000 participants this summer.  
- Researchers can invite up to four collaborators from their institution and use tools such as GPT‑5.6 Sol Pro for tasks ranging from grant writing to genomic analysis.  
- The program includes hands‑on training, business‑grade privacy protections, and ensures that participant data is not used to train the models by default.

## Context  
The rapid adoption of AI in scientific work has already seen over 1.3 million weekly ChatGPT users generating millions of messages, with AI increasingly integrated into mathematics research and grant preparation. OpenAI’s $250 million commitment through 2027—including the NextGenAI initiative and partnerships like the DOE Genesis Mission—reflects a broader industry effort to democratize frontier AI for external scientific communities.

## Implications  
By removing cost barriers, this program could expand the pool of researchers capable of tackling complex problems, potentially accelerating breakthroughs in fields such as genomics and protein modeling. It also reinforces OpenAI’s role as a responsible steward of AI, ensuring that powerful tools remain under researcher control while safeguarding privacy and preventing misuse for model training.
