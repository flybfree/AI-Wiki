## Summary  
The article examines whether the Sumerian King List’s antediluvian reign lengths—each a multiple of 3,600 years and totaling 241,200 years—can be aligned with known Quaternary paleoclimate events such as the Younger Dryas termination. Using an exploratory statistical approach that rescales the sequence to a fixed span anchored near 11.6 ka BP, the authors compare the resulting dates against a catalog of geological events and assess significance through permutation tests.  

## Key Takeaways  
- The Sumerian reign lengths are mathematically regular but lack a statistically significant correlation with paleoclimate boundaries after rigorous testing.  
- Even the best‑performing alignment (p = 0.021) does not survive adjustment for multiple comparisons, indicating no strong evidence of intentional climate encoding.  
- The study demonstrates that coincidental matches can appear when anchoring and bandwidth are flexible, underscoring the need for a well‑defined null model.
