## Summary  
April 2026 saw a surge of LLM releases that reshaped the public‑API landscape, with GPT‑5.5 (Spud) and Claude Opus 4.7 emerging as the two most capable models for developers, while a suite of open‑source alternatives—Gemma 4 series, GLM‑5.1, Qwen 3.6‑Plus, Llama 4 Scout/Maverick, and Arcee Trinity—remained free to use under permissive licenses. - GPT‑5.5 (Spud) and Claude Opus 4.7 dominate the public‑API market, boasting high verification scores (88.7% SWE‑Bench Verified for GPT‑5.5; 64.3% SWE‑Bench Pro for Claude Opus 4.7).

## Context  
The AI field is experiencing rapid model iteration, with companies releasing new versions every few weeks to capture market share. Context length has become a critical differentiator—models like Claude Opus 4.7 offer 200K tokens, whereas many open‑source options cap at 256K. Price volatility reflects the tension between proprietary profit motives and the push for free, community‑driven alternatives.

## Implications  
These releases fragment the market, making it harder to predict which model will be “the best” for a given use case. The surge in open‑source models encourages developers to adopt local agents like Fazm, reducing reliance on cloud APIs and mitigating cost spikes. For enterprises, the mix of high‑performing proprietary services and free, permissive weights creates both opportunities (choice) and challenges (maintenance, integration).
