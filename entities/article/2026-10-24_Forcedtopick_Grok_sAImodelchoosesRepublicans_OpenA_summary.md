## Summary
A recent study conducted by Convovo, a research studio led by Cornell University professor Tapan Parikh, reveals significant political biases in large language models when they are forced to make binary choices in U.S. midterm elections. The findings indicate that SpaceXAI’s Grok 4.7 recommended Republican candidates in 92% of cases, while OpenAI’s GPT-6.1 Sol favored Democrats in 97%, with the two models agreeing on less than 10% of nearly 500 races. Although consumer-facing chatbots often refuse to make direct political endorsements, the underlying API models exhibit distinct ideological leanings that can subtly influence user perceptions through information selection and framing.

## Key Takeaways
- **Distinct Model Biases:** When explicitly prompted to choose a candidate without hedging, Grok 4.7 overwhelmingly supported Republican candidates (92%), whereas GPT-6.1 Sol predominantly selected Democratic candidates (97%). This stark divergence highlights that different AI architectures embed varying implicit values and political leanings.
- **Consumer vs. API Behavior:** There is a critical distinction between how users interact with consumer apps like ChatGPT or Grok, which typically decline to endorse specific candidates, and developers accessing models via APIs. The study demonstrates that while consumer interfaces may appear neutral, the underlying models possess inherent biases that can be accessed through direct prompting.
- **Source Selection as Bias Indicator:** The models’ choices were heavily influenced by the sources they retrieved. Grok frequently cited conservative-leaning sources such as Turning Point Action and Christian voter guides, while GPT relied more on mainstream encyclopedic sources like Wikipedia. This suggests that bias manifests not just in final recommendations but in the curation of information used to form those conclusions.

## Context
This study emerges amidst growing public interest in using AI for civic engagement, despite only 8% of Americans currently using chatbots for election information according to Pew Research. The research builds upon previous findings from organizations like the Center for AI Safety, which have documented how large language models can covertly manipulate users through word choice, framing, and sourcing. By testing models via APIs rather than consumer interfaces, researchers bypassed safety guardrails designed to maintain neutrality in public-facing products, exposing the raw ideological tendencies embedded within the training data and alignment processes of these foundational models.

## Implications
The findings underscore the urgent need for transparency and accountability in AI development, particularly as these systems become integrated into information retrieval and decision-support tools. Developers and policymakers must recognize that "neutral" interfaces may mask underlying biases that influence user understanding through subtle framing rather than explicit endorsements. This has profound implications for democratic discourse, as users relying on AI for political guidance may unknowingly be exposed to skewed information ecosystems. Furthermore, it highlights the importance of media literacy in the age of AI, urging users to critically evaluate the sources and methodologies behind AI-generated insights rather than accepting them as objective truths.
