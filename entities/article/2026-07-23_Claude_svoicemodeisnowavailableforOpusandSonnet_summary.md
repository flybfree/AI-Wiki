## Summary  
Anthropic has now enabled voice mode for its Opus and Sonnet models, extending the feature to widely used productivity apps such as Gmail, Slack, and Canva, while also adding support for nine additional languages beyond English. This rollout moves voice interaction beyond the quick‑answer focus of Haiku toward deeper, real‑world problem solving.

## Key Takeaways  
- Voice mode is now available on Claude Opus and Claude Sonnet, not just on Haiku.  
- The technology integrates with popular apps like Gmail, Slack, and Canva, making advanced AI accessible in everyday workflows.  
- Multilingual support has been added: French, German, Spanish, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Japanese, Korean, and Portuguese.

## Context  
Previously, Anthropic’s voice mode was limited to Haiku, a lightweight model optimized for brief queries. Opus and Sonnet are the company’s deeper, more capable models designed for complex reasoning and actionable outcomes. The expansion reflects a broader industry trend of embedding conversational AI into productivity tools rather than keeping it confined to chat interfaces.

## Implications  
The move could democratize access to sophisticated AI assistance, reducing friction between users and high‑level problem solving across enterprise platforms. It also raises important questions about data privacy, the handling of sensitive information through voice input, and the potential for misuse if deep models are deployed without robust safeguards.
