## Summary  
The Winona Police Department’s eight Flock license‑plate reader cameras were cut down and stolen on August 1, leaving the department without surveillance equipment at key highway intersections. Investigators also found two Buffalo County Flock cameras on the Mississippi River Bridge missing in the same manner, resulting in a total loss of roughly $24,000. The thefts are part of an ongoing investigation with no suspects identified yet.

## Key Takeaways  
- All eight Winona PD cameras were cut down and taken, costing about $3,000 each, amounting to a $24,000 loss on the department’s budget.  
- The coordinated theft left the camera poles behind, and no suspects have been identified at this time.  
- Two additional Flock cameras owned by Buffalo County were also removed from the Mississippi River Bridge.

## Context  
Flock is an automated license‑plate reader (ALPR) system that uses AI to capture vehicle plate images together with make, model, and color information for law‑enforcement purposes. The technology stores data locally and deletes it after 30 days, but its presence has sparked privacy concerns among civil liberties groups who worry about potential data sharing or misuse. Vandalism of Flock units is a growing national issue, indicating that these AI‑enabled surveillance tools are vulnerable to physical attacks.

## Implications  
The theft underscores the financial and operational risks associated with deploying AI‑based surveillance hardware in public spaces, potentially diverting resources away from investigative work. It also highlights broader questions about the security protocols needed for AI systems used by law enforcement and could influence future policy on data protection and community trust in automated policing technologies.
