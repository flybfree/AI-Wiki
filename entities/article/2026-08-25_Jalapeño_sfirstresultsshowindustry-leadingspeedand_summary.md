## Summary  
OpenAI’s Jalapeño inference chip delivers industry‑leading speed and efficiency, performing 1.5–1.9 × more AI work per watt than competing systems while cutting end‑to‑end latency by 1.7–3.6 × across GPT‑OSS 120B, DeepSeek R1, and Kimi K2.5 1T. These gains translate into faster responses, higher throughput per kilowatt, and more tokens per user for interactive workloads.

## Key Takeaways  
- Jalapeño achieves 1.5–1.9× greater AI work per watt at peak throughput compared with previous‑best systems.  
- It reduces end‑to‑end latency by 1.7–3.6× across the three public models, enabling smoother interactive agents.  
- The chip provides higher tokens‑per‑user and better throughput‑per‑kilowatt performance, widening its lead over prior benchmarks.

## Context  
The AI industry faces a tension between raw speed and power consumption; many hardware improvements favor one metric while degrading the other. Jalapeño exemplifies a full‑stack approach where model design, serving software, silicon architecture, memory, networking, and system integration are optimized together based on real workload data.

## Implications  
By delivering superior performance per unit of energy, Jalapeño makes large‑scale AI more affordable and scalable, supporting OpenAI’s mission to democratize artificial general intelligence. The results set a new benchmark for future chips, encouraging competitors to pursue similar efficiency gains and reinforcing the value of tightly coupled hardware‑software ecosystems in advancing responsible AGI development.
