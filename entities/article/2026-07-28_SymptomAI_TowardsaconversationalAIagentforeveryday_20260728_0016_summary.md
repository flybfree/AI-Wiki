## Summary  
SymptomAI is a conversational AI agent that conducts end‑to‑end symptom interviews and differential diagnosis assessments, evaluated in a randomized national study of 13,917 participants. The research compares the AI’s diagnostic output with both clinician‑provided diagnoses and physiological data from Fitbit wearables to gauge real‑world performance.

## Key Takeaways  
- SymptomAI can generate differential diagnoses that align with actual clinical assessments in a large‑scale setting.  
- Conversations leading to infectious‑disease diagnoses coincide with observable immune‑response signals, indicating potential synergy between AI and biosignal data.  
- Existing language models excel on curated vignettes but often fail to capture the variability of everyday patient speech and medical literacy.

## Context  
Current large language models demonstrate strong diagnostic abilities in controlled, synthetic medical case studies, yet these evaluations rarely reflect how patients naturally describe symptoms or how clinicians interpret real‑world interactions. The SymptomAI study addresses this gap by moving from curated data to a live, national experiment that blends conversational AI with wearable biosignals.

## Implications  
This work suggests that conversational AI agents could become practical tools for early symptom triage and differential diagnosis, especially when integrated with continuous health monitoring data. It also highlights the need for rigorous real‑world validation to ensure safety and accuracy before deploying such systems in clinical practice.
