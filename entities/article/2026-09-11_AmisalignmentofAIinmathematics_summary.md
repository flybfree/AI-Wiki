## Summary
The article argues that while Large Language Models (LLMs) have dramatically improved in their ability to solve major mathematical problems, this capability creates a severe misalignment between AI companies and the mathematical community. The author contends that using problem-solving as a primary benchmark undermines the core scientific goal of mathematics, which is conceptual understanding and insight rather than mere output generation. This shift threatens to disrupt the traditional human-centric processes of idea development, attribution, and knowledge transmission that are essential for the health of the discipline.

## Key Takeaways
- **Misaligned Goals:** The objectives of AI companies, which prioritize rapid problem-solving benchmarks, are fundamentally at odds with the goals of mathematicians, who value deep conceptual understanding and the gradual development of sophisticated ideas over time.
- **Erosion of Scientific Process:** The rush to announce AI-generated solutions bypasses critical stages of mathematical validation, such as proper writeups, isolation of new methods, and citation of previous work, leading to potential issues with attribution and plagiarism.
- **Threat to Human Transmission:** By mass-producing "true/false" statements without the necessary human integration into the mathematical canon, AI risks breaking the crucial chain of human interaction and mentorship that nurtures students and disseminates ideas effectively.

## Context
This discussion arises in a period where LLMs have achieved breakthroughs in solving outstanding problems across various fields of mathematics, gaining attention even outside academic circles. The article positions this technological advancement within a broader framework of alignment issues affecting scientific and creative professions. It highlights how modern technologies rely on mathematical tools built over generations through careful human collaboration, contrasting this slow, deliberate process with the fast-paced, automated output of current AI systems.

## Implications
For the field of mathematics, the implications are profound: if problem-solving becomes the sole metric of success, the fertile ground for new ideas may be destroyed rather than cultivated. The lack of proper integration of AI-conceived ideas into the mathematical canon could render these insights "dead," failing to become tools understood and used by future generations or the wider population. Furthermore, this trend poses a general threat to intellectual work across society by devaluing the time-consuming human processes required for genuine insight, potentially hindering the development of students and the long-term sustainability of scientific progress.
