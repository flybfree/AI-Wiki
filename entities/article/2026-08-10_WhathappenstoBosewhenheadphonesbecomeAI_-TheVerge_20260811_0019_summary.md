## Summary  
Bose is pivoting from a consumer‑focused audio brand to a B2B licensing company that sells its core technology to other firms while still producing headphones and soundbars. CEO Lila Snyder says the shift reflects both market disruption from AI wearables and a desire to monetize Bose’s R&D beyond traditional sales.

## Key Takeaways  
- Bose is transitioning into a software‑enabled licensing business, offering its noise‑cancelling and audio tech to partners like Skullcandy and Epson.  
- The move is partly driven by the emerging AI wearables market that requires advanced voice processing.  
- Snyder acknowledges challenges in balancing product innovation with new B2B revenue streams.

## Context  
The article discusses how consumer electronics are being reshaped by AI‑enabled wearables, which blur the line between headphones and voice assistants. This trend forces legacy audio brands to rethink their business models beyond hardware sales.

## Implications  
For the industry, this signals a broader shift toward platform licensing and data‑driven services in audio technology, potentially reducing reliance on pure product margins and opening new revenue avenues for AI integration.
