## Summary
Former Apple engineer Toby Boardman alleges that he was wrongfully terminated after refusing to comply with internal policies regarding the transmission of sensitive customer device identifiers to AT&T without proper authorization. The lawsuit claims that Apple’s carrier support team routinely shared International Mobile Equipment Identity (IMEI) numbers via unsecured email channels, violating the company’s own privacy standards and requiring customer releases for such data transfers. Boardman argues that his dismissal was a retaliatory pretext for raising these significant privacy concerns, while Apple maintains that the termination was based on legitimate performance issues unrelated to his whistleblowing activities.

## Key Takeaways
- **Alleged Policy Violations:** The core dispute centers on whether Apple engineers were routinely transmitting personally identifiable information (PII), specifically IMEIs linked to serial numbers, to AT&T technicians without the mandatory customer releases required by Apple’s internal privacy protocols.
- **Retaliation Claims:** Boardman asserts that his firing occurred immediately after he returned from medical leave and attempted to address both workplace accommodations for anxiety and his repeated complaints about his manager’s punitive behavior regarding these privacy breaches.
- **Legal Proceedings:** The case has escalated to a formal lawsuit filed in San Francisco Superior Court, with a jury trial scheduled for November 2027, where discovery processes will likely reveal the extent of data sharing practices between Apple and its carrier partners.
