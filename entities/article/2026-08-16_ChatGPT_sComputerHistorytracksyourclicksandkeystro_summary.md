## Summary  
OpenAI’s ChatGPT desktop app now includes a feature called Computer History that records users’ clicks and keystrokes, turning their activity into training data for AI assistance. The system builds a timeline of “events” to suggest automations or recall unfinished tasks, but it does not capture images, videos, or audio—unlike Microsoft’s Windows Recall.  

## Key Takeaways  
- The feature is opt‑in; users can exclude specific apps and websites from being logged and delete entries for finer control.  
- Computer History only stores textual events (clicks, keystrokes) rather than visual media, aiming to reduce creepiness while still improving AI relevance.  
- It mirrors the concept of Windows Recall but with a less invasive data‑collection approach that relies on activity logs instead of screenshots.  

## Context  
The rise of memory‑augmented AI is reshaping how developers and users expect assistance, prompting companies to embed contextual awareness into their products. This trend highlights both opportunities for more personalized experiences and heightened privacy concerns as AI systems begin to mine user behavior for training data.  

## Implications  
For the field, this development underscores the need for transparent, user‑controlled data practices in AI design, balancing productivity gains with ethical boundaries. It also signals a competitive dynamic between major tech firms, where each must navigate the fine line between utility and surveillance to maintain public trust.
