## Summary
Anthropic has entered into a cloud infrastructure agreement with Akamai valued at $11.6 billion over seven years, marking the largest deal in Akamai’s history and significantly expanding upon previous collaborations between the two companies. The contract is contingent on Akamai meeting specific service delivery requirements and includes a unique financial structure where Anthropic receives warrants for Akamai stock that vest as spending milestones are met. This partnership highlights a strategic shift toward utilizing general-purpose CPU infrastructure to support AI agents, diverging from the industry's typical focus on specialized GPU chips.

## Key Takeaways
- **Scale and Contingency:** The $11.6 billion commitment is more than six times larger than a previously reported $1.8 billion deal, but it is not ironclad; termination clauses exist if Akamai fails to meet delivery or availability standards.
- **Revenue Timeline:** Akamai does not expect revenue from this deal in the current year, projecting initial earnings of $150 million to $300 million starting in late 2027, scaling to an annual run rate of approximately $1.7 billion by the end of 2028.
- **Unique Warrant Structure:** Unlike typical "circular AI deals" where suppliers invest directly in AI labs, Akamai issued warrants to Anthropic for up to 5% of its stock. These warrants vest incrementally as Anthropic increases its cloud spending, potentially expanding the total deal value to $20 billion if all milestones are achieved.
- **Infrastructure Focus:** The deal emphasizes CPU infrastructure rather than the more hyped GPU market, reflecting growing demand for general-purpose computing resources needed for AI agents performing tasks like code execution and web browsing.

## Context
This agreement occurs within a broader industry trend of "circular deals," where major tech suppliers often invest in AI companies to secure long-term contracts. However, this transaction flips the common dynamic by having the supplier (Akamai) grant equity potential to the customer (Anthropic), similar to a structure previously used by AMD with OpenAI. Anthropic has historically engaged with multiple providers, including Amazon, Google, Microsoft, and AMD, though CEO Dario Amodei has stated that Anthropic participates in such investment arrangements at a smaller scale than some competitors. The deal also underscores the evolving nature of AI infrastructure needs, as AI agents increasingly require robust CPU capabilities for general-purpose tasks alongside specialized hardware for model training.

## Implications
For Akamai, this deal represents a significant strategic pivot and financial commitment, requiring approximately $5.5 billion in capacity build-out investments and additional capital expenditures for components like memory. The market reaction was positive, with Akamai shares rising up to 17% in after-hours trading, signaling investor confidence in the company’s ability to capture value in the AI infrastructure sector beyond its traditional CDN services. For Anthropic, securing substantial CPU resources supports its compute-intensive operations while providing a potential equity stake in a key supplier, aligning their financial interests with Akamai’s performance. This partnership may encourage other cloud providers to adopt similar warrant-based structures to secure long-term commitments from major AI labs, potentially reshaping how infrastructure contracts are structured in the AI industry.
