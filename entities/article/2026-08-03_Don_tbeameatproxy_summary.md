## Summary
The article argues against using AI models as a "meat proxy" in professional communications, specifically warning against pasting raw AI-generated responses directly into Slack messages, code reviews, or group chats. The author contends that this practice adds no value to the recipient and often introduces unnecessary verbosity, jargon, and potential inaccuracies. Instead, individuals should read, validate, and synthesize AI outputs before communicating them in their own words to ensure clarity and accountability.

## Key Takeaways
- **Raw AI Output Is Often Low-Value Communication**: Directly relaying AI responses forces recipients to parse verbose, jargon-heavy, and potentially hallucinated text. The author provides an example of a confusing technical sentence from Claude that required significant effort to decode, illustrating how unedited AI output burdens the reader rather than aiding them.
- **Human Synthesis Is Essential for Value**: To add value, users must act as filters by reading, understanding, and validating AI-generated content before sharing it. Writing a response in one's own words serves as a "certificate" that the sender has processed the information, ensuring accuracy and contextual relevance.
- **Code Review Accountability Is Eroded by Passive AI Use**: In software development, copying ticket descriptions into tools like Claude Code and forwarding reviewer feedback back to the AI without human intervention shifts the burden of implementation and review onto the reviewers. This makes the original developer a mere "meat proxy," obscuring who actually understands or authored the code logic.

## Context
This article reflects growing friction in workplace workflows where AI integration has outpaced best practices for human-AI collaboration. As tools like Claude Code become ubiquitous, there is a temptation to use them as shortcuts for communication and coding tasks. The term "meat proxy" highlights a specific failure mode in this adoption: humans acting as passive conduits for machine output rather than active participants in the reasoning process. This context is particularly relevant in technical fields like software engineering, where precision and clear communication are critical for safety and maintainability.

## Implications
The implications of this behavior extend to organizational efficiency and professional integrity. If teams rely on raw AI outputs for communication, they risk accumulating "noise" that slows down decision-making and increases cognitive load for all participants. In code reviews, the lack of human understanding behind AI-generated code can lead to brittle systems and unclear ownership of bugs or design flaws. Ultimately, the article suggests that true productivity gains from AI come not from delegating thought to machines, but from using AI as a tool that enhances human judgment, requiring users to maintain active engagement and critical validation skills.
