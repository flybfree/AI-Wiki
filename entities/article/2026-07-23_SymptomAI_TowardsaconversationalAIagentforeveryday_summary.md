## Summary  
SymptomAI is a conversational AI prototype that conducts end‑to‑end symptom interviews and differential diagnosis for a large, randomized national study. The research evaluates how well the AI’s diagnoses align with those of real clinicians and with participants’ wearable biosignals, revealing promising performance in infectious disease detection.

## Key Takeaways  
- SymptomAI can generate differential diagnoses that closely match expert clinician assessments when compared to real‑world medical visits.  
- The AI’s predictions for infectious diseases correlate with physiological trends (e.g., immune response signals) captured by Fitbit wearables, suggesting a multimodal diagnostic boost.  
- Existing language models excel on curated vignettes but struggle in natural conversations; SymptomAI demonstrates that conversational agents can bridge this gap.

## Context  
Current AI research often relies on synthetic or highly detailed patient cases, which do not reflect everyday variability such as medical literacy gaps or incomplete symptom reporting. This limits confidence in deploying large‑language models for real‑world symptom assessment. The SymptomAI study addresses this by using a national sample of 13,917 participants and integrating wearable data to ground model performance.

## Implications  
If conversational AI can reliably mimic clinical interviews and align with both human diagnoses and physiological signals, it could democratize early health screening, reduce barriers to care, and enable proactive interventions—potentially reshaping the landscape of preventive medicine.
