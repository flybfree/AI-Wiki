## Summary  
The article urges readers not to simply copy‑paste AI‑generated responses but to treat the model’s output as a draft that requires human editing and personal insight. It argues that while AI can provide quick, generic answers, true value comes from adding one’s own context, judgment, and perspective—even if that means saying “no strong opinion” when there is nothing to contribute.

## Key Takeaways  
- AI tools are fast at delivering generic answers; the real benefit lies in human editing and interpretation.  
- When using an AI as a drafting partner, pull out only the relevant bits, drop the rest, and write three sentences of your own.  
- If you have nothing to add, “no strong opinion” is a valid and helpful response.

## Context  
The piece reflects a growing trend where users increasingly rely on large language models for information retrieval, often treating their outputs as final answers without critical engagement. This reliance can lead to the spread of inaccurate or unoriginal content, especially in professional settings such as PR reviews, Slack messages, and collaborative workspaces.

## Implications  
Encouraging a “human‑in‑the‑loop” approach helps maintain quality, fosters critical thinking, and reduces misinformation. By insisting that AI be used as a drafting aid rather than a final source, the article supports a culture where human judgment remains central to decision‑making processes across industries.
