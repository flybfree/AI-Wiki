## Summary
This article details a three-month randomized controlled trial involving 133 patent attorneys to investigate whether AI assistance enhances or erodes professional expertise. While the study confirms that AI tools consistently improve immediate work quality across all experience levels, it reveals a critical divergence in long-term learning outcomes based on seniority. Specifically, senior lawyers developed stronger independent judgment through AI use, whereas junior lawyers showed no average skill gain, with their performance scores bifurcating into higher and lower extremes.

## Key Takeaways
- **Universal Productivity Boost:** AI assistance significantly improved drafting quality for all participants, raising scores by 0.34 standard deviations (a 10-point percentile climb) after just 10 days of use, demonstrating that AI acts as a reliable "exoskeleton" for immediate output.
- **Divergent Learning Outcomes by Seniority:** The impact of AI on skill acquisition depends heavily on experience. Senior lawyers who used AI for 90 days demonstrated stronger independent judgment than their control-group counterparts, suggesting AI can augment existing expertise.
- **Risk of Skill Atrophy for Juniors:** Junior lawyers did not show average skill gains after 90 days of AI use. Instead, their performance split into more strong and more weak scores, indicating that AI may hinder the development of foundational judgment for less experienced professionals who rely on routine work for learning.

## Context
This research addresses a growing tension in the AI industry: the gap between immediate productivity gains and long-term human capital development. Previous studies in fields like radiology and software engineering have shown mixed results, where AI boosts output temporarily but fails to persist once the tool is removed. This study stands out because it combines three rare elements: sustained AI usage over months in real-world professional settings, credible evaluation of unassisted judgment, and blinded grading by domain experts. It moves beyond short-term lab experiments to examine how AI integrates into the complex, expertise-intensive workflow of intellectual property law.

## Implications
The findings suggest that AI integration strategies must be tailored to the experience level of the workforce. For senior professionals, AI serves as a powerful tool for enhancing judgment and efficiency, potentially accelerating high-level decision-making. However, for junior employees, indiscriminate AI use may disrupt the traditional apprenticeship model, where tedious, routine tasks are essential for building foundational skills. Organizations must consider how to design AI workflows that support learning rather than bypassing it. This has profound implications for training programs, suggesting that AI tools should perhaps be restricted or carefully supervised for novices to prevent the erosion of core competencies, while being fully embraced by experienced practitioners to maximize productivity and judgment refinement.
