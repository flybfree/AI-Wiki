## Summary
Anthropic has established a new life sciences research group focused on using Claude to accelerate fundamental biology discoveries through agent-human collaboration. In early results from this initiative, Claude autonomously identified a novel enzyme system associated with DNA repeats, exhibiting characteristics reminiscent of CRISPR systems. While the specific function of this newly discovered system remains unknown, its structural features align with known programmable molecular tools capable of cutting, copying, and pasting DNA.

## Key Takeaways
- Anthropic has formed a dedicated life sciences research group and laboratory to integrate AI agents into every step of biological discovery, from hypothesis generation to experimental testing. This initiative aims to systematize the process of identifying uncharacterized protein families in large DNA datasets.
- Claude autonomously discovered a novel enzyme system based on a reverse transcriptase found in jumbo phages. While the underlying reverse transcriptase was previously known, Claude was the first to identify its defining features: an associated array of non-coding DNA sequences and an accessory protein of unknown function.
- The discovered system shares characteristics with only a handful of other known biological systems that are programmable and perform operations like cutting, copying, and pasting DNA. Although its exact function is not yet determined, these traits suggest potential utility similar to CRISPR-based tools.
