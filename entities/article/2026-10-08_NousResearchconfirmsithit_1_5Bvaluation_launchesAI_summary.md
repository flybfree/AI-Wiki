## Summary
Nous Research has officially confirmed a $1.5 billion valuation following a $90 million Series B funding round, marking a significant milestone for the three-year-old startup known for its open-source Hermes Agent. The new capital will primarily fund the company’s strategic expansion into the enterprise sector with the launch of "Hermes for Businesses," a product designed to allow corporations to deploy customized, private AI agents for complex multi-step workflows. This move signifies a pivot from a developer-focused open-source model to a commercial enterprise offering, supported by strong revenue growth projections.

## Key Takeaways
- **Major Funding and Valuation**: Nous Research raised $90 million in a Series B round led by Robot Ventures, with participation from major tech and investment firms including Nvidia, Union Square Ventures, Menlo Ventures, and Samsung. This brings the startup’s total funding to $158 million and solidifies its status as a unicorn with a $1.5 billion valuation.
- **Massive Open-Source Adoption**: The company’s open-source Hermes Agent has achieved widespread popularity, having been cloned over 24 million times. According to internal estimates, this agent currently drives approximately 2.5% of global AI token usage, demonstrating significant traction in the developer community.
- **Enterprise Pivot and Revenue Growth**: The new funding will support the launch of "Hermes for Businesses," enabling companies to use AI agents for secure, private workflows. Financially, Nous Research reported roughly $36 million in annualized revenue by mid-September 2026 and projects surpassing $100 million in revenue by the end of 2026, indicating a rapid transition from open-source popularity to commercial viability.

## Context
This development occurs within a rapidly maturing AI agent ecosystem where the focus is shifting from general-purpose chatbots to specialized, autonomous agents capable of executing complex tasks. Nous Research’s success highlights the viability of open-source models in the enterprise space, challenging the dominance of closed-source proprietary models. The involvement of hardware giants like Nvidia and Samsung in the funding round underscores the industry's recognition that software agents are critical components of the future AI infrastructure stack. Furthermore, the participation of firms like 1789 Capital, associated with Donald Trump Jr., reflects the diverse and sometimes politically connected nature of modern AI investment landscapes.

## Implications
The launch of "Hermes for Businesses" signals a broader industry trend where open-source AI tools are being packaged for enterprise consumption, addressing critical needs for data privacy and security in corporate environments. By offering customized agents that handle multi-step workflows, Nous Research is positioning itself as a direct competitor to enterprise AI solutions from major tech incumbents. This move validates the business model of open-source AI startups, proving that massive community adoption can translate into substantial commercial revenue. For the broader AI field, this suggests that the next frontier for AI agents lies not just in model capability, but in seamless integration into secure, private business operations, potentially accelerating the adoption of AI in regulated industries.
