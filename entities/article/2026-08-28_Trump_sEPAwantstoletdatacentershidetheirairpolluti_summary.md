## Summary  
The Trump administration’s EPA is proposing to eliminate the federal public‑participation requirement for air permits under New Source Review, allowing data‑center developers to proceed without community notice or comment. This change would shift permitting decisions to state and local agencies, potentially shielding facilities from environmental scrutiny.

## Key Takeaways  
- The EPA wants to scrap a 1970s rule that forces public notice for industrial air permits.  
- Data‑center developers could start construction without informing nearby residents or the public.  
- Permitting burden is being moved from federal to state/local authorities, raising concerns about weaker oversight.

## Context  
This follows the rapid rise of large AI data centers and their associated power demand; the permitting process has become a flashpoint for community opposition. The EPA’s “minor source” exemption applies to facilities with low emissions but still impacts air quality across state lines. Lee Zeldin argues it cuts unnecessary red tape, while environmental groups warn of reduced accountability.

## Implications  
If implemented, data centers could be built without public input, potentially leading to unchecked pollution and community backlash; the shift may encourage other industries to avoid transparent permitting, undermining environmental protection and trust in regulatory agencies. It also highlights tension between AI growth and local environmental concerns.
