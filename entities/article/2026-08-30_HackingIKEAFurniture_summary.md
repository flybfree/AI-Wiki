## Summary  
The author describes how they repurposed two IKEA Kallax shelving units and a discarded desktop board to create custom workbenches that combine the practicality of industrial storage with a living‑room aesthetic, avoiding the high cost of bespoke furniture. By leveraging inexpensive, readily available components—MDF panels, screws, and decorative foil—they achieved a functional, wall‑mounted solution that fits their space constraints while staying within budget.

## Key Takeaways  
- IKEA’s low‑price Kallax units enable DIY projects that rival custom furniture without the expense.  
- Repurposing existing materials (e.g., an old desk) reduces waste and cost, turning scraps into functional components.  
- Simple, modular builds allow personalization while maintaining a clean visual finish.

## Context  
This case illustrates how low‑cost, mass‑produced hardware can be reimagined through user‑centric design, echoing trends in AI‑driven product personalization and generative design that optimize material use. The integration of AR filtering on IKEA’s site also reflects the industry’s move toward immersive, data‑rich shopping experiences.

For manufacturers, this approach highlights opportunities to create “hackable” products that empower consumers with low barriers to customization, fostering a circular economy and reducing reliance on high‑margin bespoke services.
