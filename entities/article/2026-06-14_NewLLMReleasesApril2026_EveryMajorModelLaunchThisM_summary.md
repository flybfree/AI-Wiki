## Summary  
April 2026 saw a surge of new large‑language‑model releases, with OpenAI’s GPT‑5.5 (released April 23) and Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.7 (April 16) emerging as the dominant public‑API options. Meanwhile, Google’s Gemma 4 series, Zhipu AI’s GLM‑5.1, and Meta’s Llama 4 variants were rolled out at no cost, underscoring a growing competition between proprietary paid models and open‑source alternatives. The Fazm blog also highlights a macOS‑only local agent that can plug any of these APIs into a real Mac for voice‑first interaction.

## Key Takeaways  
- GPT‑5.5’s price structure ($5 in / $30 out) now outpaces Claude Opus 4.7 ($5 in / $25 out), flipping the per‑token cost hierarchy this month.  
- Open‑source models such as Gemma 4 (Apache 2.0, free weights) and GLM‑5.1 (MIT, free weights) are gaining visibility despite limited parameter counts compared with flagship proprietary releases.  
- The Fazm macOS agent demonstrates how developers can integrate any of these APIs locally without leaving their device, encouraging on‑device AI usage.

## Context  
The April 2026 LLM landscape reflects a classic tension between commercial giants (OpenAI, Anthropic) and the open‑source community. OpenAI’s delayed GPT‑5.5 launch and price increase signal continued focus on incremental gains rather than a disruptive leap to “GPT‑6.” Anthropic’s Opus 4.7 maintains its competitive edge with lower outbound costs. Meanwhile, Google and Meta are accelerating open‑source releases, leveraging Apache 2.0 licenses to attract developers who value transparency and cost‑free access.

## Implications  
These dynamics will shape market adoption: higher per‑token pricing may push users toward cheaper or fully free models, especially for research and local applications like Fazm. The proliferation of open‑source LLMs could accelerate the diffusion of smaller, efficient architectures (e.g., Gemma 4’s 31B dense model) that fit on consumer hardware, potentially reducing reliance on cloud APIs. For enterprises, the shift may drive hybrid strategies—using proprietary models for high‑value tasks while leveraging open alternatives for cost‑sensitive or privacy‑critical workloads.
