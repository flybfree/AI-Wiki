## Summary  
The Science One Framework introduces a verifiable autonomous research system built on the Chain‑of‑Evidence (CoE) principle, which requires every claim in an AI‑generated scientific paper to be backed by a traceable evidence chain. The accompanying CoE Audit automatically evaluates the integrity of these papers, detecting hallucinated references, unreproducible results, and method misalignments. Benchmarks show that baseline systems hallucinate up to 21 % of citations and often produce non‑verifiable outputs, whereas Science One achieves zero phantom references and state‑of‑the‑art performance on MLE‑Bench and Parameter‑Golf.

## Key Takeaways  
- Every research claim must be linked to concrete evidence (completeness) and that evidence must actually support the claim (correctness).  
- The CoE Audit quantifies breaks in these chains, turning trustworthiness into measurable metrics.  
- Autonomous AI agents can produce high‑quality scientific papers without sacrificing performance when built on a verifiable evidence framework.

## Context  
Large language models are increasingly used as autonomous research assistants that generate literature reviews, hypotheses, experiments, and manuscripts. Existing systems suffer from hallucinations—fabricated citations or scores—that undermine reproducibility and credibility. The broader AI landscape demands rigorous verification mechanisms to ensure that automated outputs are trustworthy and reproducible.

## Implications  
For the field of scientific AI, this framework establishes a new standard for verifiable research, potentially accelerating the adoption of autonomous agents while preserving scholarly integrity. In industry, it offers a template for auditing any AI‑generated documentation, improving reliability in data‑driven decision making and reducing costly errors from unverified claims.
