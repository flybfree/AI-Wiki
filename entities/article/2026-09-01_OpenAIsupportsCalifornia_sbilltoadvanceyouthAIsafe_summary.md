## Summary  
OpenAI backs California’s Senate Bill 1119, which aims to protect young users while keeping AI tools accessible for learning and creativity. The company argues that the bill aligns with its existing youth‑focused safeguards and could set a national standard in the absence of federal legislation.

## Key Takeaways  
- OpenAI will automatically place any user under 18 into “ChatGPT for Teens,” applying built‑in safety features such as content filters, crisis‑resource links, and parental controls.  
- The bill requires age verification, independent audits, and protection against high‑risk interactions while preserving access to educational AI functions.  
- California’s leadership on youth AI safety could influence broader policy, encouraging other states or federal action.

## Context  
The rapid adoption of generative AI by teens—nearly nine in ten use ChatGPT weekly for learning and productivity—creates a unique challenge: balancing innovation with developmental needs. OpenAI has already launched teen‑specific features and advocated for the Parents & Kids Safe AI Act, making California’s bill a natural extension of its policy agenda.

## Implications  
If enacted, SB 1119 could shape industry standards by mandating age‑appropriate safeguards without stifling AI utility, prompting other jurisdictions to adopt similar frameworks. This may accelerate research into developmental‑focused AI design and reduce long‑term safety risks for the first generation of digital natives.
