## Summary  
Naïve, an AI‑driven infrastructure startup, has raised $28.5 million in a Series A round to automate the grunt work of setting up and running a company. Its platform lets developers provide a prompt that provisions everything from legal entities and cloud resources to email accounts and payment gateways, with only limited human involvement for KYC/KYB steps and token budgeting.

## Key Takeaways  
- Naïve’s API automates the majority of foundational business setup tasks using AI agents.  
- The service depends on users supplying a token budget and completing brief manual checks such as KYC.  
- Early traction shows over 30,000 developer customers and a run‑rate revenue increase of tenfold to low double‑digit millions.

## Context  
The broader AI landscape is seeing rapid adoption of autonomous agents that can perform complex tasks, yet existing tools rarely integrate legal, financial, or cloud services into a single workflow. Naïve addresses this gap by bundling these components behind one API, enabling developers to spin up fully operational businesses with minimal manual effort.

## Implications  
This approach could dramatically lower the time and cost of launching startups, democratizing access to enterprise‑grade infrastructure for AI agents. However, it also raises concerns about governance—ensuring agents stay within budget, respecting human approvals—and the sustainability of token costs as agents run continuously. The model may reshape how companies are built, turning what was once a months‑long setup into an automated, near‑instant process.
