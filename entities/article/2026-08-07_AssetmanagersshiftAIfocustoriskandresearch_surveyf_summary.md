## Summary  
A survey of 225 senior technology leaders in UK asset‑management firms shows that the sector is entering a second wave of AI adoption, moving away from low‑value back‑office automation toward higher‑impact applications such as risk modelling, investment research and software development. While many companies have piloted AI tools, only a minority view these projects as truly transformative, and persistent barriers—including poor data quality, legacy systems, leadership resistance and skill gaps—slow enterprise‑wide rollout.

## Key Takeaways  
- **Strategic shift:** Asset managers are prioritising risk modelling, research assistance and software development over back‑office automation.  
- **Data & legacy constraints:** 81 % cite poor data quality and outdated systems as the primary obstacle to scaling AI solutions.  
- **Pilot vs. transformation gap:** Although 98 % have launched at least one AI pilot, only 12 % describe AI as a true business transformer.

## Context  
The UK asset‑management industry manages roughly £10 trillion in assets, making it the world’s second‑largest centre after the US. The broader AI landscape is characterised by rapid innovation and increasing demand for data‑driven decision support across finance and insurance sectors. However, sector‑specific challenges such as regulatory scrutiny, legacy infrastructure, and fragmented governance create a unique environment where generic AI trends do not translate directly into operational value.

## Implications  
The findings underscore that successful AI integration in asset management hinges on overcoming data and system modernization hurdles rather than merely adopting new tools. Firms that invest early in robust data pipelines, modernise legacy architectures, and up‑skill their talent will be better positioned to realise the higher‑value use cases that drive competitive advantage. Conversely, those stuck with fragmented governance and resistance risk widening the gap between pilot successes and enterprise transformation, potentially ceding market leadership to more agile peers.
