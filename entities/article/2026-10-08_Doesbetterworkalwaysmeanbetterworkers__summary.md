## Summary
This article details a three-month randomized controlled trial involving practicing patent attorneys to investigate whether AI assistance enhances or erodes professional expertise. The study reveals that while AI tools consistently improve immediate work quality across all experience levels, their impact on long-term skill development varies significantly by seniority. Specifically, senior lawyers demonstrated stronger independent judgment after using AI, whereas junior lawyers showed no average skill gain, with their performance scores splitting into higher and lower extremes.

## Key Takeaways
- **Universal Productivity Boost:** Access to AI tools significantly improved drafting quality for all participating lawyers, raising performance scores by 0.34 standard deviations equivalent to a 10-point percentile climb, regardless of experience level.
- **Divergent Learning Outcomes:** The effect of AI on on-the-job learning is dependent on seniority; senior lawyers developed stronger judgment capabilities after 90 days of AI usage, while junior lawyers did not show a consistent average improvement in independent skills.
- **Polarization of Junior Performance:** For junior lawyers, AI usage resulted in a bimodal distribution of outcomes, where some individuals improved significantly while others performed worse, suggesting that AI may not uniformly support skill acquisition for less experienced professionals.

## Context
The research addresses a critical gap in understanding how AI integrates into professional workflows over extended periods. While previous studies in fields like radiology and software engineering have shown that AI can act as a temporary "exoskeleton" that boosts output but fails to persist as a learning tool, this study provides a more nuanced view by focusing on a highly expertise-intensive profession: intellectual property law. The experiment utilized a then-unreleased Google Labs AI patent writing assistant, now part of Gemini Notebook, to test sustained AI usage embedded in ordinary professional work rather than short-term tasks. This context is vital for understanding the long-term implications of AI adoption in knowledge-based industries where judgment and nuanced expertise are paramount.

## Implications
These findings suggest that AI integration strategies must be tailored to the experience level of the workforce. For senior professionals, AI can serve as a powerful tool for enhancing judgment and refining expertise, potentially accelerating their decision-making processes. However, for junior professionals, AI may not automatically translate into better independent skills and could even hinder development if not managed carefully. The polarization of junior performance indicates that without proper supervision or structured training, AI might create a divide between those who leverage it effectively and those who rely on it too heavily, potentially eroding foundational skills. This highlights the need for organizations to design AI adoption frameworks that support guided learning for novices while leveraging AI’s benefits for experienced workers, ensuring that technology augments rather than replaces the critical development of professional judgment.
