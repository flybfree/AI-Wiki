## Summary  
A large cohort study of over 260,000 individuals demonstrates that waist circumference (WC) and waist‑to‑hip ratio (WHR) provide a superior predictor of cardiovascular outcomes than body mass index (BMI) alone. Even in people classified as normal or overweight by BMI, elevated central adiposity raises the risk of heart disease, stroke, and mortality by 15 %–50 %, while obesity with low WC does not confer additional risk except for lower all‑cause mortality. The findings highlight that traditional BMI thresholds misclassify cardiovascular risk when they ignore fat distribution.

## Key Takeaways  
- Central adiposity is a stronger predictor of heart disease than BMI, especially in individuals whose BMI suggests normal or overweight weight.  
- High waist circumference or WHR in normal‑weight and overweight groups correlates with a 15 %–50 % increase in major cardiovascular events.  
- Obesity combined with low WC does not elevate risk (except for reduced all‑cause mortality), underscoring the need to consider both BMI and fat distribution.

## Context  
The article reflects broader trends in health analytics where multimodal data—combining weight metrics with body‑composition measures—improve predictive accuracy. In AI‑driven clinical decision support systems, integrating such physiological variables can yield more reliable risk scores than relying solely on a single index like BMI, aligning with the push for personalized medicine and reduced diagnostic error.

## Implications  
Healthcare providers and AI developers must update risk assessment algorithms to include WC and WHR alongside BMI. Ignoring central fat distribution could lead to underestimation of cardiovascular disease risk, potentially resulting in delayed interventions or inappropriate management strategies. This shift is essential for maintaining the integrity of predictive models and ensuring equitable patient outcomes across diverse BMI categories.
