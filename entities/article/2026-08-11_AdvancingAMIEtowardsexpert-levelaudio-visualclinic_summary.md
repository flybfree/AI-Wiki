## Summary  
The article describes the evolution of AMIE from a text‑based system to real‑time video consultations that achieve expert‑level performance in a randomized controlled study, integrating multimodal perception and physical examination guidance. This advancement directly tackles the limitation of text‑only AI by preserving visual and auditory cues essential for clinical reasoning.

## Key Takeaways  
- AMIE (Video) delivers expert‑level diagnostic reasoning during live video consultations, comparable to human physicians.  
- The system perceives non‑verbal cues and guides patient actors through virtual physical examinations, enhancing multimodal interaction.  
- Validation was performed in a multi‑arm randomized study with 30 board‑certified primary care physicians, demonstrating clinical feasibility.

## Context  
Current AI research focuses on text‑only medical dialogue, which discards critical visual and auditory information. The push toward multimodal AI reflects broader industry trends to embed human‑like interaction into healthcare technology, requiring integration with existing clinical workflows and regulatory approval processes.

## Implications  
This development could streamline access to specialist care, reduce clinician workload by handling routine examinations, and improve diagnostic accuracy through richer data capture. However, successful deployment will depend on maintaining physician trust, ensuring patient privacy, and navigating regulatory pathways for AI‑assisted video consultations.
