## Summary  
Instinct, a stealth‑mode AI personal assistant created by Spear Street Technology, has generated buzz for its “magical” task‑automation abilities, but it also raises serious privacy and security alarms. Early testers have highlighted the company’s Terms of Service, which grant Instinct a perpetual, irrevocable license to use any user data—including screenshots, cursor movements, and keyboard inputs—for training its models and for entering binding agreements on behalf of users.

## Key Takeaways  
- **Broad, permanent data rights:** The TOS allow Instinct to access, store, transmit, and modify all material a user shares with it, effectively giving the AI a “license” to use that data forever.  
- **Comprehensive device integration:** Instinct can read email, messages, calendar events, location, screen captures, and even audio from users’ devices without explicit per‑task consent.  
- **Limited user control over data deletion:** Early adopters reported that Gmail records could not be removed by the assistant, prompting a later fix that adds a manual “delete external data” tool.

## Context  
Instinct’s launch occurs amid a wave of AI assistants (e.g., OpenClaw) that promise seamless personalization. The broader industry is grappling with how to balance powerful, context‑aware agents against user privacy expectations and regulatory demands for consent and data minimization. Stealth releases like Instinct illustrate the tension between rapid innovation and the need for transparent, accountable AI practices.

## Implications  
If companies continue to grant perpetual, irrevocable licenses over user data without robust opt‑out mechanisms, it could normalize a model where personal information is treated as a commodity rather than a protected asset. This may push regulators toward stricter consent frameworks, mandatory data‑deletion tools, and clearer liability clauses for AI agents that act on behalf of users. For the field, Instinct serves as a cautionary case study: powerful assistants must be paired with strong privacy safeguards to maintain public trust and avoid legal backlash.
