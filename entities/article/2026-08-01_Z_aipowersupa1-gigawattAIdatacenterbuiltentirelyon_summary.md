## Summary  
Chinese AI developer Z.ai has completed a 1‑gigawatt data center that relies exclusively on domestically manufactured chips, with no Nvidia silicon involved. The facility now powers multiple computing clusters each containing more than ten thousand chips and is used to train its GLM model family.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Z.ai operates a 1 gigawatt data center built entirely on Chinese‑made chips (Huawei Ascend), eliminating reliance on Nvidia and aligning with China’s push for self‑sufficient AI hardware.  
- The site hosts several clusters each holding over ten thousand chips, enabling large‑scale training of open‑weight models such as GLM‑5.2 without any foreign semiconductor support.  
- Although the facility draws a gigawatt of power, Chinese accelerators like Huawei’s Ascend deliver lower performance per watt than Nvidia Blackwell, limiting compute efficiency for AI workloads.  

## Context  
China is investing billions in a nationwide network of AI data centers to reduce dependence on foreign technology, with at least 80 % of the underlying hardware sourced from domestic suppliers. This trend reflects broader policy goals to create a sovereign supply chain for critical AI components. However, bottlenecks remain: SMIC’s most advanced stable node is already above 93 % utilization, and limited HBM memory constrains how many Ascend chips can be produced. Consequently, even a 1‑gigawatt data center may not fully realize the intended compute capacity.  

## Implications  
The shift highlights a growing competition between domestic and global AI infrastructure, potentially slowing adoption of cutting‑edge Nvidia silicon in China while underscoring the need for robust local supply chains. It also raises questions about model performance benchmarks, as training on less efficient hardware may lag behind systems using Nvidia Blackwell, affecting both research outcomes and commercial competitiveness.
