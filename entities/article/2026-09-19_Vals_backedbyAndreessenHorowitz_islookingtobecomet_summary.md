## Summary
Vals, a startup backed by Andreessen Horowitz, aims to establish a new gold standard for AI benchmarking by addressing the limitations of current evaluation methods. Founded by Rayan Krishnan, the company seeks to move beyond abstract academic tests toward practical, industry-specific evaluations that measure an AI's ability to perform high-quality work comparable to humans.

## Key Takeaways
- **Moving Beyond Academic Metrics:** Current benchmarks often rely on outdated metrics that fail to keep pace with rapid advancements in model capabilities; Vals intends to bridge this gap by testing "real-world" impacts.
- **Proprietary and Practical Testing:** Unlike many systems where test materials are public—allowing models to be trained specifically to "cheat" the exam—Vals keeps its specific test materials private and focuses on complex tasks in fields like law, finance, and coding.
- **Safety and Risk Assessment:** The company's evaluation framework includes analyzing negative implications, such as how models might behave if left unchecked, with specific focus areas including biosecurity, cybersecurity, and the application of the Geneva Convention.

## Context
The AI industry currently relies heavily on benchmarks to market model superiority; however, these metrics have become increasingly easy for companies to "game" or manipulate through targeted training. As AI becomes more deeply integrated into society, there is a growing need for objective, third-party verification that ensures models can actually perform the tasks they claim to be capable of, rather than just scoring well on standardized tests.

## Implications
This shift toward specialized, private, and high-stakes benchmarking could fundamentally change how AI companies market their products and how regulators assess model safety. By moving from "general knowledge" scores to "functional competence" metrics, Vals may provide the infrastructure needed for enterprise adoption in sensitive sectors like medicine and law. Furthermore, by incorporating risk factors like recursive self-improvement and biosecurity into their benchmarks, the company is positioning itself as a critical gatekeeper for AI safety and reliability rather than just another data provider.
