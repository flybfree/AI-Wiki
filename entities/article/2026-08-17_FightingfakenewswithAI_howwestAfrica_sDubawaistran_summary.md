## Summary  
The Centre for Journalism Innovation and Development (CJID) in Abuja has created Dubawa AI, a fact‑checking tool designed to help Nigerian journalists and the public verify claims that could affect society. By training its machine‑learning models on locally sourced data, Dubawa aims to outperform generic global AI systems that often “hallucinate” or fail to understand African news contexts.

## Key Takeaways  
- Local AI models are essential because most global models lack knowledge of Africa and tend to generate inaccurate information about the continent.  
- The platform is built to detect high‑impact claims such as health, political, and social statements that can influence public opinion.  
- Compliance with Nigeria’s Data Protection Regulation (DPR) is a priority, requiring internal data storage and adherence to local privacy rules.

## Context  
Nigeria hosts more than 148 million active internet subscribers, making it Africa’s largest online market. At the same time, generative AI has made deepfakes and synthetic media increasingly convincing, pressuring journalists to verify content before publication. Global AI models are often trained on European or American data, which can produce unreliable results for African news cycles.

## Implications  
Developing region‑specific AI strengthens trust in West African media, curtails the spread of misinformation, and aligns with broader efforts to create responsible, locally compliant AI ecosystems. It also signals a shift toward investment in African‑centric data infrastructure, encouraging further innovation that respects national regulations while delivering practical benefits for journalists and citizens alike.
