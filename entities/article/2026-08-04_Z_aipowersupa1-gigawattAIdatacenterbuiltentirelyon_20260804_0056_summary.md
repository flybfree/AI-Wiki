## Summary  
Z.ai, the Chinese AI developer formerly known as Zhipu, has completed a 1‑gigawatt data center that is powered entirely by domestically manufactured chips—most likely Huawei’s Ascend accelerators—and now runs multiple clusters of over 10 000 chips each. The facility will train its GLM model family and represents one of the largest AI compute sites ever built in China, despite delivering less training performance per watt than comparable Nvidia‑based facilities.

## Key Takeaways  
- [Z.ai’s data center uses only Chinese‑made silicon (Huawei Ascend), with zero Nvidia hardware involved.  
- [China’s domestic AI chip supply chain is strained: SMIC’s 7nm‑class N+2 node runs above 93 % utilization and HBM shortages limit further accelerator production.  
- [A gigawatt of Chinese accelerators still provides less usable training compute than a gigawatt consumed by Nvidia Blackwell parts, reflecting performance‑per‑watt gaps.

## Context  
China is planning to invest roughly $295 billion over five years in a nationwide AI data‑center grid, with at least 80 % of the underlying technology sourced from Chinese suppliers. This massive push is constrained by the high utilization of SMIC’s most advanced stable node and limited domestic HBM memory, which together cap how many Ascend chips can be manufactured to meet the power demand.

## Implications  
The development underscores both China’s ambition to build a self‑sufficient AI infrastructure and its technical limitations: reliance on domestic silicon reduces compute efficiency compared with leading Nvidia solutions, potentially slowing model training speed and limiting competitive edge. For global AI research, the case highlights how supply‑chain constraints can shape innovation timelines and may reinforce regional divisions in hardware performance versus cost.
