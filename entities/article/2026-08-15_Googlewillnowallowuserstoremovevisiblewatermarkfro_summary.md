## Summary  
Google will now allow users to toggle off the visible watermark on its AI‑generated images, videos, and songs while preserving the invisible SynthID watermark and C2PA metadata that ensure provenance. The setting is available in the Gemini app, Flow video editor, and upcoming Search integration for the Nano Banana, Omni, and Lyria models.

## Key Takeaways  
- [The visible watermark can be disabled via Settings > Media Watermark without removing underlying AI provenance markers.]  
- [Only the Nano Banana, Omni, and Lyria generative models support the toggle; other services may follow later.]  
- [Google emphasizes a balance between creative control and safety, keeping invisible SynthID watermarks for detection.]

## Context  
This move follows growing industry pressure to reduce visually obtrusive AI labels that hinder professional use while complying with EU regulations requiring transparency. Anthropic’s recent decision to add visible watermarks to Claude outputs highlights regulatory divergence within the sector.

## Implications  
Enabling user‑controlled watermark removal may boost adoption of Google’s Gemini across creative and enterprise workflows, yet could complicate provenance tracking if invisible markers are removed; it underscores the tension between usability and accountability in AI content labeling.
