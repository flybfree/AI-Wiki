## Summary  
AI firms are secretly buying, scanning, and destroying millions of physical books to harvest “untouched” training data for their large language models, thereby monopolizing pre‑2022 knowledge on private servers. The article warns that this practice is legal but ethically a crime against humanity, and urges volunteers worldwide to scan rare books before the cultural heritage vanishes.

## Key Takeaways  
- AI companies such as Anthropic’s “Project Panama” are acquiring secondhand books, scanning them, training models, then destroying the originals.  
- The destruction is cheaper than lossless scanning and helps competitors stay out of the data pool while avoiding legal exposure.  
- Anna’s Archive calls on global volunteers to scan and upload these rare books now, lest they be lost forever.

## Context  
The rapid advancement of AI has driven a fierce race for high‑quality training data, prompting firms to seek “clean” sources that predate the digital age. While scanning is technically feasible, many companies choose destruction to eliminate any risk of competitors using the same material and to keep knowledge locked behind corporate walls.

## Implications  
If physical books continue to be destroyed without preservation, a significant portion of human cultural heritage will disappear from public access, concentrating all knowledge in proprietary databases that only AI developers can read. This undermines open scholarship, fuels monopolistic control over information, and jeopardizes the long‑term availability of foundational texts for future generations.
