## Summary  
Atlassian has introduced monthly “AI wallets” that cap each employee’s spend at $500–$2,000 to curb the practice of “tokenmaxxing,” where staff overuse AI tokens and incur steep costs. The firm continues to support its R&D teams with these budgets and has never refused any request for additional funds.

## Key Takeaways  
- Atlassian limits staff AI spending via $500–$2,000 monthly wallets across four AI products, pausing usage when the limit is reached.  
- The move directly opposes “tokenmaxxing,” a trend where other tech firms encourage employees to use as much AI as possible and even publish leaderboards for top users.  
- Employees can request extra funds, but Atlassian’s policy has never denied such requests.

## Context  
Tokenmaxxing refers to the excessive consumption of AI tokens—measuring roughly four characters per token—leading to rapid cost escalation. OpenAI charges $5 per million tokens, while Anthropic’s models charge $10 per million. Companies like Uber have reportedly blown through their AI budgets in months, prompting stricter financial controls.

## Implications  
This shift signals a broader industry move toward responsible AI spending: balancing innovation with fiscal discipline. By capping usage and providing transparent wallets, Atlassian sets a precedent that may influence other firms to adopt similar safeguards, potentially reducing wasteful token consumption while still fostering experimentation in an “AI‑first” culture.
