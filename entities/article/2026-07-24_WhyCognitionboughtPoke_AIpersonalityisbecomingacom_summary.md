## Summary  
Cognition has acquired Poke, the AI assistant that chats like a friend, for a “low‑nine‑figure” valuation, integrating its personality‑driven interaction model into Cognition’s coding assistant Devin. The move reflects a growing belief that how an AI agent engages users can be as valuable as the underlying models, aiming to make Devin feel more like a human colleague than a sterile tool.

## Key Takeaways  
- **Strategic acquisition:** Cognition paid “low nine figures” for Poke, gaining its personality model and interaction expertise.  
- **Enhanced product synergy:** Poke’s friendly, slang‑rich dialogue will be woven into Devin to boost user experience and perceived value.  
- **Future integration roadmap:** After the 2026‑year transition, Poke may leverage Cognition’s SWE‑1.7 model while Devin could adopt a more personable tone, potentially creating a fully combined assistant.

## Context  
The broader AI landscape is shifting from purely functional outputs to human‑centric experiences; companies are recognizing that personality, humor, and proactive assistance can differentiate their assistants in crowded markets. This trend mirrors the rise of “AI agents” that act as co‑workers rather than mere utilities, influencing everything from customer service to internal productivity tools.

## Implications  
For AI developers and investors, this acquisition signals a new competitive advantage: investing in user‑experience design can unlock higher engagement and monetization potential. It also suggests that future AI products may be evaluated not just on model accuracy but on how seamlessly they integrate personality and workflow, reshaping product roadmaps and market expectations across the industry.
