## Summary  
The article reports a Max‑Planck study showing that under specific conditions, AI‑discovered strategies can be adopted by humans and transmitted across generations, suggesting machines may shape long‑term cultural evolution. It argues that novel machine solutions become part of human knowledge only when they are initially hard to find, learnable, and clearly advantageous.  

## Key Takeaways  
- A strategy must first be difficult for humans to discover before it can spread.  
- The strategy must remain learnable from the AI output to be adopted by participants.  
- Its advantage must be unambiguous so that it is recognized and preserved across generations.

This research addresses a growing concern in AI development: while systems like AlphaGo or AlphaZero uncover unconventional strategies, their impact on human cognition remains unproven. The study bridges cognitive science and machine learning, exploring how algorithmic insight can become part of collective wisdom.
