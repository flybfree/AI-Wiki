## Summary  
Bose’s CEO Lila Snyder explains how the company is shifting from a pure consumer‑focused audio brand to a B2B licensing powerhouse, selling its core technology—including noise‑cancelling and high‑fidelity sound—to partners such as Skullcandy and Epson. This move reflects both a strategic response to emerging AI wearables that require sophisticated audio processing and an attempt to preserve Bose’s reputation while adapting the headphone market disrupted by Apple, Samsung, and other entrants.

## Key Takeaways  
- Bose is transitioning from a product‑centric to a licensing‑centric model, selling its technology rather than only its own branded devices.  
- The company has acquired high‑end audio brands (McIntosh Labs, Sonus faber) and sold the Professional division, reshaping its R&D focus toward B2B opportunities.  
- AI wearables are a key driver for this shift, as they demand advanced audio processing that Bose can now provide to external manufacturers.

## Context  
The rapid rise of AI‑enabled wearables—devices that blend headphones with voice‑control and health sensors—creates a new market where seamless audio integration is essential. Traditional headphone makers face pressure from smartphone manufacturers who embed their own earbuds, while companies like Bose are exploring how to monetize the underlying audio algorithms through licensing.

## Implications  
This transformation underscores that AI will not merely replace consumer electronics but also reshape business models across the industry. By becoming a technology supplier rather than just a product seller, Bose illustrates a broader trend where firms leverage AI capabilities for cross‑industry revenue streams, potentially redefining value creation in both hardware and software domains.
