## Summary  
The article introduces PhotoScan, a deep‑learning system that extracts three‑dimensional body‑composition metrics—body fat percentage, android‑to‑gyndoid (A/G) ratio and visceral‑to‑subcutaneous (V/S) ratio—directly from standard smartphone photos. By leveraging data from the UK Biobank and a validation cohort of 677 adults, PhotoScan predicts insulin resistance with accuracy comparable to gold‑standard DXA scans while avoiding radiation exposure. This approach bridges the gap between wearable biosensors and clinical imaging for early cardiometabolic risk assessment.  

## Key Takeaways  
- PhotoScan can estimate body composition from ordinary smartphone images with near‑DXA precision.  
- The model predicts insulin resistance using A/G and V/S ratios, metrics unavailable to bioelectrical impedance devices.  
- The system is non‑invasive, scalable, and requires no specialized clinical infrastructure.
