## Summary  
Etched, an AI‑chip startup founded by Harvard dropouts, has secured a $300 million Series C round that pushes its valuation to $10.3 billion, the highest ever for a Sequoia‑led Series C. The funding follows earlier rounds that doubled its value in seven months and comes as Etched reports successful silicon production, $1 billion of orders, and systems capable of running diverse AI models beyond transformers.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Etched’s valuation has surged to $10.3 B after a Sequoia‑led Series C, reflecting strong investor confidence despite early skepticism about its full‑system approach.  
- The company’s low‑voltage prefill chip and cluster‑scale memory interconnect promise faster inference with reduced heat and cost, challenging the dominance of high‑power Nvidia solutions.  
- Etched can run transformer models as well as alternative architectures like Mamba and Mixture‑of‑Experts, proving its chips are not limited to specific LLMs.  

## Context  
The AI hardware market is dominated by large firms such as Nvidia that specialize in high‑power GPUs optimized for transformer inference. Etched’s emergence highlights a growing interest in specialized, low‑energy silicon designed for broader model compatibility and cost efficiency. The startup also aligns with industry trends toward custom ASICs and memory‑centric architectures to address the compute bottlenecks of large language models.  

## Implications  
Etched’s success could accelerate adoption of heterogeneous AI chips, encouraging competitors to develop more flexible hardware that supports diverse model families without sacrificing performance. It may also pressure incumbents to innovate beyond pure GPU solutions, fostering a multi‑vendor ecosystem where low‑power inference becomes mainstream rather than niche. This shift could reshape data‑center energy consumption and lower the total cost of AI deployment.
