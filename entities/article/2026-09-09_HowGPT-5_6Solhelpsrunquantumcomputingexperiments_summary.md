## Summary  
The article describes how MIT’s EQuS group integrated GPT‑5.6 Sol—an AI agent built on Codex—into its quantum‑computing experiment pipeline to automate routine measurement workflows. By providing the model with detailed skills for calibrating a six‑qubit superconducting chip, Sol could run measurements, analyze data, and adapt parameters without human intervention, freeing researchers to concentrate on higher‑level analysis and design.

## Key Takeaways  
- GPT‑5.6 Sol can autonomously execute the repetitive, interdependent steps of qubit calibration, dramatically cutting manual effort.  
- The AI’s ability to reason about measurement parameters makes it well‑suited for tasks where each result influences subsequent operations.  
- By delegating routine work to an agent, human scientists gain more time for experimental interpretation and strategic planning.

## Context  
This integration exemplifies the broader trend of applying generative AI to laboratory automation across physics, chemistry, and materials science. As quantum hardware matures, labs will need increasingly sophisticated control software; AI agents can act as “digital assistants,” handling data acquisition, preprocessing, and decision loops that traditionally required extensive human oversight.
