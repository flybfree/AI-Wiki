## Summary  
England is on track to become one of the first nations to eliminate hepatitis C, a liver‑affecting virus that can be cured with antiviral therapy. Since 2015 more than 100 000 people have been diagnosed and treated, achieving an 80 % treatment coverage but still falling short of the 90 % diagnostic target and the WHO‑set 65 % mortality reduction goal by 2030.  

## Key Takeaways  
- England has already cured >95 % of diagnosed patients, meeting the primary elimination metric.  
- The disease remains largely silent; up to 84.6 % of those infected have been identified, leaving a sizable undiagnosed population at risk.  
- Targeted outreach to high‑risk groups (e.g., former Soviet‑Soviet states) is crucial to close the diagnostic gap and achieve full elimination.
