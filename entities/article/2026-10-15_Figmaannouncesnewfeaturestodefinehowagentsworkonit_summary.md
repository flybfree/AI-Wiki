## Summary
Figma has announced a suite of new features at its first Config event in India, focusing on defining how AI agents operate within its design platform. The core of this update is "Figma Specs," a Markdown-based editor that allows teams to codify their design language, libraries, and guidelines into a centralized reference document. By integrating these structured rules with Figma’s AI agents and code layers, the company aims to steer non-deterministic AI outputs toward predictable, brand-compliant results while enhancing the integration between design and development workflows.

## Key Takeaways
- **Introduction of Figma Specs:** Figma launched a Markdown editor that enables users to define their design language and embed references to canvas elements, FigJam diagrams, and code layers. This serves as a centralized source of truth for both human teams and AI agents to adhere to specific brand rules.
- **Enhanced Agent Control and Code Integration:** To address the non-deterministic nature of AI, Figma is providing tools to guide agents toward predictable outcomes. Additionally, the "code layers" feature, which adds coding capabilities to designs for interactive prototypes, has moved from private beta to public beta, allowing users to ask agents to generate or modify code directly within the design canvas.
- **Strategic Expansion in India and CMS Integration:** Figma highlighted its growing presence in India, noting it is the largest market outside the US and serves 40% of the top 100 companies on the Bombay Stock Exchange. The company also announced deeper integration with the headless CMS Payload (acquired last year) to support dynamic website generation and content management, alongside support for over 20 new tools via its Model Context Protocol (MCP).

## Context
This announcement occurs amidst a broader industry shift where AI is increasingly integrated into software development lifecycles. Figma’s chief design officer, Loredana Crisan, highlighted a critical disconnect in the current AI landscape: while AI has increased code production by 17 times, actual software releases have only increased by 1.3 times. This suggests that much of the AI-generated code is used for exploration and design rather than shipping final products. By introducing structured constraints like Figma Specs, Figma is attempting to bridge the gap between rapid AI-assisted prototyping and reliable, production-ready software delivery.

## Implications
These developments signal a maturation of AI tools in professional design environments, moving beyond simple generation to governed creation. For the industry, this implies that successful AI integration in design requires more than just powerful models; it demands robust frameworks for context and rule adherence. By enabling agents to strictly follow defined design systems, Figma reduces the risk of brand inconsistency and hallucination in AI outputs. Furthermore, the expansion into India and the integration of headless CMS capabilities suggest that design platforms are evolving into comprehensive product development hubs, blurring the traditional lines between design, content management, and engineering. This could force competitors to adopt similar "guardrail" features to remain viable in enterprise markets where compliance and predictability are paramount.
