## Summary
Recent data from the Edinburgh-based Childlight – Global Child Safety Institute reveals a staggering 8,400% increase in reports of AI-related child sexual abuse material (CSAM) between 2023 and 2025. The number of reported cases surged from approximately 4,700 to over 400,000, driven largely by the proliferation of deepfakes and "nudify" apps that generate photorealistic but fake images used to exploit children. This alarming trend has prompted experts to call for governments to recognize this form of sexual exploitation as a preventable public health emergency requiring urgent intervention.

## Key Takeaways
- **Exponential Growth in AI Exploitation:** Reports of AI-generated child sexual abuse material rose from about 4,700 in 2023 to more than 400,000 in 2025, representing an 8,400% increase in just two years. This data highlights the rapid evolution of online threats facing children as generative AI tools become more accessible and sophisticated.
- **Mechanisms of Exploitation:** Although the images are synthetic, they are actively used to blackmail children into providing money or additional images. The research indicates that these fake but realistic depictions create a new vector for coercion and abuse, distinct from traditional forms of exploitation.
- **Global Scale and Offline Context:** Childlight estimates that over 300 million children worldwide suffer from technology-facilitated sexual exploitation annually. The study also underscores the persistence of offline abuse, noting that nearly one-third of girls and more than one in ten boys in North America experienced in-person sexual abuse during childhood, suggesting a complex interplay between digital and physical safety risks.

## Context
This surge occurs within a broader global context where generative AI tools are increasingly integrated into consumer applications, often without adequate safeguards for minors. The rise in reports correlates with the widespread availability of "nudify" apps and deepfake generators that can manipulate images with high photorealism. Parallel data from the Internet Watch Foundation (IWF) supports these findings, showing that AI-generated CSAM found online surged significantly in 2025 and continued to grow in 2026, with IWF analysts assessing over 6,310 illegal AI images in early 2026 alone. This indicates a systemic failure in current content moderation systems to keep pace with the speed of AI-generated content production.

## Implications
The findings necessitate an immediate shift in how governments and tech companies approach child safety online. Experts argue that sexual exploitation facilitated by AI should be treated as a public health emergency, requiring coordinated policy responses rather than piecemeal technical fixes. For the AI industry, this highlights the urgent need for stricter regulatory frameworks, improved detection algorithms for synthetic abuse material, and ethical guidelines for image-generation tools. Furthermore, the data suggests that protection systems are currently ill-equipped to handle the hybrid nature of modern exploitation, where digital coercion can have tangible offline consequences. Stakeholders must leverage evidence from surveys and police records to target resources effectively, ensuring that interventions address both the technological origins of the abuse and its real-world impact on victims.
