## Summary  
Students who use AI to help with schoolwork tend to score lower in the OECD’s PISA assessments, but the impact depends on how and why they use it; intentional, moderate use that is paired with training in evaluating AI output can even offset or improve performance. The study highlights a nuanced relationship between AI adoption, frequency of use, and socioeconomic background.

## Key Takeaways  
- AI users generally perform worse than non‑users, especially when they rely on AI for specific tasks such as summarizing texts or draft assignments.  
- Students who receive training to assess the quality of AI‑generated information show little penalty and may outperform those who do not use AI at all.  
- AI adoption is more common among students from advantaged socioeconomic backgrounds, reflecting better access to hardware, internet, and paid tools.

## Context  
The OECD’s Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) 2025, the first edition since AI became mainstream in schools, examined over 760,000 students across 91 countries. The survey measured science, math, and reading outcomes while mapping AI usage patterns, noting that frequent or task‑specific use correlates with poorer results.

## Implications  
For education policy and industry stakeholders, the findings suggest that AI should be integrated as a supportive tool rather than a shortcut; curricula must include instruction on critical evaluation of AI outputs to preserve the cognitive struggle essential for learning. Moreover, equity initiatives are needed to ensure that resource disparities do not widen achievement gaps caused by uneven access to AI technology.
