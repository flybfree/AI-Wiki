## Summary
Epic Games has mandated an "AI-first" development approach for an upcoming, unannounced shooter game featuring Marvel and Star Wars characters, a project that has been delayed from its original March release window. According to reports from Bloomberg, developers are being instructed to rely heavily on AI tools, such as Anthropic’s Claude, for both code generation and art creation, effectively eliminating hand-written code and traditional manual art workflows. This strategic shift marks a significant move toward fully AI-accelerated production pipelines within major gaming studios.

## Key Takeaways
- Epic Games has issued an internal directive to developers working on a Disney collaboration project to use AI tools like Anthropic’s Claude for generating code and art, with a specific instruction of "No more hand-written code."
- The upcoming shooter game, which features characters from Marvel and Star Wars, has been delayed from its initial March release schedule as the studio pivots to this new AI-centric development methodology.
- Epic Games spokesperson Jake Jones confirmed the company is "embracing AI-accelerated development," signaling a corporate-wide strategy to integrate AI deeply into core production processes rather than using it merely as a supplementary tool.

## Context
This development occurs within a broader industry trend where major technology companies, including Meta and Google, are increasingly deploying AI tools for game development. Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney has previously advocated for the normalization of AI in game production, arguing that "AI will be involved in nearly all future production" and pushing for platforms like Steam to remove "made with AI" tags. This shift follows significant operational changes at Epic, including the layoff of over 1,000 employees earlier in 2026 due to declining engagement with Fortnite since 2025. The move also builds upon Epic’s existing use of AI in Fortnite, such as the previously featured AI Darth Vader, but represents a much more aggressive integration of AI into the foundational creation of assets and code.

## Implications
The shift to fully AI-generated code and art in a high-profile AAA game project signals a potential turning point in how video games are produced, raising questions about the future role of human developers in creative and technical roles. If successful, this approach could drastically reduce development costs and timelines, potentially leading to a surge in content production across the industry. However, it also highlights the tension between efficiency-driven automation and the preservation of traditional craftsmanship in game design. As Epic leverages its $1.5 billion investment from Disney to pioneer this workflow, other studios may feel pressured to adopt similar strategies to remain competitive, potentially accelerating the displacement of traditional coding and artistic roles in favor of AI supervision and curation.
