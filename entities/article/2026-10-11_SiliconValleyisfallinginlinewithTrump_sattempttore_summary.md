## Summary
Major technology leaders, including Elon Musk, Marc Benioff, Jensen Huang, and Satya Nadella, have publicly adopted President Donald Trump’s proposed rebranding of artificial intelligence as “super intelligence.” This shift follows Trump’s executive order mandating the use of the new terminology by government agencies and his declaration that using the term "AI" makes one an enemy of the administration. The article highlights how Silicon Valley executives are capitulating to this political pressure to maintain favorable relationships with the current administration, despite the broader public largely continuing to use the term AI.

## Key Takeaways
- **Political Compliance Over Technical Accuracy:** Prominent tech CEOs are renaming their products and initiatives to align with Trump’s terminology, such as SpaceXAI becoming SpaceXSI and Salesforce’s AIForce becoming SIForce, despite the term "super intelligence" being widely viewed as inaccurate or exaggerated by the general public.
- **Strategic Alignment with the Administration:** The adoption of the new terminology is part of a broader strategy by Silicon Valley to ingratiate itself with the Trump administration during his second term, contrasting with the more contentious relationship seen during his first term.
- **High-Level Participation in Policy Shifts:** Key figures like Jensen Huang and Satya Nadella attended a White House summit on AI safety where the executive order was announced, signaling their willingness to engage with and support the administration’s narrative to avoid political fallout or regulatory disadvantages.

## Context
This development occurs within a specific political landscape where the Trump administration has actively sought to influence the narrative and regulation surrounding artificial intelligence. The push to rename AI to "super intelligence" is not merely a semantic change but a political maneuver intended to frame AI as a transformative, potentially dangerous force that requires specific governance. The article notes that while the general population has not adopted this terminology, the tech industry’s leadership is responding to direct political incentives. This situation reflects a broader trend of tech companies navigating complex political environments by aligning their public messaging with the preferences of powerful political figures to secure regulatory stability and avoid conflict.

## Implications
The widespread adoption of Trump’s terminology by industry leaders suggests that political pressure can significantly influence corporate branding and public discourse in the tech sector. For the AI industry, this highlights the vulnerability of technical terminology to political manipulation, potentially complicating public understanding and regulatory frameworks. If "super intelligence" becomes the standard in government and corporate communications, it may shift public perception of AI from a tool to a singular, overarching entity, which could impact how safety regulations and ethical guidelines are framed. Furthermore, this capitulation underscores the power dynamics between the tech industry and the government, indicating that maintaining political favor may require compromising on technical precision or public messaging strategies. This could set a precedent for future interactions between tech leaders and political administrations, where compliance with political narratives becomes a strategic necessity for business operations.
