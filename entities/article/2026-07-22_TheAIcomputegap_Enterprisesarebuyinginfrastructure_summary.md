## Summary  
Enterprises are rapidly expanding their AI infrastructure while remaining blind to the true cost and utilization of the compute they purchase. The result is a widening “compute gap” where spending outpaces the ability to measure or control economics, leading to high churn intent and under‑utilized GPUs.

## Key Takeaways  
- **High spend, low visibility:** 83 % report GPU utilization at 50 % or less, yet most organizations plan to invest heavily in AI‑specialized clouds that are barely used today.  
- **Rapid vendor churn:** 64 % intend to switch or add an infrastructure provider within a year (38 % within the next quarter), driven more by integration and total cost of ownership than headline token price.  
- **Economic blind spots:** Fewer than half can rigorously track compute costs, and only one‑fifth are aware of the upcoming shift from GPU to memory‑bandwidth inference.

## Context  
The article is part of VentureBeat’s Pulse Research series, which surveys 107 mid‑market enterprises (101–1,000 employees) on their AI infrastructure maturity. The data reflect a single Q2 2026 wave and are cross‑sectional rather than longitudinal; multiple‑select questions allow percentages to exceed 100 %. The focus is on the gap between aggressive investment and limited economic insight.

## Implications  
The compute gap signals that current AI deployments may be financially unsustainable, prompting costly vendor turnover without clear ROI. If enterprises cannot quantify true cost per inference or anticipate the shift to memory‑centric models, they risk over‑spending on under‑utilized hardware while missing strategic opportunities in emerging compute paradigms. This underscores a need for standardized cost‑tracking tools and early adoption of next‑generation inference architectures to align spending with measurable value.
