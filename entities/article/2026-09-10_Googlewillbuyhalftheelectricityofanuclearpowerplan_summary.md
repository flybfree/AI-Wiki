## Summary
Google has announced a record €13 billion investment in Finland, marking its largest single infrastructure commitment in Europe to support growing AI demand. As part of this package, Google signed a 22-year contract with Finnish utility Fortum to purchase up to 50% of the electricity generated by the Loviisa nuclear power plant. This deal aims to secure stable, low-carbon energy for three new data centers and an expanded existing facility, positioning Finland as a strategic hub for AI infrastructure development.

## Key Takeaways
- **Record Investment Scale:** The €13 billion package represents Google’s largest single investment in Europe, funding the construction of new data centers in Kajaani, Muhos, and Vaala, alongside the expansion of an existing site in Hamina. Construction is scheduled for 2027 and 2028, with projected support for over 37,000 jobs during the building phase.
- **Long-Term Nuclear Energy Contract:** Google secured a 22-year agreement with Fortum to buy up to half of the output from the Loviisa nuclear plant, which currently produces about 10% of Finland’s electricity. This commitment provides financial certainty for the utility and supports an investment program to extend the plant's life and increase its generating capacity.
- **Strategic Location Selection:** Finland was chosen due to its cool climate, which reduces cooling energy needs for servers, a clean energy mix, and an uncongested power grid. The investment is expected to boost Finland’s GDP by €3.6 billion annually, according to Google’s estimates.

## Context
This move occurs amid intense competition among technology giants to secure reliable power supplies for AI services, which require vast computational resources. Earlier in the week, TikTok also announced a $1 billion data center investment in Finland, highlighting a broader industry trend of leveraging Nordic countries' stable grids and renewable energy sources. Google’s parent company, Alphabet, recently raised its global spending plans to as much as $205 billion to expand computing capacity, underscoring the urgency of securing long-term energy contracts to support AI products like Gemini, Search, Maps, and YouTube.

## Implications
The deal illustrates a critical shift in how major tech companies approach sustainability and infrastructure planning, moving beyond simple renewable purchases to direct partnerships with nuclear utilities for baseload power stability. By integrating dedicated funds for biodiversity, education, and workforce development, Google aims to mitigate local community impacts while securing the energy density required for AI scaling. This partnership sets a precedent for long-term corporate-utility collaborations that could influence future energy procurement strategies across the tech industry, emphasizing the intersection of high-performance computing demands with national energy security goals.
