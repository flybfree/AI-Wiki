## Summary  
The Serpentine Gallery has launched the Future Art Ecosystems R&D Fellowship, a six‑month research programme that funds four emerging artists—Alice Bucknell, Reina Mun, Alfredo Salazar‑Caro and Auriea Harvey—to investigate how artificial intelligence is reshaping cultural production, institutions and public life. The fellowship is supported by Glass Castle, a philanthropic arm of Quadrature Capital, and will be overseen by judges such as Lila Ibrahim (Google DeepMind) and Mariana Mazzucato, linking creative practice with industry, academia and policy.

## Key Takeaways  
- Each fellow receives £10,000 plus mentorship for a six‑month research project exploring AI’s cultural impact.  
- The programme deliberately bridges art, technology, academia and policy through an interdisciplinary jury of experts like Lila Ibrahim and Mariana Mazzucato.  
- Funding is backed by Glass Castle, reflecting a long‑term commitment to integrating philanthropy with cutting‑edge artistic inquiry.

## Context  
The fellowship emerges amid rapid advances in AI that are already influencing creative workflows, algorithmic authorship and environmental considerations within the art world. It aligns with broader industry trends toward interdisciplinary collaboration between technologists and artists, while also reflecting a growing emphasis on sustainability—evidenced by Serpentine’s partnership with Italian design studio Formafantasma as ecology advisors.

## Implications  
This initiative matters because it institutionalises a dialogue that can shape ethical AI development, inform policy around cultural impact, and embed environmental thinking into artistic practice. By funding artists to conduct rigorous research on AI’s societal effects, the Serpentine aims to influence how emerging technologies are designed, understood and used across creative and public spheres.
