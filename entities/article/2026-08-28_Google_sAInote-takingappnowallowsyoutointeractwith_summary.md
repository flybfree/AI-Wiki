## Summary  
Google’s Gemini Notebook now features an “Expert Intelligence” function that extracts information from the books users have purchased, letting them ask questions and generate related content such as plans or infographics. The feature also includes privacy safeguards so only owners of a title can view its full text, directing others to purchase it if they wish.

## Key Takeaways  
- [Expert Intelligence pulls data directly from purchased books into Gemini Notebook for interactive queries and AI‑generated outputs.]  
- [Non‑purchasers see the book listed as a source but cannot access its content, preserving ownership rights.]  
- [Google plans to extend Expert Intelligence beyond books to scholarly articles, magazines, and newspapers.]

## Context  
This development builds on Gemini Notebook’s broader AI capabilities, allowing users to curate a personal “dream team” of authors whose work is instantly available as a knowledge source. It reflects the industry trend of integrating proprietary data into consumer‑facing AI tools to boost productivity and create new value streams for creators.

## Implications  
The integration opens fresh revenue opportunities for publishers while raising ethical concerns about data ownership, consent, and the potential for surveillance if users share notebooks with others. As AI becomes more capable at synthesizing external knowledge, such features could reshape how individuals and organizations access and monetize intellectual property.
