## Summary
Virginia Governor Abigail Spanberger has issued an executive order and established a "Data Center Accountability Framework" to mitigate the rapid expansion of data centers and the associated risks of artificial intelligence. The initiative aims to give local communities more oversight over development, regulate environmental impacts like noise and energy consumption, and create a dedicated task force to address AI-related harms such as workforce displacement and privacy concerns.

## Key Takeaways
- **Regulatory Restrictions:** Executive Order 22 specifically bans state officials from signing non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) for data center projects and mandates expedited noise regulations and reviews of backup-generation operations.
- **Local Oversight & Subsidies:** The new framework seeks to eliminate "by-right" approvals—which previously allowed developers to build without significant oversight—while also removing some state subsidies and establishing environmental guardrails.
- **AI Task Force:** A newly formed task force will evaluate how the state can mitigate AI risks, specifically focusing on how existing laws can be applied to prevent harm and manage the displacement of workers by automation.
- **Mixed Reactions:** While environmental groups praised the move as a step toward accountability, some organizations argued that the order does not go far enough because it fails to address data centers already constructed or currently in the pipeline.

## Context
This move occurs during a period where state governments are increasingly taking the lead on AI and infrastructure policy due to perceived stagnation at the federal level. The article notes that Virginia is already considered the "data center capital of the world," making it a primary target for regulation as the energy demands of large-scale AI models intensify. Other states, including California, New York, and Texas, have also recently taken executive actions to rein in rapid data center development and address AI safety concerns.

## Implications
For the tech industry, these regulations signal a shift from a "growth-at-all-costs" era to one of localized accountability and environmental scrutiny. As data centers become more power-hungry and community-impacting, developers will likely face longer approval timelines, higher compliance costs, and a loss of "by-right" privileges. This trend suggests that the geographic expansion of AI infrastructure may slow down as states prioritize local energy stability and resident protections over rapid industrial scaling.
