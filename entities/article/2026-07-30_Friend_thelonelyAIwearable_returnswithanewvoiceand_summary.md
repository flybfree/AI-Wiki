## Summary  
Friend, an AI wearable launched by Avi Schiffmann two years ago that sent users text messages about their day, returns with a built‑in speaker capable of delivering a consistent personality voice. The upgraded version costs $249, nearly triple its original price, and the product’s purpose is framed as a confidant rather than a functional assistant.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Friend now includes a speaker that projects a unique AI personality.  
- Price increased from $99 to $249, reflecting premium branding over utility.  
- The device is positioned as a loneliness‑combat companion, not a productivity tool.  

## Context  
Friend competes with other AI wearables such as Humane’s AI pin, which collapsed after poor sales. The broader trend shows many consumer AI products struggle to gain traction due to high price and limited functionality. These challenges echo the failure of other AI‑driven wearables that promised personalization but delivered minimal value.  

## Implications  
The launch underscores the difficulty of monetizing emotional‑support AI devices; it may set a precedent for premium pricing in niche wellness tech but also highlights market skepticism toward AI companionship, potentially influencing future product development. Such skepticism could push developers toward clearer utility metrics, making it harder for purely emotional products to justify high costs.
