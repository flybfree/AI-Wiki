## Summary  
The author argues that many software products appear to function well for developers but collapse under ordinary user use, revealing a “bug blindness” problem where hidden flaws are only noticed by those who scrutinize them. By comparing their own extensive bug‑spotting experience with typical users’ limited perception, the writer demonstrates that severe usability issues—where normal users cannot complete tasks without workarounds—often exist despite internal teams claiming the product is fine.

## Key Takeaways  
- Users rarely encounter the same edge cases that expose critical bugs because they do not reproduce them.  
- Products can be internally “great” while delivering a poor or unusable experience for ordinary users, leading to launch failures.  
- Leveraging AI‑generated user simulations can help surface hidden problems before release.

## Context  
In the broader AI and software development landscape, rapid iteration and automated testing often focus on high‑level functionality rather than real‑world usability. This shift can mask systemic flaws that only become apparent when diverse human behaviors are simulated, a challenge amplified by large language models capable of mimicking typical user interactions.

## Implications  
If companies continue to rely solely on internal expert reviews without incorporating AI‑driven user emulation, they risk deploying products that fail in the market. Addressing bug blindness through systematic cross‑validation with synthetic users is essential for building trustworthy, user‑centric AI systems and maintaining competitive advantage.
