## Summary  
Snapchat announced that its recommendation system will no longer promote videos that are entirely AI‑generated, a move aimed at preserving Spotlight’s reputation for authentic human creativity. While the platform will continue to allow creators to use Snapchat’s own AI tools for editing or enhancement, fully synthetic content will be excluded from recommendations and reward programs.

## Key Takeaways  
- **No full AI videos in Spotlight:** The algorithm will filter out any video that is 100 % AI‑generated, ensuring only human‑made content appears in recommendations.  
- **AI tools remain permissible:** Users can still employ Snapchat’s built‑in AI editing features to improve their own videos, but the final product must contain human input.  
- **Industry alignment:** This policy mirrors recent actions by LinkedIn, Substack, Meta, and YouTube, which are all tightening controls on low‑quality “AI slop.”  

## Context  
The surge of criticism against AI‑generated content—often termed “AI slop”—has prompted a wave of corporate responses. Platforms such as LinkedIn now let users flag suspicious posts, Substack offers AI‑detection tools for newsletters, Meta removed an Instagram AI photo‑modification feature after backlash, and YouTube clarified that monetizable AI content is prohibited. These initiatives collectively reflect a broader industry effort to balance innovation with user trust and platform integrity.

## Implications  
For creators, the new rule may reduce exposure if they rely solely on AI generation, pushing them toward more authentic storytelling; for Snapchat’s algorithm, it strengthens its brand promise of human‑centric content while potentially limiting viral reach for synthetic material. The shift also signals a competitive pressure across social media to differentiate genuine user experiences from machine‑produced output, influencing future platform policies and user expectations.
