## Summary  
Massachusetts has introduced a new executive order that forces data centers larger than 25 megawatts to supply clean power or contribute to a ratepayer protection fund, while also pausing sales‑tax exemptions for developers. The rule requires at least 40% of the center’s electricity by 2030 to come from approved renewable sources such as wind, solar, and hydro.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Data centers >25 MW must generate clean power or fund a state‑run protection fund.  
- Massachusetts mandates a minimum of 40% renewable energy by 2030, with increasing targets each year.  
- The governor is temporarily halting sales‑tax exemptions for new data center projects.  

## Context  
The push reflects a growing trend among U.S. states to curb the environmental impact of large‑scale computing facilities that power artificial intelligence and other high‑energy applications. Earlier this year Texas required audits by its public utility commission, and New York halted construction of 50 MW or larger centers, signaling broader regulatory pressure as climate concerns intensify.  

## Implications  
These regulations could slow the expansion of data‑center capacity in Massachusetts, potentially delaying AI model training and deployment that rely on such infrastructure. At the same time, they incentivize greener technologies and may set a precedent for other states to adopt similar clean‑energy standards, reshaping market dynamics and influencing investor confidence.
