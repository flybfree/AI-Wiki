## Summary  
Rippling, an HR‑software provider that invested heavily in AI tokens early in the year, discovered a runaway spending problem that threatened to consume up to 40 % of its R&D budget. To address this, it launched an “AI Spend Console” that tracks individual employee and team usage, identifies high‑spending engineers, and measures whether their AI activity translates into real productivity gains. The tool was created after a shocking CFO presentation revealed that AI spend could rival 40 % of compensation in the R&D unit.

## Key Takeaways  
- Employees are disproportionately driving AI token consumption, with some spending $50 k per month.  
- Firms must negotiate usage caps and leverage cheaper alternative models like GLM 5.2 to curb costs.  
- Without visibility into ROI, AI budgets risk becoming a “token‑maxxing” liability rather than a strategic asset.

## Context  
The surge in AI adoption across enterprises has led many companies to adopt multiple foundation models from different labs—frontier models for performance and cheaper open‑weight alternatives like SpaceX’s Grok or Databricks’ GLM 5.2—for coding tasks. This fragmentation creates both opportunity and expense, as firms lack a unified way to compare model costs versus output quality.

## Implications  
If AI spend continues unchecked, it can erode profit margins and distract teams from core product development. By providing granular ROI data, tools like Rippling’s console empower organizations to align AI investment with measurable business outcomes, fostering smarter procurement decisions and preventing the “AI‑burn” that threatens long‑term competitiveness in a rapidly evolving market.
