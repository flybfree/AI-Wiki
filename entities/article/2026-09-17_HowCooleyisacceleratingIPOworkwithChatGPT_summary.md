## Summary
Cooley, a prominent international law firm specializing in capital markets and initial public offerings (IPOs), has launched "GO Public," a proprietary AI product built on OpenAI’s ChatGPT Work. The tool utilizes an agentic harness to automate the synthesis of massive amounts of data, allowing the firm to provide more tailored and faster starting points for IPO preparations. By automating the high-volume, repetitive information-gathering tasks, Cooley aims to redirect human expertise toward high-level strategy and decision-making.

## Key Takeaways
- **Agentic Workflow:** Unlike simple chatbots, GO Public uses an "agentic harness" that provides a controlled workflow where AI performs automated steps while lawyers remain in the loop to review and validate the output.
- **Shift from Precedent to Client-Centricity:** Traditionally, legal teams began IPO preparation by adapting precedents from similar companies; GO Public allows Cooley to begin with the specific client’s data and public sources to create a more customized foundation immediately.
- **"Speed to Quality":** The firm emphasizes that the goal isn't just faster production but "speed to quality," where AI handles the "first cut" of data sorting so that human lawyers can focus on high-value areas requiring judgment, market experience, and strategic thinking.
- **Collaborative Development:** Cooley worked closely with OpenAI to combine the firm’s deep subject-matter expertise in capital markets with advanced AI engineering to ensure the tool produces reliable, accurate outcomes for complex legal transactions.

## Context
This development occurs during a period where the legal industry is rapidly exploring how Large Language Models (LLMs) can handle "knowledge work." While many industries have adopted AI for creative or administrative tasks, the legal field—specifically in high-stakes areas like IPOs—requires a higher degree of accuracy and "baked-in" institutional knowledge. Cooley’s initiative represents a move toward "agentic" workflows where the AI acts as a sophisticated assistant capable of navigating complex, multi-step processes rather than just answering isolated questions.

## Implications
For the legal industry, this signifies a shift in the value proposition of law firms from "information processors" to "strategic advisors." As AI becomes more capable of synthesizing thousands of documents and creating initial drafts, the competitive advantage for firms will increasingly rely on their ability to integrate these tools into high-quality workflows. Furthermore, it demonstrates a blueprint for how professional services can maintain quality control—by keeping humans in the loop to validate AI outputs—while still achieving significant gains in efficiency and "time to market" for clients.
