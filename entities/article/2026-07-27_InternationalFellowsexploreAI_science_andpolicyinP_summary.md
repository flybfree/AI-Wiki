## Summary  
The U.S. Army War College International Fellows Class of 2027 spent a week in Pittsburgh learning about artificial intelligence, scientific research, and public policy through visits to the Army Artificial Intelligence Integration Center (AI2C) and Carnegie Mellon University’s Software Engineering Institute.  The program brought together 78 fellows from partner nations, exposing them to expert discussions on how science drives national power, how software engineering underpins AI resilience, and how policy shapes global scientific leadership.

## Key Takeaways  
- Science is presented as an instrument of national power that requires strategic investment in research and development.  
- Software engineering and AI are essential for building resilient military systems and advancing cybersecurity capabilities.  
- International fellowships create a platform for cross‑national exchange, fostering shared knowledge and collaborative problem‑solving.

## Context  
The article highlights the growing integration of AI within U.S. Army doctrine, emphasizing that artificial intelligence is no longer a niche technology but a core component of modern warfare.  Carnegie Mellon University’s institute has been a pioneer in applying software engineering to defense challenges since 1984, and its partnership with the Army’s AI2C illustrates a broader trend toward interdisciplinary collaboration between academia, government, and industry.

## Implications  
This fellowship underscores the need for a unified AI strategy that aligns scientific research, software development, and policy across military units.  It also signals the importance of cultivating global talent pipelines to ensure that cutting‑edge AI capabilities are accessible worldwide, thereby strengthening both national security and international cooperation in the rapidly evolving field of artificial intelligence.
