## Summary
Recent polling data from The New York Times and Siena University reveals that a significant majority of likely voters, specifically 61 percent, oppose the construction of new AI data centers in their communities. Despite this widespread opposition, neither major political party has established a decisive advantage on the issue, as support levels remain relatively split among Trump voters while Harris supporters lean heavily against such developments.

## Key Takeaways
- **Overwhelming Opposition**: A substantial 61 percent of surveyed likely voters oppose AI data center construction, with only 14 percent expressing strong support, indicating deep public resistance to this infrastructure expansion.
- **Primary Concerns**: The main drivers for opposition are environmental and community impacts; 32 percent cited water usage and environmental concerns, while 21 percent focused on the impact on local communities, surpassing general distrust of AI at 18 percent.
- **Low Electoral Priority**: Despite high opposition rates, AI data centers rank below one percent in importance for most voters when deciding their midterm election choices, with economic issues and foreign policy dominating voter priorities instead.

## Context
This poll follows a broader trend of declining public sentiment toward AI infrastructure, including previous NBC News and Gallup polls that highlighted similar resistance to local construction projects. The backlash appears bipartisan but varies by demographic, with younger voters (18-29) showing slightly more engagement with the issue compared to older demographics who prioritize traditional economic concerns like inflation and the state of the economy.

## Implications
The data suggests that while AI developers face significant public headwinds regarding local infrastructure, the issue has not yet become a primary electoral wedge for either political party. However, as environmental regulations tighten and community pushback grows, companies may need to adjust their deployment strategies to address water usage and local community impacts more proactively to mitigate regulatory risks and public relations challenges.
