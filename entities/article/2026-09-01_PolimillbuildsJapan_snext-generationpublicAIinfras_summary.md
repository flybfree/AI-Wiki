## Summary  
Polimill has launched QommonsAI, a generative‑AI platform built on OpenAI’s GPT models that aims to become the shared “public OS” for Japanese municipalities, addressing labor shortages and fragmented workflows. By consolidating standardized data from assembly minutes, welfare records, legal texts and other domains into a searchable knowledge base, QommonsAI delivers consistent, secure AI assistance across 1,050 municipalities and over 550,000 public employees.

## Key Takeaways  
- **Labor‑shortage mitigation:** Generative AI can automate routine tasks in government, freeing staff for higher‑value work.  
- **Cross‑municipality consistency:** A unified data layer and common interface eliminate service gaps that arise from disparate municipal systems.  
- **Security‑usability balance:** QommonsAI retains open‑model flexibility (GPT) while embedding operational controls to meet audit requirements.

## Context  
Japan’s public sector is grappling with a shrinking workforce and the need for digital transformation, prompting municipalities to explore AI solutions that are both efficient and compliant. The broader trend worldwide shows governments adopting generative AI to streamline services, yet many face challenges of data silos and security concerns. Polimill’s approach reflects an emerging model where open‑source models are wrapped in governance‑grade controls.

## Implications  
The success of QommonsAI could inspire other countries to build national AI infrastructures that standardize public data while preserving regulatory oversight. It demonstrates that a “public OS” is technically feasible, encouraging further investment in interoperable government platforms and setting a benchmark for secure, user‑friendly AI deployment in civic administration.
