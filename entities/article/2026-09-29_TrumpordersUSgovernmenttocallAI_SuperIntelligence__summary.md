## Summary
President Donald Trump has signed an executive order mandating that the US government replace the term "artificial intelligence" with "Super Intelligence" in all official policy documents, websites, and press releases. The administration argues that "artificial" implies falsity or weakness, whereas "super" accurately reflects the technology's power and potential for good use. This linguistic shift aims to reframe public perception of AI capabilities while maintaining existing regulatory frameworks through agencies like the Department of Justice and FBI.

## Key Takeaways
- **Official Terminology Change:** The US executive branch will no longer acknowledge the term "artificial intelligence" or "AI" in official settings, strictly using "Super Intelligence" instead. This change applies to new policy documents and press releases but does not require altering past regulations.
- **Rationale for Rebranding:** Trump stated that "super is the best word of all" because it is simple and reflects the technology's brilliance, contrasting it with "artificial," which he equated to "fake news." The move was reportedly supported by Chinese President Xi Jinping during a recent White House visit.
- **Regulatory Stance:** Despite the semantic shift, Trump emphasized that existing government oversight mechanisms remain in place, citing automatic regulation through the Department of Justice and FBI. He also advocated for "tremendous self-regulation" by tech companies, particularly regarding data center construction and community impact.

## Context
This development occurs against a backdrop of growing global debate over AI safety and governance. The article notes that Trump first proposed this rebranding in a speech to the United Nations General Assembly, aiming to alleviate fears about AI advancing too quickly or posing safety risks. "Superintelligence" is typically an industry term for hypothetical, highly advanced AI systems, but the administration is applying it broadly to statutory definitions of current AI technologies. The policy shift was announced alongside the launch of America.gov and followed a luncheon with prominent tech leaders, including Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Tesla/SpaceX CEO Elon Musk.

## Implications
The renaming could create significant confusion in legal, technical, and public discourse by decoupling official government language from established scientific and industry terminology. While it does not change existing laws, it may complicate international cooperation or academic research if US officials refuse to use standard terms like "AI." Furthermore, the emphasis on self-regulation and community support for data centers suggests a strategy of leveraging economic incentives to mitigate local opposition to infrastructure expansion, potentially shaping how future AI hardware deployments are managed within the United States.
