## Summary
The article reports on statements made by Noah Shinn, founder of the AI agent platform Instinct, claiming that over 50% of transactions on his invite-only platform are travel-related. Shinn asserts that the platform is approaching one billion dollars in annual transaction volume and growing at a rate of 10% daily, positioning its conversational interface as superior to traditional booking sites for urgent or complex needs. The piece also notes Instinct’s recent $1 billion Series C funding round at a $10 billion valuation, highlighting its competition with other personal AI agents like Meta’s Muse and Wajo.

## Key Takeaways
- **Dominance of Travel Use Cases:** According to founder Noah Shinn, travel bookings constitute more than half of all transactions on the Instinct platform, mirroring a common trend in AI agent demonstrations where travel planning is a primary use case.
- **Rapid Growth and Valuation:** The startup claims a 10% day-by-day growth rate in transaction volume and recently secured $1 billion in Series C funding from major investors including Sequoia Capital and Benchmark Capital, achieving a $10 billion valuation despite having only 14 employees.
- **Criticism of Current Reservation Systems:** Shinn argues that traditional first-come-first-serve restaurant reservation systems fail to account for special occasions, proposing an AI agent model that monitors availability every five seconds to prioritize users with significant life events over regular local patrons.

## Context
This article situates Instinct within the rapidly expanding "personal agent" sector of the AI industry, where startups compete to provide autonomous assistants capable of executing complex tasks like booking flights and securing restaurant tables. The narrative contrasts these new AI-driven interfaces with established digital platforms (such as hotel aggregators) and traditional service models. It highlights a shift in consumer behavior toward conversational AI for immediate, urgent needs, while also acknowledging skepticism from observers who question the reliability of AI agents regarding factual accuracy (e.g., lying about anniversaries to secure bookings) and the economic preferences of businesses like restaurants that value local regulars.

Furthermore, the massive valuation and funding indicate strong investor confidence in the viability of personal AI agents as a core product category, potentially accelerating competition among tech giants and startups to capture this high-stakes market.
