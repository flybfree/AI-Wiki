## Summary  
OpenAI emphasizes its ongoing commitment to responsible AI in Europe, aligning its governance practices with the EU AI Act and two new Codes of Practice on General‑Purpose AI (GPAI) and transparency. The company outlines how it tests models, publishes system cards, uses red‑team exercises, and maintains transparent model specifications, while also building internal frameworks such as a Preparedness Framework and Frontier Governance Framework to manage risks and comply with emerging legal requirements.

## Key Takeaways  
- OpenAI has strengthened its responsible AI approach by contributing to the EU’s GPAI Code of Practice and the transparency code for AI‑generated content.  
- The company employs rigorous testing, red‑team collaboration, system cards, and a public Model Spec to ensure safety, security, and transparency.  
- Internal frameworks like the Preparedness Framework and Frontier Governance Framework translate these principles into concrete risk assessments, reporting, and incident response processes.

## Context  
The EU AI Act is entering its implementation phase, requiring high‑risk AI systems to meet strict governance standards. Europe’s regulatory landscape is pushing tech firms to adopt transparent, risk‑based frameworks that balance innovation with public safety. OpenAI’s actions reflect broader industry trends toward collaborative standards and proactive risk management.

## Implications  
For the field of AI, these practices set a benchmark for how large models can be responsibly developed and deployed in regulated markets. They encourage other companies to adopt similar governance structures, fostering trust among users, regulators, and policymakers while supporting continued innovation across Europe’s AI ecosystem.
