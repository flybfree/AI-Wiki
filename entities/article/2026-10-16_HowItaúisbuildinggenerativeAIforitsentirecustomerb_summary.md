## Summary
Itaú Unibanco, a major Brazilian financial institution, is leveraging OpenAI’s generative AI models to create a unified, personalized conversational interface called "ia.i" for its 70 million customers. The initiative focuses on making technology invisible by solving real financial problems through simple, direct interactions across text, voice, and other modalities. By utilizing a diverse range of OpenAI models tailored for different complexity levels, Itaú aims to act as a financial copilot, helping customers organize their finances and make better decisions.

## Key Takeaways
- **Technology as Invisible Infrastructure:** Itaú’s CTO emphasizes that the goal is not to showcase AI capabilities but to make the technology disappear by delivering simple, direct, and useful solutions that solve everyday financial problems. The user experience must be seamless, with AI serving as a background enabler rather than the centerpiece.
- **Multi-Model Strategy for Scale and Efficiency:** Itaú chose OpenAI’s models because they offer a spectrum of capabilities, from low-latency, cost-efficient models for simple queries to complex reasoning models for grounded, high-stakes financial advice. This allows the bank to handle a wide range of customer interactions within a single product architecture.
- **Personalization through "Customer DNA":** The system relies on a proprietary layer called "Customer DNA," which aggregates financial context, habits, and life stages of approximately 70 million customers. Governed by responsible AI controls, this data layer enables GPT models to provide progressively more relevant and individualized interactions, moving beyond generic advice to tailored financial guidance.

## Context
This case study highlights a significant shift in the financial services industry toward "agentic" AI assistants that act as personal financial copilots. With 80% of Brazilians expressing a desire for better financial organization and 65% seeking AI-driven guidance, Itaú is responding to a clear market demand for accessible financial literacy tools. The integration of multimodal interfaces (text, voice, image) reflects a broader industry trend toward making AI accessible to non-technical users, particularly in emerging markets where smartphone penetration is high but financial literacy may be lower. The use of OpenAI’s ecosystem underscores the growing reliance on large language model providers to power enterprise-scale customer service transformations.

## Implications
Itaú’s approach demonstrates how large enterprises can successfully deploy generative AI at scale by prioritizing user-centric design over technical complexity. For the AI industry, this validates the effectiveness of using a portfolio of models rather than a single monolithic model, allowing for optimized cost-performance trade-offs in production environments. Furthermore, the emphasis on "responsible AI" and governance layers like "Customer DNA" suggests that future AI deployments in regulated industries will require robust data governance frameworks to ensure personalization does not compromise privacy or fairness. This case serves as a blueprint for other banks and service providers looking to transition from transactional digital banking to advisory, AI-driven financial companionship.
