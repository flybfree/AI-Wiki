## Summary  
During a House Science Committee hearing, Rep. Jim Baird pressed White House Office of Science and Technology Policy Director Michael Kratsios on how the federal government should allocate resources to AI research. The White House responded by issuing a memo that directs all agencies—including HHS and NIH—to prioritize AI alongside foundational biological sciences in FY2028 R&D budgets, with plans due within 90 days. This directive signals a formal national strategy to embed AI into the core of federal science funding.

## Key Takeaways  
- The White House has mandated that every federal department align its 2028 research budget around nine priority areas, explicitly including artificial intelligence.  
- HHS and NIH are required to integrate AI priorities into their existing scientific roadmaps, creating a cross‑agency coordination mechanism.  
- The decision is timed for implementation within the next quarter, underscoring urgency in shaping future federal AI investment.

## Context  
The broader AI landscape is rapidly evolving, with private sector breakthroughs in machine learning, natural language processing, and generative models driving both commercial and scientific applications. Federal funding traditionally emphasizes foundational biology to support health‑related research; however, the inclusion of AI reflects a shift toward interdisciplinary innovation that can accelerate drug discovery, diagnostics, and public‑health interventions.

## Implications  
This policy change will reshape how federal agencies prioritize R&D, potentially unlocking new collaborations between health sciences and artificial intelligence. For industry stakeholders, it signals increased government backing for AI tools that could be leveraged in clinical research, suggesting a more favorable regulatory and funding environment.
