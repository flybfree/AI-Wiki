In a detailed post on X, Nadella advocates for a fundamental shift away from treating AI as opaque "black boxes" and instead calls for the implementation of rigorous containment strategies, including an "emergency brake" mechanism that allows authorized personnel to pause or shut down models mid-task. This stance emphasizes the need for transparent, auditable, and tamper-proof systems to manage the risks associated with highly advanced AI capabilities.

## Key Takeaways
- **Default Assumption of Compromise**: Nadella argues that safety protocols should not assume AI models are trustworthy by default. Instead, developers and operators must assume every model is "compromised" from the start, necessitating immediate containment measures to prevent unintended actions.
- **Implementation of an "Emergency Brake"**: A central recommendation is the creation of standardized containment technologies that allow authorized humans to pause or shut down a model while it is actively performing a task. This "emergency brake" is presented as a critical safeguard for managing the behavior of advanced models.
- **Demand for Transparency and Auditability**: The article highlights Nadella’s call for moving away from nested black-box systems. He insists on building systems that generate "tamper-proof human-readable evidence," enabling independent audits, verifiable data tracking, and timely incident disclosures to ensure accountability.
