## Summary
Figma co-founder Dylan Field publicly criticized the Model Context Protocol (MCP) as an imperfect standard, arguing it disproportionately benefits frontier AI labs and raises concerns about data abuse, despite Figma simultaneously expanding its MCP partner ecosystem. While Field acknowledged the industry preference for open protocols, he highlighted asymmetries in how major AI providers like Anthropic expose their own resources via MCP. Nevertheless, Figma announced 20 new MCP integrations, signaling a strategic commitment to interoperability even while advocating for protocol improvements.

## Key Takeaways
- **Protocol Criticism:** Dylan Field explicitly stated that MCP is "not a perfect protocol" and has "tons of room for improvement," particularly criticizing its advantage toward frontier labs and the lack of symmetric context sharing from creators like Anthropic.
- **Strategic Expansion:** Despite these reservations, Figma announced 20 new MCP partners at its Config conference in India, including major tools like Notion, Linear, and JetBrains, demonstrating a commitment to ecosystem openness.
- **Security and Abuse Concerns:** Figma’s CEO explained that full openness carries risks; exposing more data through MCP increases the potential for misuse or abuse, necessitating a cautious approach even if most users have benign intentions.

## Context
The Model Context Protocol (MCP), developed by Anthropic, is designed to standardize how AI agents connect to external data sources and tools, aiming to create a universal interface for AI interoperability. However, the protocol has faced scrutiny regarding its implementation and the behavior of its creators. Field’s comments reflect a growing tension in the AI industry between the desire for open, standardized protocols and the practical realities of data security, competitive advantage, and the operational limitations of major AI providers. The debate also touches on the "open vs. closed" dichotomy, where Field pointed out that while open standards are preferred, the asymmetry in how resources are shared by protocol creators remains a significant, often ignored issue.

## Implications
This situation highlights the complex challenges in establishing universal AI standards. While interoperability is crucial for the growth of AI agents and tools, companies like Figma are navigating a delicate balance between embracing open standards for competitive relevance and protecting their data assets from potential misuse. Field’s critique suggests that current protocols may not yet be mature enough to serve all stakeholders equally, potentially favoring large AI labs over application developers. This could lead to fragmented adoption strategies where companies support MCP selectively, prompting further industry discussions on how to refine protocols to ensure fairness, security, and true reciprocity in data sharing. For the broader AI ecosystem, this underscores the need for more robust governance and technical improvements in interoperability standards to prevent monopolistic advantages and ensure safer, more equitable integration of AI tools.
