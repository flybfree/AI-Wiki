## Summary
OpenAI has released a new dataset through its Economic Research division, offering researchers detailed insights into how consumers globally use ChatGPT for professional tasks. The data maps user interactions to 165 Intermediate Work Activities (IWAs) across nearly 100 countries from Q1 2025 to Q3 2026, revealing a trend where work-related usage is becoming less concentrated in top activities and more specialized by country over time. This release aims to provide a standardized framework for studying AI’s evolving role in the global economy and labor market.

## Key Takeaways
- **Global Work Activity Mapping:** OpenAI has categorized ChatGPT usage into 165 Intermediate Work Activities (IWAs) based on the O*NET framework, allowing researchers to analyze specific tasks like programming, marketing content development, and business data analysis across nearly 100 countries.
- **Increasing Specialization Over Time:** Data from Q1 2025 to Q3 2026 shows a decrease in concentration of work messages among the top ten most common activities, dropping from an average of 56.0% to 51.1%. This indicates that users are increasingly utilizing ChatGPT for a wider variety of specialized tasks rather than relying on a few dominant use cases.
- **Discrepancy with Traditional Labor Data:** Comparisons between ChatGPT usage and actual U.S. job activities reveal significant differences, largely because 43% of employment-weighted tasks in mapped U.S. occupations fall into "Other IWAs" (such as manual labor or vehicle operation) that are not represented in the ChatGPT sample, highlighting a gap between AI-assisted cognitive work and physical or routine labor.

## Context
This release is part of OpenAI’s broader initiative to provide transparency and empirical data regarding the societal impact of large language models. By leveraging O*NET, a standardized occupational framework used by the U.S. Department of Labor, OpenAI aligns its proprietary usage data with established economic metrics. This move reflects a growing industry demand for rigorous, third-party-verifiable data on AI adoption rates, moving beyond anecdotal evidence to quantitative analysis that can inform policy and academic research on labor displacement and augmentation.

## Implications
The availability of this granular, longitudinal dataset is significant for economists, policymakers, and business leaders seeking to understand the precise mechanisms through which AI influences productivity and job structures. It enables researchers to track how AI adoption varies by region and industry, potentially identifying sectors where AI serves as a complement rather than a substitute for human labor. Furthermore, the observed shift toward specialized work activities suggests that AI tools are becoming more integrated into complex professional workflows, necessitating updated strategies for workforce training and economic planning to address both the augmentation of cognitive tasks and the persistent exclusion of physical or routine manual labor from current AI capabilities.
