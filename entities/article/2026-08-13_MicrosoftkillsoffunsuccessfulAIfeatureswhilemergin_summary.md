## Summary  
Microsoft is consolidating its two Copilot apps—one for consumers and one for Microsoft 365 users—by phasing out several under‑performing AI features such as Group Chats, AI‑generated podcasts, Copilot Labs experiments, Deep Research, the animated character Mico, and the standalone Copilot app. The move aims to create a simpler, more cohesive experience while acknowledging that some of its earlier initiatives failed to earn user loyalty.

## Key Takeaways  
- Microsoft will retire Group Chats, AI‑generated podcasts, Copilot Labs experimental features, Deep Research, Mico, and the standalone Copilot app by August 18, 2026.  
- Professional users will receive a replacement for Deep Research through the “Researcher” feature within Microsoft 365 Copilot.  
- Files generated by the old Copilot app will be migrated to OneDrive during the transition.

## Context  
The consolidation follows a wave of similar AI‑app mergers across the industry, including Anthropic’s integration of Cowork into ChatGPT and Google’s addition of deep research capabilities to Gemini. These consolidations reflect a broader trend where companies are streamlining fragmented AI experiences to compete with more unified competitors like ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini.

## Implications  
For developers and users alike, the consolidation signals that Microsoft views long‑term success in AI as dependent on cohesive, high‑impact features rather than experimental or niche tools. It also underscores the competitive pressure to deliver reliable, user‑centric AI experiences, potentially accelerating investment in core Copilot capabilities while marginalizing less effective experiments.
