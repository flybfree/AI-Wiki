## Summary
Recent polling data from The New York Times and Siena University reveals that a significant majority of likely voters, specifically 61 percent, oppose the construction of new AI data centers in their local communities. This widespread opposition is driven primarily by concerns regarding environmental impact, water usage, and general distrust of artificial intelligence technology. Despite this strong sentiment against infrastructure development, AI remains a low-priority issue for most voters when determining their midterm election choices.

## Key Takeaways
- **Overwhelming Opposition:** A substantial 61 percent of surveyed likely voters expressed opposition to building data centers, with only 14 percent strongly supporting such projects. Among those opposed, the majority favored regulatory limits rather than a total ban.
- **Primary Concerns:** The leading reasons for this backlash include environmental and water usage issues (cited by 32 percent), negative impacts on local communities (21 percent), and general distrust or dislike of AI technology (18 percent).
- **Political Neutrality on Issue:** While opposition is high across the board, it does not strictly follow party lines. Trump voters were nearly split (49% support vs. 45% oppose), whereas Harris supporters leaned more heavily toward opposition (74%). Furthermore, AI ranked below one percent in importance for most demographics compared to the economy and inflation.

## Context
This article highlights a growing disconnect between the rapid expansion of AI infrastructure and public sentiment. Previous polls from NBC News and Gallup have already indicated low approval ratings for AI-related projects, suggesting this is not an isolated incident but part of a broader trend. The backlash mirrors similar community resistance seen in other tech-heavy regions like Loudoun County, Virginia, where data center density has sparked local protests.

## Implications
The intense public opposition poses significant challenges for the AI industry's growth trajectory. As companies rush to build massive data centers to support large language models and generative AI tools, they face increasing regulatory hurdles and community pushback. This suggests that future projects may require more transparent engagement with local communities regarding resource usage, particularly water consumption. If voters continue to view AI infrastructure as a negative force, it could slow down the deployment of necessary computing power, potentially affecting the pace of technological innovation and economic development in the sector.
