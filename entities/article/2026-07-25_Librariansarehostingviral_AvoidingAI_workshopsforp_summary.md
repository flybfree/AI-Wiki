## Summary  
Librarians across the United States are organizing “Avoiding AI” workshops that teach participants how to disable Apple Intelligence, Gemini and other consumer‑AI features. The sessions have gone viral, drawing crowds far beyond typical library class sizes and reflecting a growing public backlash against Big Tech’s push for mandatory AI adoption.

## Key Takeaways  
- Librarians are leading grassroots digital‑literacy workshops that empower users to control their own AI usage rather than being forced into it.  
- The “Avoiding AI” events have attracted thousands of virtual attendees, demonstrating a strong community demand for practical guidance on disengaging from AI tools.  
- These workshops address widespread frustration with AI features that appear intrusive, such as automatic summarization of short messages and email rewrites.

## Context  
The rapid rollout of AI assistants across smartphones, laptops and cloud services has created a perception that using these tools is unavoidable. Media hype amplifies the sense that AI is an integral part of daily life, prompting many users to feel pressured rather than informed about their options.

## Implications  
For libraries and information professionals, this grassroots model shows how community‑based education can counterbalance corporate influence in technology adoption. It also signals a broader societal need for transparent digital‑rights discourse, potentially inspiring similar initiatives beyond the library sector.
