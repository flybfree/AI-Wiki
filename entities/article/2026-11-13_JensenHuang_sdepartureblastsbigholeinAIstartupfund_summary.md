## Summary
Jensen Huang’s resignation as CEO of Nvidia after 33 years has created a significant funding vacuum for AI startups, particularly those in the neocloud and open-source sectors. Investors and founders report that Huang personally drove aggressive capital deployment into these companies, acting as a primary champion for the ecosystem. His departure raises serious concerns about the sustainability of current valuation models and the availability of capital for early-stage AI ventures under new leadership.

## Key Takeaways
- **Personalized Investment Strategy:** Most significant investment decisions were made directly by Jensen Huang, meaning his absence disrupts the decision-making pipeline that favored high-risk, high-reward bets in neoclouds and open-weight models.
- **Shift to Conservatism:** Interim CEO Colette Kress is expected to adopt a more conservative financial approach, likely reducing the massive capital injections that previously sustained startups like CoreWeave, Crusoe, and Reflection AI.
- **Ecosystem Dependency:** Nvidia’s strategy of providing large checks in exchange for chip usage created a quasi-monopoly effect; without this support, smaller competitors may gain traction, but many existing startups face an immediate liquidity crisis.

## Context
Nvidia has been the dominant force in the AI hardware market, leveraging its financial power to shape the software and infrastructure landscape. Over recent years, the company participated in 105 investment rounds annually, often as the lead investor. This strategy was not merely financial but strategic, ensuring startups remained locked into Nvidia’s hardware ecosystem. The article highlights a critical dependency within the AI industry: many "AI-native" startups rely on Nvidia’s capital to bridge the gap between development and revenue generation, particularly in expensive sectors like data center build-outs and open-source model training.

## Implications
The departure of Jensen Huang signals a potential structural shift in the AI startup funding landscape. The immediate implication is a "funding hole" that could lead to lower valuations, increased consolidation, or the failure of startups that relied on Nvidia’s patronage rather than organic revenue growth. Furthermore, this change may benefit rival hardware providers like AMD or Intel by reducing Nvidia’s ability to lock customers into its ecosystem through financial incentives. For the broader industry, it underscores the fragility of venture capital ecosystems heavily dependent on a single corporate sponsor, potentially slowing innovation in open-source and European AI sectors that Huang specifically championed.
