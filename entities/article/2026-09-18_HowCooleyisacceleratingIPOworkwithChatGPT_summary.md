## Summary
Cooley, a prominent international law firm specializing in capital markets and initial public offerings (IPOs), has launched "GO Public," a proprietary AI product built on OpenAI's ChatGPT Work. The tool utilizes an agentic harness to automate the synthesis of massive amounts of data, allowing the firm to provide more tailored starting points for IPO preparations. By automating the preliminary "first cut" of information gathering and sorting, Cooley aims to shift human effort toward high-level strategic decision-making and judgment.

## Key Takeaways
- **Agentic Workflow:** Unlike simple chatbots, GO Public uses an agentic harness that provides a controlled workflow where AI performs automated steps while lawyers remain in the loop to review and validate the output.
- **Shift from Precedent to Client-Centricity:** Traditionally, legal teams began IPO preparations by adapting precedents from similar companies; GO Public allows Cooley to start with the specific client's data and curated precedents to create a more customized foundation.
- **"Speed to Quality":** The firm emphasizes that the goal isn't just faster production but "speed to quality," where AI handles the labor-intensive sorting of information so that human expertise can be concentrated on the most impactful, high-value aspects of a transaction.
- **Collaborative Development:** The tool was created through a close partnership between Cooley’s legal engineers and OpenAI to ensure that the firm's decades of "baked-in" capital markets know-how were accurately translated into an AI framework.

## Context
This development occurs during a period where the legal industry is rapidly exploring how Large Language Models (LLMs) can handle high-volume, document-heavy tasks. As AI becomes more capable of reasoning and synthesis, law firms are moving beyond simple "search" tools toward "agentic" systems that can perform multi-step workflows with minimal human intervention.

## Implications
This represents a significant shift in the professional services model from "hours billed for manual labor" to "value-added consulting." By automating the initial data synthesis—which is often the most time-consuming part of an IPO—Cooley is redefining how law firms compete. It suggests a future where the competitive advantage of a firm lies in its ability to train and refine proprietary AI models that reflect their specific expertise, rather than just the sheer volume of manual hours their staff can provide.
