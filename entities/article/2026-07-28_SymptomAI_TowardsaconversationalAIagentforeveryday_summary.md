## Summary  
SymptomAI is a conversational AI agent that conducts end‑to‑end symptom interviews and differential diagnostic assessments, evaluated in a randomized national study of 13,917 participants. The research compares the AI’s diagnoses with those of real clinicians and physiological data from Fitbit wearables to gauge performance across everyday clinical scenarios.

## Key Takeaways  
- SymptomAI can generate differential diagnoses that align closely with human clinician assessments in a large‑scale, real‑world setting.  
- Conversations leading to infectious‑disease diagnoses coincide with measurable immune‑response trends captured by Fitbit biosignals, suggesting AI can detect early physiological cues.  
- The study highlights the importance of using naturalistic patient language and varying medical literacy rather than only curated synthetic vignettes for reliable performance.

## Context  
Current large language models excel on curated medical case studies but often fail to reflect how patients actually describe symptoms—complete with incomplete information, varying literacy levels, and unscripted dialogue. This gap limits the practical deployment of AI in everyday symptom checking, where accessibility and real‑world variability are crucial. SymptomAI addresses this by operating within a naturalistic conversation flow rather than relying on synthetic data.

## Implications  
The findings demonstrate that conversational AI can serve as a viable auxiliary tool for early symptom triage, potentially expanding access to diagnostic support in underserved areas. By integrating physiological signals, the approach could enable earlier detection of conditions such as infections, prompting timely medical follow‑up and improving health outcomes while respecting ethical boundaries by not replacing clinician judgment.
