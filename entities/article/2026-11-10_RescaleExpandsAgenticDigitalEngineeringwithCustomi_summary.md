## Summary
Rescale has introduced new agentic digital engineering capabilities designed to allow R&D teams in advanced industries to customize AI agents for complex engineering workflows. By providing a structured framework known as an "engineering-specific agentic harness," the platform ensures that AI-driven tasks remain reviewable, traceable, and aligned with strict industry standards. This expansion enables organizations to move from automating individual tasks to managing end-to-end engineering processes while maintaining human oversight and preserving institutional knowledge.

## Key Takeaways
- **Engineering-Specific Agentic Harness:** Rescale’s new "Agentic Projects" provide a structured framework that allows engineers to customize AI agents with specific contexts, skills, tools, and guardrails. This ensures agents operate within defined boundaries and meet the rigorous standards required for high-stakes engineering work, such as aerospace or nuclear design.
- **Traceability and Knowledge Preservation:** The platform generates project-specific "work graphs" that provide full traceability of agent actions and capture critical learnings. These learnings are stored in "project memory," allowing teams to share approved insights across departments, build domain expertise, and drive continuous process improvements without losing institutional knowledge.
- **Enhanced Interoperability and Local Access:** Rescale introduced "Rescale Desktop" to extend platform capabilities to engineers’ local machines and expanded Model Context Protocol (MCP) connectors for better interoperability with external tools like Claude and Codex. Additionally, the AI assistant "Rescale Edison" now features persistent memory, enabling faster workflows through natural language intent descriptions.

## Context
This development occurs as engineering organizations increasingly integrate AI agents into production environments, shifting focus from isolated task automation to comprehensive process orchestration. The article highlights a critical industry challenge: generic AI tools often lack the specific context and compliance structures needed for regulated industries like nuclear power and aerospace. By partnering with major entities such as Kairos Power and Oak Ridge National Laboratory (ORNL), Rescale demonstrates the practical application of these tools in real-world scenarios, specifically for designing advanced reactors like the Hermes 2 fluoride-salt-cooled reactor. This reflects a broader industry trend toward "agentic scaffolding," where AI is not just a tool but a structured component of the engineering lifecycle, requiring robust governance and traceability mechanisms to be viable in safety-critical sectors.

## Implications
The introduction of customizable agentic workflows with built-in guardrails signifies a maturation of AI in industrial R&D, moving beyond experimental pilots to trusted production tools. For industries dealing with high-consequence outcomes, such as nuclear energy and aerospace, the ability to ensure every AI-generated result is reviewable and traceable is essential for regulatory compliance and safety assurance. This approach addresses the "trust gap" that has historically hindered AI adoption in engineering by providing a transparent audit trail and preserving human-in-the-loop oversight. Furthermore, by enabling knowledge sharing through project memory and improving interoperability via MCP standards, Rescale facilitates scalable innovation across distributed teams. This could accelerate the development of complex systems by reducing repetitive simulation tasks and capturing tacit engineering knowledge, ultimately making advanced manufacturing more efficient and accessible to a wider range of R&D organizations.
