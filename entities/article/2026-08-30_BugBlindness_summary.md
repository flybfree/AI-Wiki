## Summary  
The article argues that many people miss bugs because they experience the same issues repeatedly, creating a perception of “bug blindness.” The author demonstrates that severe product flaws are often hidden from users and executives, yet he personally observes them, and LLMs can simulate normal user behavior to reproduce these problems. He suggests that curing this blindness is beneficial for both programmers and non‑programmers.

## Key Takeaways  
- People tend to overlook bugs because they encounter the same problems repeatedly, creating a false sense of normalcy.  
- Severe usability failures are often hidden from users despite internal confidence in product quality.  
- LLMs can replicate user behavior to expose hidden issues that affect real‑world experience.

## Context  
The phenomenon reflects broader challenges in software development where product teams prioritize perceived functionality over actual user experience. In AI and tech industries, rapid iteration can mask defects that only surface under specific conditions, leading to poor adoption rates and diminished trust.

## Implications  
If these blind spots persist, products may launch with critical flaws that alienate users, damage brand reputation, and increase support costs. Addressing bug blindness through systematic testing and human‑in‑the‑loop validation is essential for trustworthy AI systems and user‑centric design.
