## Summary  
SymptomAI is a conversational AI agent built on Gemini Flash 2.0 that conducts end‑to‑end symptom interviews and differential diagnosis for 13,917 consented participants in a randomized national study. The research compares the AI’s diagnostic output to clinicians’ assessments and correlates its results with wearable biosignal data, showing that AI diagnoses of infectious diseases align with physiological trends indicating an immune response.

## Key Takeaways  
- Conversational AI can achieve clinical‑level differential diagnosis performance in real‑world settings, not just on curated vignettes.  
- The AI’s diagnostic accuracy is linked to observable physiological signals from Fitbit wearables, suggesting a bridge between language and bodily health data.  
- Existing LM evaluations miss the variability of everyday patient communication, such as incomplete information or varying medical literacy.

## Context  
Current large‑language models excel on synthetic medical case studies but often fail to model natural, unstructured patient dialogue where symptoms are reported incompletely or with lay terminology. Wearable biosensors provide continuous physiological context that can augment language‑based assessments, yet few systems integrate these signals into diagnostic workflows.

## Implications  
This study demonstrates that conversational AI agents may reduce barriers to symptom assessment by offering 24/7, low‑cost screening tools, potentially expanding access to care. It also underscores the need for rigorous real‑world validation and integration of multimodal data before deploying such systems in clinical practice or public health initiatives.
