## Summary
The article introduces "Fugleramme," an open-source project that utilizes a Raspberry Pi and an e-ink display to create a real-time bird identification frame. By leveraging local AI audio classification via BirdNET-Go, the system detects avian species in its vicinity and renders them as hand-curated, 19th-century natural history illustrations on the screen. This innovative device serves as both a functional garden monitor and an artistic showcase of public-domain artwork, emphasizing privacy through fully local processing.

## Key Takeaways
- The system employs BirdNET-Go for real-time audio classification, ensuring that all data processing occurs locally without sending sensitive audio feeds to external cloud servers.
- Visual output consists of over 800 hand-curated, non-AI-generated illustrations from the 1800s, which are dynamically matched and packed onto a textured digital page based on detected species and body mass.
- The hardware is modular; while an Inky Impression e-ink panel is recommended for the intended low-power aesthetic, the software can operate as a standard web kiosk on any HDMI-connected display or network device.

## Context
This project sits at the intersection of edge computing, acoustic ecology, and digital art preservation. It reflects a growing industry trend toward "local-first" AI applications that prioritize user privacy and reduce latency by processing data on-device rather than relying on cloud-based inference engines. Furthermore, it highlights the integration of historical public-domain assets with modern machine learning models, demonstrating how traditional scientific illustration can be revitalized through contemporary technology.

## Implications
Fugleramme demonstrates the viability of lightweight AI models running efficiently on low-power hardware like the Raspberry Pi, suggesting broader applications for environmental monitoring in remote or resource-constrained areas. By combining real-time detection with aesthetic presentation, it offers a new paradigm for citizen science engagement, making ecological data visually appealing and accessible. The project also underscores the importance of maintaining high-quality, non-AI-generated artistic archives as valuable resources for training and visualizing AI outputs, ensuring that technological advancement does not entirely displace traditional craftsmanship and historical documentation.
