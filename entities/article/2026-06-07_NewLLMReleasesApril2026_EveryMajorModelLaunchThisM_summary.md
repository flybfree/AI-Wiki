## Summary  
April 2026’s AI landscape is dominated by two flagship large‑language models—OpenAI’s GPT‑5.5 and Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.7—each offering strong verification scores on benchmark suites despite modest parameter counts. While OpenAI raised its per‑token pricing, Anthropic kept its model affordable, creating a clear market shift toward cost‑effective, high‑performing APIs. Meanwhile, open‑source alternatives such as Google’s Gemma 4 and Zhipu AI’s GLM‑5.1 remain free but are constrained by shorter context windows.

## Key Takeaways  
- GPT‑5.5 (Spud) and Claude Opus 4.7 hold the top public‑API rankings with SWE‑Bench verification rates above 87 %.  
- OpenAI’s pricing increased to $30 per million tokens, whereas Anthropic maintained lower costs at $25 out per token, altering competitive dynamics.  
- Free open‑source models like Gemma 4 and GLM‑5.1 are available but limited by context length (≤256 K tokens).

## Context  
In early 2026, the AI community saw a surge of model launches aimed at capturing market share and improving benchmark performance. OpenAI’s GPT‑5.5 was released on April 23 after being delayed from the original April 14 date, while Anthropic introduced Claude Opus 4.7 on April 16. Google unveiled Gemma 4 series models with Apache‑licensed weights, and Zhipu AI announced GLM‑5.1, a 744B MoE model released under MIT. These releases reflect a trend of incremental upgrades rather than radical architectural leaps.

## Implications  
The price divergence between proprietary giants and open‑source projects will influence developer adoption: enterprises may favor Anthropic’s cheaper API for cost‑sensitive workloads, while researchers continue to rely on free models despite context limits. As the April 30 retirement of 1M‑token beta Sonnet 4.5/4.6 proceeds, migration to Opus 4.7 and GPT‑5.5 will become standard practice, accelerating the shift toward higher‑capacity, lower‑cost deployment pipelines.
