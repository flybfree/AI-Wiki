## Summary  
The article argues that many engineers deliberately avoid confronting the fundamentals of their disciplines, preferring to treat emerging technologies—especially AI—as novel leadership challenges rather than as extensions of well‑established practices. This avoidance fuels a cycle of repeated mistakes across fields such as data science, cryptocurrency, and software engineering, ultimately undermining the promise of AI integration.

## Key Takeaways  
- Engineers often distrust institutional knowledge that predates modern software, seeking to appear cutting‑edge instead of mastering existing principles.  
- The pursuit of novelty can lead them to reinvent familiar concepts (e.g., “reinvented bus stops”), wasting time and resources.  
- This mindset blinds engineers to the value of disciplined fundamentals like TDD or PRDs, causing misaligned expectations when working with AI agents.

## Context  
The broader AI landscape is increasingly portrayed as a leadership problem rather than an engineering one; companies tout “AI‑driven transformation” while neglecting the technical rigor required. The article situates this trend within a pattern where each new domain (data science, crypto, etc.) is treated as a fresh frontier, echoing the classic “always has been” meme that discourages learning from past experiences.

## Implications  
If engineers continue to sidestep historical lessons, AI projects will remain superficial and prone to failure. The field risks perpetuating costly redundancies, eroding trust in both AI tools and engineering teams, and missing opportunities where deep understanding could yield genuine innovation.
