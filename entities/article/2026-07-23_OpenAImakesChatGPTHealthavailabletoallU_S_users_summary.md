## Summary  
OpenAI is expanding its ChatGPT Health feature to all U.S.-based users over 18 on every plan, allowing the model to draw insights from connected health data such as Apple Health, MyFitnessPal, and hospital records. The rollout also moves health‑related queries beyond a dedicated hub, while OpenAI reiterates that its AI is not intended for diagnosis or treatment.

## Key Takeaways  
- **Universal access:** ChatGPT Health is now available to all U.S. users on web and iOS across free, Go, Plus, and Pro plans.  
- **Model performance:** The smallest GPT 5.6‑Luna model outperforms the previous GPT 5.5 version on OpenAI’s open‑source HealthBench benchmark.  
- **Safety disclaimer:** OpenAI maintains that its services are “not intended for use in the diagnosis or treatment of any health condition” and does not use user data to train models.

## Context  
The broader AI landscape is seeing a wave of health‑focused features from competitors such as Anthropic and Google, which also aim to integrate personal health information into conversational agents. However, many studies have warned that AI bots are unreliable for medical advice, creating a tension between innovation and patient safety.

## Implications  
This rollout could normalize the use of AI in wellness tools, potentially increasing user engagement with health‑related queries. At the same time, it raises ethical concerns about misinformation, liability, and the responsibility to ensure that users verify any health information before acting on it.
