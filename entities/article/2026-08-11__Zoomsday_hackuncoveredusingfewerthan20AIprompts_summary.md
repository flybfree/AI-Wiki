## Summary  
The “Zoomsday” hack reveals that a critical Zoom vulnerability can be uncovered and weaponized using fewer than 20 prompts on publicly available AI models, allowing attackers to hijack any participant’s device during a call without prompting the user. Researchers at A Security demonstrated this exploit in a single day with an AI agent, showing how rapidly accessible AI tools can accelerate traditional nation‑state‑level attacks.  

## Key Takeaways  
- The attack leverages Zoom’s annotation feature and requires only <20 prompts to generate a functional exploit.  
- Researchers achieved the exploit within 24 hours using an AI agent and models that are freely accessible, bypassing the need for months‑long nation‑state resources.  
- The compromise is stealthy: victims see no visual cue or error indicating their device has been taken over.  

## Context  
This incident highlights a broader trend in artificial intelligence research where generative AI agents can automate security testing and vulnerability discovery. Previously, such work was considered the domain of elite, state‑backed teams with limited resources; now anyone with internet access can run sophisticated attacks at scale. The democratization of AI tools is reshaping threat landscapes, making rapid exploitation a realistic possibility for both malicious actors and defensive researchers alike.  

## Implications  
The speed of discovery via few prompts underscores the need for continuous patching cycles and proactive monitoring in cloud‑based communication platforms. As AI can now be employed to find and exploit flaws faster than human analysts, organizations must adopt AI‑augmented security practices that include automated vulnerability scanning and rapid response protocols. Moreover, the ease with which such attacks can be executed without user interaction raises concerns about privacy erosion and the potential for large‑scale data breaches across enterprise environments.
