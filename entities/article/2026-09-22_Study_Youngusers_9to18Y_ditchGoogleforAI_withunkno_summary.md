## Summary
This article discusses a comprehensive literature review conducted by researchers from NTNU’s Department of Teacher Education and various Czech universities regarding the impact of generative AI on children's cognitive development. The study analyzed 173 international research articles to determine if AI tools strengthen or weaken critical thinking and problem-solving skills in students aged 9 to 18.

## Key Takeaways
- **The Dual Nature of AI:** Researchers found that AI is neither inherently beneficial nor detrimental; rather, its impact depends entirely on how it is utilized. It can serve as a constructive tool for exploring different viewpoints or as a shortcut that leads to superficial thinking and overdependence.
- **Lack of Longitudinal Data:** A significant finding is the current lack of solid knowledge regarding how AI affects younger children specifically, as these tools are being integrated into their lives while they are still in the critical stage of developing foundational cognitive skills.
- **Research Limitations:** The existing studies on this topic are currently not considered fully objective and suffer from a strong regional imbalance, meaning our understanding of the long-term effects of AI on early education is still quite limited.

## Context
The research arrives at a time when generative AI (such as ChatGPT) has rapidly permeated the educational landscape, moving from an experimental tool to a primary source of information for students. As younger generations increasingly turn to AI instead of traditional search engines like Google, the "thinking process" is being outsourced to machines earlier in life than ever before.

## Implications
For the field of education and AI development, these findings highlight a critical need for "pedagogical guardrails." Because the technology has a dual effect, the focus must shift from simply providing access to AI toward teaching students how to use it as a scaffold for inquiry rather than a replacement for thought. If left unchecked, the risk of children losing the ability to engage in independent problem-solving could have long-lasting consequences on human cognitive development and academic integrity.
