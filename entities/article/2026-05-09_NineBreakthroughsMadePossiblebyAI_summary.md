## Summary  
This article highlights nine AI‑driven breakthroughs that are reshaping science, education, safety, health, and the arts at UC San Diego. It focuses on three major health advances: an AI model that reveals how a gene (PHGDH) contributes to Alzheimer’s disease; a machine‑learning tool called MycoBCP that accelerates the search for tuberculosis treatments by detecting subtle bacterial changes; and AI‑based reconstruction of heart muscle activity from external signals, enabling non‑invasive cardiac monitoring. Together these examples illustrate how artificial intelligence is turning complex biological problems into actionable solutions.

## Key Takeaways  
- AI uncovers hidden disease mechanisms, such as the “moonlighting” role of PHGDH in Alzheimer’s pathology.  
- Deep learning combined with existing microbiology methods creates MycoBCP, a first‑ever machine‑learning approach to bacterial imaging that speeds TB drug discovery.  
- AI reconstructs intracellular heart cell activity from external signals, opening new avenues for real‑time cardiac monitoring.

## Context  
Artificial intelligence is increasingly integrated into biomedical research, moving beyond simple data classification to generate mechanistic insights and novel therapeutic tools. The Gates Foundation’s funding of the MycoBCP project underscores growing industry interest in AI‑enabled public health solutions that address global disease burdens like tuberculosis.

## Implications  
These breakthroughs promise faster diagnosis, more effective treatments, and earlier detection for Alzheimer’s and TB, potentially saving millions of lives. For the broader field, they demonstrate that AI can bridge gaps between high‑level scientific theory and practical medical applications, accelerating innovation cycles and reshaping how health challenges are tackled worldwide.
