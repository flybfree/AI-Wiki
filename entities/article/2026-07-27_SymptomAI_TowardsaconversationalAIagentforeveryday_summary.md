## Summary  
SymptomAI is a conversational AI agent that conducts end‑to‑end symptom interviews and differential diagnostic assessments, evaluated in a large‑scale randomized study of 13,917 participants. The research compares the AI’s diagnoses to those of real clinicians and to physiological data from Fitbit wearables, showing that AI‑generated infectious disease labels often align with immune‑response trends observed beforehand.

## Key Takeaways  
- SymptomAI demonstrates diagnostic accuracy comparable to human clinicians when using everyday patient language, not just curated vignettes.  
- The system’s predictions for infectious diseases correlate with measurable physiological changes captured by wearables, indicating a biologically grounded decision process.  
- Deploying conversational AI in real‑world settings can overcome geographic and financial barriers that limit access to traditional doctor visits.

## Context  
Current large language models excel at differential diagnosis on synthetic or highly detailed medical cases but struggle with the messy, incomplete, and linguistically variable symptom reports of everyday patients. Existing benchmarks fail to capture how patients actually speak about their health, limiting confidence in deploying AI for routine symptom checking across diverse populations.

## Implications  
This study proves that conversational AI can be a viable tool for early symptom triage, potentially expanding preventive care reach while providing clinicians with data‑driven insights from both patient narratives and wearable biosignals. The findings suggest a shift toward integrating AI agents into primary care workflows to reduce barriers and improve diagnostic timeliness.
