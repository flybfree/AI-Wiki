## Summary  
SymptomAI presents a conversational AI agent built on Gemini Flash 2.0 that conducts end‑to‑end symptom interviews and differential diagnostic assessments for a large, real‑world study of 13,917 participants. The research compares the AI’s diagnoses against those made by clinicians and physiological data from Fitbit wearables to evaluate performance in everyday clinical contexts.

## Key Takeaways  
- SymptomAI achieves clinically useful differential diagnosis accuracy on a national scale, outperforming many existing language models that rely on synthetic vignettes.  
- The AI’s predictions align with participants’ later diagnoses by healthcare providers and coincide with physiological trends indicating immune activation, suggesting strong real‑world relevance.  
- The study demonstrates that conversational AI can bridge the gap between curated benchmarks and everyday patient interactions, handling varying medical literacy and incomplete symptom reports.

## Context  
Current large language models excel at differential diagnosis when evaluated on highly curated case studies, yet these evaluations often ignore the messiness of real conversations—such as patients’ limited health knowledge or fragmented symptom reporting. This research fills that gap by deploying an AI agent in a live, national‑scale setting, providing empirical evidence of performance under naturalistic conditions.

## Implications  
The findings suggest that conversational AI can be integrated into primary care workflows to triage symptoms and flag potential conditions, potentially reducing unnecessary visits while supporting early detection. For the industry, this work validates the commercial promise of symptom‑focused chatbots and encourages further investment in real‑world validation studies that combine clinical outcomes with physiological data.
