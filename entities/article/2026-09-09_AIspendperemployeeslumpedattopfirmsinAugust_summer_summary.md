## Summary  
AI spend per employee at the top 1% of firms fell sharply in August, dropping about 10 % to $7,205. The decline is driven by lower token costs from price cuts by OpenAI and Anthropic, which have made AI tools cheaper but also slowed uptake of the most advanced frontier models. While overall adoption remains modest, the data suggest a potential warning sign for companies that rely heavily on early‑stage revenue to recoup massive AI infrastructure investments.

## Key Takeaways  
- [Critical point 1] AI spend per employee in the top 1% of firms fell nearly 10 % to $7,205.  
- [Critical point 2] Token costs have dropped to $0.68 per million tokens after OpenAI and Anthropic cut prices, encouraging cheaper usage but discouraging adoption of newer frontier models.  
- [Critical point 3] Only 6.4 % of AI‑spending businesses used model‑serving or inference platforms in August, indicating limited scaling.

## Context  
The article notes that the slowdown coincides with a period when many companies are on vacation, but it also points to broader market forces: competition between OpenAI and Anthropic is driving down prices and making AI more accessible. A separate U.S. Census survey shows only 22 % of businesses report using AI, suggesting that Ramp’s spending data may overstate adoption because its client base is tech‑focused. The summer lull could be a temporary dip rather than a permanent trend.

## Implications  
For model builders and hyperscalers with hundreds of billions in chip orders, the reduced spend signals that early revenue capture may not materialize as expected, threatening their business models. Labs must prioritize winning over non‑technical users through co‑working tools to sustain growth. The broader industry faces a shift where lower costs could accelerate adoption but also flatten the premium revenue streams that currently fund AI development.
