## Summary  
Rijul Dahiya’s “The Architecture of Machine Learning Systems” opens with a focus on the mathematical foundations—representation learning and non‑linear function approximation—that underpin modern deep learning. He explains how DL frameworks abstract away the intricacies of differentiation and computation graphs, allowing practitioners to concentrate on high‑level model design rather than low‑level calculus.

## Key Takeaways  
- Deep learning systems are built on mathematical principles of representation learning and non‑linear function approximation.  
- Modern frameworks hide complex gradient calculations and graph structures, simplifying implementation for developers.  
- The architecture begins with these core concepts before moving to higher‑level system design.  

## Context  
Representation learning is a central theme in contemporary AI research, enabling machines to extract meaningful features from raw data. Deep learning frameworks such as TensorFlow and PyTorch have become industry standards because they provide the computational scaffolding needed for large‑scale training while preserving the theoretical underpinnings of non‑linear approximation.

## Implications  
Understanding this architecture matters because it directly influences model efficiency, scalability, and interpretability in real‑world applications. By recognizing how abstraction layers work, engineers can optimize training pipelines, reduce resource consumption, and ultimately deliver more reliable AI systems across sectors such as healthcare, finance, and autonomous vehicles.
