## Summary  
Artificial intelligence is driving rapid, transformative breakthroughs across multiple domains at UC San Diego, from uncovering the molecular cause of Alzheimer’s to accelerating tuberculosis drug discovery and enabling unprecedented visualizations inside heart cells. These advances illustrate how AI not only augments existing research but also creates entirely new scientific possibilities that were once considered speculative.

## Key Takeaways  
- AI enables precise identification of disease‑causing genes, such as PHGDH in Alzheimer’s, by modeling protein 3D structures and revealing hidden functional roles.  
- Deep learning combined with traditional bacterial cytological profiling yields the first machine‑learning tool for detecting subtle changes in drug‑resistant tuberculosis cells, promising faster therapeutic development.  
- AI reconstructs intracellular heart‑cell activity from external signals, opening new avenues for real‑time cardiac monitoring and disease detection.

## Context  
The rapid integration of artificial intelligence into biomedical research reflects a broader industry trend where machine learning accelerates hypothesis generation, data analysis, and experimental design across health sciences. Funding bodies like the Gates Foundation are increasingly supporting AI‑driven projects that address global health challenges, underscoring a shift toward collaborative, technology‑enhanced discovery pipelines.

## Implications  
These breakthroughs signal that AI will become an indispensable partner in drug development, genetic research, and medical diagnostics, reducing timelines from years to months. For the field, it means greater precision medicine, earlier disease detection, and more effective public health interventions—ultimately reshaping how humanity tackles complex biological problems.
