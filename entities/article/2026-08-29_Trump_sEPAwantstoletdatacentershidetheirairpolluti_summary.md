## Summary  
The Trump administration’s EPA is proposing to eliminate the federal public‑participation requirement for air permits, moving the burden of notification and community input to state and local agencies. Advocates warn that this change could allow data‑center developers to obtain permits in secret, bypassing residents who might be affected by construction or emissions.

## Key Takeaways  
- The EPA’s proposed rule would scrap the 1970s New Source Review public notice process for “minor” polluters, including data centers.  
- By delegating permitting decisions to state agencies, developers could avoid public scrutiny and community opposition.  
- Critics argue that removing federal oversight undermines transparency and environmental protection.

## Context  
The rise of generative AI has spurred a surge in new data‑center construction, which often competes with nearby residential areas for land and power. Historically, the EPA’s New Source Review rule required developers to inform the public before building facilities that emit pollutants, ensuring community awareness and input. The proposed change reflects broader federal efforts to streamline permitting, but it also shifts responsibility away from Washington to state officials who may lack resources or political will to enforce strict standards.

## Implications  
If data centers can operate under secret permits, the environmental impact of their energy‑intensive operations could be less visible and regulated, potentially leading to higher local air pollution without adequate safeguards. This loophole might accelerate the deployment of AI infrastructure at the expense of community health and long‑term sustainability goals for the broader tech industry.
