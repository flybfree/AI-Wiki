## Summary  
Google has unveiled its new WeatherNext 3 AI weather model, which delivers hourly forecasts with five‑times higher spatial and temporal resolution than the previous version. The update leverages real‑time satellite observations to improve rain and snow predictions by up to 50 % for locations lacking ground stations.

## Key Takeaways  
- [The new model produces a global picture five times sharper than Google’s earlier WeatherNext 2, using a 5‑kilometer grid versus the previous 25‑kilometer grid.]  
- [Hourly forecasts are generated from fresh satellite data, enabling faster predictions and better accuracy for fast‑moving weather systems.]  
- [The AI model fills gaps in remote regions where ground observations are sparse, boosting precipitation forecast reliability by up to half a day ahead.]

## Context  
Weather forecasting has traditionally relied on supercomputers that simulate atmospheric physics with inherent time lags. AI models such as Google’s WeatherNext 3 aim to accelerate and sharpen predictions by recognizing patterns in historical data while integrating live satellite inputs, offering a hybrid approach that combines physics‑based simulation with machine learning.

## Implications  
This advancement could transform how industries—from agriculture to renewable energy planning—rely on weather forecasts, providing more precise short‑term outlooks that reduce operational risk and improve resource allocation. As AI continues to embed itself in real‑time data streams, such models may set a new benchmark for accuracy and responsiveness across the global forecasting ecosystem.
