## Summary  
TerraPower’s molten‑salt‑cooled reactor is touted as a “secret weapon” that can keep AI data centers running without the need for large battery banks or frequent ramping of nuclear output. By storing excess heat in a giant vat of molten sodium, the plant can instantly generate steam when demand spikes, smoothing out the rapid power swings typical of GPU‑heavy workloads. This thermal‑storage approach leverages nuclear’s high capacity factor (≈92 %) while addressing the ramp‑rate limits that plague conventional SMRs.

## Key Takeaways  
- The reactor stores surplus heat in molten sodium, allowing it to supply steam on demand without changing its core power output.  
- This thermal storage compensates for the fast‑ramping capability of small modular reactors (SMRs) and reduces reliance on costly battery banks for data‑center loads.  
- By keeping the plant at peak capacity most of the time, TerraPower can amortize its high capital expenditure over many operational hours.

## Context  
AI data centers demand a stable, always‑on power supply because GPU training and inference cause abrupt load spikes that strain natural‑gas turbines and make large battery storage expensive. Renewable sources like wind and solar are also intermittent, creating a mismatch between generation and consumption. Nuclear reactors traditionally provide baseload electricity with a high capacity factor, but their slow ramp rates limit flexibility; SMRs improve this to about 10 % per minute, yet still require additional resources to handle the rapid load changes typical of AI workloads.

## Implications  
If successful, TerraPower’s thermal‑storage reactor could lower the overall cost and carbon intensity of powering AI infrastructure, shifting the industry away from massive battery arrays toward more efficient heat‑based solutions. This would accelerate the adoption of small modular reactors on the grid, improve grid stability for high‑density data centers, and support the broader goal of decarbonizing computing while keeping energy prices competitive.
