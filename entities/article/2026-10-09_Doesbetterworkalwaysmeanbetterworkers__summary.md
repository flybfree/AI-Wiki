## Summary
A three-month randomized controlled trial involving practicing patent attorneys reveals that while routine AI usage consistently improves work quality, its impact on professional skill development varies significantly by seniority. Senior lawyers demonstrated stronger independent judgment after using the tool, whereas junior lawyers showed no average skill gain, with their performance scores splitting into higher and lower outcomes. This suggests that AI assistance does not uniformly enhance human expertise across all experience levels.

## Key Takeaways
- **Divergent Learning Outcomes by Seniority:** The study found that senior attorneys who used an AI patent writing assistant for 90 days exhibited stronger unassisted judgment compared to those who did not use the tool. In contrast, junior lawyers showed no average improvement in independent skills; instead, their performance distribution widened, resulting in both higher and lower scores when graded without AI assistance.
- **Immediate Productivity Gains:** Regardless of seniority, access to the AI tool led to immediate improvements in drafting quality. At the 10-day mark, AI-assisted lawyers achieved a 0.34 standard deviation increase in drafting scores, equivalent to a 10-point rise in percentile rankings, as evaluated by independent legal experts using Likert scales.
- **Methodological Rigor:** The experiment addressed common gaps in AI research by combining sustained usage over months, evaluation of unassisted judgment, and blinded grading by domain experts. This approach distinguishes between temporary "exoskeleton" boosts that disappear when tools are removed and genuine long-term skill acquisition.

## Context
This research addresses a critical tension in the integration of AI into professional workflows: whether AI serves as a tool for genuine expertise building or merely a crutch for immediate output. Previous studies have shown mixed results, with some indicating improved performance for less-experienced workers when AI is embedded in training, while others demonstrate that gains often vanish once the tool is removed. By focusing on intellectual property law—a highly expertise-intensive field—the study provides a controlled environment to test these hypotheses over a sustained period rather than short-term tasks.

## Implications
The findings suggest that organizations cannot assume uniform benefits from AI deployment across all employee tiers. For senior professionals, AI may act as a catalyst for refining judgment and enhancing independent capabilities. However, for junior employees, reliance on AI may hinder the development of foundational skills or create inconsistent performance outcomes. This implies that firms must tailor AI integration strategies based on employee experience levels, potentially requiring different training protocols or supervision structures to ensure that AI tools contribute to long-term expertise rather than just short-term productivity.
