## Summary  
DesktopFly is a macOS desktop application that visualizes a three‑dimensional fruit fly whose behavior is generated by a live spiking simulation of the real FlyWire connectome. The system renders 23,210 neuron soma positions and 668 neurons running a 1 kHz leaky‑integrate‑and‑fire circuit with ~19,000 signed synaptic connections, allowing the fly to walk, groom, sleep, and flee when its cursor approaches. Interaction is achieved by clicking regions of the brain window, which triggers real neuron spikes and produces the corresponding action.

## Key Takeaways  
- [The 3D fly’s body movements are driven solely by a small neural circuit (≈668 neurons) with thousands of real synaptic connections, illustrating how complex behavior can emerge from a compact connectome.]  
- [Interaction is permission‑free and runs in real time on macOS, using the cursor as a looming stimulus that activates the Giant Fiber escape neuron within ~4 ms, mirroring actual fly physiology.]  
- [The brain window is fully interactive: hovering pauses rotation, clicking stimulates nearby neurons, and the resulting behavior follows the exact downstream circuit logic.]

## Context  
This project sits at the intersection of neuroscience and AI/ML research, demonstrating a bridge between biological connectomes and computational modeling. By using FlyWire’s open‑source connectome data to drive a real‑time simulation on consumer hardware, it showcases neuromorphic principles—where artificial neurons mimic biological spiking dynamics—without requiring specialized hardware. The work also highlights the potential of open‑source neurodata to power interactive AI experiences.

## Implications  
The integration of live neural simulations into desktop applications could inspire brain‑computer interfaces and edge‑AI systems that operate with low latency and minimal energy, echoing real animal behavior. It may encourage further research on how small, well‑characterized circuits can generate rich, adaptive actions, offering a template for future AI models that prioritize biological plausibility over raw performance.
