## Summary
Inherent, a London-based AI startup founded by former Google DeepMind employees, claims its newly released agent, Faraday, outperformed larger frontier models from Anthropic and OpenAI in replicating scientific research findings. Despite using a significantly smaller model with only 27 billion parameters, Faraday demonstrated superior performance on tasks requiring independent reproduction of published results without prior knowledge of the answers. The company emphasizes that its success stems not just from accuracy but from cultivating "research taste" through reinforcement learning, aiming to build AI capable of discovering new scientific knowledge rather than merely verifying existing data.

## Key Takeaways
- **Efficiency Over Scale:** Faraday outperformed much larger models like Claude Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.5 by utilizing a compact Qwen 3.6 model with 27 billion parameters, challenging the assumption that superior performance requires massive parameter counts or higher training costs.
- **Focus on "Research Taste":** Inherent prioritizes teaching agents to discern which experiments are worth conducting and how to design them effectively, using reinforcement learning to reward good outcomes rather than hard-coding rules, aiming for generalization across scientific fields.
- **Collaborative AI Design:** The company avoids building proprietary coding tools, instead leveraging existing software like OpenAI’s GPT-5.5 Codex, and aims to create agents that act as proactive teammates who independently investigate questions and present findings, rather than passive assistants that only answer user queries.

## Context
This development occurs within a competitive landscape where many startups founded by DeepMind alumni have remained in stealth mode or focused on theoretical advancements without concrete public demonstrations. Inherent emerged from stealth with a $50 million seed round, distinguishing itself by releasing a tangible product early. The article highlights a shift in AI research strategies, moving away from brute-force scaling toward more efficient, taste-driven models that mimic the iterative and curious nature of human scientific inquiry. This aligns with broader industry trends seeking to make AI agents more autonomous and scientifically rigorous, potentially reducing the computational barriers to entry for advanced AI capabilities.

## Implications
The success of Faraday suggests that smaller, efficiently trained models can compete with frontier-scale systems in specialized domains like scientific replication, which could democratize access to high-performance AI tools. By focusing on "research taste" and collaborative workflows, Inherent is pushing the field toward AI agents that function as genuine scientific partners rather than static knowledge bases. This approach may influence how future AI systems are trained, emphasizing reinforcement learning for generalization over rule-based programming. Furthermore, it challenges the dominant narrative in AI development that larger models are inherently superior, potentially encouraging a shift toward more resource-efficient and scientifically grounded AI architectures.
