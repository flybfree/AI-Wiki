## Summary  
Anthropic’s Claude autonomously generated the first end‑to‑end computer‑checked proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem (FLT) in 11 days, producing a Lean formalization that contains 13 million lines and proves 29 500 intermediate theorems. The result demonstrates that AI can now produce robust, multi‑layered mathematical artefacts without human supervision, offering a new benchmark for the verification of complex proofs.

## Key Takeaways  
- **Rapid autoformalization:** Claude produced a complete FLT proof in 11 days, far faster than the traditional months‑long effort required by humans.  
- **Robust Lean artefacts:** The proof spans 13 million lines of Lean code and validates 29 500 intermediate theorems, showing that AI‑generated formal artifacts are reliable enough to be built upon.  
- **Verification as a new frontier:** This work shifts the focus from generating novel mathematics (as in recent AI‑driven Riemann hypothesis attempts) to automatically checking proofs, which could streamline validation of mathematical results.

## Context  
The effort builds on a multi‑year community initiative launched in 2024 by Kevin Buzzard at Imperial College London to formalize Wiles’s FLT proof using the Lean proof assistant. Anthropic researcher Tianyi Peng tested whether Claude could contribute, and the outcome exceeded expectations, delivering an autonomous, end‑to‑end formalization that integrates algebra, harmonic analysis, geometry, and number theory—areas previously requiring extensive manual encoding.

## Implications  
If AI can reliably produce such large, layered proofs, it will dramatically reduce the time mathematicians spend verifying results, potentially accelerating research and lowering the risk of errors. This could usher in an era where the entire body of mathematical knowledge is routinely checked by automated systems, fostering greater confidence in the field’s foundations and enabling faster dissemination of new theorems.
