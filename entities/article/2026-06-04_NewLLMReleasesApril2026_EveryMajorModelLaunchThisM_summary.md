## Summary  
April 2026 saw a fierce “two‑horse race” for the public‑API top spot between OpenAI’s GPT‑5.5 (released April 23) and Anthropic’s Claude Opus 4.7 (released April 16). The article outlines the pricing, performance metrics, upcoming releases of other models such as Gemma 4 open weights, GLM‑5.1, and Meta’s Llama 4 variants, while noting that Claude Mythos remains a preview and GPT‑6 has not materialized.  

## Key Takeaways  
- **Pricing shift:** OpenAI doubled GPT‑5.4’s cost to $30 out per 1 M tokens, whereas Anthropic kept Claude Opus 4.7 at $25 out per 1 M tokens, making Claude the cheaper high‑performing option for many workloads.  
- **Claude Mythos is still a preview:** The gated “Mythos” model (April 7) remains in development with no public launch date, while Opus 4.7 is the current production release.  
- **Open‑source momentum:** Google’s Gemma 4 series (31B dense and MoE variants) and Zhipu AI’s GLM‑5.1 are fully open‑weighted and free, offering competitive alternatives to proprietary APIs.  

## Context  
The rapid cadence of model releases in early 2026 reflects the industry’s shift toward incremental capability gains rather than dramatic leaps; OpenAI’s “GPT‑6” narrative is a marketing placeholder for GPT‑5.5. Meanwhile, open‑source models are gaining traction with permissive licenses (Apache 2.0) and free access, challenging the dominance of closed proprietary APIs.  

## Implications  
For developers, the price differential between GPT‑5.5 and Claude Opus 4.7 influences cost‑sensitive projects, while open‑source alternatives provide flexibility and lower overhead but may lack the same performance guarantees. The continued rollout of models like Llama 4 Scout/Maverick underscores Meta’s push to democratize large‑language‑model access on consumer hardware (e.g., Fazm). Overall, the month signals a maturing market where speed, cost, and openness are converging forces shaping AI adoption across enterprises and startups.
