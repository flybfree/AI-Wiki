## Summary  
OpenAI is expanding its free “ChatGPT for Teachers” program to 55 additional U.S. school districts, bringing the tool to more than 100,000 educators and staff across 30 states. The initiative also introduces a 16‑state National Data Privacy Agreement that creates a uniform privacy framework, allowing districts to adopt ChatGPT for Teachers without negotiating separate contracts each time.

## Key Takeaways  
- OpenAI is reaching over 300,000 educators through new district partnerships in 20 states.  
- A shared 16‑state data‑privacy agreement streamlines compliance and reduces legal friction among districts.  
- ChatGPT for Teachers remains a free, teacher‑only tool available until June 2028.

## Context  
The rollout reflects the growing expectation that AI tools in education must be both accessible and privacy‑compliant. By partnering with large public school systems—including one‑fifth of America’s top 20 districts—the program demonstrates how commercial AI can scale responsibly within K–12 settings, leveraging existing state‑level privacy processes.

## Implications  
This expansion signals a shift toward standardized, district‑wide AI governance models that balance innovation with legal safeguards. For the broader education sector, it sets a precedent for how private AI providers can embed privacy infrastructure into public school ecosystems, potentially influencing future procurement policies and trust in AI‑enabled learning tools.
