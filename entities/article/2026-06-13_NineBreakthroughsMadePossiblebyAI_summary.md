## Summary  
UC San Diego researchers are leveraging artificial intelligence to solve pressing medical challenges, from uncovering the genetic roots of Alzheimer’s disease to creating AI‑driven tools that could accelerate tuberculosis treatment. These breakthroughs demonstrate how machine learning can transform biomedical research and translate into tangible therapies for patients worldwide.

## Key Takeaways  
- AI reveals PHGDH as a hidden driver of Alzheimer’s by modeling 3D protein structures, exposing its “moonlighting” role in gene regulation.  
- The Pogliano lab developed MycoBCP, an AI‑powered platform that detects subtle changes in tuberculosis cells, speeding up drug discovery for drug‑resistant strains.  
- Engineers used AI to reconstruct intracellular heart‑muscle activity from external signals, enabling real‑time monitoring of cardiac health.

## Context  
The rapid integration of deep learning into biomedical research reflects a broader industry shift where AI serves as both a diagnostic assistant and an experimental catalyst. Companies are increasingly adopting similar approaches to compress timelines for drug development, reduce reliance on animal models, and uncover hidden biological mechanisms that were previously invisible to traditional methods.

## Implications  
These advances matter because they promise earlier disease detection, more effective treatments, and cost‑effective research pipelines that could be scaled across global health systems. For the AI field, they highlight the growing relevance of domain‑specific models in life sciences, encouraging investment in interdisciplinary collaborations between computer scientists and clinicians to keep pace with emerging medical needs.
