## Summary
Zach Yadegari, the 19-year-old founder of the AI-powered calorie tracking app Cal AI, revealed at TechCrunch Disrupt that his company’s rapid rise to viral fame and $30 million in revenue was driven primarily by a strategic deployment of a "small army" of influencers. Yadegari detailed how Cal AI utilized a linear, performance-based payment model to recruit influencers, particularly targeting college students with tight-knit communities, which ultimately led to the app’s acquisition by MyFitness in March 2026.

## Key Takeaways
- **Influencer-Led Growth Strategy:** Cal AI’s success was not driven by traditional advertising but by recruiting a large volume of influencers, specifically targeting "broke college kids," who were paid based on quantifiable reach and user conversion metrics. This approach created a predictable, linear growth formula where each post reliably converted viewers into users.
- **Performance-Based Compensation Model:** Yadegari negotiated rates with influencers by promising specific payouts in exchange for guaranteed scale of reach. The strategy involved paying influencers to continue posting until they met specific user acquisition targets, ensuring cost-effective marketing and high ROI.
- **Rapid Exit and New Ventures:** After generating over $30 million in revenue in just two years, Cal AI was acquired by MyFitness. Yadegari has since launched a new AI agent startup, Persona, which has already raised $10 million and aims to integrate AI seamlessly into daily life through a hardware ecosystem.
