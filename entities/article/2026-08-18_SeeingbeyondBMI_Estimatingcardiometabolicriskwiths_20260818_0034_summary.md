## Summary  
PhotoScan is a deep‑learning system that extracts three‑dimensional body‑composition metrics—body fat percentage, android‑to‑gyneoid (A/G) ratio and visceral‑to‑subcutaneous (V/S) ratio—directly from ordinary smartphone photos. The model was trained on the UK Biobank and validated in a clinical cohort of 677 adults, achieving accuracy for insulin‑resistance prediction that rivals that of gold‑standard DXA scans. By linking these compositional biomarkers to HOMA‑IR scores, PhotoScan offers a non‑invasive, scalable way to flag early cardiometabolic risk before fasting glucose rises.

## Key Takeaways  
- PhotoScan can estimate body‑fat percentage and A/G/V/S ratios from 2D smartphone images with accuracy comparable to DXA.  
- The model predicts insulin resistance using HOMA‑IR thresholds, detecting risk years before clinical diabetes appears.  
- Unlike wearables or BIA sensors, PhotoScan provides structural compositional data that wearables cannot capture.

## Context  
The rise of AI‑driven health monitoring leverages everyday devices to collect multimodal signals. Smartphones already carry high‑resolution cameras, enabling passive imaging for body‑composition analysis. This approach complements continuous physiological tracking from wearables and routine lab tests, forming a holistic view of metabolic health that is currently limited by the need for expensive, radiation‑exposed DXA scans.

## Implications  
For clinicians, PhotoScan could enable point‑of‑care screening programs that are cost‑effective and radiation‑free. For the AI industry, it demonstrates how computer vision can be applied to medical diagnostics, opening pathways for large‑scale population health studies and early‑intervention interventions. The technology also supports regulatory pathways for non‑invasive imaging tools, potentially accelerating adoption of AI in preventive cardiometabolic care.
