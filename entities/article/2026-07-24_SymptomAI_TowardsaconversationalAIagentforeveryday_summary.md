## Summary  
SymptomAI is a conversational AI agent built on Gemini Flash 2.0 that conducts end‑to‑end symptom interviews and differential diagnostic assessments for everyday users. The project presented a randomized national study involving 13,917 participants who interacted with one of five experimental agents; the resulting diagnoses were later compared to clinician evaluations and wearable biosignal data.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Conversational AI can achieve diagnostic accuracy comparable to human clinicians in certain disease categories, especially infectious‑disease etiologies that align with physiological trends captured by Fitbit devices.  
- Real‑world patient conversations—replete with varying medical literacy and incomplete information—are better reflected than synthetic vignettes used in prior evaluations.  
- The system demonstrates the potential to bridge accessibility gaps in healthcare by providing a scalable, low‑cost symptom‑assessment tool that can be deployed at scale.  

## Context  
Current language models excel on curated medical case studies but often fail to model the messy, incomplete, and socially mediated nature of everyday patient reports. Barriers such as geographic access, cost, and systemic inequities limit traditional diagnostic interviews. SymptomAI addresses these gaps by operating within a national‑scale, in‑situ setting where participants naturally express symptoms during a chat interface.  

## Implications  
If validated further, SymptomAI could democratize preliminary health consultations, reducing the burden on clinicians and enabling earlier detection of conditions that may be missed without wearable data integration. However, its deployment must remain strictly as an assistive tool—not a replacement for professional diagnosis—to uphold ethical standards and patient safety.
