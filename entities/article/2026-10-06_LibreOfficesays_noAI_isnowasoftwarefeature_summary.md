## Summary
The Document Foundation, the nonprofit behind the open-source software LibreOffice, has officially declared that it will not integrate artificial intelligence features into its default software installation for the foreseeable future. This decision is framed as a deliberate design choice to prioritize user privacy and data security, ensuring that documents remain on local devices rather than being uploaded to remote servers for processing. While the organization does not reject AI technology entirely, it asserts that current AI integrations fail to meet its strict criteria for data sovereignty and independence from single providers.

## Key Takeaways
- **Privacy-First Design Philosophy**: LibreOffice’s refusal to include built-in AI is driven by a commitment to keeping user data local. The organization emphasizes that for organizations handling confidential or legally privileged data, the only verifiable assurance is that data never leaves the machine, a standard that current cloud-based AI services cannot guarantee.
- **Conditional Openness to AI**: The stance is not a permanent rejection of AI but a rigorous assessment of current technology. LibreOffice states there is currently "no integration" that satisfies its requirements, such as preventing reliance on a single AI provider or ensuring data stays on-device. However, users who wish to use AI can still do so by installing extensions that connect to local AI models.
- **Resistance to "Enshittification"**: The decision counters the industry trend of "enshittification," where software becomes bloated with unnecessary AI features like chatbots that degrade user experience. LibreOffice positions itself as an alternative that avoids these intrusive features, appealing to users who prioritize functionality and control over trendy but potentially invasive AI additions.

## Context
This announcement occurs within a broader industry landscape where major tech giants are aggressively embedding generative AI into productivity suites, often requiring network connections and data uploads to remote servers. LibreOffice, as a prominent open-source alternative to proprietary suites like Microsoft Office, represents a growing movement of software developers and users seeking to escape the ecosystem of Silicon Valley tech giants. The article highlights a growing tension between the convenience of cloud-based AI tools and the fundamental rights of users to control their own data and software environment.

## Implications
LibreOffice’s stance signals a potential shift in how open-source communities approach AI integration, prioritizing ethical and security considerations over feature parity with commercial competitors. By making "no AI" a core feature, LibreOffice may attract a significant demographic of privacy-conscious users, legal professionals, and organizations with strict data compliance requirements. This could pressure other software developers to reconsider the default inclusion of AI features and offer more transparent, local-first alternatives. Furthermore, it underscores the importance of data sovereignty in the AI era, suggesting that future AI adoption in professional software will depend heavily on meeting rigorous privacy standards rather than just offering convenience.
