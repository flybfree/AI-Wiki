## Summary  
The study shows that giving first‑year Bocconi students access to ChatGPT improves the quality and coherence of their marketing recommendations, while a separate causal‑reasoning training boosts originality but does not raise overall rubric scores. When both tools are combined, students achieve higher‑scoring work with more varied ideas, suggesting AI can complement—rather than replace—critical‑thinking development.

## Key Takeaways  
- ChatGPT access raises the professionalism of student outputs by adding more ideas and clearer logic.  
- Critical‑thinking training increases the diversity and distinctiveness of ideas but does not improve scores on a goal‑oriented rubric.  
- Combining AI with causal‑reasoning exercises yields the best results, highlighting the need for holistic assessment.

## Context  
This experiment reflects broader trends where generative AI is increasingly integrated into education to support student learning. Researchers at Bocconi University partnered with OpenAI Economic Research to test how AI tools and non‑technical thinking skills interact in a real‑world assignment, providing empirical evidence on their complementary effects.

## Implications  
For educators and institutions, the findings suggest that grading must evolve beyond measuring factual correctness or task completion; it should also evaluate originality and breadth of ideas. Policies that reward diverse thinking may better prepare students for innovation‑driven workplaces where AI handles routine tasks while humans focus on creative problem solving.
