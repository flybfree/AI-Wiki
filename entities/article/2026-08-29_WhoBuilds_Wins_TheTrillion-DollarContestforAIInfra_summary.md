## Summary  
The article argues that the decisive factor in the AI race is no longer model performance but the ability of private capital and corporations to build, finance, power, and scale the massive infrastructure required for AI deployment. While governments set strategic priorities, the real architects are technology firms and investors who dominate the trillion‑dollar infrastructure construction, turning capital markets into a primary instrument of geopolitical competition.  

## Key Takeaways  
- Private capital, not state funding, is the main driver behind the trillion‑dollar AI infrastructure buildout.  
- Capital markets now function as strategic instruments that can misalign with national interests if mismanaged.  
- The Pax Silica initiative highlights a potential alliance among states, but technology firms and investors remain the primary builders.  

## Context  
The broader AI landscape is shifting from model‑centric competition to infrastructure‑centric rivalry, mirroring trends in cloud computing and semiconductor supply chains where private investment fuels national advantage. This shift reflects how AI’s economic competitiveness is increasingly tied to who can secure compute capacity, data centers, and regional hubs.  

## Implications  
If capital markets continue to dominate infrastructure provision, policymakers risk creating a dependency that could undermine strategic autonomy; conversely, aligning private investment with national goals may unlock unprecedented scaling power for AI technologies worldwide.
